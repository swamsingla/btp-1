# 1.6.1 Subsets of set of real numbers

> **Grade 11** | **Chapter 1: SETS**

## Introduction  
Understanding subsets of real numbers is fundamental to building a strong foundation in mathematics. This section explores key subsets like natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, and irrational numbers, along with their relationships. By the end, you’ll be able to identify these subsets and analyze their hierarchies within the real number system.  

## Explanation  
### Key Concepts  
- **Natural Numbers (N):** The set of positive integers starting from 1: $ N = \{1, 2, 3, 4, \dots\} $.  
- **Integers (Z):** The set of whole numbers including negatives, zero, and positives: $ Z = \{\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\} $.  
- **Rational Numbers (Q):** Numbers expressible as $ \frac{p}{q} $, where $ p, q \in Z $ and $ q \neq 0 $. For example, $ \frac{7}{2} $ or $ -\frac{5}{1} $.  
- **Irrational Numbers (T):** Real numbers that are *not* rational, e.g., $ \sqrt{2} $, $ \pi $, or $ \sqrt[3]{5} $.  

> **Subset Relationships:**  
> $ N \subset Z \subset Q $, $ Q \subset R $, $ T \subset R $, and $ N \nsubseteq T $.  

> **Interval Notation (brief):**  
> For $ a, b \in R $ with $ a < b $, intervals like $ [a, b] $, $ (a, b) $, etc., represent subsets of $ R $.  

### Properties  
- Every natural number is an integer, but not vice versa.  
- Rational numbers include integers (since $ \frac{p}{1} $ is integer).  
- Irrational numbers cannot be written as fractions of integers.  

## Solved Examples  
**Example 1:** Determine whether $ \sqrt{9} $ belongs to $ N $, $ Z $, $ Q $, or $ T $.  
**Solution:**  
- $ \sqrt{9} = 3 $, which is in $ N $, $ Z $, and $ Q $.  

**Example 2:** Is $ \pi $ a member of $ Q $? Justify.  
**Solution:**  
- No. $ \pi $ is irrational (cannot be expressed as $ \frac{p}{q} $), so it belongs to $ T $.  

**Example 3:** Verify the relation $ N \subset Z $.  
**Solution:**  
- Every element of $ N $ (e.g., 1, 2, 3) is also in $ Z $, proving $ N \subset Z $.  

## Practice Problems  
1. 🟢 Identify the subset(s) containing $ -\frac{3}{2} $:  
   - (A) $ N $  
   - (B) $ Z $  
   - (C) $ Q $  
   - (D) $ T $  
   **Answer:** C  

2. 🟡 Prove $ Q \subset R $ using the definition of real numbers.  
   *(Hint: Real numbers include all rational and irrational numbers.)*  

3. 🔴 Which of the following is *not* true?  
   - (A) $ Z \subset Q $  
   - (B) $ T \subset R $  
   - (C) $ N \subset T $  
   - (D) $ Q \cup T = R $  
   **Answer:** C  

## Summary  
- $ N \subset Z \subset Q \subset R $; $ T $ is the complement of $ Q $ in $ R $.  
- Rational numbers include integers and fractions; irrationals cannot be expressed as fractions.  
- Interval notation defines subsets of $ R $ based on bounds.  

## Student Corner  
**Common Mistakes:**  
- Confusing $ \mathbb{Z} $ (integers) with $ \mathbb{N} $ (natural numbers). Remember $ \mathbb{N} $ excludes zero and negatives.  
- Assuming all real numbers are rational—irrationals like $ \pi $ exist!  

**Exam Tip:** Draw a Venn diagram to visualize subset relationships between $ N $, $ Z $, $ Q $, $ T $, and $ R $.
