# Human Respiratory System

We breathe in and out continuously to get oxygen and get rid of carbon dioxide. Breathing is the physical act of moving air in and out of the body. However, respiration is the chemical process that uses this oxygen to release energy inside the cells. Without breathing, a person usually cannot survive for more than a few minutes.

## The Pathway of Air

The respiratory system is the specific system in the body that handles breathing and respiration. Just as food follows a specific pathway through the digestive system, air follows a specific pathway through this system. The pathway through which the air is inhaled and exhaled involves various parts of the respiratory system assisting in the process of breathing and respiration.

The respiratory system begins with a pair of nasal openings called nostrils. We use these nostrils to inhale and exhale air. Once we breathe in, the air passes into a pair of small passages called the nasal passages. Have you noticed tiny hair inside your nostrils? These tiny hairs, along with mucus, help trap dust and dirt from the air we breathe in. This is why we should breathe through the nose and not through the mouth. 

From the nasal passages, the air reaches our lungs through the windpipe. The windpipe is a tube that carries the air from the throat down into the chest. The windpipe then forms two branches, which enter the two lungs.

## Inside the Lungs

Inside the lungs, these two branches further divide into smaller and finer branches. These branches keep splitting into tinier tubes that end in small balloon-like sacs called alveoli. The lungs are large organs that hold these alveoli. Our lungs are protected by the rib cage, which is the bony structure surrounding the chest.

## Gas Exchange

The respiratory system consists of various parts as shown in the human respiratory system diagram. In this system, the exchange of gases follows a specific pathway. The primary purpose of the alveoli is to allow gas exchange to happen. Oxygen from the inhaled air passes through the walls of the alveoli and into the blood. At the same time, carbon dioxide from the blood passes into the alveoli to be exhaled out of the body. This exchange of gases is the core function of respiration.

## Worked Example

Imagine a person is running on a playground. Because they are moving fast, they need a lot of energy. Their cells are working hard and producing a lot of carbon dioxide. The respiratory system responds by speeding up the breathing process. The person inhales more air through their nostrils. This extra air travels down the windpipe and into the lungs. It reaches the alveoli, where a large amount of oxygen quickly moves into the blood to provide energy. At the same time, the extra carbon dioxide moves out of the blood and into the alveoli to be breathed out. This shows how the specific pathway of the respiratory system is always working to keep the body supplied with the oxygen it needs.

## Key points

- The respiratory system is the specific system in the body that handles breathing and respiration, and the exchange of gases follows a specific pathway.
- The system begins at the nostrils, where tiny hairs and mucus trap dust and dirt from the inhaled air.
- Air travels from the nasal passages into the windpipe, which then forms two branches entering the two lungs.
- Inside the lungs, the branches divide into finer tubes that end in small balloon-like sacs called alveoli.
- The lungs are protected by the rib cage.
- The alveoli are the site where oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves the blood for exhalation.
