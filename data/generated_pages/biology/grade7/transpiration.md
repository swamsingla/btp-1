# Transpiration

Plants need water to live and grow. They take in water through their roots to use in photosynthesis. However, the plant cannot use all this water. A large amount of water escapes from the leaves into the air. This process of water loss from the leaves is called transpiration. It matters because it helps pull more water up from the roots to the leaves, keeping the plant's water transport system running smoothly.

## The Role of Stomata

Transpiration mainly happens through tiny pores on the surface of the leaves. These pores are called stomata. Stomata are small openings that allow gases to move in and out of the leaf. When water evaporates from these pores, it creates a pulling force. This pull draws water up through the stem from the roots. Without stomata, the plant would not be able to release the excess water it takes in.

## Factors Affecting Transpiration

Several things in the environment can change how much water a plant loses. One major factor is light. When there is bright sunlight, the stomata usually open wider to let in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. This opening allows more water to escape. Another factor is temperature. Hotter air can hold more water vapor, which can increase the rate of water loss from the leaves. Wind can also speed up transpiration by blowing away the moist air around the leaves, making it easier for more water to evaporate.

## The Water Transport System

Water enters the plant through the roots along with minerals from the soil. These minerals are very important nutrients for the growth of plants. Once inside the roots, the water travels up through the stems. It moves through thin tube-like structures to reach the leaves. The leaves are the primary site for photosynthesis where the plant makes its food. The water is essential for this process to happen.

## Worked Example

Imagine a plant absorbs 500 milliliters of water from the soil in one day. The plant uses about 50 milliliters of this water to build its tissues and perform photosynthesis. The rest of the water is released into the air through its leaves. To find out how much water is lost through transpiration, you subtract the water used from the total water absorbed. 

$500 - 50 = 450$

In this example, the plant loses 450 milliliters of water to transpiration.

## Key points

*   Transpiration is the loss of water from the leaves of a plant.
*   Stomata are the tiny pores on leaves through which water escapes.
*   Minerals are important nutrients taken up by the roots along with water.
*   The leaves are the primary site for photosynthesis.
*   The food prepared in the leaves is transported to all parts of the plant.
