# Human Digestive System

The human digestive system is a long tube that runs from your mouth to your anus. It breaks down the food you eat into tiny parts that your body can use for energy, growth, and repair. Without this system, you would not be able to get the nutrients you need from your meals.

## The Mouth and Oesophagus

When you eat, you use your teeth to break food into smaller pieces. Your saliva mixes with the food to make it soft and easy to swallow. The food then travels down a tube called the oesophagus. Muscle cells in the walls of the oesophagus contract and relax in a wave-like manner. This movement pushes the food down into the stomach.

## The Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ that churns the food. It has muscle cells in its wall that help mix and break down the food. Other cells in the inner lining of the stomach produce digestive juices and acid. These juices and acid help break down the food further so the body can absorb it.

## The Small and Large Intestine

After the stomach, the food moves into the small intestine. The small intestine is very long and has a large surface area. This helps the body absorb the nutrients from the food. The remaining waste then moves into the large intestine. The large intestine absorbs water from the waste. The leftover material is then passed out of the body.

## Worked example

The extracts explain that different cells in the digestive system have different shapes and structures that help them perform their specific functions. For example, muscle cells in the oesophagus are thin, flexible, and spindle-shaped. This shape allows them to contract and relax easily to push food down.

If a muscle cell in the oesophagus were round and rigid, it could not change its shape. It would be unable to push the food forward. The spindle shape is essential for this wave-like movement. This shows how the unique shape of a cell relates to its function.

## Key points
- The digestive system breaks down food into tiny parts for the body to use.
- Muscle cells in the oesophagus are thin, flexible, and spindle-shaped.
- The stomach has muscle cells that churn the food and cells that produce digestive juices and acid.
- Different parts of the digestive system are made up of different types of cells.
- The unique shape, size, and structure of cells help them carry out their specific functions.
