# Diversity of Organisms

Look around your neighbourhood. You will see many different plants and animals living together. This variety of life is called the diversity of organisms. It matters because this variety helps nature stay healthy. When there are many different living things, the environment can survive changes and stay balanced.

## What is a Habitat?

A habitat is simply a place where an organism lives. It can be a large forest or a small pond. It could even be just the bark of a tree. Every habitat has living things and non-living things. The non-living things are called abiotic components. Examples of abiotic components are air, water, soil, and sunlight.

## Living and Non-Living Interactions

Living organisms, or the biotic community, depend on abiotic components for their survival. For example, a plant needs sunlight and water to make its food. Animals also need these non-living things to stay alive. Plants and animals also depend on each other for nutrition, respiration, and reproduction. These are interactions among the biotic components. Both types of interactions are important for survival in any habitat.

## How Organisms Adapt

The plants and animals interact with each other and adapt to survive in the surrounding conditions they live in. Adaptation means that a living thing changes its habits or body to fit its habitat. For example, a fish has gills to breathe underwater. A bird has feathers to keep warm in cold weather. They do this so they can survive and grow in their specific habitats.

## The Biosphere

The Earth is full of life. This includes trees, shrubs, herbs, to animals, insects, and tiny organisms invisible to the naked eye. All living beings, along with the places where they live, make up the biosphere. This includes land, water, and air, where life interacts with its surroundings to survive and grow.

## Worked example

Imagine you find two different habitats near your school. The first habitat is a small garden. The second habitat is a muddy pond. In the garden, you see grass, butterflies, and earthworms. The grass is a living organism. The butterfly is a living organism. The earthworm is a living organism. The soil, water, and sunlight are the abiotic components. The grass uses sunlight and water to make food. The butterfly drinks nectar from the flowers. The earthworm lives in the soil.

Now look at the muddy pond. You see water, mud, and tiny green plants. You also see small fish and frogs. The water, mud, and sunlight are the abiotic components. The tiny green plants are living organisms. The fish and frogs are living organisms. The plants use sunlight to make food. The fish and frogs eat the plants or each other. Both the garden and the pond are habitats where living and non-living things interact to survive.

## Key points

* A habitat is simply a place where an organism lives, such as the bark of a tree.
* Abiotic components are non-living things like air, water, soil, and sunlight.
* Biotic components are the living organisms that depend on abiotic components to survive.
* Organisms adapt to their surrounding conditions so they can survive and grow.
* All living beings and their habitats together make up the biosphere.
