# Adolescence and Puberty

Adolescence and puberty are the years when a child's body and mind change to become an adult body and mind. These changes mark the beginning of a stage called adolescence, which is a natural part of growing up. It is important to understand these changes because they help your body function properly and prepare you for the future. By staying informed and making healthy decisions, you can build a strong foundation for your life ahead.

## The Role of Hormones

Many changes during adolescence, including menstruation and other signs of puberty, are mainly due to hormones. Hormones are certain chemicals produced in our bodies. They play a crucial role in regulating various aspects of growth and development, contributing to the proper functioning of the body. They are produced in different parts of the body and are released at an appropriate time in response to signals from the brain. Some hormones influence mood and behaviour. For example, you might feel more emotional or notice shifts in your energy levels. This is simply because your body is releasing these chemicals at different times to help you grow.

## The Menstrual Cycle

An important internal change associated with adolescent girls is the onset of the menstrual cycle. It recurs generally every 28–30 days and is more commonly known as 'the period'. Many healthy girls may have longer or shorter menstrual cycles ranging from 21–35 days. The menstrual cycle is an important natural process and is one of the signs of good reproductive health. The phase of the cycle when blood discharge occurs from the body is referred to as menstruation. It may last for three to seven days. Some girls may feel pain or discomfort in the lower abdomen during these days. Usually, by the age of 45–55, menstruation stops naturally, marking the end of the reproductive capability in a woman's life.

## Emotional and Behavioural Changes

Adolescence is not just about physical changes or changes associated with reproductive capability but also about emotional and behavioural changes. You might notice that you react differently to situations or feel more independent. These shifts are a normal part of the process of growing up. Both boys and girls experience such changes gradually, and these changes are a natural part of the process of growing up. It is helpful to talk to a trusted adult or friend when you feel overwhelmed by these new feelings.

## Breaking Myths About Menstruation

There are many wrong beliefs about menstruation that often lead to unnecessary fear, shame, or even guilt. These beliefs have given rise to certain myths and taboos. Among such myths and taboos, physical isolation of menstruating girls is unfortunately still quite prevalent. Menstruation is a natural process, and such myths have no scientific basis. By encouraging a scientific outlook towards menstruation, we can help society appreciate the need to promote good reproductive health of women and contribute positively towards a healthier lifestyle for women.

## Worked Example

Consider a girl who has just started puberty. Her menstrual cycle is 28 days long. She wants to know when her next period is likely to start if her last period began on the 1st of the month. To find this out, we look at the standard duration of the cycle. A typical cycle is 28–30 days. If her cycle is 28 days long, her next period will likely begin on the 29th of the month. If her cycle is 30 days long, it will begin on the 31st. This shows how the cycle length helps predict the timing of menstruation.

## Key points
- Adolescence is the stage of physical and emotional changes that leads to adulthood.
- Hormones are chemicals produced in the body that regulate growth, development, and mood.
- The menstrual cycle is a natural process occurring every 28–35 days, lasting 3 to 7 days.
- Menstruation marks the beginning of reproductive capability in females.
- Menstruation is a natural process with no scientific basis for myths or taboos.
