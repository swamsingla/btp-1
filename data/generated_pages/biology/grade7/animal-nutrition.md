# Nutrition in Animals

Nutrition in animals is the process by which living beings take in food, break it down into simpler substances, and use those substances to grow, repair their bodies, and release energy. Every animal, from the smallest amoeba to a large cow, needs a nutritious diet to perform well, especially during adolescence, which is a period of growth and development marked by several changes in the body. The food we eat provides essential nutrients like proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, and minerals. For example, calcium from milk, millets, curd, cheese, and paneer helps in optimal bone growth, while iron from spinach, kidney beans, and dried fruits like raisins and figs helps in the formation of blood.

## Feeding Mechanisms in Different Animals

Animals exhibit variations in the structure and function of the alimentary canal to adapt to different ways of digesting different kinds of food. Some animals, like birds, do not have teeth but instead have a muscular chamber called a gizzard. In the gizzard, food is broken down by the contraction and relaxation of the walls, often with the help of grit (small stones) that the birds swallow.

## Digestion in Amoeba

Amoeba is a microscopic organism that uses a process called phagocytosis to ingest its food. It extends its pseudopodia, which are temporary projections of its cell body, to surround a food particle like a bacterium or a tiny algae cell. Once the food is completely enclosed, it forms a food vacuole inside the cytoplasm. Inside this vacuole, digestive enzymes break down the complex food components into simpler, soluble forms that can be absorbed by the cell. The undigested waste is then expelled out of the cell through the cell membrane.

## Digestion in Grasshoppers

Grasshoppers are insects that have a distinct digestive system adapted for their diet of plant material. They possess a mouthpart called a mandible, which is used for cutting and chewing leaves. The chewed food then passes into the crop, which acts as a temporary storage area. From the crop, the food moves into the gizzard, where mechanical grinding occurs. After the gizzard, the food enters the midgut, where most of the chemical digestion takes place through the action of enzymes. The remaining undigested material then passes into the hindgut, where water is absorbed before the waste is finally excreted.

## Digestion in Ruminants

Cows and buffaloes are examples of ruminants, which are grass-eating animals. They partially chew the grass and swallow it into their stomachs. In the stomach, partial digestion of the food takes place. The partially digested food is then brought back to the mouth for gradual chewing in a process called rumination. A cow spends about 8 hours a day just chewing the food! The thoroughly chewed food then passes down the alimentary canal for further digestion.

## Worked Example

Consider a cow that has just swallowed a mouthful of grass. According to the rules of rumination, the food does not get fully digested in the first pass through the stomach. Instead, the cow will later regurgitate this partially digested food back into its mouth to chew it again. If a farmer observes a cow chewing continuously for several hours, it is not because the cow is eating more grass, but because it is actively practicing rumination to ensure the grass is thoroughly broken down before final digestion.

## Key points

- Nutrition in animals is the process of taking in food, breaking it down, and using it for growth, repair, and energy release.
- Different animals have adapted different feeding mechanisms, such as birds using a gizzard with grit to break down food without teeth.
- In amoeba, food is ingested by phagocytosis into a food vacuole for digestion.
- Grasshoppers use their mouthparts and a gizzard to mechanically break down plant matter.
- Ruminants like cows partially digest grass in their stomach and later regurgitate it to the mouth for thorough chewing, a process called rumination.
