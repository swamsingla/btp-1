# Diseases — Types

A disease is a condition that affects the normal working of the body or mind. It can happen when one or more organs or organ systems stop functioning properly. Understanding the different types of diseases helps us know how they spread and how to prevent them.

## Causes of Disease

Diseases can be caused by many different factors. Some diseases are caused by germs like bacteria, viruses, fungi, worms, or even by protozoa, which are single-celled organisms. These disease-causing organisms are called pathogens. Other diseases may result from poor nutrition or an unhealthy lifestyle. Some diseases, like scurvy, anaemia, and goitre, are caused by a lack of specific nutrients in the diet and are called deficiency diseases.

## Communicable and Non-Communicable Diseases

Diseases can be grouped into two major types based on their causes and how they spread:

Diseases caused by pathogens are called communicable diseases. They can spread from one person to another. Some examples are typhoid, dengue, flu, chickenpox, and COVID-19. These pathogens can enter our body through the air we breathe or by consuming contaminated food or water. One common way they spread is through the air, when an infected person coughs or sneezes, or through direct contact like shaking hands, or indirectly by sharing personal items of an infected person. Some pathogens are also spread by insects like mosquitoes and houseflies, which are called vectors.

Some diseases, like cancer, diabetes, or asthma, are not caused by pathogens and do not spread from one person to another. They are usually linked to lifestyle, diet, and/or environment. These are called non-communicable diseases.

## Short-Term and Chronic Diseases

The duration of a disease is another way to classify them. Some diseases last for a short time, while others can continue for a long time and need regular treatment or care.

Diseases such as cancer, diabetes, and asthma may often persist for a long time, more than 3 months, and are referred to as chronic diseases. Diabetes is a common disease which is becoming more prevalent in adults as well as children. It often develops due to a combination of hormonal imbalances, unhealthy eating habits, lack of physical activity, being overweight or obese, and other reasons.

## How to Prevent and Control Diseases

You might have heard the phrase ‘Prevention is better than cure.’ It is important to protect ourselves from both communicable and non-communicable diseases.

The natural ability of our body to fight diseases is known as immunity. Our body has a special system called the immune system that helps fight against diseases. You might have taken some drops or injections in your childhood to protect yourself from certain diseases, such as polio, measles, tetanus, and hepatitis. These are vaccines that help prevent serious infections caused by viruses and bacteria.

A vaccine helps our body fight certain diseases by training the immune system to recognise and attack harmful germs. This provides what is known as acquired immunity—protection developed after exposure to a pathogen or a vaccine.

## Worked Example

Let us look at two cases.

Imagine a student in your class who has the flu. The flu is a communicable disease caused by a virus, which is a type of pathogen. It can spread from one person to another through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes. It lasts for a short time.

To prevent the flu, the student should cover their mouth and nose while coughing or sneezing, wash their hands frequently, and not share personal items.

If another student in the same class develops diabetes, it is a non-communicable disease. It is not caused by pathogens and cannot spread from one person to another. It is a chronic disease that may persist for a long time and need regular treatment or care. It is often linked to lifestyle, diet, and/or environment. Diabetes often develops from unhealthy eating habits, lack of physical activity and being overweight, so eating a balanced diet and exercising regularly address some of its causes.

## Key points

- A disease is a condition that affects the normal working of the body or mind and can be caused by pathogens, poor nutrition, or an unhealthy lifestyle.
- Communicable diseases are caused by pathogens and can spread from one person to another through air, direct contact, or vectors like insects.
- Non-communicable diseases are not caused by pathogens and do not spread from one person to another; they are often linked to lifestyle, diet, and/or environment.
- Some diseases last for a short time, while chronic diseases persist for a long time, more than 3 months, and need regular treatment or care.
- Vaccines help prevent diseases by training the immune system to recognise and attack harmful germs, providing acquired immunity.
