# Cell Organelles

Every living cell is surrounded by a membrane that keeps its contents separate from the outside environment. Large and complex cells, like those in plants and animals, need many different chemical activities to survive. To keep these activities from interfering with each other, the cell uses tiny, membrane-bound structures called organelles. This internal organization is one of the main features that distinguishes eukaryotic cells from prokaryotic cells.

## The Cytoplasm and Internal Structure

When you look at a cell under a microscope, you will see three main features in almost every cell: the plasma membrane, the nucleus, and the cytoplasm. The cytoplasm is the fluid content inside the plasma membrane. It takes up very little stain in temporary mounts of cells, such as onion peel or human cheek cells. The cytoplasm contains many specialised cell organelles, and each of these performs a specific function for the cell. 

The significance of having internal membranes is highlighted by the example of viruses. Viruses lack any membranes and therefore do not show characteristics of life until they enter a living body and use its cell machinery to multiply.

## The Nucleus

The nucleus is one of the most important components of the cell. It is a membrane-bound organelle that houses the cell's genetic material and controls its activities. The nuclear membrane has tiny pores that allow materials to pass between the nucleus and the cytoplasm. Inside the nucleus are the chromosomes, which are made of DNA and protein, and a dense body called the nucleolus. In cells that lack a defined nucleus, such as bacteria, the genetic material lies in an irregular region called the nucleoid.

## The Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi Apparatus

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a large network of membrane-bound tubes and sheets. It comes in two kinds. The rough ER (RER) looks rough because ribosomes are attached to its surface, and these ribosomes make proteins. The smooth ER (SER) has no ribosomes and helps in the manufacture of fats and lipids. In the liver cells of vertebrates, the SER also helps to detoxify poisons and drugs. The ER also acts as a set of channels that carry material around inside the cell.

The Golgi apparatus, first described by Camillo Golgi, is a stack of flattened membrane-bound sacs. Materials made in the ER are sent to the Golgi apparatus, where they are modified, stored and packaged for dispatch to other parts of the cell or outside it. The Golgi apparatus also helps to make complex sugars and forms the lysosomes.

## Mitochondria

Mitochondria are often called the powerhouses of the cell. They release energy from food in the form of ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the energy currency of the cell, which the cell uses to make new chemicals and to do mechanical work. A mitochondrion has two membranes: the outer one is porous, while the inner one is deeply folded, which increases the surface area available for ATP-making reactions. Mitochondria have their own DNA and ribosomes, so they can make some of their own proteins.

## Lysosomes

Lysosomes are small membrane-bound sacs filled with digestive enzymes. They form the waste-disposal system of the cell: they break down foreign material that enters the cell, such as bacteria and food, as well as worn-out cell organelles. When a cell is damaged, the lysosomes may burst and the enzymes digest the cell itself. For this reason lysosomes are sometimes called the "suicide bags" of the cell.

## Plastids

Plastids are present only in plant cells. There are two types of plastids – chromoplasts (coloured plastids) and leucoplasts (white or colourless plastids). Chromoplasts containing the pigment chlorophyll are known as chloroplasts. Chloroplasts are important for photosynthesis in plants. Chloroplasts also contain various yellow or orange pigments in addition to chlorophyll. Leucoplasts are primarily organelles in which materials such as starch, oils and protein granules are stored.

The internal organisation of the Chloroplast consists of numerous membrane layers embedded in a material called the stroma. These are similar to mitochondria in external structure. Like the mitochondria, plastids also have their own DNA and ribosomes.

## Vacuoles

Vacuoles are membrane-bound storage sacs for solid or liquid contents. In animal cells they are small, but in plant cells they are very large and filled with cell sap, which gives the cell turgidity and rigidity. Plant vacuoles store useful substances such as amino acids, sugars, organic acids and some proteins. In a single-celled organism like Amoeba, the food vacuole holds the food the Amoeba has taken in, and in some unicellular organisms special vacuoles expel excess water and wastes.

## Ribosomes

Ribosomes are the sites where proteins are made. Unlike most other organelles, they are not enclosed by a membrane. Some ribosomes float freely in the cytoplasm, and others are attached to the rough ER. Ribosomes are found in prokaryotic as well as eukaryotic cells.

## Worked Example

Imagine you are looking at an onion peel under a microscope. You see a large region inside each cell that takes up very little stain: this is the cytoplasm. Each cell also has a plasma membrane around it and a nucleus. Now think about a pancreas cell that makes a protein for the body. The protein is first made by ribosomes on the rough ER, then passes to the Golgi apparatus, where it is modified and packaged, and the mitochondria supply the ATP that powers the whole process. If you looked at a leaf cell instead, you would also find chloroplasts, which are present only in plant cells, and a large central vacuole.

## Key points
- Every cell has a membrane around it to keep its own contents separate from the external environment.
- Large and complex cells use membrane-bound organelles to keep their many chemical activities separate from each other.
- The cytoplasm is the fluid content inside the plasma membrane, and it contains the organelles.
- The nucleus holds the genetic material and controls the cell's activities.
- The rough ER (with ribosomes) helps make proteins, and the smooth ER helps make fats and lipids.
- The Golgi apparatus modifies, stores and packages materials, and lysosomes digest waste and foreign material.
- Mitochondria release energy as ATP and have their own DNA and ribosomes.
- Plastids are present only in plant cells, and there are two types: chromoplasts and leucoplasts. Chloroplasts are important for photosynthesis.
- Vacuoles are storage sacs, very large in plant cells and small in animal cells.
- Ribosomes make proteins and are not enclosed by a membrane.
- Prokaryotes lack a defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotic cells have both.
- Viruses lack any membranes and do not show characteristics of life until they enter a living body and use its cell machinery to multiply.
