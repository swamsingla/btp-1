# Crop Variety Improvement

Crop variety improvement is the science of breeding plants to get better results for farmers and consumers. It involves selecting and crossing plants to create new strains that produce more food, resist diseases, and have better quality. This matters because it helps feed a growing population using the same amount of land. By improving the genetic makeup of crops, scientists ensure that farmers can grow reliable harvests even when conditions are difficult.

## Why We Need Better Crops

Different crops provide different nutrients for our bodies. Cereals such as wheat, rice, maize, millets and sorghum provide us carbohydrate for energy requirement. Pulses like gram (chana), pea (matar), black gram (urad), green gram (moong), pigeon pea (arhar), lentil (masoor), provide us with protein. And oil seeds including soyabean, ground nut, sesame, castor, mustard, linseed and sunflower provide us with necessary fats. Vegetables, spices and fruits provide a range of vitamins and minerals in addition to small amounts of proteins, carbohydrates and fats. 

Since weather conditions such as drought and flood situations are unpredictable, varieties that can be grown in diverse climatic conditions are useful. Similarly, varieties tolerant to high soil salinity have been developed. Cultivation practices and crop yield are related to weather, soil quality and availability of water. 

## The Goals of Variety Improvement

There are several main goals when scientists improve crop varieties. One major goal is to increase the amount of food produced per acre. This is called higher yield. 

Another goal is to improve the quality of the product. Quality considerations of crop products vary from crop to crop. Baking quality is important in wheat, protein quality in pulses, oil quality in oilseeds and preserving quality in fruits and vegetables.

A third goal is to make crops resistant to harmful living organisms and environmental conditions. Crop production can go down due to biotic (diseases, insects and nematodes) and abiotic (drought, salinity, water logging, heat, cold and frost) stresses under different situations. Varieties resistant to these stresses can improve crop production.

Scientists also try to change how long a crop takes to grow. The shorter the duration of the crop from sowing to harvesting, the more economical is the variety. Such short durations allow farmers to grow multiple rounds of crops in a year. Short duration also reduces the cost of crop production. Uniform maturity makes the harvesting process easy and reduces losses during harvesting.

Developing varieties for wider adaptability is another target. This helps in stabilising the crop production under different environmental conditions. One variety can then be grown under different climatic conditions in different areas.

Finally, scientists look for desirable agronomic characteristics. Tallness and profuse branching are desirable characters for fodder crops. Dwarfness is desired in cereals, so that less nutrients are consumed by these crops. Thus developing varieties of desired agronomic characters help give higher productivity.

## Methods of Creating New Varieties

One way of incorporating desirable characters into crop varieties is by hybridisation. Hybridisation refers to crossing between genetically dissimilar plants. This crossing may be intervarietal (between different varieties), interspecific (between two different species of the same genus) or intergeneric (between different genera).

Another way of improving the crop is by introducing a gene that would provide the desired characteristic. This results in genetically modified crops.

For new varieties of crops to be accepted, it is necessary that the variety produces high yields under different conditions that are found in different areas. Farmers would need to be provided with good quality seeds of a particular variety, that is, the seeds should all be of the same variety and germinate under the same conditions.

## Worked Example

Let us look at an example of how dwarfness helps in cereals. In cereals, dwarfness is desired because the crop then consumes fewer nutrients. 

Imagine a farmer has two plots of land. In the first plot, the farmer grows a tall variety of wheat. In the second plot, the farmer grows a dwarf variety of wheat. The farmer provides the exact same amount of water, soil quality, and sunlight to both plots. Because the dwarf variety uses fewer nutrients on its height, more of the available nutrients in the soil can go towards producing grain. So the dwarf variety is likely to give a higher grain yield per acre than the tall variety, even though both plots get the same care and resources.

## Key points
* Crop variety improvement focuses on breeding plants for higher yield, better quality, and disease or stress resistance.
* Crops are grouped by their main nutrient, such as cereals for carbohydrates, pulses for proteins, and oilseeds for fats.
* The main goals of improvement include higher yield, improved quality, resistance to pests and weather, shorter maturity duration, wider adaptability, and desirable physical traits.
* Hybridisation is the process of crossing genetically dissimilar plants, which can be done between different varieties, species, or genera.
* For a new variety to be successful, it must produce high yields under different conditions, and farmers must have access to uniform, high-quality seeds.
