# Photosynthesis

Plants, algae, and some bacteria are called autotrophs. This means they make their own food. They do not need to eat other organisms to get the energy they need to live and grow. Instead, they use a process called photosynthesis. In this process, they take in simple substances from their surroundings and convert them into stored energy. This stored energy takes the form of carbohydrates. These carbohydrates act as the plant's internal energy reserve, much like glycogen in the human body. Photosynthesis is the foundation of life on Earth because it provides the food and oxygen that nearly all other living things depend on.

## The Overall Chemical Reaction

At its core, photosynthesis is a chemical reaction. Autotrophs take in carbon dioxide and water as their raw materials. Using the energy from sunlight and a green pigment called chlorophyll, they convert these raw materials into glucose, which is a type of sugar or carbohydrate. The glucose produced, $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$, is used by the plant to provide energy for its daily functions. Any glucose that the plant does not use immediately is stored as starch. Starch serves as the internal energy reserve to be used as and when required by the plant. The chemical equation for this process is written as $6 \mathrm{CO_2} + 12 \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow [ \text{Sunlight} ]{\text{Chlorophyll}} \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6 \mathrm{O_2} + 6 \mathrm{H_2O}$.

## The Three Main Events

The process of photosynthesis does not happen all at once in a single step. It involves three distinct events. First, light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll. Second, the light energy is converted into chemical energy, and water molecules are split into hydrogen and oxygen. Third, carbon dioxide is reduced to form carbohydrates. These steps do not necessarily have to take place one after the other immediately. For example, desert plants take up carbon dioxide at night and prepare an intermediate compound. Then, during the day, they use the energy absorbed by chlorophyll to complete the reaction.

## The Role of Chlorophyll

Chlorophyll is the green pigment that makes photosynthesis possible. If you look at a cross-section of a leaf under a microscope, you will notice that some cells contain green dots. These green dots are cell organelles called chloroplasts, which contain chlorophyll. Chlorophyll is absolutely essential for the process. Scientists have proven this through simple experiments. For instance, if you take a plant with variegated leaves, which have both green and non-green areas, and keep it in a dark room for a few days, the plant will use up all of its stored starch. Then, if you place the plant in sunlight, the green areas will produce new starch, while the non-green areas will not. This happens because chlorophyll is only present in the green parts of the leaf.

## How Plants Take in Carbon Dioxide

For photosynthesis to occur, plants must take in carbon dioxide from the air. They do this through tiny pores on the surface of their leaves called stomata. Massive amounts of gaseous exchange take place in the leaves through these pores for the purpose of photosynthesis. However, these pores are a double-edged sword. While they allow carbon dioxide to enter, they also allow large amounts of water to escape from the plant. To prevent losing too much water, the plant closes these pores when it does not need carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. The opening and closing of the stomatal pore is controlled by specialized cells called guard cells. When water flows into the guard cells, they swell and the pore opens. If the guard cells shrink, the pore closes.

## The Importance of Sunlight

Sunlight is the energy source that powers the entire process. Without light, the chemical reaction cannot proceed, and the plant cannot produce its food. This is why a plant kept in a dark room for a few days uses up its stored starch, and makes new starch only when it is placed back in sunlight.

Carbon dioxide is also needed. In an experiment, one plant is kept in an environment without carbon dioxide, while another is kept in an environment with carbon dioxide. Only the plant exposed to carbon dioxide is able to produce starch. Together with the role of light, this shows that carbon dioxide and sunlight, along with water, are the raw requirements of photosynthesis.

## Water and Other Nutrients

While carbon dioxide and sunlight are the main ingredients for making food, plants also need other raw materials to build their bodies. Water used in photosynthesis is taken up from the soil by the roots in terrestrial plants. In addition to water, plants absorb other essential minerals from the soil, such as nitrogen, phosphorus, iron, and magnesium. Nitrogen is an essential element used in the synthesis of proteins and other compounds. It is taken up in the form of inorganic nitrates or nitrites, or as organic compounds prepared by bacteria from atmospheric nitrogen.

## Worked Example

Let us use the equation $6 \mathrm{CO_2} + 12 \mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6 \mathrm{O_2} + 6 \mathrm{H_2O}$ to count molecules. Suppose a leaf makes 10 molecules of glucose.

- Carbon dioxide used: each glucose needs 6 molecules of carbon dioxide, so $10 \times 6 = 60$ molecules.
- Oxygen released: each glucose comes with 6 molecules of oxygen, so $10 \times 6 = 60$ molecules.
- Water used: 12 molecules are used per glucose and 6 are formed again, so the net use is $10 \times (12 - 6) = 60$ molecules.

The carbon in the 10 glucose molecules (60 carbon atoms) comes entirely from the 60 carbon dioxide molecules, and the oxygen released comes from the splitting of water molecules. Any glucose not needed immediately is stored as starch.

## Key points

*   Photosynthesis is the process by which autotrophs convert carbon dioxide and water into carbohydrates using sunlight and chlorophyll.
*   The overall chemical equation is $6 \mathrm{CO_2} + 12 \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow [ \text{Sunlight} ]{\text{Chlorophyll}} \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6 \mathrm{O_2} + 6 \mathrm{H_2O}$.
*   The process involves three main events: absorption of light energy by chlorophyll, conversion of light energy to chemical energy, and reduction of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates.
*   Chlorophyll is located in chloroplasts within the cells of the leaf.
*   Plants take in carbon dioxide through stomata, which are controlled by guard cells.
*   Sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water are all essential for photosynthesis.
*   Excess glucose is stored as starch, while other nutrients like nitrogen are taken up from the soil.
