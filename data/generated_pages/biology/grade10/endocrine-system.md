# Endocrine System

The human body needs to coordinate its activities so that it can respond to the world around it. You may already know about the nervous system, which uses electrical signals to send messages quickly. However, the body also needs a slower, wider method to control and coordinate different organs. The endocrine system is a second way of control and coordination in our body, using chemical signals called hormones. These hormones are secreted by glands and travel through the blood to reach specific target organs. Understanding how this system works is important because it controls everything from how tall you grow to how your body handles stress.

## Hormones and the Body's Response to Danger

When an animal, such as a squirrel, experiences a scary situation, its body must prepare for either fighting or running away. Both of these activities are complicated and will use a great deal of energy. If the body relied only on electrical impulses via nerve cells, the range of tissues instructed to prepare would be limited. On the other hand, if a chemical signal were sent, it would reach all cells of the body and provide the wide-ranging changes needed. This is done in many animals, including human beings, using a hormone called adrenaline that is secreted from the adrenal glands.

Adrenaline is secreted directly into the blood and carried to different parts of the body. The target organs or the specific tissues on which it acts include the heart. As a result, the heart beats faster, resulting in a supply of more oxygen to our muscles. The blood to the digestive system and skin is reduced due to contraction of muscles around small arteries in these organs. This diverts the blood to our skeletal muscles. The breathing rate also increases because of the contractions of the diaphragm and the rib muscles. All these responses together enable the animal body to be ready to deal with the situation.

## The Thyroid and Metabolism

Thyroid hormones play a vital role in regulating the body's basic functions. We have all seen salt packets which say ‘iodised salt’ or ‘enriched with iodine’. Iodine is necessary for the thyroid gland to make thyroxin hormone. Thyroxin regulates carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism in the body so as to provide the best balance for growth. Iodine is essential for the synthesis of thyroxin. In case iodine is deficient in our diet, there is a possibility that we might suffer from goitre. One of the symptoms in this disease is a swollen neck.

## Growth and Development

Growth is also controlled by hormones, so that body parts grow in the right places at the right times. The hypothalamus plays an important role in the release of many hormones. For example, when the level of growth hormone is low, the hypothalamus releases growth hormone releasing factor which stimulates the pituitary gland to release growth hormone.

Growth hormone is one of the hormones secreted by the pituitary. As its name indicates, growth hormone regulates growth and development of the body. If there is a deficiency of this hormone in childhood, it leads to dwarfism. Sometimes we come across people who are either very short (dwarfs) or extremely tall (giants).

You must have noticed many dramatic changes in your appearance as well as that of your friends as you approached 10–12 years of age. These changes associated with puberty are because of the secretion of testosterone in males and oestrogen in females.

## Blood Sugar Regulation

The pancreas is another critical gland in the endocrine system. Do you know anyone in your family or friends who has been advised by the doctor to take less sugar in their diet because they are suffering from diabetes? As a treatment, they might be taking injections of insulin. This is a hormone which is produced by the pancreas and helps in regulating blood sugar levels. If it is not secreted in proper amounts, the sugar level in the blood rises causing many harmful effects.

If it is so important that hormones should be secreted in precise quantities, we need a mechanism through which this is done. The timing and amount of hormone released are regulated by feedback mechanisms. For example, if the sugar levels in blood rise, they are detected by the cells of the pancreas which respond by producing more insulin. As the blood sugar level falls, insulin secretion is reduced.

## Worked Example

Imagine a student, Rohan, who is about to give a big exam. His body experiences a sudden surge of adrenaline. According to the rules of how adrenaline works, his heart will beat faster, his breathing rate will increase, and blood flow to his digestive system will decrease. If Rohan had a snack just before the exam, the reduced blood flow to his digestive system means his body is diverting energy away from digestion and towards his skeletal muscles, preparing him to react quickly if needed.

## Key points

- The endocrine system is a second way of control and coordination in our body, using chemical signals called hormones.
- Adrenaline, secreted from the adrenal glands, prepares the body for action by increasing heart rate and breathing while diverting blood to muscles.
- Iodine is essential for the thyroid gland to make thyroxin hormone, which regulates metabolism; a lack of iodine can cause goitre.
- The hypothalamus releases growth hormone releasing factor to stimulate the pituitary gland to release growth hormone.
- Growth hormone regulates growth and development; a deficiency in childhood leads to dwarfism.
- Puberty is driven by testosterone in males and oestrogen in females.
- Insulin, produced by the pancreas, regulates blood sugar levels; a lack of it causes blood sugar to rise.
- The timing and amount of hormone released are regulated by feedback mechanisms, such as the pancreas detecting blood sugar levels.
