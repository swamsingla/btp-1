# Sexual Reproduction — Overview

Reproduction is the process by which living organisms produce their young ones. In asexual reproduction, a single organism can produce offspring on its own. However, in many plants and animals, two parents are required to create a new generation. This process, known as sexual reproduction, involves the combination of genetic material from two different individuals. It is a fundamental biological process that ensures the continuation of a species and introduces new traits into a population.

## Why Sexual Reproduction Matters

Sexual reproduction is not just about creating offspring; it is a powerful mechanism for generating genetic diversity. In any population of organisms, DNA copying is not absolutely accurate. These small errors, or variations, are the raw material for evolution. While a single organism might not benefit from a new variation, a population thrives on it because it increases the chances that some individuals will survive changing environments or diseases.

The challenge is how to speed up this process of creating new variations. DNA copying alone is a fairly slow process because each new variation builds upon the accumulated variations from previous generations. By combining DNA from two different individuals, sexual reproduction creates entirely new combinations of traits. Each offspring is genetically unique, which is crucial for the long-term survival of the species.

However, combining the DNA of two parents creates a major problem. If every new generation received a full set of DNA from both parents, the amount of DNA would double with each generation, eventually overwhelming the cell. To solve this, multicellular organisms evolved a specialized cell division process called meiosis. This process reduces the chromosome number by half, creating special cells known as germ-cells. When two germ-cells combine, the chromosome number is restored to its original amount, ensuring the new organism develops correctly.

## The Role of Gametes

In sexual reproduction, the specialized cells that carry the genetic information are called gametes. As organisms become more complex, these germ-cells also specialize. One germ-cell becomes large and contains stored food reserves to nourish the developing embryo. The other germ-cell becomes smaller and motile, capable of traveling to the other gamete.

By convention, the smaller, motile germ-cell is called the male gamete, while the larger, non-motile germ-cell containing the food stores is called the female gamete. This specialization is a key feature of sexual reproduction in higher organisms.

## Fertilisation and the Zygote

The actual fusion of the male and female gametes is called fertilisation. This event is critical because it merges the genetic material from two distinct individuals, forming a new cell called a zygote. The zygote is the very first cell of a new organism. It contains a complete set of chromosomes, with half inherited from the male parent and half from the female parent.

Because the zygote contains a full complement of genetic information, it has the potential to grow and develop into a complex organism with specialized tissues and organs. To do this, it requires sufficient energy stores, which is why the female gamete is designed to be large and nutrient-rich.

## The Process in Flowering Plants

Flowering plants, or angiosperms, provide a clear example of sexual reproduction. The reproductive parts of a flower are the stamens and the pistil. The stamen is the male reproductive part and produces pollen grains, which are often yellowish in colour. The pistil is the female reproductive part, located in the centre of the flower. It consists of three parts: the swollen bottom part is the ovary, the middle elongated part is the style, and the terminal sticky part is the stigma.

The ovary contains structures called ovules, and each ovule houses a female gamete. For fertilisation to occur, the male gamete produced by the pollen grain must fuse with the female gamete inside the ovule. This transfer of pollen from the stamen to the stigma is called pollination. If the pollen is transferred to the same flower, it is called self-pollination. If it is transferred to a different flower on the same plant or another plant, it is called cross-pollination.

Agents such as wind, water, or animals facilitate this transfer of pollen. Once the pollen lands on a suitable stigma, a tube grows out of the pollen grain and travels down the style to reach the ovary. Here, the male and female gametes fuse, completing the process of fertilisation.

## From Zygote to Seed

After fertilisation, the zygote divides several times to form an embryo within the ovule. The ovule develops a tough protective coat and gradually transforms into a seed. Meanwhile, the ovary grows rapidly and ripens to form a fruit. The other flower parts, such as the petals, sepals, stamens, style, and stigma, typically shrivel and fall off.

The seed contains the future plant, or embryo, which remains dormant until it encounters appropriate conditions. Under the right environment, the embryo develops into a seedling in a process known as germination.

## Worked example

Consider a bee that carries pollen from the stamen of one sunflower plant to the stigma of a flower on a different sunflower plant. This is an example of cross-pollination because the pollen is transferred to a different flower. If the pollen lands on the stigma and a tube grows down the style to the ovary, the male and female gametes can fuse. This fusion of the germ-cells is fertilisation, which produces a zygote. The zygote will then divide to form an embryo, which will eventually develop into a new plant inside a seed.

*   It generates genetic variations by combining DNA from two different parents, which helps ensure the survival of the species.
*   Meiosis is a specialized cell division that reduces the chromosome number by half in germ-cells.
*   The male gamete is typically small and motile, while the female gamete is large and contains food stores.
*   Fertilisation is the fusion of the male and female gametes to form a zygote.
