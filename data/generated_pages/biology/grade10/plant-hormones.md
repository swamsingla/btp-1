# Plant Hormones

Plants do not have a nervous system or muscles, yet they respond to their environment. They use chemical messengers called hormones to control growth and coordinate their movements. These hormones are produced in one part of the plant and travel to another to trigger a response, allowing the plant to bend towards light or stop growing when needed.

## How Plants Move Without Muscles

Animals use a nervous system and muscles to move quickly. Plants cannot do this. However, they still react to touch, light, and gravity. There are two main ways plants move. Some movements are very fast and do not involve growth. Other movements are slow and depend entirely on growth.

When you touch a sensitive plant like chhui-mui, its leaves fold up and droop almost instantly. This happens because plant cells change shape. They do this by moving water in and out of their cells, which makes them swell or shrink. This movement happens at a point different from where the plant is touched. Electrical and chemical signals travel from cell to cell to tell the plant that a touch has happened.

Other movements are much slower because they rely on growth. For example, a climbing pea plant uses tendrils to grab onto fences. When a tendril touches a support, the side touching the object grows slower than the far side. This difference in growth makes the tendril curl around the support.

## Directional Growth and Tropisms

Plants also grow in specific directions in response to environmental triggers like light and gravity. These directional movements are called tropisms. They can be either towards the stimulus or away from it.

You can observe this by placing a germinated seed in a box with light coming from one side. After a few days, the shoot will bend towards the light, and the root will bend away from it. If you turn the box, the new growth will change direction to follow the new light source. This movement towards light is called phototropism.

Plants also show geotropism, which is growth in response to gravity. Plant roots always grow downwards, and shoots usually grow upwards and away from the earth. If a plant detects water, it will grow its roots towards the water source, which is called hydrotropism. If it detects chemicals, the growth is called chemotropism. An example of chemotropism is when pollen tubes grow towards ovules during reproduction.

## Chemical Communication in Plants

Fast movements, like the folding of a sensitive plant, require rapid information transfer. Electrical impulses are good for this, but they have limits. They can only reach cells connected by nervous tissue, and cells need time to reset before sending another impulse.

Because of these limits, plants use chemical communication. When a plant is stimulated, cells release chemical compounds. These compounds diffuse around the cell and are detected by nearby cells. This method is slower than electrical impulses, but it can reach all cells in the plant and work steadily.

## Plant Hormones and Their Roles

Plant hormones are these chemical compounds that help coordinate growth, development, and responses to the environment. They are made in one part of the plant and simply diffuse to the area where they act. There are several key hormones that either promote or stop growth.

Auxins are a type of hormone that helps cells grow longer. When a plant detects light, auxin is made at the shoot tip and moves towards the shady side of the shoot. This high concentration of auxin on the shady side makes those cells grow longer, causing the plant to bend towards the light.

Gibberellins are another group of hormones that help the stem grow, similar to auxins. Cytokinins are hormones that promote cell division. Because of this, they are found in high amounts in places where cells divide quickly, such as in fruits and seeds.

However, plants also need hormones to stop growing. Abscisic acid is a hormone that inhibits growth. It can cause the wilting of leaves, which is a way for the plant to conserve water during stressful conditions.

## Worked example

Imagine you are growing a potted plant near a window. The light comes from the left side of the plant. According to the rules of plant hormones, the plant will produce auxin at its shoot tip. The auxin will move towards the shady right side of the shoot. This will cause the cells on the right side to grow longer than the cells on the left side. As a result, the plant will appear to bend towards the left, towards the window.

## Key points

- Plants use hormones for chemical communication because they lack a nervous system.
- Movement can be fast (like in the sensitive plant) and independent of growth, or slow and dependent on growth.
- Directional growth in response to stimuli is called tropism (e.g., phototropism towards light, geotropism towards gravity).
- Auxins, gibberellins, and cytokinins are hormones that promote plant growth.
- Abscisic acid is a hormone that inhibits growth and causes wilting.
