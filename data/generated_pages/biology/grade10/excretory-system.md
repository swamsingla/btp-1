# Human Excretory System

Every living cell in your body performs metabolic activities that generate waste materials. Just as you breathe out carbon dioxide, your body produces nitrogenous wastes that must be removed to stay healthy. The biological process involved in the removal of these harmful metabolic wastes from the body is called excretion. In humans, this is handled by a complex network of organs that work together to filter the blood, store the waste, and release it safely.

## The Excretory Organs

The excretory system of human beings includes a pair of kidneys, a pair of ureters, a urinary bladder, and a urethra. The kidneys are located in the abdomen, one on either side of the backbone. Their primary job is to filter out waste products from the blood. Just as $CO_{2}$ is removed from the blood in the lungs, nitrogenous waste such as urea or uric acid are removed from blood in the kidneys. The filtered liquid, called urine, passes through the ureters and is stored in the urinary bladder until it is released through the urethra.

## The Nephron

To understand how urine is produced, you have to look at the basic filtration unit inside the kidneys. Each unit has a cluster of very thin-walled blood capillaries. Each capillary cluster in the kidney is associated with the cup-shaped end of a coiled tube called Bowman's capsule that collects the filtrate. Each kidney has large numbers of these filtration units called nephrons packed close together. 

As the filtrate flows along the tube, some substances in the initial filtrate, such as glucose, amino acids, salts, and a major amount of water, are selectively re-absorbed as the urine flows along the tube. The amount of water re-absorbed depends on how much excess water there is in the body, and on how much of dissolved waste there is to be excreted. 

To see how this works in a daily scenario, imagine you have just finished a heavy workout and drank a large amount of water. Your body will re-absorb a smaller amount of water from the filtrate, allowing you to excrete a larger volume of urine to remove the excess fluid. On the other hand, if you are running a marathon in the hot Indian sun and sweating heavily, your body will re-absorb a much larger amount of water from the filtrate, resulting in a smaller volume of highly concentrated urine. 

## Urine Formation

The initial filtrate in the kidneys is about 180 L daily in a healthy adult. However, the volume actually excreted is only a litre or two a day, because the remaining filtrate is re-absorbed in the kidney tubules. The urine forming in each kidney eventually enters a long tube, the ureter, which connects the kidneys with the urinary bladder. 

Urine is stored in the urinary bladder until the pressure of the expanded bladder leads to the urge to pass it out through the urethra. The bladder is muscular, so it is under nervous control. As a result, we can usually control the urge to urinate.

## Dialysis

Kidneys are vital organs for survival. Several factors like infections, injury, or restricted blood flow to kidneys reduce the activity of kidneys. This leads to accumulation of poisonous wastes in the body, which can even lead to death. In case of kidney failure, an artificial kidney can be used. An artificial kidney is a device to remove nitrogenous waste products from the blood through dialysis.

Artificial kidneys contain a number of tubes with a semi-permeable lining, suspended in a tank filled with dialysing fluid. This fluid has the same osmotic pressure as blood, except that it is devoid of nitrogenous wastes. The patient's blood is passed through these tubes. During this passage, the waste products from the blood pass into dialysing fluid by diffusion. The purified blood is pumped back into the patient. This is similar to the function of the kidney, but it is different since there is no re-absorption involved.

## Worked example

Let us look at a numerical example of how the re-absorption process works. A healthy adult produces an initial filtrate of about 180 L daily. If the body only excretes a volume of 1.5 L a day, we can calculate how much filtrate was re-absorbed. 

The total filtrate produced is 180 L. The actual volume excreted is 1.5 L. The remaining filtrate that was re-absorbed is the difference between these two values. 

$180 \text{ L} - 1.5 \text{ L} = 178.5 \text{ L}$

Therefore, 178.5 L of the initial filtrate was re-absorbed by the kidney tubules in a single day.

## Key points

*   Excretion is the biological process involved in the removal of harmful metabolic wastes from the body.
*   The human excretory system consists of kidneys, ureters, a urinary bladder, and a urethra.
*   The basic filtration unit in the kidneys is the nephron, which contains a cluster of thin-walled blood capillaries associated with Bowman's capsule.
*   Substances like glucose, amino acids, salts, and water are selectively re-absorbed from the filtrate as urine flows along the kidney tubules.
*   The initial filtrate in the kidneys is about 180 L daily, but only about a litre or two is actually excreted due to re-absorption.
*   In kidney failure, dialysis is used to remove nitrogenous waste products from the blood using an artificial kidney.
*   Dialysis involves passing the patient's blood through tubes in a dialysing fluid, allowing waste to diffuse out without re-absorption.
