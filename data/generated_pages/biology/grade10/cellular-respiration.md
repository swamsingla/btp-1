# Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to release energy. This energy is captured in a molecule called ATP. ATP acts as the energy currency of the cell. Without cellular respiration, organisms would not have the power to carry out life processes.

## The First Step: Glycolysis

The process of breaking down glucose begins in the cytoplasm. In this step, a six-carbon glucose molecule is split into three-carbon molecules called pyruvate (two pyruvate molecules from each glucose molecule). This initial breakdown is called glycolysis. It is the starting point for both aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

## Aerobic Respiration

When oxygen is present, the pyruvate molecules enter the mitochondria. Here, they undergo further breakdown in the presence of air. This aerobic process completely breaks down each pyruvate molecule to yield three molecules of carbon dioxide and water. The release of energy during this aerobic process is a lot greater than in the anaerobic process.

## Anaerobic Respiration

Sometimes, oxygen is not available in sufficient amounts. In such cases, cells take an alternative pathway that does not involve oxygen. In yeast, the pyruvate is converted into ethanol and carbon dioxide. Since this process takes place in the absence of air (oxygen), it is called anaerobic respiration. In human muscle cells, when there is a lack of oxygen during sudden activity, the pyruvate is converted into lactic acid. This build-up of lactic acid in our muscles during sudden activity causes cramps.

## The Role of ATP

The energy released during cellular respiration is immediately used to synthesise a molecule called ATP. ATP is the energy currency for most cellular processes. The energy released during the process of respiration is used to make an ATP molecule from ADP and inorganic phosphate.

$$
\mathrm{ADP} + \textcircled {\mathbb {P}} \xrightarrow {\text{Energy}} \mathrm{ADP} \sim \textcircled {\mathbb {P}} = \mathrm{ATP}
$$

Endothermic processes in the cell then use this ATP to drive the reactions. When the terminal phosphate linkage in ATP is broken using water, the energy equivalent to 30.5 kJ/mol is released. Think of how a battery can provide energy for many different kinds of uses. It can be used to obtain mechanical energy, light energy, electrical energy and so on. Similarly, ATP can be used in the cells for the contraction of muscles, protein synthesis, conduction of nervous impulses and many other activities.

## Worked Example

Let us apply the energy value of ATP breakdown to a simple case. Imagine muscle cells that must contract. They break the terminal phosphate linkage in ATP using water, and each mole of ATP broken down releases 30.5 kJ of energy.

If the cells break down 2 moles of ATP, the energy released is:

$$
2 \times 30.5 = 61 \text{ kJ}
$$

This energy drives the contraction of the muscle. Just like the battery in the earlier comparison, the ATP is used up and the cell must make it again from ADP and inorganic phosphate, using the energy released by respiration.

## Comparing the Two Pathways

The two pathways differ in where they happen, what they need and what they give. Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm in both. After that, the aerobic pathway continues in the mitochondria and needs oxygen. It ends with carbon dioxide and water and releases a lot more energy. The anaerobic pathway needs no oxygen. In yeast it ends with ethanol and carbon dioxide, and in human muscle cells it ends with lactic acid. A muscle cell uses the anaerobic pathway only when oxygen runs short during sudden activity, and the lactic acid that builds up causes cramps.

## Key points
*   Cellular respiration breaks down glucose to release energy for life processes.
*   Glycolysis breaks down a six-carbon glucose molecule into a three-carbon pyruvate in the cytoplasm.
*   Aerobic respiration breaks down pyruvate in the presence of oxygen to give carbon dioxide and water.
*   Anaerobic respiration breaks down pyruvate without oxygen, yielding ethanol and carbon dioxide in yeast, or lactic acid in human muscles.
*   ATP is the energy currency of the cell, synthesised from ADP and inorganic phosphate using energy from respiration.
