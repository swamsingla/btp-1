# Balanced Diet

A balanced diet means eating the right mix of different food components in the correct amounts to keep your body healthy and strong. It matters because every person has different nutritional needs based on their age, gender, physical activity, and overall health status. Just as a car needs the right fuel to run smoothly, your body needs the right nutrients to work properly every day.

## Different People, Different Needs

Nutritional requirements are not the same for everyone. A growing child needs different amounts and types of nutrients compared to their grandparents or a professional athlete. Your body changes its needs depending on how active you are and whether you are sick or healthy. For example, a marathon runner needs extra energy during a race, while an older person might need less food overall but still requires the right vitamins to stay healthy.

## Energy-Giving Foods

Carbohydrates and fats are your body's main sources of energy. Carbohydrates provide instant energy, which is why a marathon runner drinks glucose water during a race to keep going. You can find carbohydrates in cereals like wheat, rice, and maize, as well as in vegetables like potato and sweet potato, and fruits like banana, pineapple, and mango. Common sugar is also a type of carbohydrate. Fats, such as ghee and various oils, store energy for later use. Nuts and seeds are rich sources of fat. Animals store fat as an energy reserve too: the thick layer of fat under a polar bear's skin keeps it warm and feeds it through the long Arctic winter, when food is hard to find.

## Body-Building Foods

Proteins are essential for the growth and repair of your body, which is why they are called body-building foods. Milk products and pulses are excellent sources of protein. Animal sources include milk, paneer, egg, fish, and meat, while plant sources include pulses, beans, peas, and nuts. Sportspersons need larger quantities of proteins to build their muscles, and growing children need the right amount of protein for their proper growth and development.

## Protective Foods and Deficiency Diseases

Some foods protect your body from diseases caused by a lack of essential nutrients. Vitamin C helps cure scurvy, a disease that causes bleeding and swollen gums. This disease was common among sailors during long voyages before they discovered that eating lemons and oranges helped them recover. Another example is goitre, which causes swelling at the front of the neck. In the 1960s, scientists found that people in the Himalayan region and the Northern plains of India suffered from this disease because their local soil lacked iodine. Adding iodine to common salt to create iodised salt successfully reduced these symptoms.

## Worked Example

Imagine a Grade 6 student named Rohan. He plays cricket every afternoon, so he needs more energy-giving foods like rice and potatoes to keep his stamina up. However, he also needs enough proteins from eggs or pulses to repair his muscles after the game. If Rohan only ate carbohydrates and fats, he would have energy but his body would not grow or repair itself properly. To have a truly balanced diet, he must include carbohydrates for energy, fats for stored energy, and proteins for growth and repair.

## Key points

*   Nutritional needs vary according to age, gender, physical activity, and health status.
*   Carbohydrates and fats are energy-giving foods that fuel daily activities.
*   Proteins are body-building foods that help in growth and repair.
*   Scurvy is caused by a Vitamin C deficiency and can be cured by eating citrus fruits like lemons and oranges.
*   Goitre is caused by an iodine deficiency and can be prevented by consuming iodised salt.
