# Fossil Fuels

Fossil fuels are natural resources that power many things we use every day, like cars, buses, and factories. They come from the remains of ancient organisms that lived millions of years ago. Because they take so long to form and we use them up quickly, they are a limited supply.

## Ancient Remains

Fossil fuels are made from the remains of ancient organisms. These ancient plants and animals died long ago and got buried deep under the Earth. Over millions of years, heat and pressure transformed their remains into solid, liquid, and gas forms. This slow process means we cannot make new fossil fuels fast enough to replace the ones we use today.

## Types of Fossil Fuels

There are three main types of fossil fuels: coal, petroleum, and natural gas. Coal is a solid fossil fuel. Petroleum is a liquid fossil fuel, and we often call it oil. Natural gas is a gas fossil fuel. Each type formed under different conditions and is used for different purposes. For example, coal is often burned to produce electricity, while petroleum is refined to make petrol and diesel for vehicles.

## Exhaustible Resources

Fossil fuels are exhaustible resources, which means they will eventually run out. They are also called non-renewable resources because they are not produced or replenished within a reasonable period of time. Once we use up all the coal, oil, or gas in the Earth, it is gone forever. This is why scientists are looking for other ways to generate power that will not disappear.

## Worked example

Imagine a school that keeps a store of 400 litres of diesel for its bus, and the bus uses one quarter of that store every week. One quarter of 400 litres is $\frac{1}{4} \times 400 = 100$ litres. So 300 litres are left after the first week, 200 litres after the second, 100 litres after the third, and after the fourth week the store is empty. The school can buy more diesel, but the petroleum it is refined from took millions of years to form, and no new petroleum is being made in the weeks the bus is running. This is what it means to call fossil fuels exhaustible and non-renewable: the supply underground can only go down.

## Key points
- Fossil fuels are formed from the remains of ancient organisms.
- The three main fossil fuels are coal, petroleum, and natural gas.
- They take millions of years to form.
- They are exhaustible and non-renewable resources.
- They are found in limited quantities and get exhausted once used.
