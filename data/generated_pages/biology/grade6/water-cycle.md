# Water Cycle

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on Earth. Water moves up into the air, comes back down as rain or snow, and then flows back into rivers, lakes, and oceans. This cycle is important because it helps distribute and replenish water across the planet. It also ensures that the total amount of water on Earth stays the same.

## Evaporation

Evaporation is the process where liquid water turns into water vapour, which is a gas. The Sun heats up water in oceans, rivers, and lakes. Because of this heat, the water evaporates and rises into the air. The warmer the water and the air above it, the faster this happens, which is why far more water evaporates from a lake on a hot sunny day than on a cool cloudy one.

## Condensation

As water vapour rises higher into the sky, it gets cooler. When the vapour cools down, it changes back into tiny liquid water droplets. This process is called condensation. These tiny droplets gather together to form clouds.

## Precipitation

When clouds become too heavy with water droplets, the water falls back to Earth. This falling water is called precipitation. It can fall as rain, snow, or hail. Once the water reaches the ground, it either flows into ponds, lakes, rivers, and oceans or seeps into the ground.

## Groundwater

When rain falls on the surface of the Earth, some of it seeps into the ground. This process of surface water seeping through soil and rocks is called infiltration. The water that seeps through and gets stored in the pore spaces of sediments and the openings in rocks beneath the surface is called groundwater. These underground layers of sediments and rocks that store water in pore spaces are called aquifers.

## Worked Example

Imagine a sunny day in summer. The Sun heats up a large lake. Because of the heat, water from the lake evaporates and rises into the air as water vapour. As the air gets higher, it cools down, and the vapour condenses to form a cloud. Later, the cloud gets heavy, and the water falls back to the ground as rain. Some of this rainwater flows into a nearby river, while some seeps into the ground to become groundwater.

## Key points
* The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on Earth, and it keeps the total amount of water on the planet the same.
* Evaporation happens when the Sun heats liquid water and turns it into water vapour.
* Condensation is when water vapour cools down and forms clouds.
* Precipitation is when water falls back to Earth as rain, snow, or hail.
* Some rainwater seeps into the ground to become groundwater stored in aquifers.
