# Air Pollution

The air around us is a delicate balance of gases that supports all life on Earth. However, human activities like burning fossil fuels and releasing factory smoke disturb this balance, leading to a state where the air becomes contaminated. This contaminated air, also known as polluted air, harms both people and nature. It can cause breathing problems in humans, damage crops, and lead to dangerous weather events. To protect our health and the environment, we must understand the different types of air pollution and the specific threats they create.

## Greenhouse Gases and Global Warming

When we burn fossil fuels like coal and oil, we release invisible gases into the atmosphere. These gases are called greenhouse gases, which include carbon dioxide and methane. Normally, plants and tiny ocean plankton absorb carbon dioxide from the air as they grow, keeping the Earth's temperature balanced. However, burning fossil fuels releases extra carbon that has been locked underground for millions of years. The Earth cannot absorb this extra carbon fast enough, so the heat builds up in the atmosphere. This trapped heat causes global warming. Even a small increase in global temperature can melt ice caps, raise sea levels, flood coastal cities, and cause extreme weather conditions. These long-term changes in temperature, rainfall, and weather patterns are collectively called climate change.

## Smog and Acid Rain

Air pollution from factories, vehicles, and the burning of fuels directly harms the environment and human health. One of the most visible results of this pollution is the formation of smog. Smog is a thick, dirty haze that hangs in the air and reduces visibility. Breathing in smog can cause serious respiratory problems in people. Furthermore, the chemicals released during fuel combustion react with water in the atmosphere, leading to acid rain. Acid rain damages crops, harms forests, and pollutes water bodies, disrupting the natural ecosystems that plants and animals need to survive.

## The Ozone Layer

The Earth's atmosphere also plays a crucial role in protecting life by maintaining a delicate balance of gases like oxygen. If there is a little less oxygen in the air, or a little lower temperature of the Earth, or a little less ozone in the atmosphere, it could endanger life on Earth. The ozone layer acts as a protective shield in the sky, absorbing harmful radiation from the Sun. When human actions disturb this delicate balance, the protective ozone layer can be weakened. This makes the Earth's surface more vulnerable to the Sun's harmful rays, putting both human health and nature at risk.

## Worked Example

Normally, trees, plants, and ocean plankton absorb carbon dioxide as they grow, which keeps the Earth's heat balanced. However, when we burn fossil fuels, we release extra carbon that the Earth cannot absorb fast enough, causing heat to build up. Let us see how this works with a simple, made-up example. Imagine a large city whose burning of coal releases 1000 tons of extra carbon dioxide into the atmosphere in one day. The local forests and plants can absorb only 200 tons of this carbon dioxide over the same period. Because 1000 tons were released but only 200 tons were absorbed, 1000 - 200 = 800 tons of extra carbon dioxide remain in the air. In this simplified example, the imbalance means the air holds more carbon dioxide than before, and this adds to global warming.

## Key points

*   Air pollution occurs when the air becomes contaminated, for example by burning fossil fuels like coal and oil.
*   Greenhouse gases, such as carbon dioxide and methane, trap extra heat in the atmosphere, causing global warming and climate change.
*   Climate change leads to melting ice caps, rising sea levels, flooded coastal cities, and extreme weather.
*   Air pollution directly causes breathing problems in humans, damages crops, and leads to the formation of smog and acid rain.
*   A delicate balance of oxygen and ozone in the atmosphere is essential to protect life on Earth from harm.
