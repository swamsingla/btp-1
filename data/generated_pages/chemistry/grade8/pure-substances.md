# Pure Substances

Have you ever noticed the word 'pure' written on the packs of some consumables, such as milk, ghee, and spices? The word 'pure' has slightly different meanings in common usage and in science. In common usage, 'pure' means unadulterated products, which are free from illegal additions like cheaper, poor-quality substances meant to increase quantity or reduce cost. However, in science, a pure substance is the one that has no other substance present in it. Even products that look pure to the naked eye can be considered impure if they are made of more than one substance. Understanding this distinction matters because it helps us classify the materials around us, from the air we breathe to the water we drink.

## Common Usage and Scientific Meaning

In everyday life, we use the word pure to describe food items that have not been adulterated. Adulteration is an illegal process of adding substances which are cheaper, or of a poor quality, to a product. This is usually done to increase the quantity or reduce the manufacturing cost. However, it deteriorates the quality of the product. It can also make the product hazardous to health. In contrast, the scientific definition of a pure substance is about what the substance is made of. A pure substance is a kind of matter that cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process. When a scientist says that something is pure, it means that the substance consists of the same type of particles. This means that if you have a sample of a pure substance, every single particle in that sample is identical to every other particle. You cannot use physical methods like filtering, boiling, or freezing to break it down into something else.

## Elements

Elements are one of the two main types of pure substances. Each element is made up of identical particles. These particles are different from the particles of any other element. Elements are substances that cannot be further broken down into simpler substances. They are the building blocks of all matter. Some other examples of elements are gold, silver, sulfur, and carbon. The two substances hydrogen and oxygen, which are obtained when electricity is passed through water, are also elements. If you have a piece of pure gold, you cannot separate it into anything else using physical methods.

## Compounds

Compounds are the second main type of pure substances. Compounds are formed when different elements combine in fixed ratios to form something entirely new. The properties of compounds are different from those of elements forming that compound. The constituent elements of a compound cannot be separated by any physical method. For example, water is a compound. Water is made of two different elements: hydrogen and oxygen, combined chemically in a fixed ratio. The ratio of the number of atoms of hydrogen to oxygen in water has been found to be $2:1$. Another example is sodium chloride, which is made up of particles of sodium and chlorine in a $1:1$ ratio. In water, the particles of hydrogen and oxygen are so tightly attached to each other that it is generally impossible to separate them apart using physical methods. That is why water is a compound.

## Pure Substances Around Us

Elements, compounds and mixtures are all around us. The air we breathe is a mixture of gases like oxygen, nitrogen and carbon dioxide, while water, which is essential for life, is a compound made of the elements hydrogen and oxygen. Elements like iron and aluminium are used to construct bridges, buildings and vehicles. Chemists study how elements combine to create compounds, which has enabled them to invent medicines and vaccines to fight diseases. Engineers have also developed alloys like stainless steel, which is stronger and more durable than pure iron.

## Worked Example

Let us look at a familiar compound. Imagine you have a glass of water. Water is a compound because it is made of two different elements, hydrogen and oxygen, combined chemically in a fixed ratio. The constituent elements of a compound cannot be separated by any physical method. This means you cannot use a filter to separate the hydrogen from the oxygen. You also cannot use boiling to separate them, because when you boil water, it turns into water vapour, which is still a compound. You can get back water upon heating ice or cooling water vapour, but you will always have water. To separate hydrogen and oxygen, you would need to use a chemical process, such as passing electricity through the water, which splits it into hydrogen and oxygen.

## Key points

*   In common usage 'pure' means unadulterated; in science, a pure substance has no other substance present in it and cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process.
*   A pure substance consists of the same type of particles.
*   Elements are substances that cannot be further broken down into simpler substances.
*   Compounds are formed when different elements combine in fixed ratios to form something entirely new; their properties differ from those of their elements, and the elements cannot be separated by any physical method.
