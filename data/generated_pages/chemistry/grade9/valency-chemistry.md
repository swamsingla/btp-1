# Valency

Have you ever wondered why some materials mix easily while others refuse to combine? The answer lies in the hidden structure of atoms. Every atom has a specific combining capacity, which determines how it interacts with other atoms. This combining capacity is called valency. Valency is the number of electrons an atom can lose, gain, or share to achieve a full outermost shell of electrons. Understanding valency is essential because it explains why atoms react, how molecules are formed, and why certain elements are highly reactive while others remain completely inert.

## Electrons in the Outermost Shell

To understand valency, you first need to know how electrons are arranged in an atom. The distribution of electrons into different orbits of an atom was suggested by Bohr and Bury. The maximum number of electrons present in a shell is given by the formula $2n^2$, where 'n' is the orbit number or energy level index, 1, 2, 3, and so on. Therefore, the maximum number of electrons in different shells are as follows: the first orbit or K-shell will be $2 \times 1^2 = 2$, the second orbit or L-shell will be $2 \times 2^2 = 8$, the third orbit or M-shell will be $2 \times 3^2 = 18$, and the fourth orbit or N-shell will be $2 \times 4^2 = 32$.

The electrons present in the outermost shell of an atom are known as the valence electrons. From the Bohr-Bury scheme, we also know that the outermost shell of an atom can accommodate a maximum of 8 electrons. Electrons are not accommodated in a given shell, unless the inner shells are filled. That is, the shells are filled in a step-wise manner.

## The Octet Rule

Atoms react because they want to reach a stable state. A stable atom has a completely filled outermost shell. An outermost-shell, which had eight electrons was said to possess an octet. Atoms would thus react, so as to achieve an octet in the outermost shell. This was done by sharing, gaining or losing electrons. The number of electrons gained, lost or shared so as to make the octet of electrons in the outermost shell, gives us directly the combining capacity of the element, that is, the valency.

Inert elements, like helium and neon, have atoms with a completely filled outermost shell. These atoms show little chemical activity, meaning their combining capacity or valency is zero. Helium has two electrons in its outermost shell, which is already full for the first shell, while all other inert elements have atoms with eight electrons in the outermost shell.

## Calculating Valency

You can determine the valency of an atom in two main ways. First, if the outermost shell has 4 or fewer electrons, the valency is equal to the number of electrons in that shell. For example, hydrogen, lithium, and sodium atoms contain one electron each in their outermost shell, therefore each one of them can lose one electron. So, they are said to have valency of one. Magnesium has two electrons in its outermost shell, so its valency is two. Aluminium has three electrons in its outermost shell, so its valency is three. Carbon has four, so its valency is four.

Second, if the outermost shell has 5 or more electrons, which is close to its full capacity of 8, the valency is calculated by subtracting the number of electrons in the outermost shell from 8. For example, the fluorine atom has 7 electrons in the outermost shell. Instead of losing seven electrons, it is easier for fluorine to gain one electron. Hence, its valency is determined by subtracting seven electrons from the octet, which gives a valency of one. You can calculate the valency of oxygen in the same manner: oxygen has 6 electrons in its outermost shell, so its valency is $8 - 6 = 2$.

## Worked Example

Let us apply these rules to find the valency of magnesium and chlorine. First, we look at the electron distribution. Magnesium has 12 electrons total. According to the rules, the first orbit or K-shell takes 2 electrons, and the second orbit or L-shell takes the next 8 electrons. This leaves 2 electrons for the third orbit or M-shell. Since magnesium has 2 electrons in its outermost shell, and 2 is a small number, its valency is 2.

Next, we look at chlorine. Chlorine has 17 electrons total. The first orbit or K-shell takes 2 electrons, and the second orbit or L-shell takes the next 8 electrons. This leaves 7 electrons for the third orbit or M-shell. Since chlorine has 7 electrons in its outermost shell, and 7 is close to the full capacity of 8, we subtract 7 from 8. Therefore, the valency of chlorine is 1.

## Key points

- Valency is the combining capacity of an atom, defined as the number of electrons it can lose, gain, or share.
- The outermost shell of an atom can accommodate a maximum of 8 electrons.
- Atoms react to achieve a stable octet, which is a fully-filled outermost shell of 8 electrons.
- If the outermost shell has 4 or fewer electrons, the valency is equal to the number of electrons in that shell.
- If the outermost shell has more than 4 electrons, the valency is calculated by subtracting the number of electrons from 8.
- Atoms with a full outermost shell, like helium and neon, have a valency of zero and are chemically inert.
