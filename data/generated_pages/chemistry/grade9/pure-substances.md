# Pure Substances

When you buy milk or spices in the market, the word "pure" is used to mean unadulterated, free from illegal additions. In chemistry, however, a pure substance is defined by its composition: it contains only one kind of matter and cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process. Whether it is a solid, a liquid, or a gas, a pure substance will always have the same characteristic properties. This matters because it allows us to distinguish between simple materials and complex blends, which is the first step in understanding the chemistry of the world around us.

## Elements

Elements are one of the two main types of pure substances. They are substances that cannot be further broken down into simpler substances by any chemical or physical process. Each element is made up of identical particles, and these particles are different from the particles of any other element. Because the particles are identical, the properties of an element are fixed and consistent. For example, if you have a piece of pure gold, you cannot separate it into anything else using physical methods. Other familiar examples include sulfur, carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.

Elements are usually classified as metals, non-metals and metalloids. Metals such as iron, copper and gold are generally shiny, can be hammered into sheets and conduct heat and electricity. Non-metals such as carbon, sulphur and oxygen do not usually show these properties. Metalloids, such as silicon and germanium, have properties between those of metals and non-metals.

## Compounds

Compounds are the second main type of pure substance. They are formed when different elements combine chemically in fixed ratios to create something entirely new. The properties of a compound are different from those of the elements that form it. Because the elements are chemically bonded, their constituent elements cannot be separated by any physical method. For instance, water is a compound made of hydrogen and oxygen atoms combined in a fixed ratio of $2:1$ by number (which is $1:8$ by mass). Similarly, sodium chloride is a compound made of sodium and chlorine in a $1:1$ ratio by number. If you have a sample of a pure compound, every single particle in that sample is identical to every other particle, and the composition is the same throughout.

## Homogeneous Composition and Fixed Properties

A pure substance has a homogeneous composition, meaning its composition is the same throughout the entire sample. This uniformity ensures that the substance always exhibits fixed properties, such as a specific boiling point, melting point, and density. You may already know that mixtures, like soft drinks or soil, contain more than one pure substance and can have variable compositions. In contrast, a pure substance will always have the same characteristic properties, regardless of its source. This consistency is crucial because it means that a pure substance behaves predictably, which is essential for both scientific research and industrial applications.

## Pure Substances and Mixtures

A mixture contains two or more pure substances, and its composition can vary from sample to sample. Sea water, for example, is a mixture of water and salts, and the amount of salt differs from one place to another. A mixture does not have a fixed melting or boiling point, and its parts can usually be separated by physical methods such as filtration, evaporation or distillation. A pure substance, on the other hand, melts and boils at fixed temperatures. For example, pure water boils at $100\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ at normal atmospheric pressure, while salt water boils at a higher temperature. This is why melting and boiling points are used to test how pure a substance is.

## Worked Example

Consider a sample of pure water. Water is a compound because it is made of two different elements, hydrogen and oxygen, combined chemically in a fixed ratio. The constituent elements of a compound cannot be separated by any physical method. This means you cannot use a filter to separate the hydrogen from the oxygen, and you cannot use boiling to separate them either, because when you boil water, it turns into water vapour, which is still a compound. To separate hydrogen and oxygen, you would need to use a chemical process, such as electrolysis, which splits the water into its constituent elements. This illustrates why pure substances are defined by their inability to be separated by physical means.

## Key points

- A pure substance contains only one kind of matter and has fixed properties, such as fixed melting and boiling points.
- Elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances, and each is made of identical particles.
- Compounds are formed when elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio, and their properties differ from those of their elements.
- The elements in a compound cannot be separated by physical methods.
- A mixture contains two or more pure substances in a variable composition.
