# Isotopes and Isobars

Atoms of different elements can have the exact same total number of protons and neutrons, or they can have the exact same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. These two situations give rise to two important concepts: isotopes and isobars. Understanding these concepts helps chemists explain why the average atomic mass of an element is often a decimal number, and it shows that the number of neutrons in an atom can change without changing what element it is.

## What is an Isotope?

An isotope is an atom of the same element that has the same atomic number but different mass numbers. The atomic number is the number of protons in the nucleus, and the mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons. Because the atomic number is the same, the element does not change. However, the mass number is different because the number of neutrons is different.

The number of protons decides which element an atom is, so changing only the number of neutrons gives a heavier or lighter atom of the same element.

For example, hydrogen is an element with an atomic number of 1. This means every hydrogen atom has exactly one proton. However, there are three atomic species of hydrogen. Protium has a mass number of 1, which means it has one proton and no neutrons. Deuterium has a mass number of 2, which means it has one proton and one neutron. Tritium has a mass number of 3, which means it has one proton and two neutrons.

Another example is carbon. Some carbon atoms have a mass number of 12, while others have a mass number of 14. Both types have 6 protons, which makes them carbon atoms, but they have 6 neutrons and 8 neutrons respectively.

## What is an Isobar?

Isobars are atoms of different elements that have different atomic numbers but the same mass number. The total number of nucleons, which are the protons and neutrons, is the same for both atoms. 

To understand this, imagine two different classrooms that have the exact same total number of students, but the number of boys and girls is different. One classroom might have 20 boys and 20 girls, while another has 18 boys and 22 girls. The total number of students is 40 in both cases, but the composition is different. In chemistry, the total number of nucleons is like the total number of students, and the number of protons is like the number of boys.

For example, consider calcium and argon. Calcium has an atomic number of 20, which means it has 20 protons. Argon has an atomic number of 18, which means it has 18 protons. However, both elements have a mass number of 40. This means that the total number of protons and neutrons is 40 for both. Therefore, calcium and argon are isobars.

## Average Atomic Mass

Many elements in nature consist of a mixture of isotopes. For example, chlorine occurs in nature in two isotopic forms. One form has a mass of 35 atomic mass units, and the other has a mass of 37 atomic mass units. These two forms exist in a ratio of 3 to 1.

When we look up the atomic mass of chlorine in the periodic table, we see 35.5 u. This is because we take the average mass of all the naturally occurring atoms of that element. We calculate this by multiplying the mass of each isotope by its percentage abundance in nature and then adding the results.

For chlorine, the calculation is $\left(35 \times \frac{75}{100} + 37 \times \frac{25}{100}\right)$. This equals $\left(\frac{105}{4} + \frac{37}{4}\right)$, which simplifies to $\frac{142}{4}$, giving $35.5 \mathrm{u}$.

It is important to note that this does not mean any single atom of chlorine has a fractional mass. It means that if you take a certain amount of chlorine, it will contain both isotopes, and the average mass will be 35.5 u.

## Chemical and Physical Properties

The chemical properties of isotopes are similar, but their physical properties are different. This is because chemical reactions depend on the number of electrons, which matches the number of protons. Since isotopes have the same number of protons, they have the same number of electrons and behave the same way in chemical reactions.

However, physical properties like density and melting point depend on the mass of the atom. Since isotopes have different masses, they have slightly different physical properties.

## Applications of Isotopes

Normally, we are not concerned about taking a mixture of isotopes because their chemical properties are the same. But some isotopes have special properties which find them useful in various fields.

One major application is in medicine. An isotope of cobalt is used in the treatment of cancer. Another medical application is the use of an isotope of iodine in the treatment of goitre.

In the field of energy, an isotope of uranium is used as a fuel in nuclear reactors.

## Worked Example

Let us look at an example to find the average atomic mass of an element.

Imagine an element, let us call it X, that has two isotopes. The first isotope has a mass of 10 u, and it makes up 60 percent of the natural element. The second isotope has a mass of 11 u, and it makes up the remaining 40 percent.

To find the average atomic mass, we multiply the mass of each isotope by its percentage and add the results.

First, we calculate the contribution of the first isotope: $10 \times 0.60 = 6.0 \mathrm{u}$.

Next, we calculate the contribution of the second isotope: $11 \times 0.40 = 4.4 \mathrm{u}$.

Finally, we add these two values together: $6.0 + 4.4 = 10.4 \mathrm{u}$.

Therefore, the average atomic mass of element X is 10.4 u. This means that if you take a sample of element X from nature, its average mass will be 10.4 u.

## Key points

- Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different mass numbers because they have different numbers of neutrons.
- Isobars are atoms of different elements with different atomic numbers but the same mass number.
- The average atomic mass of an element is the weighted average of the masses of its naturally occurring isotopes, calculated using their percentage abundances.
- The chemical properties of isotopes are similar because they have the same number of protons, but their physical properties are different because they have different masses.
- Some isotopes have special applications, such as an isotope of cobalt for cancer treatment and an isotope of uranium for nuclear reactors.
