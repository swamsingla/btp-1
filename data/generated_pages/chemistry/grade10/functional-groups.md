# Functional Groups

Carbon is a very friendly element. It loves to form bonds with many other atoms besides hydrogen. When carbon forms a chain, it can hold hands with other elements like halogens, oxygen, nitrogen, and sulphur. The element that steps in to replace a hydrogen atom in the chain is called a heteroatom. These heteroatoms group together with carbon to form functional groups. A functional group is the specific part of a molecule that gives the compound its chemical properties. Even if the carbon chain gets longer or shorter, the functional group is the boss. It decides how the compound will react with other substances.

## Heteroatoms and Functional Groups

In a hydrocarbon chain, one or more hydrogen atoms can be swapped out. The new element that takes the hydrogen's place is called a heteroatom. These heteroatoms are the building blocks of functional groups. When they join the carbon chain, the valency of the carbon remains satisfied. This means the carbon atoms still have all their hands full, keeping the molecule stable. The functional group is the exact arrangement of these heteroatoms. It sits on the carbon chain and acts as the chemical identity badge for the whole molecule.

## Common Functional Groups

There are several important functional groups that you will meet in Grade 10. They are defined by the heteroatom they contain. For example, if an oxygen atom is part of the group, it can form an alcohol, an aldehyde, a ketone, or a carboxylic acid. If a halogen like chlorine or bromine is present, it forms a haloalkane. The formula for the alcohol functional group is —OH. The aldehyde, ketone, and carboxylic acid groups also contain oxygen, but they arrange the atoms differently. Each specific arrangement creates a unique set of chemical rules.

## Homologous Series

A homologous series is a family of carbon compounds. In this family, the same functional group takes the place of hydrogen in a carbon chain. The length of the carbon chain can change, but the functional group stays the same. Because the functional group is the same, the chemical properties of the family are also very similar. For instance, consider the compounds $\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{OH}$, $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{5}\mathrm{OH}$, $\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{H}_{7}\mathrm{OH}$, and $\mathrm{C}_{4}\mathrm{H}_{9}\mathrm{OH}$. They all contain the alcohol functional group. Therefore, their chemical properties are very similar. 

As the carbon chain gets longer, the molecular mass increases. This causes a gradation in physical properties. The melting and boiling points increase as the molecular mass goes up. Solubility in a particular solvent also changes in a similar way. However, the chemical properties stay the same because they are determined solely by the functional group.

## Worked Example

Let us look at a homologous series to see how it works. Consider the alkanes. Methane is the first member with the formula $\mathrm{CH}_{4}$. The next member is ethane with the formula $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$. The difference between methane and ethane is a $-CH_{2}$-unit. The next member is propane, $\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{H}_{8}$, which also differs from ethane by a $-CH_{2}$-unit. The next pair is propane and butane, $\mathrm{C}_{4}\mathrm{H}_{10}$. The difference between propane and butane is also a $-CH_{2}$-unit. 

We can check the molecular masses. The atomic mass of carbon is 12 u and the atomic mass of hydrogen is 1 u. The mass difference between propane and butane is $(12 \times 1 + 1 \times 2) = 14$ u. This confirms that the difference is exactly one $-CH_{2}$-unit.

## Key points

- A heteroatom is an element that replaces hydrogen in a carbon chain to form a functional group.
- Functional groups determine the chemical properties of a carbon compound, regardless of the length of the carbon chain.
- A homologous series is a group of compounds with the same functional group, where each successive member differs by a $-CH_{2}$-unit.
- As molecular mass increases in a homologous series, physical properties like melting and boiling points increase.
- Chemical properties remain similar in a homologous series because they are determined solely by the functional group.
