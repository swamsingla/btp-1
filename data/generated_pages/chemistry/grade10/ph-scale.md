# pH Scale

The pH scale is a simple way to tell if a liquid is an acid or a base. It measures how much hydrogen ion, written as $\mathrm{H^{+}(aq)}$, is in a solution. A higher concentration of these ions means the liquid is more acidic and the pH number will be lower. We use this scale in everyday life to keep our environment healthy. For example, if the pH of river water drops too low because of acid rain, the fish and other aquatic life living in the river can die. Similarly, farmers and gardeners check the soil in their backyards to make sure the pH is just right for the plants to grow strong and healthy.

## The pH Formula

To understand the scale, we use a simple mathematical formula. The formula is $pH = -\log[H^{+}]$. Here, the $p$ in pH stands for 'potenz', which is a German word meaning power. The square brackets around $\mathrm{H^{+}}$ show the concentration of the ions in the solution. 

When we use this formula, a neutral solution always has a pH of exactly 7. If a solution has a pH less than 7, it is an acid. If a solution has a pH greater than 7, it is a base, also called an alkali. The scale generally goes from 0, which is very acidic, all the way up to 14, which is very alkaline.

## Measuring pH

How do we actually find out the pH of a liquid? We can use a pH meter or a universal indicator. A universal indicator is a special mixture of several different indicators. When you put this indicator paper into a liquid, it changes colour based on the concentration of hydrogen ions. Generally, paper impregnated with the universal indicator is used for measuring pH.

If the liquid is very acidic, the indicator will turn red. If the liquid is neutral, it will turn green. If the liquid is a base, it will turn blue or purple. This colour change makes it very easy to see the acidic or basic nature of the solution without needing to do any complex math.

## Everyday Importance

The pH scale matters a lot for living things. Our own bodies only work properly within a very narrow pH range of 7.0 to 7.8. If the pH changes too much outside this range, we get sick. Even the air we breathe can have a pH. When the pH of rain water is less than 5.6, it is called acid rain. When this acid rain flows into rivers, it lowers the pH of the river water, which makes it very difficult for aquatic life to survive.

## pH in the Digestive System and the Mouth

Our stomach produces hydrochloric acid. It helps in the digestion of food without harming the stomach. During indigestion the stomach produces too much acid, which causes pain and irritation. To get rid of this pain, people use bases called antacids. One such remedy is milk of magnesia, a mild base, which neutralises the excess acid.

Tooth decay starts when the pH of the mouth falls below 5.5. Tooth enamel, made up of calcium phosphate, is the hardest substance in the body. It does not dissolve in water, but it is corroded when the pH in the mouth is below 5.5. Bacteria present in the mouth produce acids by degrading the sugar and food particles remaining in the mouth after eating. Using toothpastes, which are generally basic, for cleaning the teeth can neutralise the excess acid and prevent tooth decay.

## Self-defence by Animals and Plants

Many animals and plants protect themselves with chemicals. When a honey-bee stings, it leaves an acid (methanoic acid) which causes pain and irritation. Using a mild base like baking soda on the stung area gives relief. Stinging hair of nettle leaves inject methanoic acid causing burning pain, and rubbing a dock plant, which often grows beside the nettle in the wild, gives relief.

## Worked example

Let us see how the formula works with numbers. Suppose a solution has a hydrogen ion concentration $[H^{+}] = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ mol/L. Then $pH = -\log(10^{-3}) = 3$. Since 3 is less than 7, the solution is acidic.

Now take a second solution with $[H^{+}] = 1 \times 10^{-9}$ mol/L. Then $pH = -\log(10^{-9}) = 9$. Since 9 is greater than 7, this solution is a base.

For pure water, $[H^{+}] = 1 \times 10^{-7}$ mol/L, so $pH = 7$, which is neutral.

Compare the first two solutions. Their pH values differ by 6 units, which means the hydrogen ion concentration of the first is $10^{6}$ times that of the second. In general, a fall of one pH unit means ten times as many hydrogen ions. This is why a single number on the pH scale tells us so much about how acidic or basic a solution is.

## Key points

- The pH scale measures the concentration of hydrogen ions in a solution, and $pH = -\log[H^{+}]$.
- A solution with pH 7 is neutral, a pH below 7 means an acid and a pH above 7 means a base; the scale runs from 0 to 14.
- The more hydrogen ions in a solution, the lower its pH.
- Universal indicator shows different colours at different pH values, so it can be used to estimate the pH of a liquid.
- Our body works within a narrow pH range of 7.0 to 7.8, and rain with a pH below 5.6 is called acid rain.
- Tooth decay starts when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5, and antacids relieve indigestion by neutralising excess stomach acid.
