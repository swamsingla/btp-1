# Acids, Bases and Indicators

Understanding the difference between acidic, basic, and neutral substances is essential in everyday life. From the sour taste of a lemon to the cleaning power of household bleach, chemistry is all around us. To tell these substances apart, scientists use special tools called indicators. These indicators change colour when they are mixed with different types of solutions, making it easy to identify whether a liquid is an acid or a base.

## The pH Scale and Universal Indicator

We can use a universal indicator, which is a mixture of several indicators, to distinguish between an acid and a base. The universal indicator shows different colours at different concentrations of hydrogen ions in a solution. To make sense of these colours, chemists use a scale called the pH scale. The p in pH stands for ‘potenz’ in German, meaning power. On this scale, we generally measure pH from 0, which is very acidic, to 14, which is very alkaline. 

You can think of the pH value simply as a number that indicates the acidic or basic nature of a solution. The rule is straightforward: the higher the hydronium ion concentration, the lower the pH value will be. For instance, a neutral solution has a pH of 7. Any value less than 7 represents an acidic solution, while values greater than 7 indicate a basic solution. As the pH value increases from 7 to 14, it represents an increase in OH- ion concentration, meaning the strength of the alkali increases. Generally, paper impregnated with the universal indicator is used for measuring pH in a laboratory setting.

## Types of Indicators

While the universal indicator gives a wide range of colours, specific indicators are used for more targeted testing. Common indicators include litmus, phenolphthalein, and methyl orange. These substances change colour at specific pH ranges, allowing you to pinpoint the exact nature of a solution. Litmus is a classic example, turning red in acids and blue in bases. Phenolphthalein and methyl orange are also widely used in titrations and qualitative analysis to detect the presence of acids or bases in a mixture.

## Worked example

Let us apply the rule about the pH scale to a common household scenario. Imagine you are testing two different liquids using universal indicator paper. The first liquid is lemon juice, which is known to be highly acidic. According to the rules of the pH scale, a highly acidic solution has a low pH value. Therefore, the pH of the lemon juice would be below 7, in fact close to 2. 

The second liquid is a solution of baking soda mixed with water. This is a basic solution. Because the pH scale increases in basicity as it goes from 7 to 14, the pH of the baking soda solution would be above 7, around 8 to 9. By comparing the colour of the indicator paper, you can easily tell that the lemon juice has a lower pH than the baking soda solution.

## Key points

*   The pH scale measures the acidic or basic nature of a solution from 0 to 14.
*   A pH of 7 is neutral, less than 7 is acidic, and greater than 7 is basic.
*   Higher hydronium ion concentration results in a lower pH value.
*   Universal indicator is a mixture of several indicators that show different colours at different ion concentrations.
*   Paper impregnated with universal indicator is generally used for measuring pH.
