# Water and Its Properties

Water is a very important substance that is all around us. We use it to drink, to wash our clothes, and to grow our food. It is a special substance because it can exist in three different forms, which are called states. Understanding how water behaves helps us understand many natural processes on Earth.

## The Three States of Water

Water can be seen in three different states in our daily life. In the solid state, water exists as ice. When you heat ice, it melts and changes into a liquid. If you heat the liquid water even more, it changes into its gaseous state, which we call water vapour. 

Think about a glass of water you leave outside on a cold winter morning. By the next day, the liquid water has frozen into solid ice. If you bring that ice inside and place it near a warm stove, the ice will melt and turn back into liquid water. If you continue to heat the liquid, it will eventually turn into invisible water vapour. This is a very common process that happens all the time in nature.

## Changing the States of Water

The state of water can change when we add or remove heat. If we want to change ice into water, we have to supply heat to it. This process is called melting. If we want to change water into ice, we have to take away the heat. This process is called freezing.

We can change the state of water by placing it in a cold environment, such as a freezer. Water freezes and is converted into ice. If we take the ice out of the freezer, it melts and is converted into water. The same rules apply to other substances. For example, a candle is made of wax. If you heat the solid wax, it melts into a liquid. If you cool the liquid wax, it turns back into a solid. You might have seen coconut oil turn solid during the winter season.

## The Water Cycle

The water cycle is the continuous journey of water as it changes between its three states and moves around the Earth. It is a very important process that provides water for all living things.

The cycle begins when the sun heats up liquid water in rivers, lakes, and oceans. The heat causes the liquid water to turn into water vapour, which is the gaseous state. This water vapour rises into the air. As it goes higher, the air gets cooler. The cool air causes the water vapour to change back into tiny liquid drops. This process is called condensation.

These tiny liquid drops join together to form clouds. When the drops become too heavy, they fall back down to the Earth as rain. The rain collects in rivers and oceans, and the cycle starts all over again.

## Water as a Solvent

A solvent is a substance that can dissolve other substances to form a mixture called a solution. Water is an excellent solvent. This means that many different things can mix and dissolve in water.

When you add sugar to a glass of water and stir it, the sugar disappears. The sugar has dissolved into the water. The water is the solvent, and the sugar is the substance being dissolved. This property of water is very useful because it helps our bodies absorb nutrients from the food we eat, and it helps plants absorb minerals from the soil.

## Worked Example

Let us follow one sample of water through all three of its states. You pour 500 mL of liquid water into a tray and put the tray into a freezer. By the next morning the water has lost enough heat to freeze into solid ice. The ice holds the same amount of water you poured in, because freezing changes the state of the water and not the quantity of it.

You take the tray out and leave it on the kitchen counter. The ice now absorbs heat from the warm air of the room and melts back into 500 mL of liquid water. You pour that water into a pan and heat it on a stove. The water boils and leaves the pan as water vapour, which is its gaseous state.

Each of those three changes happened for the same reason: heat was either supplied or taken away. At every stage the substance in the tray or the pan was still water.

## Key points

- Water exists in three states: solid ice, liquid water, and gaseous water vapour.
- Supplying heat changes ice into water, and water into water vapour.
- Removing heat changes water vapour into water, and water into ice.
- Melting is the change from solid to liquid and freezing is the change from liquid to solid.
- The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between its states and across the Earth, driven by heat from the Sun.
- Water is an excellent solvent, which is why substances such as sugar dissolve in it.
