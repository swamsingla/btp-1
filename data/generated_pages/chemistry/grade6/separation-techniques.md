# Separation Techniques

A mixture is made of two or more different substances that are mixed together but not joined by a chemical bond. So a mixture can be separated back into its original parts. Scientists use special methods to separate the different parts of a mixture. This is important because it helps us get pure substances back from the things we use every day.

## Sieving and Handpicking

Sieving is a method that separates solids from a mixture based on variations in particle size using a sieve. Imagine you are sifting wheat flour before making rotis. The fine flour passes through the small holes of the sieve, while the larger bits of bran are held back. This is how sieving separates a mixture of solids whose particles are of different sizes.

Handpicking is used for separating solid materials on the basis of differences in size, colour and shape from a mixture. Suppose you are eating rice and you find a small stone mixed in. You can use your fingers to pick the stone out of the rice. This simple action is called handpicking, and it is a very useful way to separate unwanted items from food.

## Settling and Pouring

Sedimentation is the process of settling down of heavier insoluble component at the bottom of a liquid. When you leave muddy water in a bucket, the heavier dirt particles sink to the bottom. This happens because gravity pulls the heavy dirt down. Decantation is when the liquid is removed by tilting the vessel. After the dirt has settled, you can carefully pour the clean water into another container without disturbing the mud at the bottom.

## Evaporation and Filtration

Evaporation is the process in which a liquid gets converted into its vapour. It can be used to separate a solid dissolved in a liquid. If you leave a cup of salty water out in the hot sun, the water will slowly disappear. The salt will be left behind at the bottom of the cup. This happens because the water turns into a gas and leaves the salt behind.

Filtration can be used to separate insoluble solid components from a liquid. Imagine you have a mixture of sand and water. You can pour this mixture through a filter paper. The filter paper will trap the solid sand particles. The clean water will pass through the paper and collect in a container below. This method is very useful for separating solid dirt from water.

## Working with Mixtures

Churning is used to extract butter from curd. You can take fresh curd and put it in a traditional churn. If you move the churn up and down quickly, the milk fats will separate from the liquid buttermilk. The solid butter will form at the top. This is a great example of how physical force can separate parts of a mixture.

Separation of magnetic and non-magnetic substances by using a magnet is called magnetic separation. If you have a pile of iron nails mixed with plastic beads, you can pass a magnet over the pile. The magnet will pull out the iron nails. The plastic beads will stay behind because they are not magnetic.

## Worked example

You have a mixture of 100 grams of sand and 50 grams of salt, and you want to separate the two. Sand does not dissolve in water but salt does, and that single difference is enough to do it.

First, you add 200 millilitres of water to the mixture and stir. The 50 grams of salt dissolves completely. The 100 grams of sand does not dissolve and settles at the bottom.

Next, you pour the whole mixture through a filter paper. The sand is trapped on the paper and the salt water passes through into a bowl below. You dry the paper and recover the 100 grams of sand. Finally, you heat the salt water until all the water has turned to vapour and left the bowl, and the 50 grams of salt is left behind in it. The sand and the salt are now in separate containers, 100 grams of one and 50 grams of the other.

## Key points
- Sieving separates solids based on particle size using a sieve.
- Handpicking separates solids based on differences in size, colour, and shape.
- Sedimentation lets heavy insoluble parts settle at the bottom of a liquid.
- Decantation removes the liquid by carefully tilting the vessel.
- Filtration separates insoluble solids from liquids using a filter.
- Evaporation turns a liquid into vapour to separate it from a dissolved solid.
- Churning extracts butter from curd by physical movement.
- Magnetic separation uses a magnet to pull out magnetic materials.
