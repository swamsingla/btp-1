# Area of Triangles

Area tells us how much flat space a shape covers. We measure this space in square units, like square centimetres or square metres. Knowing the area of a triangle is very useful. You might use it to find the area of a slice of land or a piece of cloth.

## From Rectangles to Triangles

You already know how to find the area of a rectangle. You multiply its length by its width. A triangle is closely related to a rectangle. If you draw a diagonal across a rectangle, you create two triangles. Each of these triangles covers exactly half of the rectangle.

## The Triangle Area Rule

Because a triangle is half of a rectangle, we can find its area easily. We use a simple rule: multiply the base by the height, and then divide by 2. The base is the bottom side of the triangle. The height is the straight distance from the base to the top point.

The formula for the area of any triangle is:
$$ A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h $$
This rule works for all triangles. It does not matter if the triangle looks tall or short, wide or narrow.

Here is a worked example. Imagine a triangle with a base of $10$ cm and a height of $6$ cm. First, multiply the base and the height: $10 \times 6 = 60$ square cm. Next, divide this number by 2. $60 \div 2 = 30$. So, the area of this triangle is $30$ square cm.

## Different Triangles, Same Rule

Triangles can look very different, but the rule stays the same. You might see a right-angled triangle. You might also see a long, skinny triangle. Even if the sides look completely different, you still use the same formula. 

Imagine a right-angled triangle. The two shorter sides form the base and the height. You can calculate its area exactly the same way. The shape of the triangle does not change the rule. You just need to find the correct base and the correct height.

## Key points
* The area of any triangle is half the area of a rectangle with the same base and height.
* The formula for area is $A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$.
* The base is the bottom side, and the height is the perpendicular distance to the top point.
* This rule works for all types of triangles.
* Area is always measured in square units.
