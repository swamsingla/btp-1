# Decimals

Decimals are the natural extension of the Indian place value system to represent decimal fractions ( $\frac{1}{10}$ ,  $\frac{1}{100}$ ,  $\frac{1}{1000}$ , and so on) and their sums. They matter because they allow us to measure and record quantities that are smaller than a whole unit, such as the weight of spices or the length of a piece of cloth.

## Fractions with Powers of 10
A fraction with a denominator that is a power of 10 can be written as a sum of fractions and then as a decimal. For example, the fraction $\frac{254}{1000}$ can be expanded as $\frac{200}{1000} + \frac{50}{1000} + \frac{4}{1000}$, which simplifies to $\frac{2}{10} + \frac{5}{100} + \frac{4}{1000}$. This sum is written as the decimal 0.254. Similarly, $\frac{847}{1000}$ is 0.847, $\frac{173}{100}$ is 1.73, and $\frac{23}{1000}$ is 0.023.

## Dividing by 10, 100, 1000
There is a simple rule to divide any number by a number of the form 1 followed by zeroes — 10, 100, 1000, etc. To divide 123 by 10, first write the dividend as it is and place a decimal point at the end, giving 123. Then, count the number of zeroes in the divisor, which is 1 zero for the number 10. Finally, move the decimal point from the end of the dividend left by the same number of places as the count of zeroes. If needed, add zeroes in front. Thus, $123 \div 10 = 12.3$. For $24 \div 100$, the result is 0.24, and for $678 \div 1000$, the result is 0.678.

## Locating Decimals on a Number Line
A decimal number can be located on a number line by dividing the unit between two whole numbers into 10 equal parts. For instance, the decimal number 1.4 is equal to 1 unit and 4 tenths. This means that the unit between 1 and 2 is divided into 10 equal parts, and 4 such parts are taken. Therefore, 1.4 lies between 1 and 2 on the number line.

## Comparing Decimal Numbers
To compare decimal numbers, we look at the digits at each place value starting from the left. For example, to determine which is larger, 6.456 or 6.465, we compare the digits at the units, tenths, hundredths, and thousandths places. Both numbers have 6 units and 4 tenths. At the hundredths place, the first number has 5 hundredths, whereas the second number has 6 hundredths. The number with the larger digit at this position is the greater of the two, so 6.465 is larger than 6.456.

## Addition and Subtraction of Decimals
The standard procedure for adding and subtracting whole numbers can be used to add and subtract decimals. For example, if Priya requires 2.7 m of cloth for her skirt and Shylaja requires 3.5 m for her kurti, the total quantity of cloth needed is the sum $2.7 + 3.5 = 6.2$ m. To find how much longer Shylaja's cloth is compared to Priya's, we calculate the difference $3.5 - 2.7 = 0.8$ m.

## Worked example
Consider the fraction $\frac{603}{100}$. To write this as a decimal, we first expand the numerator: $\frac{600}{100} + \frac{0}{100} + \frac{3}{100}$, which simplifies to $6 + \frac{0}{10} + \frac{3}{100}$. The sum of these fractions is $6 + 0.03$, which equals 6.03.

## Key points
* Decimals are an extension of the Indian place value system to represent decimal fractions.
* Fractions with denominators of 10, 100, 1000, etc., can be written as sums of fractions and then as decimals.
* To divide by 10, 100, or 1000, write the dividend, place a decimal point at the end, and move the decimal point left by the number of zeroes in the divisor.
* Decimal numbers can be located on a number line by dividing the unit between two whole numbers into 10 equal parts.
* Decimals can be compared by examining the digits at each place value, starting from the left.
* Decimals are added and subtracted using the same standard procedure as whole numbers.
