# Simple Interest

Have you ever wondered how a bank rewards you for keeping your money with them, or how much extra you must pay when you borrow from a friend? Simple interest is the extra money, called interest, that a bank or lender charges you for borrowing a certain amount, or the extra money that a bank pays you for keeping your money. It is a basic way to calculate the cost of borrowing or the return on saving, and it is widely used in everyday life.

## The Three Main Parts
To calculate simple interest, you need to understand three specific terms. The principal is the original amount of money that is borrowed or deposited. The rate is the rate of interest per annum, which means the interest calculated for one year. The time is the duration, usually measured in years, for which the money is borrowed or kept in the bank.

## The Formula
The rule for finding simple interest uses a direct formula. The formula is $I = \frac{PRT}{100}$, where $I$ is the interest, $P$ is the principal, $R$ is the rate, and $T$ is the time in years. This formula works because it takes the principal amount and multiplies it by the rate and the time, then divides the result by 100 to find the correct interest value.

## The Total Amount
Once you know the simple interest, you can easily find the total amount of money. The total amount is simply the principal plus the interest. If you want to calculate it in one step, you can use the formula $A = P \left(1 + \frac{RT}{100}\right)$, where $A$ is the total amount.

## Worked Example
Suppose you borrow ₹5000 from a bank. The principal $P$ is ₹5000. The bank charges a rate of interest per annum of 5%. You keep the money for 2 years. To find the interest, you use the formula $I = \frac{5000 \times 5 \times 2}{100}$. This simplifies to $I = \frac{50000}{100}$, which means the interest is ₹500. The total amount you must pay back is ₹5000 + ₹500, which equals ₹5500.

## Key points
- Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount.
- The rate of interest per annum is always calculated for a period of one year.
- The formula for simple interest is $I = \frac{PRT}{100}$.
- The total amount is the principal plus the simple interest.
- Compound interest, where interest is calculated on previous interest, is taught in later grades.
