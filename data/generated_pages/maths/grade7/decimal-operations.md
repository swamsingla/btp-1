# Decimal Operations

Decimal operations allow us to perform arithmetic with numbers that include fractional parts. These operations are essential in everyday life, such as calculating the total length of cloth needed for a project or finding the distance a student walks to school. By extending the standard rules for whole numbers to include tenths, hundredths, and thousandths, we can solve a wide variety of practical problems.

## Multiplication of Decimals

Multiplying decimals follows a straightforward rule derived from multiplying their fractional equivalents. To multiply two decimals, you first remove the decimal points and multiply the resulting whole numbers together. Then, you count the total number of digits to the right of the decimal points in the original numbers. The decimal point in your final product is placed so that it has the same total number of digits to its right.

For example, consider the product of $5.96$ and $24.8$. If you know that $596 \times 248 = 147808$, you can immediately write down the product of the decimals. The first number has 2 decimal places, and the second has 1, giving a total of 3 decimal places. Therefore, the product is $147.808$.

## Multiplication and Natural Numbers

A key question in decimal arithmetic is whether the product of two decimals can be a natural number. The answer is yes. The number of decimal places you expect in a product is the sum of the decimal places in the two numbers being multiplied, but trailing zeros in that result can cancel out, sometimes leaving zero decimal places even when the sum was not zero. For instance, $0.5 \times 2$: the whole-number product is $5 \times 2 = 10$, and since $0.5$ has 1 decimal place and $2$ has none, we place the decimal point 1 digit from the right, giving $1.0$, which is the natural number $1$. Similarly, $2.5 \times 0.4$: the whole-number product is $25 \times 4 = 100$, and with 2 decimal places expected, this gives $1.00$, which is also the natural number $1$.

## Addition and Subtraction of Decimals

Adding and subtracting decimals relies on the standard place value procedures used for whole numbers. You align the numbers by their decimal points and perform the operation column by column, carrying over or borrowing as necessary. This method ensures that tenths are added to tenths, hundredths to hundredths, and so on.

For example, if Priya requires $2.7$ m of cloth and Shylaja requires $3.5$ m, the total quantity needed is $6.2$ m. The calculation is $2.7 + 3.5 = 6.2$. Similarly, to find how much longer Shylaja's cloth is compared to Priya's, you subtract $2.7$ from $3.5$, which gives $0.8$ m.

## Division by Powers of Ten

Dividing a number by a power of ten, such as $10$, $100$, or $1000$, is a simple process. You write the dividend as it is and place a decimal point at the end. Then, you count the number of zeroes in the divisor. Finally, you move the decimal point to the left by the same number of places as the count of zeroes. If you run out of digits, you add zeroes in front to complete the move.

For example, $24 \div 100 = 0.24$. Here, the divisor $100$ has 2 zeroes, so the decimal point in $24$ is moved 2 places to the left, adding a zero in front. Likewise, $678 \div 1000 = 0.678$, and $12 \div 1000 = 0.012$.

## Comparing Products to the Original Numbers

When multiplying decimals, the resulting product can be greater than, less than, or equal to the numbers being multiplied, depending on the values involved. This behavior mirrors the multiplication of fractions.

If both numbers are greater than 1, such as $3.4 \times 6.5$, the product is greater than both numbers. If both numbers are between 0 and 1, such as $0.75 \times 0.4$, the product is less than both numbers. If one number is between 0 and 1 and the other is greater than 1, such as $0.75 \times 5$, the product is less than the number greater than 1 and greater than the number between 0 and 1.

## Worked example

To find the product of $5.8$ and $1.24$, we first multiply $58$ and $124$ to get $7192$. The multiplier $5.8$ has 1 digit after the decimal point, and the multiplicand $1.24$ has 2 digits after the decimal point. The sum of these digits is 3. Therefore, we place the decimal point in $7192$ so that there are 3 digits to its right, resulting in $7.192$.

## Key points

*   To multiply two decimals, multiply the numbers without their decimal points, then place the decimal point in the product so that the total number of decimal places equals the sum of the decimal places in the original numbers.
*   The product of two decimals can be a natural number if the total number of decimal places in the product is zero.
*   Adding and subtracting decimals is performed by aligning the decimal points and using standard whole number procedures for each place value.
*   To divide by $10$, $100$, or $1000$, move the decimal point to the left by the number of zeroes in the divisor.
*   The size of a decimal product relative to the original numbers depends on whether the numbers are greater than or less than 1.
