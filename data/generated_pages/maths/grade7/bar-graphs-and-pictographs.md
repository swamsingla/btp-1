# Bar Graphs, Pictographs and Pie Charts

You may already know how a pictograph and a bar graph turn a list of numbers into a picture. This page goes one step further: putting two or more sets of data on the same graph so that they can be compared directly, and reading such a graph in an orderly way. Presenting data as a picture so that it can be understood more clearly is called data visualisation.

## Clustered Column Graphs

A column graph uses vertical bars to represent values. The height of each bar corresponds to the amount of data it represents. When we have data from two different categories for the same groups, we can combine them into a single graph by drawing the bars side by side. This is called a clustered column graph or a double column graph.

To help people who find difficulty in distinguishing colours, or when things are printed in greyscale, we can use dots and slanted lines within the bars to clearly separate the data.

Imagine the monthly onion prices in two cities, Yahapur and Wahapur, over a year. The table below shows the prices.

|  | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yahapur | 25 | 24 | 26 | 28 | 30 | 35 | 39 | 43 | 49 | 56 | 59 | 44 |
| Wahapur | 19 | 17 | 23 | 30 | 38 | 35 | 42 | 39 | 53 | 60 | 52 | 42 |

To visualise this, we draw a clustered column graph. For each month, we draw two bars side by side: one for Yahapur and one for Wahapur. We use different colours to clearly separate the data from the two places.

The relative heights of the bars tell us where onions are costlier in each month. We can also visually estimate the difference by referring to the markings along the vertical line.

## Interpreting Graphs with a Two-Step Process

Often there is a lot of information in graphs to understand. We can follow a 2-step process to simplify making sense of the data in graphs.

### Step 1: Identify what is given

Notice how the graph is organised, what scale is used, and what patterns the data shows.

For example, consider a graph showing the number of worldwide rocket launches by different organisations for the years 2021, 2022, and 2023. For each organisation, the numbers of rocket launches for the years 2021, 2022, and 2023 are shown as three adjacent bars. The scale used is 1 unit length = 20 rockets.

The 'Others' category indicates multiple organisations worldwide that are clubbed together to keep the graph short. In the graph of onion prices the months are shown in order, January to December, so that the change over time can be seen; here, changing the order of the organisations does not affect the meaning.

### Step 2: Infer from what is given

Analyse and interpret each of your observations.

For the rocket launch graph, we can say that the USA, China, and Russia are the leading rocket launching countries in the given time period. SpaceX launched about twice the number of rockets in 2022 compared to 2021. And it launched about 35 more rockets in 2023 compared to 2022.

The number of rockets launched by Arianespace decreased every year. United Launch Alliance launched more rockets in 2022 than in 2021. They launched fewer rockets in 2023 than in both the years 2022 and 2021. Other organisations launched about 25 rockets in 2023.

## Comparing Data Over Time

A clustered graph can also compare two places across a whole year. Take two cities, City 1 and City 2, and the hours of daylight each one gets in every month, that is, the time for which the Sun is at least partly above the horizon. Dividing a month's daylight hours by the number of days in that month gives the average daylight hours per day, and a clustered bar graph shows these averages with two bars for every month.

Step 1 is to identify what is given. The horizontal line shows the months of the year, and the vertical line shows the average daylight hours per day, using the scale 1 unit = 5 hours. The month of June has the maximum value for City 1 and the minimum value for City 2.

Step 2 is to infer from what is given.

The average number of daylight hours per day in City 1 increases from January, reaching a maximum of about 17–18 hours in June. It then decreases, reaching a minimum of about 6 hours in December.

The average number of daylight hours per day in City 2 decreases from January, reaching a minimum of about 9 hours in June. It then increases, reaching a maximum of about 15 hours in December.

The maximum and minimum values in City 1 are more extreme than those of City 2. That is, the maximum number of daylight hours per day of City 1 is more than that of City 2, and the minimum number of daylight hours per day of City 1 is less than that of City 2.

In June, City 1 experiences daylight for about $\frac{3}{4}$th of the full day (24 hours), whereas during December–January, it only experiences daylight for about $\frac{1}{4}$th of the full day.

## Worked example

Use the table of onion prices above to compare the two places in three different months. In March the price is 26 in Yahapur and 23 in Wahapur, so Yahapur's bar is the taller one in that cluster, and the difference is $26 - 23 = 3$. In May the price is 30 in Yahapur and 38 in Wahapur, so now Wahapur's bar is the taller one, by $38 - 30 = 8$. In June both prices are 35, so the two bars in that cluster have the same height. On the graph each of these comparisons is made at a glance, by seeing which bar in the cluster is taller and reading how much taller from the markings on the vertical line.

## Key points

*   A clustered column graph uses vertical bars to represent values and can show data from two different categories side by side.
*   We can follow a 2-step process to identify and interpret information presented in graphs.
*   Different colours, or dots and slanted lines within the bars, keep the sets of data apart.
*   The relative heights of the bars in a cluster show which value is greater, and the markings on the vertical line show by how much.
*   Months are shown in order so that change over time can be seen; categories with no natural order can be placed in any order.
