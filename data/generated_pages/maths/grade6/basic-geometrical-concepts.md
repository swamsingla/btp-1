# Points, Lines and Planes

Geometry is the study of shapes, sizes, and the spaces they take up. It is the foundation for everything from building bridges to designing video games. To understand more complex shapes, you must start with the most basic building blocks of 'plane geometry'. The point, the line and the plane are so simple that they cannot be described in terms of anything more basic, so geometry takes them as given and describes everything else from them. This page explains five building blocks: the point, the line segment, the ray, the line, and the plane.

## The Point

A point is the most basic idea in geometry. It represents an exact location in space. Think of it like the tip of a pencil touching a piece of paper. A point has no length, no width, and no thickness. You cannot measure a point because it is just a position. In geometry, we usually name a point with a single capital letter, like $A$ or $B$.

## The Line Segment

A line segment is a part of a line that has two fixed endpoints. Imagine you have a piece of string and you tie a knot at both ends. The string between the two knots is a line segment. It has a specific, measurable length. We write the name of a line segment by putting a bar over the two letters of its endpoints. For example, if the endpoints are $A$ and $B$, we write the segment as $\overline{AB}$.

## The Ray

A ray is a part of a line that has one fixed endpoint, but it goes on forever in one direction. Imagine a flashlight shining a beam of light into a dark room. The flashlight is the starting point, and the beam of light travels out into space forever. In geometry, we name a ray using its starting point and another point on the ray. We draw a small arrow above the letters. For example, if the ray starts at $O$ and passes through $A$, we write it as $\overrightarrow{OA}$. 

You can name a ray in more than one way, as long as the starting point is always the first letter. For example, if a ray starts at $O$ and passes through $A$, it also passes through $B$. You can call it $\overrightarrow{OA}$ or $\overrightarrow{OB}$. However, you cannot write it backwards. You cannot write $AO$ because that would mean the ray starts at $A$, which changes its direction completely.

## The Line

A line is a straight path that extends forever in both directions. It has no endpoints at all, which is what separates it from a line segment and from a ray. Because it never stops, a line has no measurable length: you can measure a segment in centimetres, but you cannot say how long a line is.

This also explains the two shapes above. A line segment is a piece cut out of a line between two chosen points, and a ray is what is left of a line when you keep one endpoint and travel in only one direction. We name a line by any two points lying on it, with a double-headed arrow above to show it runs both ways, so the line through $A$ and $B$ is written $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$.

## The Plane

A plane is a flat, two-dimensional surface that extends forever in all directions. It has no thickness, just like a sheet of paper, but unlike a piece of paper, it never ends. You can think of the surface of a calm lake or a perfectly flat mirror. In geometry, we usually name a plane using a single capital letter, like $P$, or by three points that lie on it.

## Worked example

Let us look at a worked example to see how these terms fit together. Imagine you are looking at a straight road. The road has a start gate at point $O$. A car drives away from the gate along the road. The distance from the gate to the first mile marker is 1 kilometre. This distance from the gate to the marker is a line segment $\overline{OA}$. The car keeps driving past the marker and goes on forever. The path of the car is a ray $\overrightarrow{OA}$. If we imagine the road is perfectly flat and stretches out to the horizon in both directions, it acts like a line. If we imagine the road is the surface of a giant, flat sheet of glass, it acts like a plane.

## Key points
* A point is a location with no size, length, width, or thickness.
* A line segment has two endpoints, giving it a fixed length.
* A ray has one endpoint and continues infinitely in one direction.
* A line extends infinitely in both directions and has no thickness.
* A plane is a flat, two-dimensional surface that extends infinitely in all directions.
