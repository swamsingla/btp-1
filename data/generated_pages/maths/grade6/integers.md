# Integers

You already know how to count using whole numbers like 1, 2, 3, and 4. You also know about zero, which represents nothing, but did you know there are numbers that are less than zero? These are called negative numbers, and together with the positive numbers and zero, they make up a special group of numbers called integers. Understanding integers is very useful. For example, we use them to describe temperatures below freezing, to track money in a bank account, and to count floors in a building that are below ground level.

## The Number Line

You may have already seen a number line. Usually, it starts at zero and goes to the right with the counting numbers 1, 2, 3, and so on. But integers include numbers to the left of zero as well. These numbers are negative. The full list of integers looks like this: ..., -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, .... Zero is special because it is neither a positive nor a negative number. We do not put a plus or minus sign in front of it.

## Positive and Negative Numbers

A number with a plus sign in front is called a positive number. A number with a minus sign in front is called a negative number. For example, +5 is a positive number and -5 is a negative number. In everyday life, you can think of positive numbers as amounts you gain, and negative numbers as amounts you lose.

## The Building of Fun

Imagine a tall building called the 'Building of Fun'. The ground floor of this building is called the 'Welcome Hall' and it is Floor 0. If you want to go up one floor to the Food Court, you press the 'plus' button on the lift once. We write this movement as +1. The Food Court is on Floor +1. If you want to go up two floors to the Art Centre, you press the 'plus' button twice. We write this as +2, and the Art Centre is on Floor +2.

Now, what about the floors below the ground? To go down one floor to the Toy Store, you press the 'minus' button once. We write this movement as -1. The Toy Store is on Floor -1. To go down two floors to the Video Games shop, you press the 'minus' button twice. We write this as -2, and the Video Games shop is on Floor -2.

## Addition and Movement

We can use addition to keep track of our movement in the lift. The rule is: Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor. For example, if you start at the Food Court, which is Floor +1, and you press the 'plus' button twice (which is a movement of +2), you will reach Floor +3. We can write this as $(+1)+(+2)=+3$.

## Credits and Debits

Integers are also used in banking. Suppose you open a bank account with ₹100. This is a credit, so your balance starts at +₹100. The next day, you make ₹60 at your job and deposit it. This is another credit. Your new balance is ₹160.

Then, you pay your electric bill of ₹30. This is a debit, which we can think of as a negative number. Your bank balance is now ₹130.

The next day, you make a major purchase for ₹150. This is another debit. Your bank balance is now -₹20. This means you are in debt. Some banks do allow your account balance to become negative temporarily.

The next day, you make ₹200 at your business. Your new balance is ₹180.

## Worked example

Let's apply the rules for adding integers. Suppose you are at Floor -2. You press the 'plus' button three times to go up to Floor +1. We can write this as $(-2)+(+3)=+1$.

Now, let's try another one. Suppose you are at Floor +5. You press the 'minus' button four times to go down to Floor +1. We can write this as $(+5)+(-4)=+1$.

## Key points

- Integers are the numbers ..., -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...
- A positive number has a plus sign in front of it and a negative number has a minus sign
- Zero is neither positive nor negative
- We use addition to keep track of movement: Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor
- Credits are positive numbers and debits are negative numbers
