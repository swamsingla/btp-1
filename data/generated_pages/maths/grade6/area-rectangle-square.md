# Area of Rectangle and Square

Understanding area is a fundamental skill in mathematics. Area measures the amount of flat space inside a two-dimensional shape. Knowing how to calculate the area helps you solve real-world problems, such as figuring out how much paint you need for a wall, how much grass seed to buy for a garden, or how much wrapping paper to cover a gift box. This page gives the rules for finding the area of two very common shapes: the square and the rectangle.

## Defining the Square and the Rectangle

Before calculating area, it helps to know exactly what makes a square and a rectangle. 

A square is a shape where all four sides are equal in length, and all four of its interior angles are $90^{\circ}$. Because every side is the same length, a square is a very regular and balanced shape. 

A rectangle is also a shape with four sides and four interior angles that are all $90^{\circ}$. However, unlike a square, a rectangle has two pairs of opposite sides. The sides that lie opposite to each other are equal in length. For example, in a rectangle named ABCD, the sides AB and CD are one pair of opposite sides and are equal to each other, while AD and BC are the other pair and are equal to each other. 

Because a square has all sides equal, it is actually a special type of rectangle. A rectangle where all four sides are equal is simply a square. 

## Finding the Area of a Rectangle

To find the area of a rectangle, you multiply its length by its width. The formula for the area of a rectangle is $A = l \times w$, where $A$ stands for area, $l$ stands for length, and $w$ stands for width. 

Imagine you have a rectangular floor tile that is 6 cm long and 4 cm wide. To find out how many square centimeters of space the tile covers, you multiply the length by the width. So, you calculate $6 \times 4 = 24$. The area of the tile is $24 \text{ cm}^2$. 

It is important to remember that area is always measured in square units, such as square centimeters ($\text{cm}^2$), square meters ($\text{m}^2$), or square feet ($\text{ft}^2$). You cannot just say the area is 24; you must include the square unit to show that you are measuring a flat, two-dimensional space. 

## Finding the Area of a Square

Finding the area of a square is very similar to finding the area of a rectangle, but because a square has equal sides, the rule is a little simpler. To find the area of a square, you multiply the length of one side by itself. This is written as $A = s \times s$, or simply $A = s^2$, where $s$ stands for the length of one side of the square.

Let us look at a square with a side length of 6 cm. Because all four sides of a square are equal, you can calculate the area by multiplying the side length by itself. You calculate $6 \times 6 = 36$. The area of the square is $36 \text{ cm}^2$. 

You can think of this as a grid of tiny squares inside the big square. If the side is 6 cm, the grid is 6 squares long and 6 squares wide, giving you a total of 36 small squares.

## Worked example

Let us apply these rules to a practical situation to make sure you understand how to use them. 

Imagine you are helping your mother plant marigolds in a rectangular garden bed. The garden bed is 10 meters long and 3 meters wide. To figure out how much space the flowers will cover, you need to find the area of the rectangle. 

You use the formula $A = l \times w$. You substitute the length and width into the formula: $A = 10 \times 3$. This equals $30$. Therefore, the area of the garden bed is $30 \text{ m}^2$. 

Now, imagine you have a square courtyard in your school playground. You measure one side of the courtyard and find that it is exactly 8 meters long. To find the area of this square courtyard, you use the formula $A = s^2$. You substitute the side length into the formula: $A = 8^2$. This equals $8 \times 8 = 64$. The area of the square courtyard is $64 \text{ m}^2$.

## Key points

*   A square has four equal sides and four $90^{\circ}$ angles, while a rectangle has opposite sides that are equal and four $90^{\circ}$ angles.
*   The area of a rectangle is found by multiplying its length by its width: $A = l \times w$.
*   The area of a square is found by multiplying the length of one side by itself: $A = s^2$.
*   Area is always measured in square units, such as $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$.
*   You may already know how to find the perimeter of these shapes, but area measures the flat space inside a shape, which is a different concept.
