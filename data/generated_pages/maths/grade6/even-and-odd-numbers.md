# Even and Odd Numbers

Every whole number you know is either even or odd. This simple split, called **parity**, is one of the most useful tools in mathematics. It tells you how a number behaves when you share it or add it to others, and it helps you solve puzzles without guessing every single time.

## What Are Even and Odd Numbers?

An even number is any whole number that can be arranged in pairs without leaving a single item behind. This means an even number is perfectly divisible by 2. For example, the number 10 can be split into 5 pairs. Because it is divisible by 2, we can write 10 as $2 \times 5$.

An odd number is any whole number that cannot be arranged in pairs. If you try to pair up an odd number, exactly one item will always be left over. We can think of an odd number as one more than a collection of pairs. For example, 11 is one more than 10, which is 5 pairs.

## Adding Even and Odd Numbers

When you add numbers, their parity determines the parity of the final total.

If you add any number of even numbers together, the sum is always even. Since each even number can be arranged in pairs, combining them just makes a larger collection of pairs. For example, $4 + 6 = 10$, and 10 is even.

If you add two odd numbers together, the sum is always even. This happens because the extra single item from the first odd number pairs up with the extra single item from the second odd number. For example, $3 + 5 = 8$, and 8 is even.

If you add three odd numbers together, the sum is always odd. The first two odd numbers pair up to make an even number, but that even number plus the third odd number leaves one item unpaired. For example, $3 + 5 + 7 = 15$, and 15 is odd.

## The Sum of Consecutive Numbers

The numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on, alternate between odd and even. Because of this, any two consecutive numbers will always have one odd and one even number. When you add an even number and an odd number, the sum is always odd. This is because the even number has no extra items, and the odd number has one extra item that cannot be paired. For example, $6 + 7 = 13$, which is odd.

## Solving Puzzles with Parity

You can use parity to check if a puzzle has a solution. Imagine a puzzle where you must place five cards into five boxes. The five cards are all odd numbers, and the boxes must add up to 30. Since 30 is an even number, and adding five odd numbers always results in an odd number, this puzzle has no solution.

Another example is checking the ages of two siblings born exactly one year apart. Their ages are consecutive numbers, meaning one is even and one is odd. If they claim their ages add up to 112, this is impossible. The sum of an even number and an odd number is always odd, but 112 is an even number.

## Worked example

Let us look at a puzzle involving five odd number cards. The cards are 3, 5, 7, 9, and 11. We want to know if their sum is even or odd. 

First, we add the first two odd numbers: $3 + 5 = 8$, which is even. 
Next, we add the third odd number to our result: $8 + 7 = 15$, which is odd.
Then, we add the fourth odd number: $15 + 9 = 24$, which is even.
Finally, we add the fifth odd number: $24 + 11 = 35$, which is odd. 

Since we added five odd numbers, the final result is an odd number.

## Key points

*   An even number is divisible by 2 and can be arranged in pairs.
*   An odd number leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 2.
*   The sum of any number of even numbers is always even.
*   The sum of two odd numbers is always even, but the sum of three odd numbers is always odd.
*   The sum of an even number and an odd number is always odd.
