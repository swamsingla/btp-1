# Angles

An angle is simply the amount of rotation between two rays that share a common endpoint. You can think of it like opening a pair of scissors or an animal opening its mouth wide. The more the turning, the larger the angle becomes. We need a way to measure exactly how much a ray has turned, without having to compare it to another angle.

## The Degree Unit

Mathematicians needed a standard way to assign precise measures to angles. They looked at a full circle and divided it into 360 equal parts. Each of these unit parts is called 1 degree, written as $1^{\circ}$. The measure of any angle is simply the number of $1^{\circ}$ unit parts it contains inside it. For example, an angle that contains 30 units of $1^{\circ}$ has a measure of $30^{\circ}$.

Because a full turn contains 360 degrees, the measure of a full turn is $360^{\circ}$. A straight angle is exactly half of a full turn. Since a full turn is $360^{\circ}$, a half turn is $180^{\circ}$. A right angle is exactly half of a straight angle. Since a straight angle measures $180^{\circ}$, a right angle measures $90^{\circ}$.

## Types of Angles

Angles can be grouped into different types based on their degree measures. An acute angle is an angle that is smaller than a right angle, which means its measure is less than $90^{\circ}$ and greater than $0^{\circ}$. An obtuse angle is an angle that is larger than a right angle but smaller than a straight angle, meaning its measure is greater than $90^{\circ}$ and less than $180^{\circ}$.

There is also a type of angle that is larger than a straight angle. An angle that is greater than $180^{\circ}$ and less than $360^{\circ}$ is called a reflex angle.

## Drawing an Angle with a Protractor

To draw a specific angle, you can use a protractor. Imagine you want to draw a $30^{\circ}$ angle named $\angle TIN$. In this angle, the letter I is the vertex, and the lines IT and IN are the arms. You keep one arm, say IN, as the base. The other arm, IT, must take a turn of exactly $30^{\circ}$ away from the base.

First, you draw the base line $\overrightarrow{IN}$. Next, you place the centre point of the protractor exactly on the vertex I. You align the base line IN to the 0 line on the protractor. Now, you start from 0 and count your degrees up to 30 on the protractor. You mark a point T at the label 30°. Finally, you use a ruler to join the point I and T together. The angle $\angle TIN$ is now exactly $30^{\circ}$.

## Worked example

Let us apply what we know about angle measures to a new situation. Suppose you are looking at a clock. The minute hand is pointing straight up at 12, and the hour hand is pointing straight down at 6. These two hands form a straight line. This means they have taken a half turn. Since a half turn is $180^{\circ}$, the angle formed between the hands is $180^{\circ}$. Because the measure is exactly $180^{\circ}$, this is a straight angle.

## Key points

* An angle is the amount of rotation between two rays that share a common endpoint, called the vertex.
* A full turn is divided into 360 equal parts called degrees, written as $1^{\circ}$, and the measure of an angle is the number of those parts it contains.
* A full turn is $360^{\circ}$, a straight angle is half of that at $180^{\circ}$, and a right angle is half again at $90^{\circ}$.
* An angle is acute below $90^{\circ}$, obtuse between $90^{\circ}$ and $180^{\circ}$, and reflex between $180^{\circ}$ and $360^{\circ}$.
* To draw an angle with a protractor, put its centre on the vertex, align one arm with the 0 line, mark the required degree on the scale, and join the vertex to that mark.
