# Perimeter

Imagine you are walking around a park. The total distance you cover from the moment you start until you return to your starting point is exactly the perimeter of the park. In mathematics, the perimeter of a closed plane figure is the total distance covered along its boundary when you go around it once. For a polygon, which is a closed plane figure made up of straight line segments, the perimeter is simply the sum of the lengths of all its sides. Understanding the perimeter helps you measure the total length of the outer boundary of any flat shape.

## Perimeter of a Polygon

To find the perimeter of any polygon, you add up the lengths of every side. This works because the perimeter is defined as the total distance along the outer boundary of the shape. If a shape has five sides, you simply add the length of the first side to the second, then to the third, and so on, until you have included every single side. This basic rule applies to all polygons, whether they are regular shapes with equal sides or irregular shapes with varying lengths.

## Perimeter of a Rectangle

A rectangle has four sides. The opposite sides of a rectangle are always equal in length. This means the top side is the same length as the bottom side, and the left side is the same length as the right side. Because of this property, you can calculate the perimeter by adding the length and the breadth together, and then multiplying the total by two. The formula for this is $2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$.

Consider a rectangle where the length is 12 cm and the breadth is 8 cm. The perimeter is calculated as $2 \times (12 \text{ cm} + 8 \text{ cm})$. This simplifies to $2 \times 20 \text{ cm}$, which equals 40 cm.

## Perimeter of a Square

A square is a special type of polygon where all four sides are equal in length. Because every side is the same, finding the perimeter is very straightforward. You simply take the length of one side and multiply it by 4. This is because you are adding the same number four times.

Imagine you want to put coloured tape all around a square photo frame that has a side length of 1 meter. The length of the tape required is the perimeter of the square. You can calculate this by adding the lengths of all four sides: 1 m + 1 m + 1 m + 1 m, which equals 4 m. Alternatively, you can multiply the length of one side by 4, giving you $4 \times 1 \text{ m} = 4 \text{ m}$.

## Perimeter of a Triangle

A triangle is a polygon with exactly three sides. To find its perimeter, you add the lengths of these three sides together. This rule applies regardless of whether the triangle is equilateral, isosceles, or scalene.

For example, consider a triangle that has three sides measuring 4 cm, 5 cm, and 7 cm. The perimeter is the sum of these three sides. Adding them together, we get $4 \text{ cm} + 5 \text{ cm} + 7 \text{ cm}$, which equals 16 cm.

## Worked Example

Akshi wants to put lace all around a rectangular tablecloth. The tablecloth is 3 meters long and 2 meters wide. Since the lace goes all around the edge, the length of the lace required is exactly the perimeter of the tablecloth. Using the formula for the perimeter of a rectangle, we multiply two by the sum of the length and the breadth. This gives us $2 \times (3 \text{ m} + 2 \text{ m})$. First, we add the length and the breadth: $3 \text{ m} + 2 \text{ m} = 5 \text{ m}$. Then, we multiply this sum by two: $2 \times 5 \text{ m} = 10 \text{ m}$. Therefore, the length of the lace required is 10 meters.

## Key points

* The perimeter of a closed plane figure is the total distance covered along its boundary when you go around it once.
* For a polygon, the perimeter is the sum of the lengths of all its sides.
* The perimeter of a rectangle is $2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$ because opposite sides are equal.
* The perimeter of a square is $4 \times \text{length of a side}$ because all four sides are equal.
* The perimeter of a triangle is the sum of the lengths of its three sides.
