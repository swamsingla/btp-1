# Basic Geometric Constructions

Geometric constructions are a way to draw precise shapes using a ruler and a compass. Instead of guessing where a line should go, you use the compass to mark exact distances and the ruler to measure and draw straight lines. This makes your drawings accurate and reliable. You can use these tools to create circles, squares, and rectangles, which are the building blocks of many larger designs.

## The Compass and the Circle

A compass has two arms. One arm ends in a sharp point, and the other holds a pencil. You open the compass to set the distance between the point and the pencil. This distance is the radius of the circle you will draw. To draw a circle, you fix the point on a centre, which is a specific point on your paper, and move the pencil around while keeping the point fixed. 

Imagine you mark a point $P$ on your paper. If you open your compass to $4$ cm and keep the point on $P$, the pencil will draw a perfect curve. Every point on this curve is exactly $4$ cm away from $P$. The point $P$ is the centre of the circle, and the distance from $P$ to any point on the curve is the radius. You can use this method to draw the wavy curves in a design by placing the compass at different spots and drawing arcs.

## Squares and Rectangles

A square and a rectangle are shapes with straight lines and four corners. A rectangle has opposite sides that are equal in length, and all of its angles are $90^{\circ}$. A square is a special rectangle where all sides are equal in length, and all angles are $90^{\circ}$. 

You can name a rectangle by its corners. For example, if the corners are $A, B, C, D$, you can name it $ABCD$ or $BCDA$. The names must follow the order of travel around the shape. You cannot name it $ABDC$ because that skips a corner. 

Rotating a square or a rectangle does not change its properties. If you turn a square, the sides are still equal, and the angles are still $90^{\circ}$. Therefore, a rotated square is still a square, and a rotated rectangle is still a rectangle.

## Constructing a Square

You can construct a square using a ruler and a compass. Let us say you want to draw a square with a side length of $6$ cm. First, draw a straight line segment $PQ$ that is $6$ cm long. 

Next, you need to draw a perpendicular line to $PQ$ through the point $P$. A perpendicular line meets another line at a $90^{\circ}$ angle. A protractor shows you where the $90^{\circ}$ mark is, and the perpendicular passes through $P$ and that mark. 

On this new perpendicular line, mark a point $S$ such that the distance $PS$ is $6$ cm. Now you have two sides of your square. Finally, draw a perpendicular to $PQ$ through the point $Q$. 

The last corner, $R$, lies on this second perpendicular, $6$ cm from $Q$. A compass marks it exactly: set it to $6$ cm, place the point on $Q$ and draw an arc that cuts the perpendicular. That crossing is $R$. Connect $R$ to $S$ to finish the square. 

## Finding a Point Equidistant from Two Points

Sometimes you need to find a point that is a specific distance from two different points. For example, imagine you want to draw a house. You have two points, $B$ and $C$, which form the base of the house. You need to find a point $A$ that is $5$ cm away from both $B$ and $C$. 

You can do this by using your compass. First, open your compass to a radius of $5$ cm. Place the point on $B$ and draw a full circle, or just a part of it called an arc. Next, without changing the compass width, place the point on $C$ and draw another arc. 

The two arcs will cross at a point. This crossing point is exactly $5$ cm away from $B$ and $5$ cm away from $C$. This is your point $A$. You can now connect $A$ to $B$ and $A$ to $C$ to form two sides of the house. 

## Worked example

Let us use the method for finding an equidistant point to draw a rectangle. Imagine you want to draw a rectangle with sides of $4$ cm and $6$ cm. First, draw a straight line segment $AB$ that is $6$ cm long. Next, draw a perpendicular line to $AB$ through point $A$. On this new line, mark a point $D$ such that the distance $AD$ is $4$ cm. 

Now you need to find the final corner, $C$. Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal, so $C$ must be $4$ cm away from $B$ and $6$ cm away from $D$. Open your compass to $4$ cm. Place the point on $B$ and draw an arc. Then open your compass to $6$ cm. Place the point on $D$ and draw another arc. The arcs cross in two places, and $C$ is the one on the same side of $AB$ as $D$. Connect $C$ to $B$ and $C$ to $D$ to complete your rectangle. Check that the opposite sides are equal and the angles are $90^{\circ}$.

## Key points

*   A compass is used to draw circles by keeping the point fixed and moving the pencil around at a set radius.
*   A square has all sides equal and all angles equal to $90^{\circ}$.
*   A rectangle has opposite sides equal and all angles equal to $90^{\circ}$.
*   You can find a point that is a specific distance from two other points by drawing intersecting arcs.
*   A square or rectangle remains the same shape even after it is rotated.
