# Operations on Integers

This page is about adding and subtracting integers, the positive and negative whole numbers together with zero. You may already know how to add and subtract positive numbers, but integers have special rules for their signs. Understanding these rules helps you calculate things like temperature changes, bank balances, or the movement of a lift in a building.

## Adding Integers

When you add integers, you are combining movements. A positive integer means moving forward, and a negative integer means moving backward. We can use a box of tokens to understand this. Imagine a box containing red positive tokens and green negative tokens. Each time you press a plus button, you take a positive token. Each time you press a minus button, you take a negative token.

A positive token and a negative token cancel each other out. This pair of tokens together has a value of zero. We call this a zero pair. To add integers, you can remove all the zero pairs from your collection.

For example, if you have 5 positive tokens and 3 negative tokens, you can remove 3 zero pairs. You are left with 2 positive tokens. Therefore, $(+5) + (-3) = +2$.

What if you have fewer positive tokens than negative ones? If you have 5 positive tokens and 8 negative tokens, you remove 5 zero pairs. You are left with 3 negative tokens. Therefore, $(+5) + (-8) = -3$.

## Subtracting Integers

Subtracting an integer is the same as adding its additive inverse. The additive inverse of a number is the number that gives zero when added to it. For example, the additive inverse of $+5$ is $-5$, because $5 + (-5) = 0$.

To subtract an integer, you change the subtraction sign to an addition sign and change the number to its opposite. For example, to calculate $7 - 4$, you can think of it as $7 + (-4)$. The result is $+3$.

## Rules for Signs

There are specific rules for adding and subtracting integers. When you add two positive numbers, the result is positive. When you add two negative numbers, the result is also negative. To add two negatives, you add the numbers without the signs and then place a minus sign in front of the result.

When you add a positive and a negative number, you subtract the smaller number from the greater number. Then, you place the sign of the greater number in front of the result. For example, $-5 + 3 = -2$.

Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the corresponding positive number. For example, $2 - (-3) = 2 + 3$.

## Worked example

Let's apply these rules to a real-world scenario. Imagine you are in a building and you start on the ground floor. You press the plus button 4 times, which means you go up 4 floors. Then, you press the minus button 7 times, which means you go down 7 floors.

To find your final floor, you can write this as $(+4) + (-7)$. Since 7 is greater than 4, you subtract 4 from 7, which gives 3. The sign of the greater number is negative, so the result is $-3$. You are now on the 3rd floor below the ground floor.

## Key points

- Adding a positive and a negative integer can be done by removing zero pairs to find the result.
- Subtracting an integer is the same as adding its additive inverse.
- The sum of two positives is positive, and the sum of two negatives is negative.
- When adding a positive and a negative, ignore the signs, subtract the smaller number from the greater one, and give the result the sign of the greater one.
- Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding a positive number.
