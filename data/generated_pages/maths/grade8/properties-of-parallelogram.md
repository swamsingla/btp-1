# Properties of Parallelogram

You may already know what a rectangle is, but have you ever tried drawing a four-sided shape where opposite sides are parallel, but the corners are not right angles? This shape is called a parallelogram. It is a very important type of quadrilateral because it includes rectangles as a special case. Understanding its properties helps you find unknown angles and sides in geometry problems.

## What is a Parallelogram?

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. This means if you draw a shape with two sets of parallel lines that do not meet at right angles, you have created a parallelogram. A rectangle also has parallel opposite sides, so it satisfies this definition and is a special kind of parallelogram where all angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$.

## Properties of the Angles

When you look at a parallelogram, the angles have specific relationships. If you take any two adjacent angles, their sum will always be $180^{\circ}$. This happens because adjacent angles act as interior angles on the same side of a transversal line crossing two parallel lines. For example, if one angle is $30^{\circ}$, the adjacent angle must be $150^{\circ}$ because $30^{\circ} + 150^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}$.

Additionally, the opposite angles of a parallelogram are always equal to each other. If you have a parallelogram with four angles labeled $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$, then angle $A$ is equal to angle $C$, and angle $B$ is equal to angle $D$.

## Properties of the Sides

The sides of a parallelogram also follow a strict rule. The opposite sides are equal in length. This can be proven by looking at the two triangles formed by drawing a diagonal line from one corner to the opposite corner. These two triangles are congruent (by the AAS condition), which means their corresponding sides are equal in length. Therefore, if one side is 5 cm long, the opposite side is also 5 cm long.

## Properties of the Diagonals

The diagonals of a parallelogram are the lines that connect opposite corners. A key property of these diagonals is that they bisect each other. This means the point where the two diagonals cross is exactly in the middle of both diagonals. One diagonal is split into two equal parts, and the other diagonal is also split into two equal parts at that exact same intersection point. The diagonals of a parallelogram need not be equal in length.

## Worked Example

Imagine a parallelogram where the length of the bottom side is 6 cm, the length of the left side is 4 cm, and the bottom-left angle is $70^{\circ}$. Using the properties of a parallelogram, we can find the other angles and sides.

The top side must be equal to the bottom side, so the top side is also 6 cm. The right side must be equal to the left side, so the right side is also 4 cm.

To find the other angles, we use the rule that adjacent angles add up to $180^{\circ}$. The bottom-right angle is adjacent to the $70^{\circ}$ angle, so it must be $180^{\circ} - 70^{\circ} = 110^{\circ}$. The top-left angle is also $110^{\circ}$.

Finally, the top-right angle is opposite to the bottom-left angle. Since opposite angles are equal, the top-right angle is $70^{\circ}$.

## Key points

*   A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
*   Adjacent angles in a parallelogram always add up to $180^{\circ}$.
*   Opposite angles in a parallelogram are always equal.
*   Opposite sides in a parallelogram are always equal in length.
*   The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
