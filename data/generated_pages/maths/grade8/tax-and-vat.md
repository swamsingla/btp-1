# Tax, VAT and GST

Taxes are an essential part of the economy. When we buy goods or services, we often pay extra money called tax. This extra amount goes to the government. In India, the most common tax added to our bills is the Goods and Services Tax (GST). Understanding how to calculate tax is a practical skill that uses the percentages you already know from mathematics.

## Understanding GST
GST is a tax that is applied to most goods and services sold for domestic consumption. It is an indirect tax, which means it is collected by the seller from the buyer and then paid to the government. On a bill, the GST is usually listed as a percentage of the base price of the item. The total amount you pay is the base price plus the GST. Tax rates, such as the GST rate or income tax rates, are specified as percentages. Before GST began in 2017, many states charged a similar sales tax called VAT (Value Added Tax); the percentage calculations are the same.

To calculate the GST amount on an item, you use the standard percentage formula. You take the base price of the item, multiply it by the GST percentage, and then divide the result by 100. 

For example, suppose you are buying an item that costs ₹1,200 before tax. If the GST rate on this item is 18%, you can calculate the tax amount by multiplying ₹1,200 by 18 and then dividing by 100. This calculation gives you ₹216. To find the final amount you must pay, you add the tax to the base price: ₹1,200 plus ₹216 equals ₹1,416.

## Calculating Total Bill Amounts
When you go shopping, the final amount on your bill is not just the price of the items. It is the base price plus the tax. To find the total bill amount, you can use a simple formula based on the percentage increase. You take the base price, multiply it by the GST percentage, divide by 100, and then add that result to the original base price. 

Alternatively, you can calculate the total amount directly by multiplying the base price by $1 + \frac{\text{GST rate}}{100}$. For instance, if an item costs ₹850 and the GST rate is 5%, the total amount is found by multiplying ₹850 by 1.05. This calculation results in ₹892.50. This method is very useful because it allows you to quickly determine the final price without calculating the tax amount separately.

## Profit, Loss, and Taxes
Tax is added on top of the price a shopkeeper sets, so it helps to recall profit and loss. When a shopkeeper sells an item, they want to make a profit. The profit is the difference between the selling price and the cost price. The cost price is the amount the shopkeeper originally paid to buy the item. The selling price is the price at which the shopkeeper sells the item, after any discounts and before tax.

Sometimes, taxes like GST are added to the selling price. This increases the final amount the customer pays. If a shopkeeper buys an item for ₹500 and wants to make a 20% profit, they will set the selling price at ₹600. If a GST of 18% is then added to this selling price, the final amount the customer pays will be ₹708. Understanding how these percentages interact helps you understand why prices change and how businesses operate.

## Worked example
Let us apply these rules to a practical scenario. Imagine you are buying a new mobile phone. The base price of the phone is ₹8,000. The shopkeeper offers a discount of 10% on the base price, and then a GST of 18% is applied to the discounted price. 

First, we calculate the discount amount. We take 10% of ₹8,000, which is ₹800. We subtract this from the base price to find the discounted price: ₹8,000 minus ₹800 equals ₹7,200. 

Next, we calculate the GST on the discounted price. We take 18% of ₹7,200, which equals ₹1,296. 

Finally, we add the GST to the discounted price to find the total bill amount. ₹7,200 plus ₹1,296 equals ₹8,496. This is the final amount you will pay for the mobile phone.

## Key points
- Tax, such as GST, is a percentage added to the base price of goods and services and paid to the government.
- To calculate the tax amount, multiply the base price by the tax percentage and divide by 100.
- The total bill amount is the base price plus the tax amount, which can be calculated by multiplying the base price by $1 + \frac{\text{tax rate}}{100}$.
- Profit is the amount a shopkeeper earns when the selling price is greater than the cost price.
- Loss occurs when the selling price is less than the cost price.
