# Percentage Increase and Decrease

Percentages are a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. The word per cent comes from the Latin phrase per centum, which means by the hundred or out of hundred. When you see a value like $25\%$, it means 25 out of every 100. This concept is extremely useful in everyday life, from calculating the discount on a new gadget to figuring out if a shopkeeper made a profit on an item. Understanding how to calculate and interpret percentage increase and decrease allows you to make sense of price changes, population growth, and even your test scores.

## Calculating Percentage Change

When a quantity changes, we can measure that change as a percentage. To do this, we compare the amount of change to the original amount, which we call the base. If the quantity goes up, we calculate the percentage increase. If the quantity goes down, we calculate the percentage decrease.

The formula for percentage increase is the amount of increase divided by the original amount, multiplied by 100. For example, suppose the price of 1 kg of tomatoes 3 years ago was ₹30, and the price now is ₹42. The increase in the price is ₹12. The percentage increase is $\frac{12}{30} \times 100 = 40\%$. We say the price of tomatoes increased by 40% over the last 3 years.

Similarly, the formula for percentage decrease is the amount of decrease divided by the original amount, multiplied by 100. For example, the average footfall in a theater before COVID was 160. Now it is just 100. The decrease in the footfall is 60. The percentage decrease is $\frac{60}{160} \times 100 = 37.5\%$. We say the footfall in this theater post-COVID has decreased by 37.5%.

## Profit and Loss

Percentages are very commonly used to describe profit and loss in business. The price a shopkeeper pays to purchase an item is called the cost price. The price the shopkeeper quotes to customers is called the marked price. The price the customer actually pays after bargaining is called the selling price.

If the selling price is greater than the cost price, the shopkeeper makes a profit. If the selling price is less than the cost price, the shopkeeper makes a loss. To find the percentage profit or loss, we use the cost price as our base, which we consider to be 100%.

For example, Kishanlal buys sweaters from a wholesaler at a cost price of ₹300 per sweater. He marks the price at ₹480. After bargaining, he sells the sweater at ₹430. The profit amount is ₹430 - ₹300 = ₹130. The percentage profit is $\frac{130}{300} \times 100 = 43.3\%$.

## Discounts

You have probably seen percentages mentioned when shops offer discounts. A discount means the shop is willing to reduce the price of an item. For example, a 30% off sale means the price is reduced by 30%.

Let us look at a practical example. A utensil store is offering a 35% discount on a cooker with an MRP of ₹1800. To find the selling price, we calculate the discount amount first. The discount is 35% of ₹1800, which is $0.35 \times 1800 = ₹630$. The selling price is the MRP minus the discount, which is $1800 - 630 = ₹1170$.

## Taxes

Tax rates, like the GST (Goods and Services Tax) rate, are also specified as percentages. You may have noticed GST mentioned on bills. This means that the tax is part of the amount we pay, and this amount goes to the government.

## Worked example

Let us apply the rule of percentage decrease to a new situation. Imagine a local bookstore that used to sell 200 storybooks per week. Due to the rise of e-readers, sales dropped, and now they only sell 150 storybooks per week. 

First, we find the amount of decrease in sales. The decrease is $200 - 150 = 50$ storybooks. 

Next, we use the original amount as our base. The original amount is 200 storybooks. 

Using the formula for percentage decrease, we divide the amount of decrease by the original amount and multiply by 100. The calculation is $\frac{50}{200} \times 100 = 25\%$. 

This means the bookstore's sales of storybooks have decreased by 25%.

## Key points
- A percentage is a fraction with a denominator of 100. The word per cent means by the hundred.
- To calculate percentage increase, divide the amount of increase by the original amount and multiply by 100.
- To calculate percentage decrease, divide the amount of decrease by the original amount and multiply by 100.
- There is a profit when the selling price is greater than the cost price, and a loss when it is less. Percentage profit or loss is found with the cost price as the base.
- A discount is a reduction in the marked price of an item, calculated as a percentage of the MRP.
- Tax rates like GST are specified as percentages, and the tax is part of the amount we pay.
