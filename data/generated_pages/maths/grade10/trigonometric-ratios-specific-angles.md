# Trigonometric Ratios of Standard Angles

You are already familiar with the trigonometric ratios of an acute angle in a right-angled triangle. In this section, we will find the exact values of these ratios for specific angles, namely $0^{\circ}$, $30^{\circ}$, $45^{\circ}$, $60^{\circ}$, and $90^{\circ}$. Knowing these exact values is essential because they form the foundation for solving more complex geometry problems and proving trigonometric identities.

## The 45° Ratios

To find the ratios for $45^{\circ}$, consider a right-angled triangle where one angle is $45^{\circ}$, which means the other angle is also $45^{\circ}$. In such a triangle, the two legs are equal in length, so we can suppose that the base and the perpendicular are both equal to $a$. By applying the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse is calculated as $\sqrt{a^2 + a^2}$, which simplifies to $a\sqrt{2}$.

Using these side lengths, the sine of $45^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse, which gives $\frac{a}{a\sqrt{2}}$, or $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. The cosine of $45^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, which is also $\frac{a}{a\sqrt{2}}$, or $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. The tangent of $45^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side, which simplifies to $\frac{a}{a}$, or $1$. The remaining ratios are the reciprocals of these values: the cosecant is $\sqrt{2}$, the secant is $\sqrt{2}$, and the cotangent is $1$.

## The 30° and 60° Ratios

To derive the ratios for $30^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$, we start with an equilateral triangle, where each angle is $60^{\circ}$. By drawing a perpendicular from one vertex to the opposite side, we create two identical right-angled triangles. In these new triangles, one angle is $30^{\circ}$ (at the vertex we drew from, since the perpendicular splits its $60^{\circ}$ angle in half) and the other is $60^{\circ}$ (at the base).

To find the side lengths, we suppose each side of the equilateral triangle, and so the hypotenuse of the new right-angled triangle, is $2a$. The perpendicular divides the base into two equal parts, making the base of the right-angled triangle equal to $a$. This base is the side opposite the $30^{\circ}$ angle and adjacent to the $60^{\circ}$ angle. Using the Pythagorean theorem, the perpendicular side is calculated as $\sqrt{(2a)^2 - a^2}$, which simplifies to $a\sqrt{3}$. This side is adjacent to the $30^{\circ}$ angle and opposite the $60^{\circ}$ angle.

For the $30^{\circ}$ angle, the sine is the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse, giving $\frac{a}{2a}$, or $\frac{1}{2}$. The cosine of $30^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, which is $\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2a}$, or $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The tangent of $30^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side, which is $\frac{a}{a\sqrt{3}}$, or $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

For the $60^{\circ}$ angle, the sine is the ratio of the perpendicular side to the hypotenuse, giving $\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2a}$, or $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The cosine of $60^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, which is $\frac{a}{2a}$, or $\frac{1}{2}$. The tangent of $60^{\circ}$ is the ratio of the perpendicular side to the adjacent side, which is $\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{a}$, or $\sqrt{3}$. The remaining ratios are the reciprocals of these values.

## The 0° and 90° Ratios

The values for $0^{\circ}$ and $90^{\circ}$ can be understood by imagining a right-angled triangle where one angle is made smaller and smaller until it becomes $0^{\circ}$. As this angle approaches $0^{\circ}$, the side opposite to it becomes very close to $0$, and the hypotenuse becomes almost the same as the adjacent side. Therefore, the sine of $0^{\circ}$ is defined as $0$ and the cosine of $0^{\circ}$ is defined as $1$.

Similarly, if we make the angle larger and larger until it becomes $90^{\circ}$, the adjacent side becomes very close to $0$, and the hypotenuse becomes almost the same as the opposite side. Therefore, the sine of $90^{\circ}$ is defined as $1$ and the cosine of $90^{\circ}$ is defined as $0$. Since $\tan A = \frac{\sin A}{\cos A}$, this gives $\tan 0^{\circ} = 0$, while $\tan 90^{\circ}$ is not defined, because it would need division by $\cos 90^{\circ} = 0$.

The values found above can be collected in one table:

| $A$ | $0^{\circ}$ | $30^{\circ}$ | $45^{\circ}$ | $60^{\circ}$ | $90^{\circ}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin A$ | $0$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $\cos A$ | $1$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $0$ |
| $\tan A$ | $0$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt{3}$ | not defined |

## Worked example

Consider a right-angled triangle where the hypotenuse is $10$ cm and one of the acute angles is $30^{\circ}$. To find the length of the side opposite to the $30^{\circ}$ angle, we use the sine ratio. The sine of $30^{\circ}$ is defined as the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse.

We know that the sine of $30^{\circ}$ is $\frac{1}{2}$. Setting up the equation, we have $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{10}$. Solving for the opposite side, we multiply both sides by $10$ to get the length of the opposite side as $5$ cm.

## Key points
*   The ratios for $30^{\circ}$, $45^{\circ}$, and $60^{\circ}$ can be derived using geometric constructions like equilateral and isosceles right-angled triangles.
*   As the angle increases from $0^{\circ}$ to $90^{\circ}$, the sine ratio increases from $0$ to $1$, while the cosine ratio decreases from $1$ to $0$.
*   The tangent of $45^{\circ}$ is $1$, and the tangent of $60^{\circ}$ is $\sqrt{3}$, while the tangent of $30^{\circ}$ is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.
*   The trigonometric ratios for $0^{\circ}$ and $90^{\circ}$ are defined based on the limiting behavior of the sides of a right-angled triangle.
*   The reciprocal ratios, such as cosecant, secant, and cotangent, are the reciprocals of the sine, cosine, and tangent, respectively.
