# Physical Quantities

Have you ever wondered how we know exactly how tall a tree is, how much water is in your bottle, or how long a race will take? We live in a world full of objects, but to truly understand them, we need to measure them. A physical quantity is simply any measurable attribute of the physical world. To describe the physical world around us, we use four basic physical quantities: length, mass, time, and temperature. These measurements allow us to compare objects, predict how they will behave, and communicate our findings with others.

## Length, Mass and Volume

Length is the physical quantity that tells us how long or wide an object is from one end to the other. Its unit in the International System of Units is the metre, abbreviated as m, and for shorter lengths we use the centimetre (cm) and the millimetre (mm). Mass is the physical quantity that tells us the quantity of matter in an object, and the space that matter occupies is its volume. Anything that occupies space and has mass is called matter. The units used to measure volume are the litre (L) and the millilitre (mL), and the units used to measure mass are the gram (g) and the kilogram (kg).

Kilogram is the unit of mass in the International System of Units (SI). Kilogram is abbreviated in lower case as kg. There is no space between ‘k’ and ‘g’ in kg, and no full stop after the symbol, except at the end of a sentence. While writing the mass, always leave a space between the number (numerical value) and the unit. For example, if we have mass of 7 kilograms, it would be written as $7 \text{ kg}$ and not as $7 \text{ kgs}$.

Similarly, litre is abbreviated as capital L and millilitre as mL. There is no space between ‘m’ and ‘L’ in mL. For example, if you have $500$ millilitres of water, it would be written as $500 \text{ mL}$, m will be in lower case and L will be in upper case. The SI unit for volume is cubic metre, abbreviated as $m^{3}$. The abbreviation is written with a superscript 3 to denote cubic metre. For example, if you have volume of $2$ cubic metres, it would be written as $2\ m^{3}$. Always leave a space between number (numerical value) and the unit. $1\ m^{3}=1000\ L$.

## Time and Temperature

Time is the physical quantity that allows us to measure the duration of events. We use seconds, minutes, and hours to measure time. Temperature is the physical quantity that tells us how hot or cold an object is. We use degrees Celsius to measure temperature in our everyday lives. 

## Worked Example

Let us look at how to correctly write a measurement. Imagine you have a bag of rice that weighs 3 kilograms. According to the rules, you must always leave a space between the number and the unit. Therefore, you would write the mass as $3 \text{ kg}$, not $3kg$ or $3 kgs$. Similarly, if you have a bottle of juice containing 1 litre, you would write it as $1 \text{ L}$, using a capital L. If the juice is actually $250$ millilitres, you would write it as $250 \text{ mL}$. Notice that there is no space between the m and the L in the abbreviation mL.

## Key points
* A physical quantity is a measurable attribute of the physical world, such as length, mass, time, or temperature.
* Length is measured in metres (m), with the centimetre (cm) and millimetre (mm) used for shorter lengths.
* Mass is the quantity of matter, and its units are gram (g) and kilogram (kg).
* Volume is the space occupied by matter, and its units are litre (L) and millilitre (mL).
* Always leave a space between the number and the unit, such as writing $7 \text{ kg}$ instead of $7kg$.
* The SI unit for volume is cubic metre, abbreviated as $m^{3}$, where $1\ m^{3}=1000\ L$.
