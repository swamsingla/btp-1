# Rest and Motion

Have you ever sat in a moving bus and seen trees outside the window? To you, the trees seem to be moving. But to a person standing on the ground, the bus is moving and the trees are still. This happens because rest and motion are relative. An object is at rest if it does not change its position with respect to its surroundings. An object is in motion if it changes its position with respect to its surroundings.

## Rectilinear Motion

When an object moves along a straight line, it is called rectilinear motion. Imagine a train travelling on a straight railway track between two stations. The train moves along a straight path. This is a perfect example of rectilinear motion. 

There are two types of rectilinear motion. The first is uniform motion. In uniform motion, an object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time. The second type is non-uniform motion. In non-uniform motion, an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time.

## Circular Motion

Circular motion happens when an object moves along a circular path. Think about a ceiling fan spinning in your room. The blades of the fan move in a circle. Another example is a merry-go-round in a playground. The horses on the merry-go-round move in a circular path around a fixed center point. The direction of the object keeps changing as it goes around the circle.

## Oscillatory Motion

Oscillatory motion is a to-and-fro motion. It happens around a fixed central point or mean position. A simple pendulum is a good example. It consists of a small metallic ball called a bob, hanging from a rigid support by a long thread. 

When the pendulum is at rest, it is in its mean position. If you move the bob slightly to one side and release it, it starts to swing. It moves to one extreme position, then swings back to the mean position, and then to the other extreme position. After that, it comes back to the mean position again. This complete to-and-fro movement is called one oscillation.

## Worked example

Let us look at an example of uniform and non-uniform motion. Imagine two trains, Train X and Train Y. We watch each of them for twenty minutes, and note how far it travels in each ten-minute interval. Train X covers 20 kilometres in the first ten minutes and another 20 kilometres in the next ten. Since it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, Train X is in uniform motion. Train Y covers 15 kilometres in the first ten minutes and then 25 kilometres in the next ten. Because it covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time, Train Y is in non-uniform motion.

## Key points

*   Rest and motion are relative. They depend on the observer's point of view.
*   Rectilinear motion is movement along a straight line.
*   Uniform motion covers equal distances in equal times.
*   Non-uniform motion covers unequal distances in equal times.
*   Circular motion is movement along a circular path.
*   Oscillatory motion is a to-and-fro movement around a mean position.
*   One oscillation is completed when an object moves from one extreme position to the other and back.
