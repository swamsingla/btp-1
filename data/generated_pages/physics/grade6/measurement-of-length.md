# Measurement of Length

Measuring length means finding out how long an object is from one end to the other. It helps us compare sizes accurately. If you want to know how long your pencil is, you need a tool to measure it. You cannot just guess, because different people guess different amounts. We need a standard way to measure so that everyone gets the same result.

## Choosing the Right Tool

You must choose the right tool for the job. If you want to measure the length of your pencil, you may use a 15-cm scale. Similarly, if the height of a room is to be measured, you may need a metre scale or a measuring tape. You cannot directly measure the girth of a tree or the size of your chest using a metre scale. For such measurements, flexible measuring tape, such as a tailor's tape is more suitable.

## Placing the Scale Correctly

While measuring lengths, you need to take care of a few points. First, place the scale in contact with the object along its length, so that the edge of the scale touches the object all the way along. If you hold the scale above the object, or at an angle to it, the reading you take will be longer than the object really is.

## Positioning Your Eye

Second, you must place your eye in the correct position. If you are measuring the length of a pencil by aligning it with a scale, your eye should be directly above the tip of the pencil. Looking from one side makes the tip appear to line up with a different mark, so you read a length that is too long or too short. This is why the position of the eye matters as much as the position of the scale.

## Measuring with a Broken Scale

Sometimes the ends of the scale are broken or the zero marking is not clear. In that case, you can still use the scale. Use any other full mark of the scale, say, 1.0 cm. Then you must subtract the reading of this mark from the reading at the other end. For example, if the reading at one end is 1.0 cm and at the other end, it is 10.4 cm, the length of the object is $10.4\ \text{cm} - 1.0\ \text{cm} = 9.4\ \text{cm}$.

## Measuring Curved Lines

What if you need to measure a curved line? Imagine you are fixing electric string lights on the arches of a verandah. You would need to measure the required length of the string lights. In the case of a curved line, measurements can be made with the help of a flexible measuring tape or by using a thread. The thread can then be straightened and its length can be measured using a metre scale.

## Using Body Parts

Long ago, people did not have scales. They used their own body parts to measure things. Cloth was often measured by the length of the arm, and a farmer dividing a field into beds would walk across it and count the number of his strides. The length of a foot, a fist or a finger was used in the same way.

You can try this yourself. If a whole class measures the same classroom table using their handspan, each of them gets a different number, because their handspans are of different sizes. This shows why we need standard units. The length of the table is expressed in two parts: a number and a unit. For example, if the length of the table is found to be 13 handspans, then 13 is the number and ‘handspan’ is the unit selected for the measurement. However, handspans and other similar units, such as length of hand, foot, fist or fingers, differ from person to person.

## Standard Units

Because body parts differ from person to person, we need a standard unit. India has a rich history of measurement systems dating back to ancient times. Angula (finger width), multiples of angula, dhanusa, and yojana are some of the units mentioned in ancient Indian literature, and used in measuring artefacts, architecture, and town planning. The angula is still used by traditional craftspeople like carpenters and tailors.

## Worked example

Let us say you are measuring a book with a scale. You place the scale in contact with the book. You look directly above the edge of the book to read the scale. The reading at one end is 2.0 cm and at the other end, it is 21.5 cm. To find the length of the book, you subtract the first reading from the second reading. The length of the book is $21.5\ \text{cm} - 2.0\ \text{cm} = 19.5\ \text{cm}$.

## Key points

- A scale must be placed in contact with the object along its length.
- The position of your eye should be directly above the tip of the object.
- If the zero marking is broken, use any other full mark and subtract the readings.
- Flexible measuring tape or a thread is used to measure curved lines.
- Body parts like handspans are not standard units because they differ from person to person.
