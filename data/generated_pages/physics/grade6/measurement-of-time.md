# Measurement of Time

Humans have always needed to measure time. Long ago, people noticed that nature repeats itself. The Sun rises and sets, the Moon changes shape, and the seasons change. These repeating events helped people make the first calendars. A day is one full cycle of the Sun rising and setting. Later, people wanted to know the time of day. They made special devices to measure smaller parts of a day.

## Early Methods of Measuring Time

People invented many tools to measure time within a single day. Sundials, water clocks, hourglasses, and candle clocks were some of these early devices. A sundial tells time by using the shadow of an object. The shadow moves as the Sun changes its position in the sky. 

A water clock uses the flow of water. In one type, water slowly flows out of a marked vessel. In another type, a floating bowl with a small hole at the bottom is placed on water. The bowl fills up with water and sinks after a fixed time. Then, a person lifts it and floats it again. An hourglass measures time using sand. Sand flows from one glass bulb to another. A candle clock has markings on a candle. As the candle burns, these markings show how much time has passed.

## Units of Time

We use specific units to measure time. The standard unit of time in science is the second. Its symbol is s. The larger units are the minute and the hour. We write their symbols as min and h. 

There are exactly 60 seconds in one minute. 

There are exactly 60 minutes in one hour.

When we write these units, we must follow some simple rules. We always start the unit name with a lowercase letter. We also write the symbol in lowercase letters. The symbols are always written in the singular form. For example, we write 5 s, not 5 secs. We never put a full stop after a unit symbol, unless it is at the very end of a sentence. When we write a number and a unit together, we always leave a space between them. For example, we write 15 min, not 15min.

## Measuring Short Intervals with a Stopwatch

Sometimes, we need to measure very short time intervals. A clock is good for telling the time of day. However, a stopwatch is better for measuring short durations. A stopwatch is a small device we use to measure exactly how much time passes between two events. 

Imagine you are timing a race at school. You can start the stopwatch the exact moment the runner starts. You stop the watch the exact moment the runner crosses the finish line. The number on the screen tells you the exact time interval. This helps us know exactly how fast the runner is.

## Worked Example

Let us use a stopwatch to measure a short event. Imagine you want to know how long it takes for a ball to roll down a ramp. You place the ball at the top of the ramp. You press the start button on your stopwatch. The ball rolls down and hits the floor. You immediately press the stop button. The stopwatch shows a time interval of 3 s. This means it took 3 seconds for the ball to roll down. 

Now, suppose you want to measure how long it takes to run a short distance. You run 200 metres. You start the stopwatch when you begin running. You stop the watch when you reach the 200-metre mark. The stopwatch shows 30 s. This means it took you 30 seconds to run 200 metres. You can use this exact time to calculate your speed later.

## Key points

- Time is measured using events that repeat regularly, such as the rising and setting of the Sun.
- Sundials, water clocks, hourglasses and candle clocks were early devices for measuring time within a single day.
- The standard unit of time in science is the second, and its symbol is s.
- There are 60 seconds in one minute and 60 minutes in one hour.
- Unit names and symbols are written in lower case and in the singular, with a space between the number and the unit, as in 15 min.
- A stopwatch is used to measure a short interval of time between two events.
