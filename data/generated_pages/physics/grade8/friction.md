# Friction

Friction is a force that opposes motion, or the tendency of an object to move. It acts in the direction opposite to the way an object moves or is pushed. Friction arises between two surfaces in contact. Friction is everywhere because it happens whenever two surfaces touch each other. It is the reason we can walk on the ground, and it is why a bicycle slows down when we stop pedalling.

## Static and Sliding Friction

When you push a heavy box across the floor, you might notice that it takes a lot of effort to get it moving. This is because of static friction. Static friction is the force that keeps an object at rest when you try to push it. It acts in the opposite direction to your push, balancing your effort so the box does not move. If you push harder, the static friction matches your push. The box still stays at rest. However, if you push harder than the static friction can handle, the pushing force becomes bigger than the friction force, and the box finally moves.

Once the box starts moving, it becomes easier to keep pushing. The force that now opposes the sliding motion is called sliding friction. Sliding friction is generally weaker than static friction. This is why it is much harder to get a heavy object moving than it is to keep it moving. For example, if you push a box across a rough floor, the friction between the bottom of the box and the floor is the force that opposes your push.

## Rolling Friction

Rolling friction is the force that opposes the motion of a rolling object. It is much weaker than both static and sliding friction. Think about a bicycle. When you stop pedalling, the bike slows down because of friction. However, it takes a much longer time for a bicycle to stop than it would for a sliding box of the same weight. This is because the tyres of the bicycle roll rather than slide across the road. The friction between the tyres and the ground is rolling friction, and it is much smaller than sliding friction. This is why vehicles with wheels are so easy to move.

## Factors Affecting Friction

The amount of friction between two surfaces depends on a few key things. The first factor is the nature of the surfaces in contact. A rough surface creates more friction than a smooth surface. This is because the tiny bumps and grooves on a rough surface catch onto each other and resist motion. The second factor is how hard the two surfaces are pressed together. If you press two surfaces together with a greater force, the friction between them increases. The type of friction also matters when we compare them. For the same two objects, static friction is usually the strongest, followed by sliding friction, and then rolling friction.

## Advantages and Disadvantages

Friction is not always bad; it has many useful effects in our daily lives. A major advantage of friction is that it allows us to walk without slipping. The friction between our shoes and the ground pushes against our feet, allowing us to move forward. The brakes on a car or a bicycle use friction to stop the wheels from turning. Without friction, we would not be able to write on a blackboard with a piece of chalk.

However, friction also causes disadvantages. It creates heat when two surfaces rub together. This heat can cause parts of a machine to wear out over time. Because of this, machines often need oil or grease to reduce the friction between their moving parts. Another disadvantage is that friction wastes energy. When you push a heavy object, some of your energy is lost as heat due to friction. This means you have to use more energy to move things than you would in a world without friction.

## Worked Example

A student pushes a heavy crate that is already sliding across the floor with a force of $20 \, \mathrm{N}$. The floor is rough, so the sliding friction pushes back with a force of $15 \, \mathrm{N}$. We can find the net force acting on the crate. The pushing force is $20 \, \mathrm{N}$ to the right, and the sliding friction is $15 \, \mathrm{N}$ to the left. To find the net force, we subtract the smaller force from the larger force. The net force is $20 \, \mathrm{N} - 15 \, \mathrm{N} = 5 \, \mathrm{N}$, in the direction of the push. Because there is an unbalanced force of $5 \, \mathrm{N}$, the crate speeds up in the direction of the push.

Now suppose the student pushes with only $15 \, \mathrm{N}$ while the sliding friction is still $15 \, \mathrm{N}$. The two forces are equal and opposite, so the net force is $15 \, \mathrm{N} - 15 \, \mathrm{N} = 0$. The forces are balanced, so the crate keeps moving at the same speed and does not speed up.

## Key points
* Friction is a force that always opposes motion or the tendency of motion.
* Static friction keeps objects at rest, while sliding friction opposes objects that are already moving.
* Rolling friction is the weakest type of friction and acts on rolling objects like wheels and balls.
* Rougher surfaces and greater pressing force both increase the amount of friction between two objects.
* Friction has both advantages, such as allowing us to walk and stop vehicles, and disadvantages, such as causing wear and wasting energy.
