# Lenses

Imagine looking through a flat transparent glass window pane. All the objects look the same size and shape. Would they still look the same if the transparent material had a curved surface? A lens is a piece of transparent material, usually made of glass or plastic, which has curved surfaces. When you look through a lens, objects can look different in size. A magnifying glass, for example, helps us read small print by making the letters appear bigger.

## A Drop of Water as a Lens

You can see this for yourself with a drop of water. Put a very thin coating of oil or wax on a flat strip of glass or clear plastic, and place a small drop of water on the coated spot. The oil or wax helps the water form a nice round drop. The surface of the water drop is curved outwards. Now place a paper with printed text under the strip, so that some text is directly under the drop, and look down through the drop. The letters under the drop look different, and they may appear larger than the letters nearby. The curved surface of the water drop made the size of the text look different, so the drop of water is acting like a simple lens.

## Types of Lenses

Like mirrors, lenses can be convex or concave. A lens which is thicker at the middle as compared to the edges is called a convex lens. You can think of a lentil, which is also fatter in the middle than at its rim. A lens which is thicker at the edges as compared to the middle is called a concave lens. Unlike mirrors, lenses allow light to pass through them, and we see things through a lens rather than in a lens.

These shapes affect how we see the world. When you look through a convex lens from a small distance, the object appears erect and enlarged in size. As the distance between the object and the convex lens increases, the object appears inverted. It is initially enlarged in size and then diminishes in size. On the other hand, an object placed behind a concave lens and seen through the lens, always appears erect and diminished in size. Its size changes, as its distance from the lens increases.

## How Lenses Handle Light

Lenses do not just change how we see things; they also change the path of light. When parallel beams of light hit a thin transparent glass plate, the light passes through as it is. However, a convex lens and a concave lens behave differently. The convex lens converges the light falling on it while the concave lens diverges the light. Because of this, a convex lens is also called a converging lens while a concave lens is called a diverging lens.

## Worked example

Let us see how lenses change the image of an object. Imagine you have a small toy car. You place it behind a convex lens at a small distance. When you look through the lens, the car will appear erect and enlarged in size. Now, imagine you slowly move the toy car farther away from the lens. As the distance between the object and the convex lens increases, the image will first appear inverted and enlarged. If you continue moving the car even farther away, the image will start to diminish in size. This shows how the distance of the object from the convex lens affects how it looks. Now repeat this with a concave lens. The toy car always appears erect and diminished in size, although its size changes as its distance from the lens increases.

## Key points

- A lens is a piece of transparent material, usually made of glass or plastic, with curved surfaces; unlike a mirror, light passes through it and we see things through it.
- A convex lens is thicker at the middle than at the edges; a concave lens is thicker at the edges than at the middle.
- Seen through a convex lens from a small distance, an object appears erect and enlarged; as the distance increases it appears inverted, first enlarged and then diminished.
- Seen through a concave lens, an object always appears erect and diminished in size.
- A convex lens converges light and is also called a converging lens; a concave lens diverges light and is also called a diverging lens.
