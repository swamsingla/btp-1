# Electric Circuit

An electric circuit is a complete path that allows electric current to flow through various components. When this path is complete, the components in the circuit, such as a lamp, will function. If the path is broken, the current stops flowing and the components will not work. Understanding how to build and read these circuits is essential for using everyday electrical devices safely and effectively.

## Closed and Open Circuits

A circuit must be a closed loop to allow electricity to flow. When the path is complete, it is called a closed circuit, and current can travel through the wires and components. If the path is broken, it is called an open circuit, and the current stops. A simple example is a lamp connected to a cell by wires. The lamp glows only when the wires connect to both terminals of the cell, creating a complete path. 

If you disconnect one wire, the loop is broken. The current can no longer travel through the filament of the lamp, so the lamp stops glowing. This shows that the continuity of the wire path is the critical factor for the circuit to work.

## Circuit Diagrams and Symbols

Drawing a circuit with real wires and cells is difficult to read. To make circuits easier to draw and understand, we use symbols for each component. A representation of an electrical circuit using symbols is called a circuit diagram. 

In a circuit diagram, the electric cell is drawn as two parallel lines. The long line represents the positive terminal, while the short line represents the negative terminal. The lamp is drawn as a circle with a cross inside it. By using these symbols, we can quickly see how the components are connected without drawing the physical setup.

## Direction of Electric Current

In an electrical circuit, by convention, the direction of electric current is taken to be from the positive terminal to the negative terminal of the electric cell, through the wires and the lamp outside the cell. When the terminals of the lamp are connected to the cell by wires, the current passes through the filament. 

With an incandescent lamp, it does not matter which of its terminals connects to the positive or negative terminal of the cell. The lamp will glow as long as the circuit is complete and current flows through the filament. This means you can swap the wires at the lamp's base, and the lamp will still work, provided the overall loop remains closed.

## Worked Example

Imagine you have an electric cell and an incandescent lamp. You connect one wire from the positive terminal of the cell to one terminal of the lamp, and you connect the second wire from the other terminal of the lamp to the negative terminal of the cell. 

The circuit is now closed. The electric current will flow from the positive terminal of the cell, through the filament of the lamp, and back to the negative terminal of the cell. Because the current passes through the filament, the lamp will glow.

## Key points

*   An electric circuit requires a complete, unbroken path for current to flow.
*   A closed circuit allows current to flow, while an open circuit stops it.
*   Circuit diagrams use standardized symbols to represent components like cells and lamps.
*   The long line in a cell symbol represents the positive terminal, and the short line represents the negative.
*   By convention, the direction of electric current is taken to be from the positive terminal of the cell, through the circuit, to the negative terminal.
*   For an incandescent lamp, the current direction does not affect whether the lamp glows.
