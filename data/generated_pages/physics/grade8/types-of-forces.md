# Types of Forces

Forces are pushes or pulls that happen when two objects interact with each other. A force always requires at least two objects to be present. One object applies the push or pull on the other. Without this interaction between objects, a force cannot exist. The standard unit used to measure force is the newton, which is written with a small 'n' and has the symbol N.

## Contact Forces

Contact forces are forces that act only when objects are physically touching each other. You cannot apply a contact force to an object from a distance. The interaction must involve direct or indirect physical touch. A direct touch happens when you use your hands or body parts to push or pull. An indirect touch happens when you use a tool, like a stick or a rope, to move something.

### Muscular Force

Muscular force is a type of contact force caused by the action of muscles in the body. When you perform physical activities such as walking, running, lifting, pushing, jumping, or stretching, your muscles contract and elongate to create movement. This resulting force is known as muscular force. Animals, birds, fish, and insects also use muscular forces to move around and survive in their environments. Humans have historically used the muscular force of animals to help carry out many tasks for a long time.

## Non-contact Forces

Non-contact forces are forces whose effects can be experienced even when the objects are not in physical contact with each other. These forces can act over a distance, allowing one object to push or pull another object without touching it.

### Magnetic Force

Magnetic force is a type of non-contact force. A magnet can exert a push or pull on another magnet, or on a magnetic material, without being in contact with it. When two magnets are brought close to each other, like poles (North–North, South–South) repel each other. Conversely, unlike poles (North–South) attract each other. Electromagnets, which behave like magnets, also exhibit these same attractive and repulsive actions.

## Worked Example

Consider a scenario where you push a wooden table across the floor. Your hand is one object, and the table is the second object. The force you apply is a contact force because your hand is physically touching the table. This interaction demonstrates that a force results only when two objects interact in some way. Because the force comes from the action of your muscles, it is also a muscular force.

Now suppose you pull a bucket towards you using a rope. The rope touches the bucket, and your hand holds the rope, so the touch is indirect. This is still a contact force, because the force acts only through physical contact between the objects. Your muscles supply the force, so it is a muscular force again.

Finally, bring one magnet slowly towards another magnet on a table, keeping a small gap between them. If the facing poles are unlike (North and South), the second magnet is pulled towards the first. If the facing poles are like (North and North), it is pushed away. In both cases the magnets are not touching each other, so this is a non-contact force, namely magnetic force.

To decide which type of force you are dealing with, ask one question: do the two objects need to touch, directly or through a stick or rope, for the force to act? If yes, it is a contact force. If the force can act with a gap between the objects, it is a non-contact force.

## Key points
- A force is a push or pull on an object resulting from the object's interaction with another object.
- Contact forces act only when there is physical contact between objects.
- Muscular force is a contact force caused by the contraction and elongation of muscles.
- Non-contact forces act even when objects are not in physical contact.
- Magnetic force is a non-contact force that causes attraction and repulsion between magnets.
- The SI unit of force is the newton (N).
