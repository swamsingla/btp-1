# Circuit Diagrams and Symbols

When you look at the wiring inside a table lamp or a torch, you see a tangle of coloured wires, metal clips, and tiny bulbs. If you had to draw that exact mess on a piece of paper, it would look like a spiderweb and be very hard to read. To make things easier, scientists and engineers use a special language of pictures called symbols. A representation of an electrical circuit using symbols is called its circuit diagram. This allows anyone in the world to look at a drawing and instantly understand exactly how the electricity is supposed to flow.

## The Basic Symbols

Every electrical part has its own special symbol. For example, a simple cell, which is a source of electricity, is drawn as two parallel lines. The long line represents the positive terminal, while the short line represents the negative terminal. A bulb is drawn as a circle with a cross inside it. A wire is simply a straight line connecting two parts. These symbols act like letters in an alphabet. By learning just a few of them, you can read and write the instructions for any simple machine.

## The LED Symbol

Another important symbol is for an LED, which stands for Light Emitting Diode. It looks a bit like a cell, but it has a triangle attached to it. The triangle points to the direction in which the current can flow. The two arrows indicate that light is emitted by an LED. This means that when you draw this symbol in your circuit diagram, you know that electricity is going to produce light in that specific direction.

## Drawing a Complete Circuit

To draw a working circuit, you must connect the symbols so that there is a complete path. The lamp glows only when current passes through the circuit. The direction of electric current in an electrical circuit is taken to be from the positive to the negative terminal of the electric cell. When you connect the wires, make sure the current passes through the filament of the incandescent lamp. With an incandescent lamp, it does not matter which of its terminals connects to the positive or negative terminal of the cell. The lamp will glow as long as the circuit is complete and current flows through the filament.

## The Switch

A switch is a very useful part of a circuit because it lets you control the flow of electricity. When a switch is in the 'OFF' position, the path for the current is broken and the bulb stays dark. When the switch is in the 'ON' position, the path is complete and the bulb lights up. By placing a switch in your circuit diagram, you can show exactly where the control for the machine is located.

## Worked Example

Imagine you are drawing a circuit diagram for a simple torch. You start by drawing a cell. You draw the long line on the right side and the short line on the left side. Next, you draw a bulb to the left of the cell. Finally, you connect the two parts with straight lines representing wires to form a closed loop. Since you have connected the positive terminal to one side of the bulb and the negative terminal to the other, the circuit is complete. If you were to add a switch to this diagram, you would draw it as a break in the line that can be closed.

## Key points

- A circuit diagram is a drawing that uses standard symbols to show how an electrical circuit is set up.
- In the symbol for an electric cell, the long line represents the positive terminal, while the short line represents the negative terminal.
- In the symbol for an LED, the triangle points to the direction in which the current can flow, and the arrows show that light is emitted.
- The direction of electric current is taken to be from the positive to the negative terminal of the electric cell.
- The lamp glows only when current passes through the circuit and the circuit is complete.
