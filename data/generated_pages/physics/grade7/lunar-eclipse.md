# Lunar Eclipse

The Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, so the Moon moves through the Earth's shadow. A lunar eclipse is an important event because it shows us how the Earth, the Moon, and the Sun interact in space. When this happens, the bright disc of the Moon can appear dark red in colour. Unlike a solar eclipse, you can safely watch a lunar eclipse with your naked eye.

## The Geometry of the Eclipse
A lunar eclipse happens when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, blocking the sunlight from reaching the Moon. This arrangement means the Earth's shadow falls on the full disc of the Moon. You might wonder how a much smaller object like the Moon can block the Sun from our view. This is because of the apparent size of objects. The apparent size of an object depends on both its actual physical size and its distance from you. For example, if you hold a small coin very close to your eye and look at a large building far away, the coin can completely cover the building. This is exactly how the Moon appears to cover the Sun during a solar eclipse, even though the Sun is much larger. During a lunar eclipse, however, the Earth blocks the light from reaching the Moon directly.

## Total and Partial Eclipses
There are two main types of lunar eclipses: total and partial. A total lunar eclipse occurs when the Moon is completely inside the Earth's shadow. When this happens, the Moon does not disappear completely. Instead, it starts to appear dark red in colour. This red colour is because the Earth's atmosphere bends the sunlight around the edges of the Earth and onto the Moon. 

A partial lunar eclipse happens when only part of the Moon is in the Earth's shadow and the rest of the Moon is still visible. The shadow of the Moon during a solar eclipse falls on a small area on the surface of the Earth, creating total darkness in that specific area. However, during a lunar eclipse, the Earth's shadow is much larger, so it can cover the entire Moon.

## Safe Viewing
One of the great things about a lunar eclipse is that you do not need any special equipment to watch it. Unlike a solar eclipse, where looking at the Sun directly can cause blindness, you can safely watch the eclipsed full Moon with your naked eye. During a solar eclipse, the Sun is intense enough to damage the eyes, so you must never look at it directly, even with sunglasses, binoculars, or telescopes. 

## Worked example
Imagine you are standing in a dark room and a friend is holding a flashlight. If you stand between the flashlight and a wall, you will cast a shadow on the wall. If you then place a small ball in the shadow, the ball will be in darkness because the light from the flashlight is blocked by you. A lunar eclipse is exactly like this, but in space. The Earth is like the person blocking the light, and the Moon is the small ball moving through the shadow.

## Key points
*   A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, casting its shadow on the Moon.
*   A total lunar eclipse happens when the entire Moon is in the Earth's shadow, making it appear dark red.
*   A partial lunar eclipse occurs when only part of the Moon is in the Earth's shadow.
*   You can safely watch a lunar eclipse with your naked eye, unlike a solar eclipse.
*   The apparent size of an object depends on both its actual size and its distance from the observer.
