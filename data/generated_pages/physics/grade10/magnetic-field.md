# Magnetic Field and Field Lines

When you hold a compass near a bar magnet, the needle moves. This happens because the magnet exerts a force on the compass even when they are not touching. The space around a magnet where this force can be felt is called a magnetic field. Understanding magnetic fields helps us explain how compasses work and how forces act at a distance.

## Magnetic Field Lines

Magnetic field lines are a way to show the shape and direction of a magnetic field. You can draw them yourself. Place a bar magnet on a sheet of paper fixed to a drawing board. Take a small compass and place it near the north pole of the magnet. The south pole of the compass needle will point towards the north pole of the magnet, and the north pole of the compass will point away. Mark the two ends of the needle. Now move the compass so that its south pole occupies the position where its north pole was. Continue this step by step until you reach the south pole of the magnet. Join the points you marked with a smooth curve. This curve is a magnetic field line. Repeat this process to draw many lines. You will see a pattern around the magnet. These lines represent the magnetic field around the magnet.

## Properties of Magnetic Field Lines

Magnetic field lines have several important properties. The direction of the magnetic field is taken to be the direction in which the north pole of a compass needle moves. By convention, field lines emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole. Inside the magnet, the direction of the field lines is from the south pole to the north pole. Thus, the magnetic field lines are closed curves.

The relative strength of the magnetic field is shown by how close the field lines are to each other. The field is stronger where the field lines are crowded. This means the force acting on the pole of another magnet placed in that region is greater.

No two field lines are found to cross each other. If they did, it would mean that at the point of intersection, the compass needle would point towards two directions, which is not possible.

## Earth's Magnetic Field

The Earth itself acts like a giant magnet. A compass needle is a small bar magnet that aligns itself with the Earth's magnetic field. The ends of the compass needle point approximately towards the north and south directions. The end pointing towards north is called the north seeking or north pole. The other end that points towards south is called the south seeking or south pole.

## Worked example

Imagine you are using iron filings to map the field of a bar magnet. You sprinkle the filings uniformly around the magnet on a sheet of paper. When you tap the board gently, the filings arrange themselves in a pattern. The iron filings align themselves along the field lines. This happens because the magnet exerts its influence in the region surrounding it. The iron filings experience a force, and this force makes them arrange in a pattern. The region surrounding a magnet, in which the force of the magnet can be detected, is said to have a magnetic field. The lines along which the iron filings align themselves represent magnetic field lines.

## Reading a Field Line Diagram

Suppose you have drawn the field lines of a bar magnet and marked two points on the paper. Point A lies close to the north pole, where many lines are packed together. Point B lies far from both poles, where the lines are widely spaced.

- Strength: the lines are closer together at A, so the field at A is stronger than at B. A small magnet or a piece of iron placed at A would feel a greater force than at B.
- Direction: at any point, the field direction is along the tangent to the field line through that point. A compass placed at A or B settles along this direction, and its north pole shows which way the field points.
- Crossing: you will never find a point where two lines meet, because a compass placed there would have to point in two directions at once.

| Feature of the diagram | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Lines crowded together | Strong magnetic field |
| Lines far apart | Weak magnetic field |
| Arrow on a line | Direction of the field (outside the magnet, from north pole to south pole) |
| Lines forming closed curves | The lines continue inside the magnet, from south pole to north pole |

## Using a Compass Near a Magnet

A compass needle is itself a small magnet, so it always settles along the magnetic field at its position. If you carry a compass slowly around a bar magnet and mark where the needle points at each step, the marks trace out a field line, exactly as in the activity above. Near the magnet the needle is turned strongly by the field. Far from the magnet it is affected mainly by the Earth's field, which is why a compass far from any magnet points north and south.

## Key points

- A magnetic field is the region around a magnet where its force can be detected.
- Magnetic field lines show the direction and strength of the magnetic field.
- Field lines emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole outside the magnet.
- The magnetic field is stronger where the field lines are closer together.
- No two magnetic field lines ever cross each other.
- The Earth has its own magnetic field, which causes a compass needle to point towards the north and south.
