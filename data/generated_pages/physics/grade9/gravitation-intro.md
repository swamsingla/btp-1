# Gravitation

Gravitation is the force of attraction that exists between any two objects in the universe. It is a universal force, meaning it applies to everything, from tiny grains of sand to massive planets and stars. Without this force, the planets would not orbit the Sun, and the Moon would not orbit the Earth. This force is the reason we stay on the ground and why objects fall when dropped.

## The Universal Law of Gravitation

The gravitational force between two objects depends on their masses and the distance between them. According to the universal law of gravitation, the force of attraction is directly proportional to the product of the masses of the two objects. If you have two heavy objects, they pull on each other much more strongly than two light objects do. 

At the same time, the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between the centres of the two objects. This is known as the inverse-square law. It means that if you double the distance between two objects, the gravitational pull becomes four times weaker. If you triple the distance, the pull becomes nine times weaker. 

Combining these two ideas gives us the formula for gravitational force:
$$
F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}
$$
where $F$ is the force of attraction, $M$ and $m$ are the masses of the two objects, $d$ is the distance between their centres, and $G$ is the universal gravitation constant.

## The Gravitation Constant

The constant $G$ is a fixed value that applies everywhere in the universe. Its value was found by Henry Cavendish (1731–1810) using a very sensitive balance. The accepted value of $G$ is $6.673 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{2} \mathrm{kg}^{-2}$. 

Because $G$ is so incredibly small, the gravitational force is usually only noticeable when at least one of the objects is extremely massive, like a planet or a star. 

## Why We Do Not Notice Small Gravitational Forces

You might wonder why you do not feel yourself being pulled towards your friend sitting next to you. According to the universal law of gravitation, every object attracts every other object. However, the force is extremely weak for small objects. 

Take an apple falling to the earth. According to the third law of motion, the apple does attract the earth. But according to the second law of motion, for a given force, acceleration is inversely proportional to the mass of an object. The mass of an apple is negligibly small compared to that of the earth. So, we do not see the earth moving towards the apple. The same argument applies to why the earth does not move noticeably towards the moon or why you do not move towards your friend. 

## The Centripetal Force and Orbits

The motion of the moon around the earth is due to the centripetal force. The centripetal force is provided by the force of attraction of the earth. If there were no such force, the moon would pursue a uniform straight line motion. 

We can understand this by thinking about a stone tied to a piece of thread. If you whirl the stone round, it moves in a circular path. The stone changes direction at every point, which involves a change in velocity, which means it is accelerating. The force that causes this acceleration and keeps the body moving along the circular path is acting towards the centre. This force is called the centripetal (meaning 'centre-seeking') force. In the absence of this force, the stone flies off along a straight line. This straight line will be a tangent to the circular path.

## The Importance of the Universal Law of Gravitation

The universal law of gravitation successfully explained several phenomena which were believed to be unconnected. These include the force that binds us to the earth, the motion of the moon around the earth, the motion of planets around the Sun, and the tides due to the moon and the Sun.

## Weight

The weight of an object is the force with which it is attracted towards the earth. The SI unit of weight is the newton (N). The weight is a force acting vertically downwards; it has both magnitude and direction.

We know that the earth attracts every object with a certain force and this force depends on the mass (m) of the object and the acceleration due to the gravity (g). The force of attraction of the earth on an object is known as the weight of the object. It is denoted by W. Substituting the same in the equation for force, we have:
$$
W = m \times g
$$
As the weight of an object is the force with which it is attracted towards the earth, the SI unit of weight is the same as that of force, that is, newton (N). The mass of an object remains the same everywhere, that is, on the earth and on any planet whereas its weight depends on its location because g depends on location.

## Worked example

Let us calculate the force of attraction between two students sitting next to each other. Suppose one student has a mass of $50 \mathrm{~kg}$ and the other has a mass of $60 \mathrm{~kg}$. Let us say they are sitting $0.5 \mathrm{~m}$ apart. Using the formula for gravitational force, we can calculate the force between them.

The force of attraction is directly proportional to the product of their masses. So, $M \times m = 50 \times 60 = 3000 \mathrm{~kg}^2$. 

The force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. So, $d^2 = 0.5^2 = 0.25 \mathrm{~m}^2$.

The gravitational constant $G$ is $6.673 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{2} \mathrm{kg}^{-2}$.

Using the formula $F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$, we get:
$$
F = 6.673 \times 10^{-11} \times \frac{3000}{0.25} = 6.673 \times 10^{-11} \times 12000 = 8.0076 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{~N}
$$
Thus, the force of attraction between the two students is $8.0076 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{~N}$. This is a very small force, which is why we do not feel it.

## Key points

*   Gravitation is the force of attraction between any two objects in the universe.
*   The universal law of gravitation states that the force is directly proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
*   The formula for gravitational force is $F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$.
*   The universal gravitation constant $G$ has a value of $6.673 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{2} \mathrm{kg}^{-2}$.
*   The weight of an object is the force with which it is attracted towards the earth, and is given by $W = m \times g$.
