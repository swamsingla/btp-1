# Ultrasound and Its Applications

Ultrasounds are high frequency waves. They are able to travel along well-defined paths even in the presence of obstacles. Because they can move in straight lines and bounce off solid objects, they are used extensively in industries and for medical purposes. You might already know that sound is a mechanical wave, but unlike ordinary sound, which bends around corners, ultrasound travels along well-defined paths. This ability to travel in straight lines makes it extremely useful for tasks that require precision, such as cleaning tiny electronic parts or looking inside the human body without making a single cut.

## Cleaning in Hard-to-Reach Places

One of the most practical uses of ultrasound is in industrial cleaning. Imagine trying to wash the inside of a long, winding spiral tube or a complex electronic component. It is nearly impossible to reach every corner with a sponge or a brush. Ultrasound is generally used to clean parts located in hard-to-reach places, for example, spiral tube, odd shaped parts, electronic components, etc. Objects to be cleaned are placed in a cleaning solution and ultrasonic waves are sent into the solution. Due to the high frequency, the particles of dust, grease and dirt get detached and drop out. The objects thus get thoroughly cleaned. 

The high frequency of these waves creates intense vibrations in the liquid. These vibrations physically shake the dirt and grease loose from the surface of the object, even in places where no liquid could normally reach. This method is far more effective than traditional scrubbing for delicate machinery.

## Finding Flaws in Metal Blocks

Ultrasounds can be used to detect cracks and flaws in metal blocks. This is crucial in construction of big structures like buildings, bridges, machines and also scientific equipment. The cracks or holes inside the metal blocks, which are invisible from outside reduces the strength of the structure. 

To check for these hidden problems, ultrasonic waves are allowed to pass through the metal block and detectors are used to detect the transmitted waves. If there is even a small defect, the ultrasound gets reflected back indicating the presence of the flaw or defect. Ordinary sound of longer wavelengths cannot be used for such purpose as it will bend around the corners of the defective location and enter the detector. Because ultrasound travels in straight lines, it will hit the crack directly and bounce back to the detector, revealing the exact location of the damage.

## Medical Imaging of the Human Body

In medicine, ultrasound is a lifesaver. An ultrasound scanner is an instrument which uses ultrasonic waves for getting images of internal organs of the human body. A doctor may image the patient's organs, such as the liver, gall bladder, uterus, kidney, etc. It helps the doctor to detect abnormalities, such as stones in the gall bladder and kidney or tumours in different organs. 

In this technique the ultrasonic waves travel through the tissues of the body and get reflected from a region where there is a change of tissue density. These waves are then converted into electrical signals that are used to generate images of the organ. These images are then displayed on a monitor or printed on a film. This technique is called ‘ultrasonography’. Ultrasonography is also used for examination of the foetus during pregnancy to detect congenital defects and growth abnormalities. 

Furthermore, ultrasonic waves are made to reflect from various parts of the heart and form the image of the heart. This technique is called ‘echocardiography’. Ultrasound may also be employed to break small ‘stones’ formed in the kidneys into fine grains. These grains later get flushed out with urine.

## Sonar and Navigation

Ultrasound is also used to find objects underwater. This application is known as SONAR, which stands for SOund Navigation and Ranging. Bats use ultrasound in a similar way to find their way around, and the same principle is used in human technology. A ship sends out a pulse of ultrasound into the water. If the pulse hits a submarine or a large rock, it reflects back. By measuring how long the echo takes to return, the ship can calculate the distance to the object. Because ultrasound travels in straight lines and does not bend around corners like ordinary sound, it is the perfect tool for mapping the ocean floor or avoiding underwater hazards.

## Worked Example

Let us look at how ultrasound helps doctors find a kidney stone. Suppose a doctor uses an ultrasound scanner to look at a patient's kidney. The ultrasonic waves travel through the soft tissues of the kidney. When the waves reach the kidney stone, the tissue density changes abruptly. The ultrasound gets reflected back from the stone. The machine detects this reflection and generates an image of the stone on a monitor. Because the waves travel in straight lines, the doctor can see exactly where the stone is located, even though it is completely invisible to the naked eye.

## Key points

- Ultrasound is a high-frequency wave that travels in straight paths and bounces off solid objects.
- It is used to clean hard-to-reach places by shaking dirt and grease loose in a liquid solution.
- It can detect invisible cracks inside metal blocks used in buildings, bridges, and machines.
- It creates images of internal organs, such as the liver, gall bladder, and kidneys, to find abnormalities like stones or tumours.
- It is used to examine a foetus during pregnancy to check for growth abnormalities.
- It can break small kidney stones into fine grains that are flushed out with urine.
- Ordinary sound cannot be used for flaw detection because its longer wavelengths bend around corners.
