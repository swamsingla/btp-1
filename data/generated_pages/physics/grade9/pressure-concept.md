# Pressure

Pressure is a measure of how much force is applied to a specific area. It is defined as the force per unit area acting on an object. Understanding pressure helps us explain many everyday phenomena, from why a sharp knife cuts easily to why heavy vehicles have wide tyres.

## Thrust and Pressure

When a force acts on an object perpendicular to its surface, it is called thrust. The effect of thrust depends on the area over which it acts. The thrust on unit area is called pressure. The formula for pressure is:

$$
\text{Pressure} = \frac {\text{thrust}}{\text{area}}
$$

The SI unit of pressure is newton per square metre ($\text{N m}^{-2}$), which is also called a pascal (Pa).

## Everyday Applications

The concept of pressure explains many everyday situations. For example, a camel can run easily in the desert because its wide feet exert less pressure on the sand. An army tank rests upon a continuous chain to distribute its weight over a larger area, reducing the pressure on the ground. Trucks and motorbuses have much wider tyres for the same reason. Cutting tools have sharp edges because a smaller area results in a larger pressure for the same force. For the same reason, a nail has a pointed tip, and the foundations of tall buildings are made wide.

The effect of a force can be very large when the area is tiny. Suppose you press a sewing needle with a force of $20 \, \text{N}$ and the tip has an area of about $0.1 \, \text{mm}^2$, which is $1 \times 10^{-7} \, \text{m}^2$. The pressure is $\frac{20}{1 \times 10^{-7}} = 2 \times 10^{8} \, \text{Pa}$, which is enough to pierce cloth. Pressing the same $20 \, \text{N}$ with your whole palm, of area about $0.01 \, \text{m}^2$, gives only $\frac{20}{0.01} = 2000 \, \text{Pa}$.

## Worked Example

A wooden block has a mass of 5 kg and dimensions of 40 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm. The weight of the block is $5 \, \text{kg} \times 9.8 \, \text{ms}^{-2} = 49 \, \text{N}$.

If the block is placed on a table with its sides of dimensions 20 cm × 10 cm, the area is $20 \, \text{cm} \times 10 \, \text{cm} = 200 \, \text{cm}^2 = 0.02 \, \text{m}^2$. The pressure exerted is $\frac{49 \, \text{N}}{0.02 \, \text{m}^2} = 2450 \, \text{Nm}^{-2}$.

If the block is placed on its side of dimensions 40 cm × 20 cm, the area is $40 \, \text{cm} \times 20 \, \text{cm} = 800 \, \text{cm}^2 = 0.08 \, \text{m}^2$. The pressure exerted is $\frac{49 \, \text{N}}{0.08 \, \text{m}^2} = 612.5 \, \text{Nm}^{-2}$.

## Pressure in Fluids

All liquids and gases are fluids. A solid exerts pressure on a surface due to its weight. Similarly, fluids have weight, and they also exert pressure on the base and walls of the container in which they are enclosed. Pressure exerted in any confined mass of fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions.

The air around the earth is also a fluid. The weight of the air above us presses on every surface, which is called atmospheric pressure. At sea level it is about $1.01 \times 10^{5} \, \text{Pa}$, or roughly $100\,000 \, \text{N}$ on every square metre. We do not feel it because the fluids inside our bodies push outwards with the same pressure.

## Key points

- Thrust is the force acting perpendicular to a surface, and pressure is the thrust on unit area.
- The SI unit of pressure is the pascal: $1 \, \text{Pa} = 1 \, \text{N m}^{-2}$.
- For the same force, a smaller area gives a larger pressure and a larger area gives a smaller pressure.
- Liquids and gases are fluids, and they exert pressure on the base and walls of their container.
- Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions.
