# Respiration in Plants

Plants are living beings, and like all living beings, they need energy to survive and grow. They do not eat food in the way animals do, so they must make their own energy from the food they produce. Respiration is the process that breaks down this food to release the energy plants need for their growth and development. Without this energy, a plant could not carry out its daily life processes, such as growing new leaves or absorbing water from the soil.

## What is Respiration?

Respiration is the process where glucose is broken down in the presence of oxygen, releasing carbon dioxide, water, and energy. The energy produced during respiration is used by plants for their growth and development. All parts of a plant, green or non-green, carry out respiration.

The word equation for the process of respiration is as follows:
$$
\text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen} \longrightarrow \text{Carbon dioxide} + \text{Water} + \text{Energy}
$$

To understand this better, imagine a small factory. The factory takes in raw materials, processes them, and produces a finished product. In this case, the factory is the plant, the raw materials are glucose and oxygen, and the finished products are carbon dioxide, water, and energy. The energy is the power that keeps the factory running.

## How Do We Know Plants Respire?

We can prove that plants respire by looking at the gases they produce. During respiration, plants release carbon dioxide into the air. This is different from photosynthesis, where plants take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen. To test if plants produce carbon dioxide, we can set up an experiment.

Soak some moong bean seeds in water overnight. Put a layer of cotton in a conical flask and moisten the cotton with water to keep it wet. Place the soaked seeds over the wet cotton in the conical flask. Cover the mouth of the conical flask with a cork having two holes and fit two tubes A and B through the two holes on the cork. Leave it undisturbed for 24 hours in the dark. Take two test tubes and fill them with lime water. Cover the mouth of one test tube with a cork having one hole in it. Dip one glass tube in the test tube through a hole in the cork. Connect the flask and test tube with a rubber pipe. Compare both the test tubes for any change in colour. Does the lime water turn milky in both the test tubes? The lime water turns milky due to the presence of more carbon dioxide in the flask. But where does this carbon dioxide come from? As we know, carbon dioxide is naturally present in very small quantities in the air. In the flask, additional carbon dioxide is produced by the seeds as they respire.

## Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration

There are two types of respiration: aerobic and anaerobic. Aerobic respiration is the process where glucose is broken down in the presence of oxygen. Anaerobic respiration is the process where glucose is broken down without the presence of oxygen. Anaerobic respiration is also known as fermentation.

During anaerobic respiration, glucose is broken down to produce ethanol, carbon dioxide, and a small amount of energy. The word equation for the process of anaerobic respiration is as follows:
$$
\text{Glucose} \longrightarrow \text{Ethanol} + \text{Carbon dioxide} + \text{Energy}
$$

Anaerobic respiration occurs when there is no oxygen available. For example, when we exercise vigorously, our muscles may not get enough oxygen, and they resort to anaerobic respiration. Similarly, when seeds are soaked in water, they may not get enough oxygen, and they may resort to anaerobic respiration.

## The Compensation Point

The compensation point is the point at which the rate of photosynthesis is equal to the rate of respiration. At this point, the plant is not producing any net oxygen or carbon dioxide. The compensation point is important because it helps the plant to maintain a balance between the two processes.

During the day, the rate of photosynthesis is higher than the rate of respiration. During the night, the rate of respiration is higher than the rate of photosynthesis. The compensation point is reached during the day when the rate of photosynthesis is equal to the rate of respiration.

## Worked Example

Let us consider a plant that is producing glucose at a rate of 10 grams per day. The plant is also respiring at a rate of 5 grams per day. What is the net amount of glucose produced by the plant?

The net amount of glucose produced by the plant is the difference between the rate of photosynthesis and the rate of respiration. Therefore, the net amount of glucose produced by the plant is 10 - 5 = 5 grams per day.
