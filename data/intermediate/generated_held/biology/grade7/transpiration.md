# Transpiration

Plants need water to live and grow. They absorb water and minerals from the soil through their roots. This water travels up to the leaves to help make food through photosynthesis. However, plants do not hold onto all of this water. A large amount of water escapes from the leaves into the air. This loss of water from the leaves is called transpiration. Transpiration is very important because it creates a pulling force that helps draw more water up from the roots to the rest of the plant.

## The Role of Stomata

The tiny openings on the surface of leaves that allow water to escape are called stomata. Stomata are like tiny doors on the leaf. They open to let carbon dioxide in for photosynthesis. When they are open, water vapor naturally flows out into the air. The number of these tiny openings on a leaf determines how much water can escape. If a leaf has more stomata, it will lose more water to the air.

## Factors Affecting Transpiration

The rate of transpiration changes depending on the conditions around the plant. Several environmental factors control how much water leaves the plant.

Temperature plays a big role. When the air around the plant is hot, water evaporates from the leaves faster. This means a higher temperature causes more water loss. Humidity, which is the amount of water already in the air, also matters. If the air is already full of moisture, water cannot easily leave the leaf. On a dry day, transpiration is very fast. Wind speed affects the process too. When wind blows across the leaves, it removes the moist air near the surface and replaces it with drier air. This causes the plant to lose water much more quickly.

## The Process of Respiration

While photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide, plants also need to breathe. This process is called respiration. During respiration, plants break down glucose in the presence of oxygen. This process releases carbon dioxide, water, and energy. The energy produced during respiration is used by plants for their growth and development. All parts of a plant, whether they are green or not, carry out respiration. This means that even the roots and stems need to breathe to stay alive.

## Transport of Water and Food

Water and minerals are taken up by the roots of a plant. To move this water to the leaves, plants use a special transport system. The food prepared by the leaves is transported to all parts of the plant through thin tube-like structures called the phloem. This transported food may also be stored in other parts of the plant, such as seeds and roots.

## Worked Example

Let us look at how wind affects the rate of water loss. Imagine a plant is sitting in a room with no wind. The air around the leaves is still. Now, imagine a fan is turned on in the room to blow a gentle breeze over the leaves. The moving air will sweep away the moist air layer that sits right next to the leaf surface. Because the air right next to the leaf is now drier, the water vapor inside the leaf will move out much faster. This means the rate of transpiration increases when the wind speed increases.
