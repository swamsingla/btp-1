# Soil Types and Formation

Soil is the thin layer of broken rock and organic matter that covers the ground. It is very important because it holds the water and nutrients that plants need to grow. Without soil, we could not grow our food. Soil is formed when large rocks slowly break apart into smaller pieces over many years.

## How Rocks Break Apart

Weathering is the process where large rocks break into smaller pieces. There are two main ways this happens. First, physical weathering breaks rocks apart without changing what they are made of. For example, when water gets into cracks in a rock and freezes, it expands and pushes the rock apart. Also, the roots of growing trees can push their way into cracks and break the rock into smaller pieces. Second, chemical weathering changes the rock itself. Water and chemicals in the water can react with the rock. For instance, when a rock with iron is exposed to water or air for a long time, it can turn red. This happens because the iron changes into iron oxide.

## The Soil Profile

As rocks break down, they form different layers in the ground. This stack of layers is called the soil profile. The top layer is usually the darkest and richest in organic matter from dead leaves and plants. Below that, the layers get lighter in colour and contain more broken rock. The very bottom layer is mostly solid rock that has not yet fully weathered.

## Types of Soil

The size of the broken rock pieces determines the type of soil. Sandy soil is made of large, coarse grains. It feels gritty and lets water drain through very quickly. Clayey soil is made of very tiny, smooth grains. It feels sticky when wet and holds onto water very tightly. Loamy soil is a mixture of sand and clay. It has the perfect balance, holding enough water for plants without getting too soggy.

## Worked example

Imagine you have three jars of soil. You add water to each jar and shake them well. The sand settles at the bottom of the jar first because it has large grains. The clay stays mixed in the water for a long time because its grains are very tiny. If you mix the two together in the right amounts, you get loamy soil, which is ideal for growing crops like wheat or rice in India.

## Key points

- Weathering is the breaking of rocks into smaller pieces by physical and chemical changes.
- Physical weathering includes temperature changes, tree roots, and freezing water.
- Chemical weathering includes water and chemicals reacting with rocks, such as iron turning into iron oxide.
- The soil profile is the stack of different soil layers from the top to the bottom.
- Sandy soil has large grains, clayey soil has tiny grains, and loamy soil is a mixture of both.
