# Mean, Median and Mode

When we collect numbers, we often want to find a single number that tells us what the whole group is like. This single number is called a measure of central tendency. It helps us understand the centre of our data without having to look at every single value. The three main ways to find this centre are the mean, the median, and the mode. Each one gives us a slightly different picture of the data.

## The Mean

The mean is what most people call the average. To find the mean, you add up all the numbers in your data set. Then, you divide that total by how many numbers there are. The mean is a measure of central tendency and represents the centre of the data. 

For example, if you have the numbers 4, 8, and 12, you add them to get 24. Since there are 3 numbers, you divide 24 by 3. The mean is 8. You can think of the mean as a balancing point. If you place the numbers on a number line, the mean is the spot where the total distance to the left is exactly equal to the total distance to the right.

## The Median

The median is the middle value in a set of numbers. To find it, you must first sort your numbers from smallest to largest. The median is the exact middle number. There are an equal number of values less than it and greater than it.

If you have an odd number of values, the median is simply the middle number. For example, in the sorted numbers 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, the median is 10. If you have an even number of values, you take the two middle numbers and find their average. For example, in the sorted numbers 2, 5, 10, 15, the two middle numbers are 5 and 10. You add them to get 15 and divide by 2. The median is 7.5.

## The Mode

The mode is the number that appears most often in a data set. A data set can have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode at all. If every number appears only once, then there is no mode.

For example, in the numbers 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 3, the number 3 appears three times. This makes 3 the mode. If you had the numbers 2, 2, 3, 3, 5, then both 2 and 3 are modes because they both appear twice.

## Worked example

Let us use these three ideas on a real-life problem. Imagine a group of friends went to a market. They bought 2, 4, 4, 6, and 8 mangoes. 

To find the mean, we add them up: $2 + 4 + 4 + 6 + 8 = 24$. We divide 24 by 5, which gives a mean of 4.8. 

To find the median, we sort them first: 2, 4, 4, 6, 8. The middle number is 4, so the median is 4. 

To find the mode, we look for the most common number. The number 4 appears twice. The other numbers only appear once. Therefore, the mode is 4.
