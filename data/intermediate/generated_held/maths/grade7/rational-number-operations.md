# Operations on Rational Numbers

You already know how to work with integers and fractions. Rational numbers are simply numbers that can be written as a fraction. They include positive and negative fractions, and whole numbers. This page explains how to add, subtract, multiply, and divide them. These operations are useful for everyday tasks, such as calculating discounts while shopping or adjusting measurements in a recipe.

## Adding and Subtracting Rational Numbers

To add or subtract rational numbers, you first need a common denominator. A common denominator is a shared multiple of the bottom numbers of your fractions. Once the denominators are the same, you simply add or subtract the top numbers. The denominator stays the same.

If the rational numbers have different signs, you subtract their absolute values, and the result takes the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

### Worked example
Let us add $\frac{3}{4}$ and $-\frac{1}{2}$. The denominators are 4 and 2. The least common multiple is 4. We rewrite $\frac{1}{2}$ as $\frac{2}{4}$. Now we add the top numbers: $3 - 2 = 1$. The answer is $\frac{1}{4}$.

## Multiplying Rational Numbers

To multiply rational numbers, you multiply the top numbers together and the bottom numbers together. You do not need a common denominator for this operation. If you are multiplying a positive and a negative rational number, the result will be negative.

### Worked example
Let us multiply $-\frac{2}{3}$ by $\frac{5}{7}$. Multiply the top numbers: $-2 \times 5 = -10$. Multiply the bottom numbers: $3 \times 7 = 21$. The result is $-\frac{10}{21}$.

## Dividing Rational Numbers

To divide rational numbers, you multiply the first number by the reciprocal of the second number. The reciprocal is the fraction flipped upside down. For example, the reciprocal of $\frac{4}{9}$ is $\frac{9}{4}$.

### Worked example
Let us divide $-\frac{5}{6}$ by $\frac{1}{3}$. We multiply $-\frac{5}{6}$ by the reciprocal of $\frac{1}{3}$, which is 3. We write 3 as $\frac{3}{1}$. Multiply the top numbers: $-5 \times 3 = -15$. Multiply the bottom numbers: $6 \times 1 = 6$. The result is $-\frac{15}{6}$. We can simplify this by dividing both the top and bottom by their highest common factor, which is 3. The simplified answer is $-\frac{5}{2}$.

## Properties of Multiplication

Multiplication of rational numbers follows certain properties that make calculations easier. The commutative property means that the order of the numbers does not change the product. For example, $a \times b = b \times a$.

The distributive property is also very useful. It states that multiplying a number by a sum is the same as multiplying the number by each addend separately and then adding the results. This can be written as $a(b + c) = ab + ac$.

### Worked example
Let us use the distributive property to multiply $23$ by $27$. We can write $23 \times (27 + 1) = 23 \times 27 + 23$. The increase in the product is $23$.

## Key points

*   Rational numbers can be written as fractions and include positive and negative values.
*   To add or subtract, find a common denominator and combine the top numbers.
*   To multiply, multiply the top numbers and the bottom numbers directly.
*   To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
*   The distributive property, $a(b + c) = ab + ac$, helps simplify multiplication problems.
