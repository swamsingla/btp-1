# Algebraic Expressions

An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase built from numbers, variables, and arithmetic operations like addition, subtraction, and multiplication. We use these expressions to describe real-world situations and solve problems. For example, if you know how many items you have and how many each bag contains, you can write an expression to find the total. When an expression has more than one operation, we need clear rules to find its value.

## Brackets in Expressions

Sometimes, an expression without context can be evaluated in more than one way. Just as a comma in a sentence changes its meaning, brackets in mathematics tell us exactly how to evaluate an expression. We must always find the value of the expressions inside the brackets first before performing other operations.

Consider an example. Mallesh brought 30 marbles to the playground, and Arun brought 5 bags of marbles with 4 marbles in each bag. To find the total number of marbles, we need to multiply the bags by the marbles per bag, and then add that to Mallesh's marbles. The expression is $30 + 5 \times 4$. If we add 30 and 5 first, we get 140. However, Mallesh is right because we must multiply 5 and 4 first to get 20, and then add 30 to get 50. We clarify this order using brackets: $30 + (5 \times 4) = 30 + 20 = 50$.

## Terms in Expressions

When there are no brackets, we use the notion of terms to determine the order of operations. Terms are the parts of an expression separated by a ‘+’ sign. For example, in $12 + 7$, the terms are 12 and 7. We can mark them as $12 + 7 = 12 + 7$.

To find the terms in an expression with subtraction, we convert subtraction to addition by using the inverse of the number. The inverse of a given number has the sign opposite to it. For example, the inverse of 14 is -14, and the inverse of -14 is 14. Thus, subtracting 14 from 83 is the same as adding -14 to 83: $83 - 14 = 83 + (-14)$. The terms of the expression 83 - 14 are 83 and -14.

All subtractions in an expression are converted to additions in this manner to identify the terms. Here are more examples:
$-18 - 3 = -18 + (-3)$
$6 \times 5 + 3 = 6 \times 5 + 3$
$2 - 10 + 4 \times 6 = 2 + (-10) + 4 \times 6$

Note that $6 \times 5$ and $4 \times 6$ are single terms as they do not have any ‘+’ sign.

## Worked example
Suppose Irfan bought a pack of biscuits for ₹15 and a packet of toor dal for ₹56. He gave the shopkeeper ₹100. We can write the expression for the change he gets back as $100 - (15 + 56)$. Evaluating the expression within the brackets first, we get 100 minus 71, which is 29. So, Irfan will get back ₹29.

## Key points
* Expressions are built from variables, constants, and arithmetic operations.
* Brackets specify the order of operations, telling us to evaluate the inside first.
* Terms are the parts of an expression separated by a ‘+’ sign.
* Subtraction is converted to addition of the inverse to identify the terms.
* Always follow the order of operations to evaluate expressions correctly.
