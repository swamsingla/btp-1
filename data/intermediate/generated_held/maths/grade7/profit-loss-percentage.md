# Profit, Loss and Discount

When you buy a notebook or a toy, you pay a certain amount of money to the shopkeeper. When the shopkeeper sells it to you, they might charge more or less than what they paid. The difference between these amounts is called profit, loss, or discount. Understanding these ideas helps you make smart choices when you go shopping or when you help your parents run a small business.

## Cost Price and Selling Price

The cost price is the amount of money the shopkeeper pays to buy an item from the manufacturer or wholesaler. The selling price is the amount of money the customer pays after any bargaining or discounts. If the selling price is higher than the cost price, the shopkeeper makes a profit. If the selling price is lower than the cost price, the shopkeeper makes a loss. For example, if a shopkeeper buys a toy for ₹50 and sells it for ₹60, they make a profit of ₹10. If they sell it for ₹40, they make a loss of ₹10.

## Percentage Profit and Loss

To understand how big the profit or loss is, we calculate it as a percentage of the cost price. The cost price is always taken as 100% to find the percentage profit or loss. The formula for percentage profit is $\frac{\text{profit amount}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100$. The formula for percentage loss is $\frac{\text{loss amount}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100$. 

Let us look at an example. Suppose a shopkeeper buys a box of crayons for ₹40 and sells it for ₹50. The profit amount is ₹10. To find the percentage profit, we use the formula: $\frac{10}{40} \times 100 = 25\%$. This means the shopkeeper made a profit of 25% on the cost price.

## Discount

Sometimes, shops offer a discount to attract customers. A discount is a reduction in the marked price of an item. The marked price is the price written on the tag or MRP of the item before any bargaining. The selling price is the price after the discount is applied. For example, if a book has a marked price of ₹100 and the shopkeeper gives a discount of 20%, the discount amount is 20% of ₹100, which is ₹20. The selling price will be ₹100 - ₹20 = ₹80.

## Net Profit and Expenses

When a shopkeeper sells many items, they might make a gross profit. Gross profit is the total selling amount minus the total cost price of the goods. However, the shopkeeper also has other expenses like rent, electricity, and salaries. The net profit is the gross profit minus these other expenses. 

For example, if a shopkeeper sells goods worth ₹10,000 and the cost price of those goods was ₹6,000, the gross profit is ₹4,000. If the other expenses are ₹1,000, the net profit is ₹3,000.

## Worked Example

Let us apply these ideas to a real-life situation. A shopkeeper buys a packet of biscuits for ₹200. She marks the price at ₹250. However, to clear the stock, she decides to sell it at a discount of 20% on the marked price. First, we find the discount amount. 20% of ₹250 is $\frac{20}{100} \times 250 = ₹50$. The selling price is ₹250 - ₹50 = ₹200. 

Now, let us find the profit or loss. The cost price is ₹200 and the selling price is ₹200. The profit amount is ₹200 - ₹200 = ₹0. The percentage profit is $\frac{0}{200} \times 100 = 0\%$. This means the shopkeeper neither made a profit nor a loss.
