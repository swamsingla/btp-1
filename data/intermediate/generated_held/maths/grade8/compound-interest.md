# Compound Interest

Compound interest is the interest calculated on the initial principal and also on the accumulated interest of previous periods. It matters because it makes your money grow much faster than simple interest over time. When you save or invest, the interest you earn starts earning its own interest.

## How It Works
With simple interest, you only earn money on the original amount. With compound interest, you earn money on your money. Every year, the interest is added to the principal. The next year, you calculate the interest on this new, larger amount. This process is called compounding. It is like a snowball rolling down a hill. It starts small, but as it picks up more snow, it grows bigger and faster.

## The Formula
The formula for compound interest is $A = P(1 + \frac{R}{100})^n$. 
Here is what each letter means:
- $A$ is the final amount of money you have.
- $P$ is the principal, which is the starting amount of money.
- $R$ is the rate of interest per year.
- $n$ is the number of years the money is invested.
This formula gives you the total amount. If you want only the interest, you subtract the principal from the final amount.

## The Power of Time
The number $n$ in the formula is very important. It represents the number of years. Because $n$ is an exponent, the interest grows very quickly as time passes. If you invest for a longer time, the final amount will be much larger. This is why it is best to start saving early. The extra years give your money more time to multiply.

## Worked example
Let us say you invest ₹10,000 at a rate of 10% per year for 3 years. Here, $P = 10000$, $R = 10$, and $n = 3$. We use the formula $A = P(1 + \frac{R}{100})^n$. First, we find the value inside the brackets. $\frac{10}{100}$ is $0.10$. So, $1 + 0.10$ is $1.10$. Next, we raise this to the power of 3. $1.10^3 = 1.10 \times 1.10 \times 1.10$. This equals $1.331$. Now, we multiply by the principal. $A = 10000 \times 1.331$. This gives us $A = 13310$. So, after 3 years, you will have ₹13,310. The total compound interest earned is ₹13,310 minus ₹10,000, which is ₹3,310.
