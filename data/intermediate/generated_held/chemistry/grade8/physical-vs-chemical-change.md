# Physical and Chemical Changes

Everything around us is constantly changing. A piece of ice melting into water is a physical change, while a piece of paper burning into ash is a chemical change. Understanding how and why these changes happen is the foundation of chemistry. By learning to tell the difference between a physical and a chemical change, you can predict what will happen when you mix different substances or apply heat.

## Physical Properties and Physical Change

Physical properties are the characteristics of matter that can be observed without changing its chemical identity. These include colour, hardness, fluidity, density, melting point, and boiling point. When a substance undergoes a physical change, its form or state changes, but its chemical nature remains exactly the same. For example, ice, liquid water, and water vapour all look different and display different physical properties, but they are chemically identical.

A physical change is also called a reversible change because the original substance can usually be recovered. If you put water in a freezer, it will turn back into ice. This is because the interconversion of states is a physical change; it occurs without a change in composition.

## Chemical Properties and Chemical Change

Chemical properties are the characteristics that describe how a substance behaves in a chemical reaction. For example, water and cooking oil are both liquids, but they differ in odour and inflammability. Water extinguishes fire, whereas cooking oil burns in air. It is this chemical property of oil that makes it different from water.

When a chemical change occurs, one substance reacts with another to undergo a change in chemical composition. A chemical change brings a change in the chemical properties of matter, and we get new substances. A chemical change is also called a chemical reaction. Because the original substance cannot be easily brought back, a chemical change is an irreversible change.

## Reversible vs Irreversible Changes

Changes around us can be grouped into two main categories: reversible and irreversible. A reversible change is one where the original substance can be recovered after the change. Melting ice and boiling water are reversible changes because you can freeze the water back into ice or condense the steam back into liquid water.

An irreversible change is one where the original substance cannot be recovered. For example, burning is a chemical change and is irreversible because you cannot turn ash back into wood or paper. When you chop a vegetable into smaller pieces, it is an irreversible change because the original size and shape of the material cannot be restored.

## Indicators of a Chemical Change

There are several clear indicators that tell you a chemical change has happened. The formation of a gas, a change in colour, or the release of heat and light are strong signs. For example, when a candle burns, both physical and chemical changes take place. The wax melts and evaporates, which is a physical change. However, the vapour of wax burning to produce a flame is a chemical change because a new substance is formed.

## Worked Example

Consider the process of lighting a candle. When you light a candle, the wax melts due to the heat of the flame. This melting of wax and its subsequent solidification are physical changes. The wax is also carried up the wick and evaporates, which is another physical change. However, the vapour of wax burning to produce a flame is a chemical change because a new substance is formed. This shows that the burning of a candle involves both physical and chemical changes.

## Key points

*   Physical properties like colour, hardness, fluidity, density, melting point, and boiling point can be observed without changing the substance's identity.
*   A physical change alters the form or state of a substance, but the chemical identity remains the same.
*   A chemical change results in the formation of a new substance with different chemical properties.
*   Reversible changes can be undone to get back the original substance.
*   Irreversible changes cannot be undone to recover the original substance.
*   Indicators of a chemical change include the formation of a gas, a change in colour, or the release of heat and light.
