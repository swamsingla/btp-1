# Heat and Temperature

When you step out of a swimming pool on a hot summer afternoon, your skin feels cold. Yet the air around you is quite warm. Why does this happen? The answer lies in the difference between heat and temperature. Temperature is simply a measure of how hot or cold a body is. Heat, on the other hand, is a form of energy that flows from one place to another. Understanding this distinction helps us make sense of everyday experiences, from feeling a cold breeze to using a thermometer to check a fever.

## Temperature Measures Hotness

A reliable measure of hotness (or coldness) of a body is its temperature. A hotter body has a higher temperature than a colder body. The difference in temperature between the two bodies tells us how hot a body is in comparison to another body. For example, during the summer, tap water might be hotter than the cold water from a matka (earthen pot). We can realize this by merely touching the two samples of water, though our sense of touch is not always reliable. 

## How Thermometers Work

To measure temperature accurately, we use a device called a thermometer. There are two main kinds of thermometers that you might see. Clinical thermometers are used to measure human body temperatures. Laboratory thermometers are used for many other purposes, such as checking the temperature of water in a beaker during a science experiment.

Clinical thermometers generally use a scale called the Celsius scale. On this scale, the unit of temperature is degree Celsius and is denoted by $^{\circ}$C. Earlier, mercury thermometers were used for measuring the body temperature. But mercury is an extremely toxic substance and is difficult to dispose of if the thermometer breaks accidentally. Digital thermometers pose no such risk and also the numbers in its display are easier to read. Therefore, mercury thermometers are being replaced by digital thermometers. Temperature in a digital thermometer is determined with the help of heat sensors.

A laboratory thermometer consists of a long, narrow, uniform glass tube which is sealed. At one end of the tube is a bulb which contains a liquid. Outside the bulb, in the tube, a narrow column of liquid can be seen. There is a Celsius scale marked along the tube. The liquid column rises or falls with change in temperature. The mark of the Celsius scale with which the top level of the liquid column coincides is the temperature reading. The liquid used in the laboratory thermometer is generally alcohol (coloured red to make it easily seen) or mercury.

## Types of Thermometers

You may already know how to measure temperature using a thermometer. During the COVID-19 pandemic, some special thermometers were used, which could measure the temperature of a person from a distance. These are non-contact thermometers, also called infrared thermometers. Such thermometers can measure temperature without touching a person's body and thus reduce the risk of spreading disease.

## Heat is a Form of Energy

Heat is a form of energy, while temperature is a measure of how hot or cold a body is. Heat is the energy that causes the liquid in a thermometer to rise or fall. It is the energy that flows from a hotter body to a colder body. When you touch a hot object, heat flows from the object into your hand. When you touch a cold object, heat flows from your hand into the object.

## Worked example

Let us look at a worked example of how to compare temperatures. Imagine you are in a hot room with a temperature of $38^\circ$C and you step outside into a room that is $30^\circ$C. The difference in temperature between the two rooms is $38 - 30 = 8^\circ$C. This difference in temperature tells us that the first room is $8^\circ$C hotter than the second room.
