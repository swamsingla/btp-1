# Plane Mirrors

A plane mirror is a perfectly flat reflective surface that bounces light back to form an image. You encounter plane mirrors every day, from the one on your bathroom wall to the rear-view mirrors on buses and cars. Understanding how these mirrors form images, and why they flip left and right, is a fundamental part of optics. When you look into a plane mirror, you are seeing a virtual image, which means the light rays do not actually converge at the location where the image appears to be. This page explores the properties of the image formed by a plane mirror, including its size, orientation, and the phenomenon of lateral inversion.

## The Virtual Nature of the Image

When you look into a plane mirror, you see a reflection of your face. This reflection is the image formed by the mirror. If you place an object, like a pen, in front of the mirror, it appears as if a similar pen is placed behind the mirror. The actual pen is called the object, and the one you see in the mirror is the image.

You cannot capture the image on a screen. If you try to place a screen behind the mirror, the image will not appear on it. Similarly, placing a screen in front of the mirror will not capture the image. This is because the light rays do not actually meet at the image location. A screen requires light rays to physically converge on its surface to form a visible picture. Since the image is virtual, it is formed by the apparent intersection of reflected light rays, not by their actual convergence.

## Image Size and Position

The size of the image formed by a plane mirror is always the same as the size of the object. If you move the pen to different positions in front of the mirror, the image of the pen will remain the same size as the pen itself. 

The distance of the image from the mirror is also related to the distance of the object from the mirror. When you stand close to the plane mirror, the image also appears to be close to the mirror. When you stand far from the plane mirror, the image appears to be far from the mirror as well. This means that the image distance is equal to the object distance.

## Image Orientation and Lateral Inversion

The image formed by a plane mirror is always upright. If you place a pen in front of the mirror, the tip of the pen appears on top in the image at every position. An upright image is called erect.

However, there is a special property of images in plane mirrors called lateral inversion. This is a perceived left-right reversal. If you raise your left arm, your image will appear to raise its right arm. If you touch your right ear, your image will appear to touch its left ear. This happens because the mirror reverses the front-back axis of the object, which makes the left side appear as the right side and vice versa.

A common example of lateral inversion is the writing on an ambulance. The word 'AMBULANCE' is often written in reverse as 'ЕСИАЛУВМА' so that it reads correctly in the rear-view mirrors of vehicles ahead of it.

## Worked Example

Imagine you place a small toy car 50 centimeters in front of a plane mirror. The image of the car will be formed 50 centimeters behind the mirror. The size of the image will be exactly the same as the size of the actual toy car. If you look at the image from the side, the top of the toy car will still be at the top, and the bottom will be at the bottom. However, if you look at the image from the front, the left side of the toy car will appear to be on the right side, and the right side will appear on the left side.
