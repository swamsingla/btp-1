# Applications of Biotechnology

Biotechnology is the use of living systems and organisms to develop products that improve human life. In Grade 12 biology, we study how scientists manipulate the genetic material of organisms to solve problems in agriculture and medicine. These applications help farmers grow more food, protect the environment, and treat diseases more safely.

## Genetically Modified Crops

To increase food production, scientists can use agro-chemical based agriculture, organic agriculture, or genetically engineered crop-based agriculture [S1]. The Green Revolution tripled the food supply, but it relied heavily on fertilizers and pesticides that are expensive for farmers in the developing world [S1]. Traditional breeding could not keep up with the growing demand for higher yields [S1]. 

To solve this, scientists turned to tissue culture. Tissue culture is a method where whole plants are regenerated from explants—any part of a plant taken out and grown in a test tube under sterile conditions in a special nutrient medium [S1]. The ability to generate a whole plant from any cell or explant is called totipotency [S1]. This nutrient medium must provide a carbon source like sucrose, inorganic salts, vitamins, amino acids, and growth regulators like auxins and cytokinins [S1]. Using these methods, it is possible to produce thousands of plants in very short durations through a process called micro-propagation [S1]. Each of these plants will be genetically identical to the original plant from which they were grown, i.e., they are somaclones [S1].

Another important application of tissue culture is the recovery of healthy plants from diseased plants. Even if a plant is infected with a virus, the meristem (apical and axillary) is free of virus [S1]. Hence, one can remove the meristem and grow it in vitro to obtain virus-free plants [S1].

Plants, bacteria, fungi, and animals whose genes have been altered by manipulation are called Genetically Modified Organisms (GMO) [S1]. GM plants have been useful in many ways. Genetic modification has made crops more tolerant to abiotic stresses (cold, drought, salt, heat), reduced reliance on chemical pesticides (pest-resistant crops), helped to reduce post-harvest losses, increased efficiency of mineral usage by plants (which prevents early exhaustion of soil fertility), and enhanced nutritional value of food, e.g., golden rice, i.e., Vitamin 'A' enriched rice [S1]. In addition to these uses, GM has been used to create tailor-made plants to supply alternative resources to industries, in the form of starches, fuels and pharmaceuticals [S1].

Some of the applications of biotechnology in agriculture that you will study in detail are the production of pest-resistant plants, which could decrease the amount of pesticide used [S1]. Bt toxin is produced by a bacterium called Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt for short) [S1]. Bt toxin gene has been cloned from the bacteria and been expressed in plants to provide resistance to insects without the need for insecticides; in effect created a bio-pesticide [S1]. Examples are Bt cotton, Bt corn, rice, tomato, potato, and soybean etc. [S1].

Specific Bt toxin genes were isolated from Bacillus thuringiensis and incorporated into several crop plants such as cotton [S1]. The choice of genes depends upon the crop and the targeted pest, as most Bt toxins are insect-group specific [S1]. The toxin is coded by a gene cryIAc named cry. There are a number of them, for example, the proteins encoded by the genes cryIAc and cryIIAb control the cotton bollworms, that of cryIAb controls corn borer [S1].

Another strategy to prevent pest infestation is RNA interference (RNAi), which occurs in all eukaryotic organisms as a method of cellular defense [S1]. For instance, the nematode Meloidegyne incognitia infects the roots of tobacco plants, reducing yield [S1]. This method involves silencing of a specific mRNA due to a complementary dsRNA molecule that binds to and prevents translation of the mRNA [S1]. Using Agrobacterium vectors, nematode-specific genes were introduced into the host plant to produce both sense and anti-sense RNA [S1]. These complementary RNAs formed a double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) that initiated RNAi, silencing the nematode's mRNA [S1]. As a result, the parasite could not survive in the transgenic host, protecting the plant [S1].

## Human Genome Research

Deriving meaningful knowledge from the DNA sequences will define research through the coming decades leading to our understanding of biological systems [S3]. One of the greatest impacts of having the human genome (HG) sequence may well be enabling a radically new approach to biological research. In the past, researchers studied one or a few genes at a time. With whole-genome sequences and new high-throughput technologies, we can approach questions systematically and on a much broader scale [S3]. They can study all the genes in a genome, for example, all the transcripts in a particular tissue or organ or tumor, or how tens of thousands of genes and proteins work together in interconnected networks to orchestrate the chemistry of life [S3].

## Recombinant Therapeutics

Recombinant DNA technological processes have made immense impact in the area of healthcare by enabling mass production of safe and more effective therapeutic drugs [S2]. Further, the recombinant therapeutics do not induce unwanted immunological responses as is common in case of similar products isolated from non-human sources [S2]. At present, about 30 recombinant therapeutics have been approved for human-use the world over. In India, 12 of these are presently being marketed [S2].

## Worked example

**Question:** How can recombinant DNA technology be used to produce a specific therapeutic protein on a mass scale?

**Answer:** A biotech company can insert the gene for the desired therapeutic protein into a bacterium. Because bacteria reproduce rapidly, they can mass-produce the protein under controlled conditions. Finally, the protein is extracted, purified, and formulated into a safe and effective medicine for human use.

- Tissue culture is a method where whole plants are regenerated from explants grown in a test tube under sterile conditions [S1].
- The ability to generate a whole plant from any cell or explant is called totipotency [S1].
- Genetically Modified Organisms (GMO) are plants, bacteria, fungi, and animals whose genes have been altered by manipulation [S1].
- GM crops can be made tolerant to abiotic stresses, resistant to pests, and enhanced for nutritional value [S1].
- Bt toxin is produced by a bacterium called Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) and has been expressed in plants to provide resistance to insects [S1].
- Recombinant DNA technological processes enable mass production of safe and more effective therapeutic drugs [S2].
- At present, about 30 recombinant therapeutics have been approved for human-use the world over [S2].
- Whole-genome sequences and new high-throughput technologies allow researchers to approach questions systematically and on a much broader scale [S3].
