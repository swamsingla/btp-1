# Mean, Median and Mode

When you look at a set of numbers, it can be hard to tell what the group is like just by scanning them. For example, if you know the test scores of your classmates, you want to find a single number that tells you how the class did overall. This single number is called a representative value or a measure of central tendency, and it helps us understand the centre of the data [S1].

## The Mean

The mean is the most common measure of central tendency that you will use [S1]. You can think of the mean as the fair-share average [S1]. If you have a pile of items and you want to share them equally among a group of people, the mean tells you how many items each person gets.

To find the mean, you add up all the numbers in your data set. Then, you divide that total by how many numbers there are. This is the same as the arithmetic mean [S1].

For example, if you have the numbers $2$, $4$, and $6$, you add them to get $2 + 4 + 6 = 12$. Since there are $3$ numbers, you divide $12$ by $3$ to get a mean of $4$.

## The Median

The median is another way to find the centre of your data [S1]. To find the median, you must first sort your numbers from smallest to largest [S1]. Once they are in order, the median is the exact middle number in the list [S1].

If your data has an odd number of values, finding the median is straightforward [S1]. For example, in the sorted list $5$, $8$, $12$, the median is $8$.

If your data has an even number of values, there is no single middle number [S1]. In this case, you find the two middle numbers and calculate their average. For example, in the sorted list $3$, $7$, $10$, $15$, the two middle numbers are $7$ and $10$. You add them to get $17$, and then divide by $2$ to get a median of $8.5$.

## The Mode

The mode is the number that appears most often in a data set [S1]. A data set can have one mode, or it can have more than one mode if several numbers appear equally often [S1].

For example, in the list $4$, $5$, $5$, $8$, $8$, $8$, the number $8$ appears three times, which is more than any other number. Therefore, the mode is $8$ [S1].

If a data set has no number that repeats, then it has no mode [S1].

## Worked example

Let us look at the ages of some students in a class: $11$, $12$, $12$, $13$, $14$. 

To find the mean, we add the ages together: $11 + 12 + 12 + 13 + 14 = 62$. There are $5$ students, so we divide $62$ by $5$ to get a mean of $12.4$ years [S1].

To find the median, we look at the sorted list and find the middle value [S1]. The middle number is $12$, so the median is $12$ years [S1].

To find the mode, we look for the number that appears most often [S1]. The number $12$ appears twice, while all the other numbers appear only once. Therefore, the mode is $12$ years [S1].

## Key points

- The mean is found by adding all values and dividing by the number of values [S1].
- The median is the middle value of a sorted data set, or the average of the two middle values if there is an even number of values [S1].
- The mode is the value that appears most frequently in the data [S1].
- These three measures help us understand the central tendency, or the representative value, of a data set [S1].
- If you have a data set with no repeating values, it has no mode [S1].
