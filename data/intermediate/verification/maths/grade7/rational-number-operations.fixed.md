# Operations on Rational Numbers

You have already worked with integers in Grade 6. Now in Grade 7, you will expand your skills to rational numbers. [S1] A rational number is any number that can be written as a fraction $\frac{p}{q}$ where $q$ is not zero. This includes fractions, decimals, and negative numbers. [S1] Learning to add, subtract, multiply, and divide these numbers is essential. [S1] It helps you solve everyday problems, like calculating discounts, dividing money, or measuring ingredients.

## Adding and Subtracting Rational Numbers

Adding and subtracting rational numbers follows the same logic as adding and subtracting integers. [S1] The key is to find a common denominator or align the decimal points. [S1] When the numbers have the same sign, you add their absolute values and keep the sign. [S1] When they have different signs, you subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger one and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

Consider the example of adding $\frac{3}{4}$ and $-\frac{1}{2}$. [S1] First, find a common denominator, which is 4. [S1] Convert $-\frac{1}{2}$ to $-\frac{2}{4}$. [S1] Now add the numerators: $3 + (-2) = 1$. [S1] The result is $\frac{1}{4}$. [S1] This rule applies whether you are working with fractions or decimals.

## Multiplying Rational Numbers

Multiplying rational numbers is straightforward. [S1] You multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. [S1] The sign of the product depends on the signs of the numbers. [S1] A positive number multiplied by a positive number is positive. [S1] A negative number multiplied by a negative number is also positive. [S1] However, a positive number multiplied by a negative number is negative.

Let us look at an example. [S1] Multiply $\frac{2}{5}$ by $-\frac{3}{4}$. [S1] Multiply the numerators: $2 \times (-3) = -6$. [S1] Multiply the denominators: $5 \times 4 = 20$. [S1] The product is $-\frac{6}{20}$, which simplifies to $-\frac{3}{10}$. [S1] This rule holds true for all rational numbers, including decimals.

## Dividing Rational Numbers

Division of rational numbers is the inverse of multiplication. [S1] To divide by a rational number, you multiply by its reciprocal. [S1] The reciprocal of a fraction is obtained by swapping its numerator and denominator. [S1] For example, the reciprocal of $\frac{3}{4}$ is $\frac{4}{3}$.

Consider the example of dividing $-\frac{7}{8}$ by $\frac{2}{3}$. [S1] First, find the reciprocal of the divisor, which is $\frac{3}{2}$. [S1] Now multiply $-\frac{7}{8}$ by $\frac{3}{2}$. [S1] Multiply the numerators: $-7 \times 3 = -21$. [S1] Multiply the denominators: $8 \times 2 = 16$. [S1] The result is $-\frac{21}{16}$. [S1] This method works for all rational numbers.

## Properties of Operations

Rational numbers follow several important properties that make calculations easier. [S1] The commutative property states that the order of numbers does not change the result. [S1] For addition, $a + b = b + a$. [S1] For multiplication, $a \times b = b \times a$. [S1] The associative property allows you to group numbers differently without changing the result. [S1] For addition, $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$. [S1] For multiplication, $(a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c)$.

The distributive property connects multiplication and addition. [S1] It states that $a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$. [S1] This property is useful for simplifying expressions and solving equations. [S1] For example, $2 \times (3 + 4) = 2 \times 3 + 2 \times 4 = 6 + 8 = 14$.

## Worked example

Let us apply the rules for adding and subtracting rational numbers to a practical situation. [S1] Suppose you have $\frac{5}{6}$ of a litre of juice. [S1] You drink $\frac{1}{3}$ of a litre, and then you pour out $\frac{1}{4}$ of a litre for a friend. [S1] How much juice is left?

First, subtract the amount you drank from the total. [S1] To subtract $\frac{1}{3}$ from $\frac{5}{6}$, find a common denominator, which is 6. [S1] Convert $\frac{1}{3}$ to $\frac{2}{6}$. [S1] Now subtract: $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$.

Next, subtract the amount you poured out. [S1] You now have $\frac{1}{2}$ litre, and you pour out $\frac{1}{4}$ litre. [S1] The common denominator is 4. [S1] Convert $\frac{1}{2}$ to $\frac{2}{4}$. [S1] Subtract: $\frac{2}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$.

Therefore, you have $\frac{1}{4}$ of a litre of juice left.

## Key points

- [S1] A rational number is any number that can be written as a fraction $\frac{p}{q}$, where $q$ is not zero.
- [S1] To add or subtract rational numbers, find a common denominator or align decimal points.
- [S1] To multiply rational numbers, multiply the numerators and the denominators.
- [S1] To divide rational numbers, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
- [S1] The commutative, associative, and distributive properties apply to rational numbers.
