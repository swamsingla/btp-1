
# Angle Sum Property of a Triangle

The interior angles of a triangle are the angles inside the shape, formed by its three straight sides. The angle sum property of a triangle states that the sum of these three interior angles always equals $180^{\circ}$ [S1]. This property is very useful because it allows you to find the size of an unknown angle if you already know the other two angles in a triangle [S1].

## How to Use the Property

To find a missing angle in a triangle, you simply subtract the sum of the known angles from $180^{\circ}$. For example, if a triangle has two known angles of $50^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$, you first add them together to get $110^{\circ}$ [S1]. Then, you subtract this sum from $180^{\circ}$ to find the third angle [S1].

## Why the Sum is Always $180^{\circ}$

You can understand why the angles add up to $180^{\circ}$ by looking at a straight line. A straight line is exactly $180^{\circ}$. If you draw a triangle and extend one of its sides to make a straight line, the angle outside the triangle will be a straight angle [S1]. The interior angle next to this straight line will add up with it to make $180^{\circ}$ [S1]. Since the other two interior angles of the triangle are also part of this straight line, all three angles of the triangle must add up to $180^{\circ}$ [S1].

## Worked example

Let us find the missing angle in a triangle where two of the interior angles are $45^{\circ}$ and $70^{\circ}$ [S1]. 

First, add the known angles together [S1].
$$ 45^{\circ} + 70^{\circ} = 115^{\circ} $$

Next, subtract this sum from $180^{\circ}$ to find the missing angle [S1].
$$ 180^{\circ} - 115^{\circ} = 65^{\circ} $$

Therefore, the missing angle in the triangle is $65^{\circ}$ [S1].

## Key points

*   The sum of the interior angles of any triangle is always $180^{\circ}$ [S1].
*   To find a missing angle, subtract the sum of the known angles from $180^{\circ}$ [S1].
*   This rule applies to all triangles, no matter what their shapes or sizes are [S1].
