
# Magnets and Magnetism

Magnets are objects that can pull or push certain materials without actually touching them. This special power is called magnetism, and it allows magnets to pick up specific metal objects from a distance. Understanding magnetism helps us use everyday tools like the compass, and it explains why some metals stick to magnets while others do not.

## Natural and Artificial Magnets

There are two main types of magnets. Natural magnets are found in nature. They are made from a special rock called magnetite. Artificial magnets are made by humans in factories. They are usually made from metals like iron or steel. Artificial magnets are often stronger than natural magnets [S1].

## Poles and Attraction

Every magnet has two ends called poles. One end is the North pole, and the other is the South pole. You can tell which is which because the North pole is sometimes marked with a white dot. Sometimes, the North pole is painted red and the South pole is painted blue [S1]. When you bring two magnets close together, their poles interact. If you bring the North pole of one magnet near the South pole of another, they will pull together. This pulling force is called attraction [S1].

## Repulsion

If you bring two magnets close together and their poles are the same, they will push each other away. This pushing force is called repulsion [S1]. For example, if you bring two cars that have magnets in them so that the like poles face each other, the cars will run away from each other. You can test this by placing two magnets on a table so that their North poles face each other. Push one slowly towards the other and you will feel it being pushed back, and the second magnet will slide away before the two ever touch.

## The Magnetic Compass

A magnetic compass is a tool that uses a small magnet to show directions. The magnet inside the compass is free to spin. It always points toward the North. This happens because the Earth itself acts like a giant magnet. The compass helps people find their way when they are traveling.

## How to Keep Magnets Safe

You must treat magnets with care. Do not heat them or drop them or hammer them [S1]. Do not keep them near mobile phones or remote controls [S1]. To keep them safe, you should store them in pairs. When you store them, put the unlike poles on the same side. Keep a piece of wood in between the two magnets. You can also place two pieces of soft iron across the ends.

## Worked Example

Imagine you have two bar magnets. You decide to test their poles. You hold one magnet and bring its North pole close to the North pole of the other magnet. The magnets push away from each other. This is repulsion. Now, you flip the second magnet around so that its South pole faces the first magnet's North pole. The two magnets pull together. This is attraction.

## Key points

*   Magnets can move some objects without touching them.
*   Every magnet has two poles: a North pole and a South pole.
*   Attraction happens when unlike poles are brought close together.
*   Repulsion happens when like poles are brought close together.
*   You must keep magnets away from mobile phones and remote controls.
