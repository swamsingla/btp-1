
# Measurement of Mass

Mass tells us how much matter is in an object. It is a basic property of everything around us, from a tiny grain of sand to a large elephant. We measure mass to know exactly how much matter an object contains. In physics, we use a special tool called a beam balance to find out the mass of different objects.

## Using a Beam Balance

A beam balance is a traditional tool used to compare the mass of two objects. It has a horizontal beam that is suspended in the middle by a hook. The beam has two pans hanging from either end. To measure the mass of an object, you place it in one pan and known standard weights in the other pan. You keep adding or removing standard weights until the beam is perfectly level. When the beam is level, the object has the same mass as the standard weights. The mass of the object is then equal to the sum of the standard weights in its pan.

## Units of Mass: Gram and Kilogram

Mass is measured in standard units. The two main units used for measuring mass are the gram and the kilogram. A gram is used for measuring the mass of very light objects, such as a paperclip or a small piece of chalk. A kilogram is used for measuring the mass of heavier objects, such as a bag of sugar or a textbook.

The kilogram is the standard unit of mass in the International System of Units, which is known as SI [S1]. It is abbreviated as kg. When you write the mass, always leave a space between the number and the unit. For example, if you have the mass of 7 kilograms, it is written as 7 kg and not as 7 kgs [S1].

## SI Units

The International System of Units, or SI, was agreed between countries so that people everywhere use the same standard measurements [S2]. Before it existed, different regions used units of their own, and a quantity measured in one place could not be compared with the same quantity measured in another. Within SI every physical quantity has one agreed base unit, which is what makes results from a laboratory in one country usable in another.

## Worked Example

Imagine you want to find the mass of a small toy car using a beam balance. You place the toy car in the left pan. In the right pan, you place a 100 g weight and a 50 g weight. The beam is perfectly level. To find the total mass of the toy car, you add the masses of the standard weights together. The total mass of the toy car is $100 \text{ g} + 50 \text{ g} = 150 \text{ g}$.

## Key points

- Mass is the quantity of matter in an object.
- A beam balance is used to compare the mass of two objects.
- The SI unit of mass is the kilogram (kg) [S1, S2].
- The units to measure mass are gram (g) and kilogram (kg) [S1].
- Always leave a space between the number and the unit, such as 7 kg [S1].
- The International System of Units, or SI units, is the standard system of units used worldwide [S2].
