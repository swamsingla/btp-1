# Pressure

Pressure is a measure of how much force is applied to a specific area. It is defined as the force per unit area acting on an object [S1]. Understanding pressure helps us explain many everyday phenomena, from why a sharp knife cuts easily to why heavy vehicles have wide tyres.

## Thrust and Pressure

When a force acts on an object perpendicular to its surface, it is called thrust [S1]. The effect of thrust depends on the area over which it acts. The thrust on unit area is called pressure. The formula for pressure is given by equation (9.20):

$$
\text{Pressure} = \frac {\text{thrust}}{\text{area}}
$$

The SI unit of pressure is newton per square metre ($\text{N m}^{-2}$), which is also called a pascal (Pa) [S1].

## Everyday Applications

The concept of pressure explains many everyday situations. For example, a camel can run easily in the desert because its wide feet exert less pressure on the sand [S1]. An army tank rests upon a continuous chain to distribute its weight over a larger area, reducing the pressure on the ground [S1]. Trucks and motorbuses have much wider tyres for the same reason [S1]. Cutting tools have sharp edges because a smaller area results in a larger pressure for the same force [S1].

## Worked Example

A wooden block has a mass of 5 kg and dimensions of 40 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm. The weight of the block is $5 \, \text{kg} \times 9.8 \, \text{ms}^{-2} = 49 \, \text{N}$ [S1].

If the block is placed on a table with its sides of dimensions 20 cm × 10 cm, the area is $20 \, \text{cm} \times 10 \, \text{cm} = 200 \, \text{cm}^2 = 0.02 \, \text{m}^2$. The pressure exerted is $\frac{49 \, \text{N}}{0.02 \, \text{m}^2} = 2450 \, \text{Nm}^{-2}$ [S1].

If the block is placed on its side of dimensions 40 cm × 20 cm, the area is $40 \, \text{cm} \times 20 \, \text{cm} = 800 \, \text{cm}^2 = 0.08 \, \text{m}^2$. The pressure exerted is $\frac{49 \, \text{N}}{0.08 \, \text{m}^2} = 612.5 \, \text{Nm}^{-2}$ [S1].

## Pressure in Fluids

All liquids and gases are fluids [S2]. A solid exerts pressure on a surface due to its weight [S2]. Similarly, fluids have weight, and they also exert pressure on the base and walls of the container in which they are enclosed [S2]. Pressure exerted in any confined mass of fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions [S2].
