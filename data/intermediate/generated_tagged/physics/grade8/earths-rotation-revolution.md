# Earth's Rotation and Revolution

Our planet is never still. It spins on its own axis while simultaneously tracing a vast path around the Sun. These two fundamental motions are the direct cause of our daily day-night cycle and the yearly cycle of the seasons. Without these movements, our world would lack the predictable rhythms that shape our weather and our daily lives.

## The Day-Night Cycle

Rotation is the spinning of an object on its own axis [S2]. The Earth rotates on an imaginary line passing through its geographic North Pole and its South Pole. This movement is much like a spinning top or a fan [S2]. The Earth completes one full rotation in about 24 hours [S2].

When the Earth turns, different parts of its surface face the Sun. The side facing the Sun experiences daylight. The side facing away from the Sun experiences night [S2]. This rotation is why the Sun appears to rise in the East and set in the West. From above the North Pole, the Earth rotates in an anti-clockwise direction, moving from West to East [S2].

Imagine you are riding a merry-go-round at a park. If you turn in an anti-clockwise direction, the trees around you appear to turn in the opposite, or clockwise, direction [S2]. The Sun appears to move across the sky for the same reason. The Earth is actually turning, and the Sun only appears to move from our point of view.

## The Changing View of the Night Sky

As the Earth also revolves around the Sun continuously, the stars seen in the night sky after sunset gradually change over a year as we look in different directions [S4]. You can notice this change by looking at the pattern of stars at a fixed time of the night on days separated by a month [S4].

## Revolution and the Seasons

While rotating on its own axis, the Earth also revolves around the Sun [S1, S2]. Revolution is the motion of an object around another object [S1]. The path an object takes while revolving around another object is called its orbit [S1]. If viewed from the top, the orbit of the Earth around the Sun is nearly circular [S1]. The Earth completes one revolution around the Sun in about 365 days and 6 hours [S1].

The Earth's axis of rotation is not upright with respect to its orbit, but is tilted [S3]. The Earth maintains this tilt as it orbits around the Sun [S3]. The tilt of the Earth's axis and the spherical shape of the Earth gives rise to seasons [S3].

When the Northern Hemisphere tilts towards the Sun, it receives more intense sunlight. The sunrays are spread over a smaller area [S3]. The Northern Hemisphere also receives sunlight for more than 12 hours, causing the summer season [S3]. In December, the situation is opposite in the Northern Hemisphere, and it experiences winter. The seasons and length of daytime are reversed in the Southern Hemisphere as compared to the Northern Hemisphere [S3].

## Solstice and Equinox

The longest day and shortest night in the Northern Hemisphere occur around 21 June. This is known as the summer solstice. After the summer solstice, the duration of a day becomes shorter [S3]. The shortest day and longest night in this Hemisphere occur around 22 December, known as the winter solstice [S3].

Around 21 March and 23 September, the daytime lasts for exactly 12 hours [S3]. In the Northern Hemisphere, these days are called the spring and the autumn equinox, respectively [S3].

## Worked example

Let us apply the rule for the day-night cycle to a simple scenario. The Earth is a sphere and the Sun lights it from one side, so at any moment one half of the Earth is in sunlight and the other half is in shadow. The half in sunlight experiences daylight and the half in shadow experiences night [S2].

If you are standing on the side of the Earth facing the Sun, it is daytime for you. After 12 hours, the Earth will have rotated halfway. You will now be on the side facing away from the Sun, and it will be nighttime. This shows that the Earth's rotation is the direct cause of our 24-hour day and night cycle.

## Key points

* Rotation is the spinning of the Earth on its axis, which takes about 24 hours and causes the day-night cycle [S2].
* Revolution is the motion of the Earth around the Sun, which takes about 365 days and 6 hours and causes the changing seasons [S1, S3].
* The Earth's axis is tilted, which causes different parts of the Earth to receive varying amounts of sunlight throughout the year [S3].
* The summer solstice occurs around 21 June with the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere [S3].
* The winter solstice occurs around 22 December with the shortest day in the Northern Hemisphere [S3].
* The equinox occurs around 21 March and 23 September, when the daytime lasts for 12 hours [S3].
