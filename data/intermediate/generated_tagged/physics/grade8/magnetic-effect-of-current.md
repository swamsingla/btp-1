# Magnetic Effect of Electric Current

The magnetic effect of electric current is the phenomenon where an electric current flowing through a conductor produces a magnetic field around it [S2]. We can detect this magnetic field using a magnetic compass, which acts as a tiny magnet [S1]. When the current flows, the compass needle deflects, proving that electricity and magnetism are deeply connected. The effect has practical uses in many devices, such as electric bells, motors, fans, loudspeakers, and electromagnets [S3].

## Oersted's Discovery

In 1820, Hans Christian Oersted, a professor at a university in Denmark, was giving a demonstration. He noticed that whenever he closed or opened an electrical circuit, the needle of a nearby magnetic compass would deflect [S3]. This meant that the electric current in the wire was creating a magnetic field that affected the compass. Oersted investigated this further and published his findings. His work proved that electricity and magnetism are linked, and it inspired other scientists to explore this connection [S3].

## The Magnetic Field of a Straight Conductor

When an electric current flows through a straight wire, it creates a magnetic field around the wire [S2]. You can observe this effect by setting up a simple circuit with a cell, a switch, and a magnetic compass placed beneath the wire [S1]. When you turn the switch to the 'ON' position, the current flows through the wire. The compass needle will deflect from its original direction. When you turn the switch to the 'OFF' position, the current stops, and the magnetic field disappears. The compass needle then returns to its original direction [S1]. This experiment clearly shows that the region around a current-carrying wire has a magnetic field [S2].

## Electromagnets

An electromagnet is a type of magnet where the magnetic field is produced by an electric current [S6]. You can make a simple electromagnet by wrapping an insulated wire around an iron nail and connecting the ends of the wire to a cell. When the current flows through the coil, it behaves like a magnet and can attract small iron objects, such as paper clips [S4]. When you disconnect the wire from the cell, the current stops, and the electromagnet loses its magnetic effect [S4].

If you insert the iron nail into the coil, the electromagnet becomes much stronger. Most electromagnets used in practice have an iron core to make them stronger [S6]. An electromagnet also has two poles, a North pole and a South pole, just like a regular bar magnet [S7]. You can find the poles by bringing a magnetic compass near the ends of the coil while the current is flowing.

The strength of an electromagnet can be changed by altering the amount of electric current or the number of turns of the coil [S8]. A battery with more cells provides a larger current, which creates a stronger magnetic field. Increasing the number of turns of the wire around the core also makes the electromagnet stronger. You can even reverse the poles of an electromagnet by changing the direction of the current [S8].

## Lifting Electromagnets

Lifting electromagnets are strong electromagnets that may be hung from cranes. The crane operator controls the magnet by switching the current ON and OFF [S9]. When the current is ON, the electromagnet lifts iron or steel objects. When the current is switched OFF, the magnetic field disappears and the objects are released [S9]. Lifting electromagnets are widely used in factories and scrap yards to move, lift and sort heavy metal items [S9].

## Worked Example

Let us see how the strength of an electromagnet changes. Suppose you wind a wire into a coil around an iron nail and connect it to one cell. A single cell gives only a small current, so the magnetic field is weak and the coil attracts only a few paper clips [S8]. Now connect the same coil to a battery with more cells. The current is larger, the magnetic field is stronger, and the coil attracts more clips [S8]. If you also wind more turns of wire around the nail, the coil becomes an even stronger magnet [S8]. Finally, if you disconnect the wire from the cell, the clips no longer cling to the nail [S4].

## Key points

- An electric current produces a magnetic field around the conductor [S2].
- The magnetic field can be detected with a magnetic compass, which deflects when current flows; Hans Christian Oersted discovered this link between electricity and magnetism in 1820 [S1, S3].
- An electromagnet is a coil of wire that acts as a magnet when current flows through it [S6].
- The strength of an electromagnet can be increased by using more cells or more turns of wire [S8].
- An electromagnet has two poles, North and South, which can be reversed by changing the direction of the current [S7].
