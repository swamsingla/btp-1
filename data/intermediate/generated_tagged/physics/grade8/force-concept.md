# Force — Concept

A force is simply a push or a pull. It is something we experience constantly in our daily lives, often without even realizing it [S2]. Whenever you open a door, throw a ball, or ride a bicycle, you are using force. Forces are not just about making things move; they are about interactions between objects. For a force to exist, at least two objects must interact with each other. One object applies the force, and the other object experiences it [S4]. The standard unit used to measure this push or pull is the newton, which is represented by the symbol N. Understanding how forces work helps us make sense of almost everything that happens around us, from a falling leaf to a rocket launching into space.

## Contact Forces

In many everyday situations, applying a force requires physical touch. This type of interaction is known as a contact force. Physical contact can be direct, like using your hands or feet, or indirect, such as using a stick or a rope to move something [S3]. 

A very common example of a contact force is muscular force. Whenever you perform a physical activity, such as walking, running, lifting, pushing, jumping, or stretching, the force is generated by the action of muscles in your body. This resulting force is known as muscular force [S3]. Muscles contract and elongate while you are doing any activity, and this action allows you to exert a push or a pull on objects [S3].

Living beings rely heavily on muscular force for movement and survival. Animals, birds, fish, and insects all use muscular forces for movement and survival [S3]. For a long time, humans have also used the muscular force of some animals to carry out many tasks [S3].

## Non-Contact Forces

Not all forces require physical contact. There are forces whose effects can be experienced even if the objects are not touching. These are called non-contact forces [S1]. 

A great example of a non-contact force is the magnetic force. If you remember learning about magnets, you know that a magnet attracts objects made of magnetic materials. When you bring two magnets close to each other, like poles (North–North, South–South) will push each other away, while unlike poles (North–South) will pull each other closer. This attraction and repulsion between objects is also a form of push and pull, which means it is a force [S1]. A magnet can exert this force on another magnet or a magnetic material without being in physical contact with it. This is a perfect example of a non-contact force at work [S1].

## Effects of a Force

When a force is applied to an object, it can cause a variety of changes or effects. A force can change the state of motion of an object. It can make a stationary object start moving, make a moving object go faster or slower, stop it, or change the direction in which it is moving. For example, when you kick a football, the force from your foot changes the ball's state from rest to motion. 

A force can also change the shape of an object. If you press a soft clay ball, you are applying a force that changes its shape. 

It is important to remember that forces are always interactions between at least two objects. When you push a table, your hand is one object applying force on another object—the table. Here, we say that your hand and the table are two objects interacting with each other [S4]. At least two objects must interact for a force to come into play [S4].

## Worked example

Imagine you are playing with a pair of bar magnets. You have one magnet sitting on a table. You take your second magnet and slowly bring its South pole close to the North pole of the first one, without letting them touch. You notice that the first magnet starts sliding towards the second one. This is because the second magnet is exerting a non-contact magnetic force on the first magnet, pulling it closer [S1].

Now, imagine you place your hand on a heavy wooden door and push it open. Your hand is in direct physical contact with the door, so you are applying a contact force, specifically a muscular force generated by your arm muscles [S3, S4]. The door then moves out of its resting state and starts moving.

## Key points

- A force is a push or pull on an object resulting from the object's interaction with another object [S4].
- Forces result only when two or more objects interact with each other [S4].
- The SI unit of force is the newton (N) [S4].
- Contact forces require physical contact between objects, and muscular force is a common example [S3].
- Non-contact forces, like magnetic force, can act on objects without physical contact [S1].
- A force can change the state of motion of an object (start, stop, speed up, slow down or change direction) and can change its shape.
