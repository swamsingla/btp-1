# Pressure in Fluids

You already know that a heavy solid object pushes down on a table because of its weight. Fluids are different from solids, but they also have weight. When a fluid is kept in a container, it pushes on the bottom and the sides of that container. The force that this push applies on each unit of area of the surface is called pressure. Understanding how fluids push is important for many everyday things, like how a water pump works or why a deep-sea submarine needs a very strong hull.

## What Are Fluids?

A fluid is any substance that can flow easily. This includes both liquids and gases [S1]. Because fluids have weight, they exert pressure on the base and walls of the container in which they are enclosed [S1]. This is just like how a solid exerts pressure on a surface due to its weight [S1].

## Pressure in Liquids

Water is a fluid we see every day. When you fill a glass of water, the water pushes against the glass. The amount of pressure a liquid exerts depends on how deep it is. As you go deeper into the liquid, the pressure gets stronger. This happens because there is more liquid above you, which adds to the total weight and the pushing force.

## Pascal's Law

Pascal's law tells us an important rule about how pressure works inside a confined fluid. According to this rule, pressure exerted in any confined mass of fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions [S1]. This means that if you push on a fluid in one spot, that pressure spreads out evenly in every direction. It does not get weaker as it travels through the fluid.

## Worked Example

Let us see how this rule works in a simple situation. Imagine a closed container completely filled with water, with a tight-fitting lid that you can press down. When you press the lid, you apply pressure on the water. Because of Pascal's law, this pressure is transmitted undiminished (without becoming smaller) in all directions [S1]. So the same pressure reaches the bottom and the sides of the container, without losing any of its strength.

## Key points

*   All liquids and gases are called fluids because they can flow [S1].
*   Fluids have weight, so they exert pressure on the base and walls of their container [S1].
*   Pressure exerted in any confined mass of fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions [S1].
*   The pressure exerted by a liquid increases as the depth of the liquid increases.
*   Pressure is the force acting on each unit of area of a surface.
