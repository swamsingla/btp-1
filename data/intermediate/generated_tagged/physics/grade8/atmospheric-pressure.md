# Atmospheric Pressure

The Earth is surrounded by an envelope of air called the atmosphere [S1]. This air has weight, so it presses on everything on the ground and on our bodies. This push of the air is called atmospheric pressure. It is always acting on us, even though we do not usually notice it.

## What Atmospheric Pressure Is

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure that the air of the atmosphere exerts on a surface. Like any pressure, it is a force acting on an area: the force the air exerts on each unit of area. The atmosphere contains nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide, and other gases in small quantities [S1]. This layer of air extends up to many kilometres above the surface of the Earth [S1].

The particles that make up a gas can move around freely [S2]. The air particles keep hitting every surface that they touch, and together these hits push on the surface. Near the ground there is a great deal of air above, so the pressure there is large.

## How It Is Measured

A barometer is an instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure. By reading a barometer, we can find the pressure of the air at a certain place and time.

## Effects of Pressure in Moving Air

Air does not only press on surfaces when it is still. Moving air, or wind, also exerts a force. The force exerted by wind creates wind pressure, which causes effects like the slamming of doors, the rattling of windows, and the fluttering of clothes [S3].

The same ideas of force and pressure also decide how air moves. A small difference in pressure can result in a gentle breeze, while a stronger pressure difference can lead to strong winds, and sometimes even cyclones [S2]. These are connected to powerful weather events, like storms and cyclones, that affect our daily lives, agriculture, and even our safety [S2].

## Worked Example

Suppose the air presses on a flat square board of side $10 \, \text{cm}$ with a force of $1000 \, \text{N}$. The side is $0.1 \, \text{m}$, so the area of the board is $0.1 \times 0.1 = 0.01 \, \text{m}^{2}$. Using the rule that pressure equals force divided by area:

$$
\text{Pressure} = \frac{1000 \, \text{N}}{0.01 \, \text{m}^{2}} = 100000 \, \text{N/m}^{2} = 100000 \, \text{Pa}
$$

Now take a board of twice the area, $0.02 \, \text{m}^{2}$. The air presses on the same place and in the same way, so the pressure is still $100000 \, \text{Pa}$. The force on this board is therefore larger:

$$
\text{Force} = \text{Pressure} \times \text{Area} = 100000 \, \text{N/m}^{2} \times 0.02 \, \text{m}^{2} = 2000 \, \text{N}
$$

So the air presses with twice the force on a board of twice the area, while the pressure stays the same.

## Key points

- The atmosphere is an envelope of air surrounding the Earth that contains nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide, and other gases in small quantities [S1].
- The atmosphere extends up to many kilometres above the surface of the Earth [S1].
- Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the air of the atmosphere, and a barometer measures it.
- The force exerted by wind creates wind pressure, which causes effects like slamming of doors, rattling of windows, and fluttering of clothes [S3].
- A small difference in pressure can result in a gentle breeze, while a stronger pressure difference can lead to strong winds, and sometimes even cyclones [S2].
