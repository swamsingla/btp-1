# Light — Sources and Shadows

Light is the energy that allows us to see the world around us. Without it, we would not be able to read our books, play outside, or even recognize the faces of our friends. This page explains where light comes from and how it creates shadows when it is blocked.

## Luminous and Non-Luminous Objects

Objects that emit their own light are called luminous objects. The Sun gives out or emits its own light and is the main source of natural light on the Earth. Stars, lightning, natural fire, and certain animals also emit their own light [S1]. In ancient times, humans learnt to create fire—the earliest form of artificial lighting. With time, they learnt to create fire using different fuels, such as animal fat, oil, wax, and gas [S1]. With the invention of electricity and different kinds of electric light sources, most of the lighting needs of humans are now met by electric lighting [S1]. 

Objects that do not emit their own light are called non-luminous objects. The Moon is a non-luminous object. It does not emit its own light. It just reflects the light emitted by the Sun that falls on it [S1]. 

## Materials and Light

When light travels, it can pass through different materials in different ways. You can see through some materials completely, while others block the light entirely. 

Materials that let almost all the light pass through them are called transparent materials. Materials that let some light pass through, but scatter it so you cannot see clearly, are called translucent materials. Materials that do not let any light pass through them at all are called opaque materials [S3]. 

## How Shadows Form

A shadow is formed when an opaque object blocks the path of light. Light travels in a straight line, so when an opaque object comes between a light source and a surface like a wall, the area behind the object receives no light. This dark area is the shadow. 

If you hold a transparent object, like a glass window, in the path of light, you will not see a shadow because the light passes through it. If you hold a translucent object, like tracing paper, in the path of light, you will see a faint shadow because only some of the light passes through. 

## Worked Example

Imagine you are standing in a dark room holding a torch. The light from the torch travels in a straight line and hits a wall, making a bright spot on the wall. If you place a piece of cardboard in front of the torch, the cardboard is an opaque object. The light cannot pass through the cardboard, so it hits the wall behind the cardboard and creates a dark shape. This dark shape is the shadow of the cardboard. If you replace the cardboard with a glass window, the light will pass right through the glass, and no shadow will appear on the wall.

## Key points

- Luminous objects emit their own light, while non-luminous objects do not.
- The Sun is the main natural source of light on Earth, and electric lights are the main artificial sources.
- The Moon is a non-luminous object that only reflects sunlight.
- Light passes completely through transparent materials, partially through translucent materials, and not at all through opaque materials.
- Shadows are created when an opaque object blocks light from reaching a surface.
