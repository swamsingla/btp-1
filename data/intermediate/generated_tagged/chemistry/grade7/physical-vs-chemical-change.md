# Physical and Chemical Changes

Everything around us is constantly changing. [S1] A piece of ice melting into water, a piece of paper burning into ash, or even a glass of milk turning into curd are all examples of change. [S3] Understanding how and why these changes happen is the foundation of chemistry. By learning to tell the difference between a physical and a chemical change, you can predict what will happen when you mix different substances or apply heat.

## What is a Physical Change?

A physical change is a change in the form or state of a substance without changing its chemical identity. [S1] When a physical change occurs, the substance can usually be brought back to its original form. For example, when ice melts into water, it is still H₂O. If you put that water in a freezer, it will turn back into ice. Other examples include melting wax, boiling water, or chopping a vegetable into smaller pieces.

## What is a Chemical Change?

A chemical change happens when a substance reacts with another substance or with heat, and a new substance is formed. [S2] In a chemical change, the original substance cannot be easily brought back. For example, when you burn a piece of wood, it turns into ash and smoke. You cannot turn that ash back into wood. Another common example is the rusting of iron, where iron reacts with oxygen in the air to form iron oxide.

## Reversible and Irreversible Changes

Changes around us can be grouped into two main categories: reversible and irreversible. [S2] A reversible change is one where the original substance can be recovered after the change. Melting ice and boiling water are reversible changes because you can freeze the water back into ice or condense the steam back into liquid water. 

An irreversible change is one where the original substance cannot be recovered. [S2] Chopping vegetables or making popcorn from corn are irreversible because the original size, shape, or state of the material cannot be restored.

## Indicators of a Chemical Change

How can you tell if a chemical change has happened? [S1] There are several clear indicators. The formation of a gas, a change in colour, or the release of heat and light are strong signs. For example, when a candle burns, the wax melts and evaporates, which is a physical change. [S1] However, the vapour of wax burning to produce a flame is a chemical change. [S1] Another indicator is the formation of a precipitate, which is a solid that forms when two liquids are mixed.

## Worked Example

Consider the process of lighting a candle. [S1] When you light a candle, the wax melts due to the heat of the flame. [S1] This melting of wax and its subsequent solidification are physical changes. [S1] The wax is also carried up the wick and evaporates, which is another physical change. [S1] However, the vapour of the wax burning to produce a flame is a chemical change because a new substance is formed. [S1] This shows that the burning of a candle involves both physical and chemical changes.

## Key points

*   A physical change alters the form or state of a substance, but the chemical identity remains the same.
*   A chemical change results in the formation of a new substance with different chemical properties.
*   Reversible changes can be undone to get back the original substance.
*   Irreversible changes cannot be undone to recover the original substance.
*   Indicators of a chemical change include the formation of a gas, a change in colour, or the release of heat and light.
