# Pure Substances

When you buy milk or spices in the market, the word "pure" is used to mean unadulterated, free from illegal additions. In chemistry, however, a pure substance is defined by its composition: it contains only one kind of matter and cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process [S2]. Whether it is a solid, a liquid, or a gas, a pure substance will always have the same characteristic properties. This matters because it allows us to distinguish between simple materials and complex blends, which is the first step in understanding the chemistry of the world around us.

## Elements

Elements are one of the two main types of pure substances. They are substances that cannot be further broken down into simpler substances by any chemical or physical process [S1]. Each element is made up of identical particles, and these particles are different from the particles of any other element [S8]. Because the particles are identical, the properties of an element are fixed and consistent. For example, if you have a piece of pure gold, you cannot separate it into anything else using physical methods [S8]. Other familiar examples include sulfur, carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.

## Compounds

Compounds are the second main type of pure substance. They are formed when different elements combine chemically in fixed ratios to create something entirely new [S8]. The properties of a compound are different from those of the elements that form it. Because the elements are chemically bonded, their constituent elements cannot be separated by any physical method [S8]. For instance, water is a compound made of hydrogen and oxygen combined in a fixed ratio of $2:1$ [S8]. Similarly, sodium chloride is a compound made of sodium and chlorine in a $1:1$ ratio. If you have a sample of a pure compound, every single particle in that sample is identical to every other particle, and the composition is the same throughout [S2].

## Homogeneous Composition and Fixed Properties

A pure substance has a homogeneous composition, meaning its composition is the same throughout the entire sample [S2]. This uniformity ensures that the substance always exhibits fixed properties, such as a specific boiling point, melting point, and density. You may already know that mixtures, like soft drinks or soil, contain more than one pure substance and can have variable compositions. In contrast, a pure substance will always have the same characteristic properties, regardless of its source [S2]. This consistency is crucial because it means that a pure substance behaves predictably, which is essential for both scientific research and industrial applications.

## Worked Example

Consider a sample of pure water. Water is a compound because it is made of two different elements, hydrogen and oxygen, combined chemically in a fixed ratio [S8]. The constituent elements of a compound cannot be separated by any physical method [S8]. This means you cannot use a filter to separate the hydrogen from the oxygen, and you cannot use boiling to separate them either, because when you boil water, it turns into water vapour, which is still a compound [S8]. To separate hydrogen and oxygen, you would need to use a chemical process, such as electrolysis, which splits the water into its constituent elements. This illustrates why pure substances are defined by their inability to be separated by physical means.
