# Solutions

A solution is a special type of mixture where two or more substances blend together so perfectly that they become completely uniform at the particle level. You encounter solutions every single day, from the lemonade you drink to the air you breathe. Understanding how solutions work helps us explain why some liquids mix perfectly while others separate, and how much of a solid can actually dissolve in a liquid.

## Solute, Solvent, and Solution

Every solution has exactly two main components: the solvent and the solute. The solvent is the component that does the dissolving, and it is usually present in the larger amount. The solute is the component that gets dissolved, and it is usually present in a smaller quantity. When you combine these two, they form a single, uniform mixture.

It is important to note that a solution is not limited to just liquids. A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances [S1]. While we often think of a solution as a liquid containing a solid, liquid, or gas, we can also have solid solutions, such as alloys, and gaseous solutions, such as air [S1]. In a solution, there is homogeneity at the particle level. For example, lemonade tastes the same throughout because the particles of sugar or salt are evenly distributed in the solution [S1].

## Types of Solutions

Solutions can be categorized based on the physical state of their solute and solvent. The most common type is a solid dissolved in a liquid, such as a solution of sugar in water. In this case, sugar is the solute and water is the solvent [S1]. Another example is tincture of iodine, which is a solution of iodine in alcohol. Here, iodine is the solute and alcohol is the solvent [S1].

We can also have gas in liquid solutions, such as aerated drinks like soda water. These contain carbon dioxide as the solute and water as the solvent [S1]. Finally, air is a mixture of gas in gas. It is a homogeneous mixture of a number of gases, with oxygen (21%) and nitrogen (78%) being the two main constituents [S1].

## Concentration of a Solution

The concentration of a solution refers to the amount of solute present in a given amount of solution. Depending upon the amount of solute present in a solution, it can be called dilute, concentrated, or saturated [S2]. Dilute and concentrated are comparative terms. For instance, if you compare two glasses of lemonade, the one with less sugar is dilute as compared to the one with more sugar, which is concentrated [S2].

Concentration can be expressed in several ways. The three methods we will focus on are mass by mass percentage, mass by volume percentage, and volume by volume percentage [S2].

### Mass by Mass Percentage

This method is used when both the solute and the solvent are measured by their mass. The formula is:

$$ \text{Mass by mass percentage of a solution} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}} \times 100 $$

### Mass by Volume Percentage

This method is used when the solute is measured by its mass and the solution is measured by its volume. The formula is:

$$ \text{Mass by volume percentage of a solution} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Volume of solution}} \times 100 $$

### Volume by Volume Percentage

This method is used when both the solute and the solvent are measured by their volume. The formula is:

$$ \text{Volume by volume percentage of a solution} = \frac{\text{Volume of solute}}{\text{Volume of solution}} \times 100 $$

## Saturated and Unsaturated Solutions

When you add a solute to a solvent, it dissolves up to a certain limit. If the amount of solute contained in a solution is less than the saturation level, it is called an unsaturated solution [S2]. This means more solute can still be dissolved in the solvent at that particular temperature.

At any particular temperature, a solution that has dissolved as much solute as it is capable of dissolving is said to be a saturated solution [S2]. In other words, when no more solute can be dissolved in a solution at a given temperature, it is called a saturated solution. The amount of the solute present in the saturated solution at this temperature is called its solubility [S2].

It is important to note that different substances in a given solvent have different solubilities at the same temperature [S2]. For example, the amount of salt and sugar or barium chloride that can be dissolved in water at a given temperature is not the same.

## Properties of a Solution

A solution has several distinct properties that set it apart from other types of mixtures. First, a solution is a homogeneous mixture [S1]. The particles of a solution are smaller than 1 nm ( $10^{-9}$ metre) in diameter, so they cannot be seen by the naked eye [S1].

Second, because of the very small particle size, they do not scatter a beam of light passing through the solution. So, the path of light is not visible in a solution [S1].

Third, the solute particles cannot be separated from the mixture by the process of filtration [S1].

Finally, the solute particles do not settle down when left undisturbed. This means that a solution is stable [S1].

## Worked example

A student prepares a solution by dissolving 25 g of common salt in 225 g of water. Calculate the concentration in terms of mass by mass percentage of the solution.

First, we need to find the mass of the solution.
Mass of solute (salt) = 25 g
Mass of solvent (water) = 225 g
Mass of solution = Mass of solute + Mass of solvent = 25 g + 225 g = 250 g

Next, we use the formula for mass by mass percentage:
Mass percentage of solution = $\frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}} \times 100$
Mass percentage of solution = $\frac{25}{250} \times 100 = 10\%$

So, the concentration of the solution is 10%.

## Key points

- A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances, where the particles are evenly distributed at the particle level [S1].
- The component that dissolves the other is the solvent, and the component that is dissolved is the solute [S1].
- Solutions can be solid in liquid (sugar in water), solid in liquid (iodine in alcohol), gas in liquid (carbon dioxide in water), or gas in gas (air) [S1].
- Concentration is the amount of solute present in a given amount of solution, and can be expressed as mass by mass, mass by volume, or volume by volume percentage [S2].
- A solution that can still dissolve more solute at a given temperature is unsaturated, and one in which no more solute can be dissolved is saturated [S2].
- The solubility of different substances in a given solvent varies at the same temperature [S2].
- The particles of a solution are smaller than 1 nm, do not scatter light, cannot be separated by filtration, and do not settle down when left undisturbed [S1].
