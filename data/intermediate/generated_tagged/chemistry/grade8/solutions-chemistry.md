# Solutions

A uniform mixture, such as salt or sugar mixed with water, is called a solution [S1]. You cannot see its components separately. This page looks at how a solution is made up, how much solute a solvent can hold, and what happens to gases dissolved in water as the temperature changes.

## Solute, Solvent and Solution

Whenever a solid is mixed with a liquid to form a solution, the solid component is called the solute and the liquid component is called the solvent. The solute dissolves in the solvent to form a solution [S1]:

$$
\text{Solute} + \text{Solvent} \rightarrow \text{Solution}
$$

When a solution is formed by mixing two liquids, it is not always clear which substance is dissolving the other. In such cases, the substance present in the smaller amount is called the solute, and the one present in the larger amount is called the solvent [S1].

Gases can also form solutions, just as water can act as a solvent in liquid solutions. Air is a gaseous solution. Since nitrogen is present in the largest amount in air, it is considered the solvent, while oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide and other gases are considered solutes [S1].

You may already know that mixtures are of two types, uniform and non-uniform. Uniform mixtures are called solutions, and their components are not visible separately. In non-uniform mixtures, such as sawdust in water or sand in water, the components can be seen with the naked eye or with a magnifying device [S4].

## Concentration

The amount of solute present in a fixed quantity of solution (or solvent) is called its concentration. Depending on the amount of solute in a fixed quantity of solution, it can be called a dilute solution (less amount of solute) or a concentrated solution (more amount of solute). Dilute and concentrated are relative terms. For example, the solution made by dissolving one spoon of salt in water is dilute compared with the one made by dissolving two or more spoons of salt [S3].

## Unsaturated and Saturated Solutions

What happens if you keep adding more salt to a given amount of water? At first the salt dissolves completely and forms a solution. After a few more spoons, a stage comes when the added salt does not dissolve completely, and the undissolved salt settles at the bottom. The water can no longer dissolve any more salt, because it has reached its limit [S2].

A solution in which more solute can still be dissolved at a given temperature is called an unsaturated solution. When the solute stops dissolving and begins to settle at the bottom, the solution is called a saturated solution at that temperature [S2]. The maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a fixed quantity of the solvent is called its solubility [S3].

## Gases in Water

Many gases, including oxygen, dissolve in water. Oxygen dissolves in water only to a small extent, but even in minute quantities this dissolved oxygen sustains all aquatic life, including plants, fishes and other organisms [S4]. The mixture of a gas in water is uniform, because the gas dissolves evenly in water to form a solution [S4].

The solubility of gases generally decreases as the temperature increases. More oxygen can dissolve in cold water, which ensures sufficient oxygen for aquatic life. When water warms up, the solubility of oxygen decreases [S4].

## Worked example

Which solution is more concentrated: 2 spoons of salt in 100 mL of water, or 4 spoons of salt in 50 mL of water? Concentration is the amount of solute in a fixed quantity of solvent [S3]. Let us compare the two solutions for the same quantity, 100 mL of water. The first solution has 2 spoons of salt in 100 mL. The second has 4 spoons in 50 mL, which is the same as 8 spoons in 100 mL, because $4 \times 2 = 8$. Since $8 \div 2 = 4$, the second solution has four times as much salt for the same quantity of water, so it is the more concentrated solution [S3].

## Key points

- A uniform mixture is called a solution. When a solid dissolves in a liquid, the solid is the solute and the liquid is the solvent; when two liquids mix, the one in the smaller amount is the solute [S1].
- Air is a gaseous solution, with nitrogen as the solvent and oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide and other gases as solutes [S1].
- Concentration is the amount of solute in a fixed quantity of solution, and dilute and concentrated are relative terms [S3].
- A solution that can still dissolve more solute at a given temperature is unsaturated, and one in which the solute stops dissolving is saturated; solubility is the maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a fixed quantity of solvent [S2, S3].
- The solubility of gases generally decreases as the temperature increases, so more oxygen dissolves in cold water, which sustains aquatic life [S4].
