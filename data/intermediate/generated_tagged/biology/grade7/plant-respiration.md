# Respiration in Plants

Plants are living beings, and just like animals, they need energy to grow and carry out their life processes. To get this energy, plants perform a process called respiration. During respiration, plants break down the food they make during the day to release energy, carbon dioxide, and water. This energy is essential for their growth and development. All parts of a plant, whether they are green or non-green, carry out this process to stay alive and function properly.

## What is Respiration?

Respiration is the process by which plants break down glucose in the presence of oxygen. This breakdown releases energy, water, and carbon dioxide. The word equation for this process is:
$$
\text{Glucose + Oxygen} \longrightarrow \text{Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy}
$$
The energy produced during respiration is used by plants for their growth and development [S1]. This means that even at night or in non-green parts of the plant, respiration continues to provide the necessary energy.

## Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration

Plants usually perform aerobic respiration, which means they use oxygen to break down glucose. This process is efficient and produces a large amount of energy. However, in certain situations, such as when there is a lack of oxygen, plants can perform anaerobic respiration. Anaerobic respiration does not require oxygen and produces less energy compared to aerobic respiration.

## Fermentation

Fermentation is a type of anaerobic respiration that occurs in plants when oxygen is not available. During fermentation, glucose is broken down without the presence of oxygen, producing alcohol or lactic acid, along with a small amount of energy. This process allows plants to survive temporarily in low-oxygen conditions. For example, when seeds are soaked in water and kept in a closed environment, they may undergo fermentation if oxygen is depleted [S1].

## Compensation Point

The compensation point is a specific stage in a plant's life where the rate of photosynthesis equals the rate of respiration. At this point, the plant produces just enough oxygen through photosynthesis to meet its own needs for respiration. This balance ensures that the plant does not lose net carbon dioxide or gain net oxygen. Understanding the compensation point helps in studying how plants manage their energy and gas exchange, especially in environments with limited light [S2].

## Worked Example

Consider a plant that is kept in a dark room for 24 hours. In the absence of light, photosynthesis cannot occur, so the plant relies entirely on respiration for energy. As the plant's cells consume glucose during this period, the word equation for respiration tells us that this glucose will be broken down in the presence of oxygen, releasing carbon dioxide and water as by-products. This is why, in an experiment with soaked moong bean seeds kept in the dark, the lime water connected to the flask turns milky. The seeds respire and release carbon dioxide, which reacts with the lime water to form calcium carbonate, causing the milky appearance [S1].

## Key points
- Respiration in plants breaks down glucose using oxygen to release energy, carbon dioxide, and water.
- Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and is the primary method for energy production in plants.
- Anaerobic respiration occurs when oxygen is absent and produces less energy.
- Fermentation is a type of anaerobic respiration that happens in low-oxygen conditions.
- The compensation point is when photosynthesis and respiration rates are equal.
