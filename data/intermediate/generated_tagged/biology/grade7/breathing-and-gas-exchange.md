# Breathing and Gas Exchange

Breathing is the continuous process of inhaling and exhaling air. It is essential for life, as humans usually cannot survive more than a few minutes without it. This process supplies the body with oxygen, which is necessary to keep us alive and active.

## The Pathway of Air

The body has a specific system for breathing and respiration called the respiratory system [S2]. The pathway through which air is inhaled and exhaled involves various parts of the respiratory system assisting in the process of breathing and respiration.

The respiratory system begins with a pair of nasal openings called nostrils through which we inhale and exhale air [S2]. The inhaled air passes into a pair of small passages called the nasal passages. Tiny hairs inside the nostrils, along with mucus, help trap dust and dirt from the air we breathe in [S2]. This is why we should breathe through the nose and not through the mouth. From the nasal passages, the air reaches our lungs through the windpipe. The windpipe forms two branches, which enter the two lungs [S2].

## The Lungs and Alveoli

In the lungs, these branches further divide into smaller and finer branches that end in small balloon-like sacs called alveoli [S2]. 

Our lungs are protected by the rib cage [S2]. 

To understand how breathing actually moves the air, consider the diaphragm. When you inhale, the diaphragm muscle below your lungs contracts and moves downwards. This increases the space inside your chest, allowing the lungs to expand and draw air in. When you exhale, the diaphragm relaxes and moves back up, pushing air out.

## The Exchange of Gases

The air we breathe in contains oxygen, and the air we breathe out contains carbon dioxide. This exchange of gases is a critical part of respiration. 

In the alveoli, the actual exchange of gases takes place. The walls of the alveoli are extremely thin and are richly supplied with blood vessels [S3]. Oxygen from the inhaled air passes from the alveoli into the blood, and carbon dioxide from the blood passes into the alveoli to be exhaled [S3].

## Different Animals, Different Breathing

Different animals have different breathing mechanisms to suit their unique habitats [S3]. Animals, such as birds, elephants, lions, cows, goats, lizards, and snakes, breathe through their lungs [S3]. 

Most aquatic animals like fish have specialised structures known as gills [S3]. The exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the blood and the gases dissolved in water takes place across the gills [S3].

Amphibians, like frogs, live both on land and in water. They use different body parts for breathing at various stages of their life [S3]. For example, tadpoles breathe through gills, while adult frogs use lungs for breathing on land and skin for gas exchange when they stay in water [S3].

To see how this pathway works in a daily situation, imagine you are walking through a dusty street in your city. The tiny hairs inside your nostrils, along with mucus, help trap dust and dirt from the air we breathe in [S2]. If you breathe through your mouth instead, the dust bypasses these filters and travels straight into your windpipe and lungs [S2]. 

## Key points

- Breathing is the process of inhaling and exhaling air, and it is essential for human survival.
- The respiratory system consists of the nostrils, nasal passages, windpipe, and lungs [S2].
- The windpipe branches into smaller passages that end in balloon-like sacs called alveoli [S2].
- The diaphragm contracts and moves downwards to allow the lungs to expand during inhalation.
- The exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the blood and the gases takes place across the alveoli [S2].
- Different animals have different breathing mechanisms, such as lungs, gills, or moist skin, depending on their habitats [S3].
