# Seed Formation and Dispersal

Plants have a clever way of making sure their families keep growing in new places. After a flower is pollinated, the plant starts making seeds. The seeds carry the plant's genetic instructions to grow. To keep the parent plant from getting crowded, the seeds need to travel far away. This process of moving seeds away from the parent is called dispersal. Without this, all the new plants would grow right next to each other and compete for sunlight and water.

## What are Gametes?

To make a new plant, the plant needs to mix its genetic instructions. Just like animals, flowering plants use special reproductive cells to do this. These cells are called gametes. Each gamete carries only half of the parent's genetic material [S1]. 

When a male gamete and a female gamete join together, they form a complete new cell. This joining of the two cells is called fertilisation. Because each parent gives half of its instructions, the new cell gets a complete set. This mixing ensures that every new seed is unique [S1].

## Fertilisation and Seed Development

Flowering plants have different parts of their flowers that act as male and female gametes. The pollen grains found inside the anther are the male gametes. The ovules, found deep inside the flower, are the female gametes [S1]. 

Pollination is the process where pollen is carried to another flower by wind, insects, or animals. When the male and female gametes combine, it is called fertilisation. This forms a zygote that becomes the seed [S1]. 

As the seed develops, the fleshy part of the flower around the ovule also grows. This part develops into a fruit [S1]. The fruit protects the seed and often provides food to attract animals.

## How Seeds Disperse

Once the seed is ready, the plant needs to move it away from the parent. One common way this happens is through animals. When birds or animals eat the fruit, the seeds often pass through their digestive system and get dropped far from the original plant [S1]. 

For example, a banyan seed might be eaten by a bird, travel to another location, and then be excreted. After the rains, this seed might sprout in a crack in a wall. When the seed gets water, it uses stored nutrients to grow roots and shoots [S1].

## Germination

After a seed lands in a new place, it needs water to start growing. This process of a seed sprouting into a new plant is called germination. During germination, the tiny roots and shoots appear. The seed uses the stored food inside it to fuel this initial growth until it can make its own food [S1].

## Worked example

Imagine a plant produces a fruit with 10 seeds inside it. A bird eats the fruit and flies 5 kilometres away. The bird then drops the seeds while resting. Because the bird flew 5 kilometres, the seeds are now 5 kilometres away from the original plant. This is a great way for the plant to spread its genetic material to a new location without having to grow there itself.

## Key points

*   Flowering plants use special reproductive cells called gametes, which carry half of the parent's genetic material [S1].
*   Fertilisation is the joining of the male and female gametes to form a zygote, which becomes the seed [S1].
*   The fleshy part of the flower around the ovule develops into a fruit to protect the seed [S1].
*   Animals often help disperse seeds by eating the fruit and dropping the seeds far away from the parent plant [S1].
*   When seeds get water, they use stored nutrients to grow roots and shoots in a process called germination [S1].
