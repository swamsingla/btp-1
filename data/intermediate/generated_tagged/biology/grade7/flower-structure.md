# Flower Structure and Function

A flower is the beautiful, colourful part of a plant that helps it make seeds and fruits [S1]. It is the plant's way of having babies [S1]. Just like animals, plants need to combine parts from a male and a female to create a new plant [S1]. Understanding how a flower is built helps us understand how plants grow and spread across the world.

## The Parts of a Flower

A flower has several important parts that work together [S1]. The outermost parts are the sepals, which are usually green and protect the flower when it is a tiny bud [S1]. Inside the sepals are the petals, which are often bright and colourful to catch the attention of insects and birds [S1]. Deep inside the flower are the male and female parts [S1]. The male parts are called stamens, which produce tiny grains called pollen [S1]. The female parts are called carpels, and they contain the ovules, which are the female reproductive cells [S1].

## Reproduction and Pollination

To make a seed, the plant needs to mix the male and female parts [S1]. The pollen grains from the stamens need to reach the carpels of a flower [S1]. This transfer of pollen is called pollination [S1]. Pollen can travel on the wind or it can stick to the bodies of insects like bees and butterflies [S1]. When an insect visits a flower to drink sweet nectar, the pollen rubs off onto its legs and body [S1]. When the insect flies to another flower, it carries the pollen with it [S1].

## Self and Cross Pollination

Pollination can happen in two different ways [S1]. Self pollination happens when the pollen from a flower's own stamens lands on its own carpels [S1]. Cross pollination happens when pollen from one flower lands on the carpels of a different flower [S1]. Cross pollination is very common and it helps plants mix their genetic material [S1]. When male and female reproductive cells join together inside the flower, it is called fertilisation [S1]. This joining creates a zygote, which is the very first cell of a new plant [S1].

## From Seed to Fruit

After fertilisation, the flower starts to change [S1]. The ovule inside the carpel develops into a hard seed [S1]. The fleshy part of the flower around the ovule grows into a fruit [S1]. This fruit protects the seed [S1]. When birds or animals eat the fruit, they often travel far away from the original plant [S1]. The seeds pass through their bodies and get dropped in new places [S1]. This helps the plant spread its family across a wide area [S1].

## Worked example

Imagine a garden with a sunflower plant. The sunflower has bright yellow petals to attract a bee. The bee lands on the flower and brushes against the stamens, picking up yellow pollen. The bee then flies to a different sunflower plant in the neighbour's garden. When the bee lands on this second flower, the pollen falls off onto the carpels. This is cross pollination. After the pollen joins with the ovules, a seed forms inside the flower head. Later, the seed drops to the ground. With the help of water and nutrients from the soil, the seed grows into a new sunflower plant.

## Key points

*   Flowers have male parts (stamens) and female parts (carpels) to help plants reproduce [S1].
*   Pollination is the process of moving pollen from the stamens to the carpels, often helped by wind or insects [S1].
*   Self pollination uses a flower's own pollen, while cross pollination uses pollen from a different flower [S1].
*   Fertilisation happens when male and female reproductive cells join to form a zygote [S1].
*   The ovule becomes a seed and the fleshy part of the flower becomes a fruit to protect the seed [S1].
