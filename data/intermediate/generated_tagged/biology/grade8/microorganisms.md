# Microorganisms

Microorganisms are living organisms that are so small they cannot be seen with the naked eye [S1]. Some of these organisms are made of just one cell, while others have many cells [S1]. They are found everywhere around us, including in water, soil, air, and even inside our bodies [S1, S3]. Because they are invisible to the human eye, we need a microscope to observe them and magnify their size [S1]. To explore these tiny living things, scientists have also created a low-cost and foldable paper microscope called a foldscope [S1]. While foldscopes may not provide the same level of details like high-powered laboratory microscopes, they make the microscopic world accessible to many people [S1].

## Unicellular and Multicellular Microorganisms

Microorganisms vary greatly in their structure and complexity. Some, like bacteria and Amoeba, are unicellular, meaning they are made of just a single cell [S1]. Others, such as some types of fungi and algae, are multicellular and consist of many cells working together [S1]. Like plants and animals, microorganisms show great diversity and vary in their shape, size, and structure [S1, S3]. You may have already seen the effects of these tiny organisms in your daily life. For example, if you leave a lemon, tomato, orange, or any other food item outside for some time, you might notice a powdery or cotton-like growth on it [S3]. This happens because the food has been infected by microbes [S3].

## Where Microorganisms Live

Microorganisms can be found in almost every environment on Earth. They live in water, soil, air, and even inside our bodies [S1, S3]. You already know that many bacteria live inside our bodies, especially in our gut, where they help us digest food [S3]. Some of these organisms can even be found in extreme climatic conditions, such as hot water springs and cold snow zones, as well as at moderate temperatures [S3]. Nature is full of life, and all living beings, along with the places where they live, make up the biosphere [S2]. This includes land, water, and air, where life interacts with its surroundings to survive and grow [S2].

## Useful and Harmful Microorganisms

Microorganisms can be both helpful and harmful to us. Some are very useful and play important roles in our daily lives and the environment. For example, decomposers break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil, which helps nature work together as a system to support life [S2]. Our gut bacteria help us digest food [S3]. However, some microorganisms can be harmful. When they infect food, they cause it to rot. You might wonder why microorganisms do not infect pickles and murabbas. This is because we add many spices with salt or sugar to them, which act as preservatives [S3]. A high concentration of salt or sugar prevents these organisms from growing on the food [S3].

## Observing Microorganisms

To observe the cells of a microorganism, we need to use a microscope that magnifies their size and makes them visible [S1]. You can use a foldscope or a microscope to explore the surfaces of leaves, stems, roots, or any other part to see them [S3]. Like plants and animals, microorganisms also show great diversity in their shapes, which can be spherical, rod-shaped, or irregular [S1, S3].

## Worked example

Imagine you leave a fresh apple on your kitchen counter for a week. After a few days, you notice a fuzzy, white or green growth on its skin. This is a colony of fungi, which are a type of microorganism. Because the apple was left in the open air, airborne microbes landed on it and began to grow, causing it to rot. If you had added a lot of salt or sugar to the apple, like in a sweet murabba, the high concentration of these ingredients would have stopped the microbes from growing, keeping the fruit preserved.

## Key points

*   Microorganisms are living organisms so small they cannot be seen with the naked eye [S1].
*   Some microorganisms are unicellular (like bacteria), while others are multicellular (like some fungi) [S1].
*   They are found everywhere: in water, soil, air, on food, and inside our bodies [S1, S3].
*   Microscopes or foldscopes are needed to observe and magnify microorganisms [S1, S3].
*   Some microorganisms are useful (like gut bacteria for digestion), while others are harmful and cause food to rot [S2, S3].
*   Salt and sugar act as preservatives that prevent microorganisms from growing on food [S3].
*   Microorganisms can survive in extreme conditions like hot springs and cold snow zones [S3].
