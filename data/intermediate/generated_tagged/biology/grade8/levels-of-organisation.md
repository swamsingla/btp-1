# Levels of Organisation

The body of a living organism is organised in a complex way [S1]. A single living thing is made up of a very large number of cells that must work together to keep the organism alive. To understand how this happens, scientists group the parts of a body into levels, each one built from the one before. These levels of organisation help us understand how simple building blocks like cells come together to form a complex living being [S1]. The levels are: Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism [S1].

## The Cell

You already know that the cell is the basic unit of life, just like a brick is the basic unit of a wall [S1]. Animals, including humans, and plants are made up of many cells, so they are called multicellular organisms [S1, S2]. Some microorganisms, such as bacteria and protozoa, are made up of just one cell and are called unicellular organisms [S2].

The life of a complex living organism begins with a single cell, the 'egg'. The egg of any organism has an amazing ability to divide repeatedly to form a complete living being made up of many cells [S1]. In a multicellular organism, cells carry out specialised functions individually but also cooperate with each other to increase the chance of survival [S2].

## From Cells to Tissues

A group of similar cells forms a type of tissue [S1]. To picture this, imagine a wall built entirely from bricks of the same size and shape. In the same way, a tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific job.

The unique shape, size, and structure of cells help them carry out their specific functions [S3]. For example, the thin, flat inner cheek cells form a protective lining on the inner surface of the cheek [S3]. A group of muscle cells in the food pipe contract and relax in a wave-like manner, pushing food down to the stomach [S3]. In plants too, cells show variation, and some plant cells form long tubes that help carry water throughout the plant [S3].

## From Tissues to Organs

Different tissues are organised to form an organ [S1]. An organ is made up of two or more different kinds of tissues working together. Just as a single brick cannot build a house, a single tissue cannot do all the complex tasks of the body, so the body combines tissues into organs.

The stomach is an example. Muscle cells in the stomach wall help churn the food, while other cells in the inner lining of the stomach produce digestive juices and acid that help break down the food [S3]. All these cells work together to make digestion possible [S3].

## From Organs to Organ Systems

Several organs work together to form an organ system that performs a major function of the body [S1]. The digestive system is an example. It includes organs such as the food pipe, the stomach, and the intestines. The food pipe uses muscle cells to push food down, and the stomach uses muscle cells to churn the food and other cells to produce acid [S3]. The intestines, which absorb nutrients from the food, are also made of tissues. Working together, these organs break down food and absorb nutrients, which is the major function of the digestive system.

## From Organ Systems to Organism

All the organ systems together make up a complete organism, like a plant or an animal [S1]. The organ systems depend on one another. For example, the digestive system provides nutrients, the respiratory system provides oxygen, and the circulatory system carries both to every cell of the body.

Animals, including humans, and plants are all examples of multicellular organisms [S1]. They begin life as a single cell, the egg, which divides repeatedly to form all the tissues, organs, and organ systems needed for life [S1].

## Worked example

Let us trace the levels of organisation in the human body for the job of digestion.

* Cell: a muscle cell in the wall of the stomach [S3].
* Tissue: a group of similar muscle cells forms muscle tissue [S1].
* Organ: the stomach, made of muscle tissue together with other tissues whose cells produce digestive juices and acid [S3].
* Organ system: the digestive system, made up of the food pipe, the stomach, the intestines and other organs that together break down food and absorb nutrients.
* Organism: the human being, whose organ systems work together as one living body [S1].

## Key points

* The cell is the basic unit of life, just like a brick is the basic unit of a wall [S1].
* A group of similar cells forms a type of tissue [S1].
* Different tissues are organised to form an organ [S1].
* Several organs work together to form an organ system that performs a major function of the body [S1].
* All the organ systems together make up a complete organism [S1].
* The levels of organisation are Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism [S1].
