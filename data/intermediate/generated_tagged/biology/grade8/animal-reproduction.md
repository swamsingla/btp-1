# Reproduction in Animals

Reproduction is the process by which living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind. Without reproduction, life would eventually disappear from Earth. Reproduction ensures that each type of organism continues to exist, maintaining the continuity of life [S3]. This process is vital because it passes on instructions, called genetic material or genes, to offspring. These genes are stored inside every cell of a living being. They act as a detailed instruction manual that tells the cell how to make blood, guide the formation of bones, muscles, or skin, and ensure a calf grows into a cow, or a kitten grows into a cat [S3].

## Sexual Reproduction

In animals, reproduction usually involves two parents, typically called male and female. In sexual reproduction, instructions from two parents combine to create offspring that are not exactly like either of the parents [S3]. These offspring share some traits with both parents, but also have differences. This mixing of genetic information helps keep useful features while allowing new ones to appear.

To prevent the genetic material from doubling every generation, each parent makes specialised reproductive cells called gametes. These carry only half of the parent's genetic material. When male and female gametes join, they form a new cell with a complete set of instructions—half from each parent. In animals, these gametes are called sperm (male) and eggs (female) [S1].

## External and Internal Fertilisation

Fertilisation is the process where the male and female gametes combine. The way this happens depends on the environment and the animal's biology.

In some animals, fertilisation takes place in water. For example, male and female fish or frogs eject sperm and eggs, respectively, into the water where they combine to form the zygote. In these animals, the development of the zygote into an embryo also takes place in water [S1].

In birds and mammals, including humans, sperm are deposited inside the female and fertilisation takes place when the sperm swim towards the egg produced by the female. After this step, birds and mammals follow different processes [S1].

## Development of the Embryo in Birds and Mammals

Birds and mammals have different strategies for developing their young.

In birds, the fertilised egg (zygote) is 'laid' by the female. The development of the zygote into an embryo happens after the egg is laid during the hatching process. The female parent puts a lot of food into each egg so it can last for the developing embryo until it hatches. This is one strategy to ensure a supply of nutrition to the embryo [S1].

In most mammals, the development of the zygote into an embryo takes place inside the body of the female. The mother's body provides all the food and oxygen the baby needs to grow until it is born. This is a different way of giving nutrition to the developing baby, compared to animals like birds that lay eggs [S1].

## Metamorphosis

Some animals do not look like their parents when they are born. Instead, they go through a process called metamorphosis, where they change their body form completely as they grow. For example, a tadpole lives in water and has gills to breathe. As it grows, it develops legs, loses its gills, and transforms into an adult frog that lives on land.

## Worked example

Let us look at how fertilisation works in different environments using the rules from our text.

Rule 1: In fish, fertilisation happens in water. The male fish releases sperm and the female fish releases eggs into the water. The sperm and eggs then combine to form a zygote [S1].

Rule 2: In birds, the female lays an egg after fertilisation. The egg contains the zygote, and it develops into an embryo outside the mother's body [S1].

Rule 3: In mammals, fertilisation happens inside the female's body, and the embryo develops inside the mother until birth [S1].

Imagine a fish farm in Kerala where a farmer is breeding goldfish. The farmer places a male goldfish and a female goldfish in the same pond. According to the rules of external fertilisation, the male goldfish will release sperm and the female goldfish will release eggs into the water. The sperm will swim to the eggs and combine with them to form a zygote. This process happens entirely in the water, just like it does for frogs [S1].

Now, imagine the same farmer wants to breed a dog. Unlike fish, dogs do not release their gametes into water. According to the rules of internal fertilisation, the male dog's sperm must be deposited inside the female dog. The sperm will then swim towards the egg inside the female dog's body. The zygote will form inside the female dog and develop into an embryo there until the puppies are born [S1].

This shows how the environment and the animal's biology determine how reproduction takes place.

## Key points

*   Reproduction ensures that each type of organism continues to exist, maintaining the continuity of life [S3].
*   Parents pass on instructions called genetic material or genes to their offspring. These genes are stored inside every cell of a living being [S3].
*   In sexual reproduction, two parents are involved, and each parent makes specialised reproductive cells called gametes. These carry only half of the parent's genetic material [S1].
*   In animals, the male gamete is called sperm and the female gamete is called egg [S1].
*   In fish and frogs, fertilisation takes place in water. The male and female eject sperm and eggs into the water where they combine to form a zygote [S1].
*   In birds, the fertilised egg is laid by the female. The embryo develops after the egg is laid during the hatching process [S1].
*   In most mammals, the development of the zygote into an embryo takes place inside the body of the female. The mother's body provides all the food and oxygen the baby needs to grow until it is born [S1].
*   Reproduction allows for small changes in the instructions that are passed down from parents to offspring. Over many generations, these small differences can add up, leading to big differences and even new life forms [S3].
