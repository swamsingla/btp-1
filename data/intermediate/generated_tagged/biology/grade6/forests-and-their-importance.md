
# Forests and Their Importance

A forest is a large area with dense growth of various types of plants, including many trees, shrubs, and herbs [S1]. Forests are incredibly important to us and to the planet. They provide us with fresh air to breathe and are a source of materials like timber. They also serve as a natural home for countless wild animals, birds, and insects, giving them food and shelter. Furthermore, the roots of forest plants hold on to the soil and prevent it from being washed away, which protects the land [S1].

## Oxygen and Timber

Forests are a vital resource because they produce the oxygen we breathe. The trees and other plants in a forest take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen through a process called photosynthesis. This oxygen is essential for almost all animals, including humans, to live and survive. Forests are not the only source of oxygen, since water plants and algae release a great deal of it as well, but they are one of the largest, and losing them would leave the air around us less healthy to breathe.

In addition to oxygen, forests provide us with timber, which is wood used to build houses, furniture, and tools. Timber is a strong and versatile material that humans have used for thousands of years. However, it takes many years to grow a new forest or restore a forest that has been cut down. Therefore, we must use timber responsibly and ensure we do not cut down trees faster than they can grow back [S1].

## Habitat for Wildlife

Forests are a natural home for many wild animals, including birds and insects [S1]. They provide food and shelter to them. In nature, every animal depends on other life forms for survival. The diversity of life forms ensures food for every living being. For example, when fruits fall from trees, they are left for animals and birds to eat [S1]. This creates a balanced ecosystem where animals have a place to live and find their meals. If we cut down too many trees, these animals lose their homes and food sources, which can lead to a decrease in wildlife populations.

## Protection of Soil

The roots of plants hold on to the soil and prevent it from being washed away [S1]. When it rains heavily, the soil can easily be washed away, but the deep roots of forest trees act like strong anchors. The leaves that fall from the trees decay and enrich the soil with nutrients. This soil is used by new plants and trees to grow. This is an example of recycling in nature. The forest floor continuously breaks down old leaves and turns them into rich food for new plants, ensuring that the soil remains fertile and healthy for future growth [S1].

## Deforestation and Conservation

Over the years, the forest cover has been decreasing, mainly due to human activities like large scale cutting of trees [S1]. This destruction of forests is called deforestation, and it is a major problem for the environment. When forests are destroyed, animals lose their homes, the soil can wash away, and the amount of oxygen in the air decreases. It takes many years to grow a new forest or restore lost forests, so the damage is very slow to fix [S1].

To help protect forests, people have started movements to save them. One such effort is the famous Chipko movement, which started in the early 1970s in Uttarakhand. Local women actively participated in this movement by encircling and hugging the trees to protect them from being felled [S1]. There are also events like Van Mahotsav, a week-long forest festival celebrated across India during the month of July. During this event, new plants and trees are planted, and awareness about respecting forests is raised [S1].

## Worked example

Let us look at a simple example of how forests protect the soil. Imagine a forest with 100 trees. Every day, each tree drops 5 dry leaves onto the ground. In one day, the total number of leaves dropped is $100 \times 5 = 500$ leaves [S1]. Over the course of a year, which has 365 days, the total number of leaves that fall to the ground is $500 \times 365 = 182,500$ leaves. These leaves decay and enrich the soil with nutrients, which helps new plants and trees to grow [S1].

## Key points

* Forests are large areas with dense growth of various types of plants that provide oxygen and timber.
* Forests provide food and shelter to many wild animals, birds, and insects.
* The roots of forest plants hold on to the soil and prevent it from being washed away.
* Deforestation is the large scale cutting of trees by humans, which decreases forest cover.
* Movements like Chipko and events like Van Mahotsav help protect and restore forests.
