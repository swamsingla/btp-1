
# Components of Food

Food is what we eat to keep our bodies alive and working. Every day, we eat a mix of different things without even thinking about it. A balanced diet gives us the fuel and materials we need to grow, play, and stay healthy. If you miss a meal, you feel tired and less energetic. This is because your body needs a steady supply of food to function.

## Carbohydrates and Fats

Carbohydrates are one of the primary sources of energy in our diet [S1]. You can find them in cereals like wheat, rice, and maize. Vegetables like potato and sweet potato, and fruits like banana, pineapple, and mango are also good sources [S1]. Even common sugar is a type of carbohydrate [S1]. Glucose provides instant energy, which is why a marathon runner drinks glucose water during and after a race [S1]. 

Fats are another kind of food component. Ghee and various kinds of oils are grouped under fat [S1]. Nuts, such as groundnuts, walnuts, coconuts, and almonds, and seeds, such as pumpkin seeds and sunflower seeds, are some sources of fat [S1]. Fat is a source of stored energy [S1]. Carbohydrates and fats provide us energy for performing various activities, so they are called energy-giving foods [S1]. 

For example, if you eat a laddoo made with besan or wheat flour and ghee, your body gets a lot of energy from it. This energy helps keep you warm in the winter.

## Proteins

Proteins are also an important part of our food [S1]. Milk products and pulses are good sources of protein [S1]. Sportspersons need proteins in larger quantities to build their muscles [S1]. People get proteins from plants as well as animals [S1]. Some excellent plant sources of protein are pulses, beans, peas and nuts [S1]. Animal sources of protein are milk, paneer, egg, fish and meat [S1]. Protein-rich foods help in growth and repair of our body, so they are called body-building foods [S1]. The right amount of protein must be included in the diet of growing children for their proper growth and development [S1].

## Vitamins and Minerals

Vitamins and minerals protect our body from various diseases. The story of Vitamin C shows this clearly. In earlier times, sailors often suffered from bleeding and swollen gums. A Scottish physician named James Lind observed in 1746 that sailors who consumed lemons and oranges recovered from these symptoms [S1]. Bleeding and swollen gums are symptoms of a disease called scurvy. Lemons and oranges help in curing scurvy because Vitamin C present in citrus fruits like lemons and oranges helps in curing this disease [S1].

Minerals matter just as much. In the 1960s, Indian scientists found that people in the Himalayan region and the Northern plains of India had symptoms of swelling at the front of the neck. This was due to a deficiency of iodine in the soil and local food supply [S1]. Swelling at the front of the neck is a symptom of a disease called goitre. The Government of India supplemented common salt with iodine to prepare iodised salt. Consumption of iodised salt visibly reduced these symptoms [S1]. Iodised salt is simply common salt mixed with required quantities of salts of iodine [S1].

## Water and Dietary Fibre

Water is an essential component of food. It helps our body absorb nutrients from the food we eat and carries waste products away. We get water from what we drink, and also from the food itself, because many fruits and vegetables contain a large amount of it.

Dietary fibre is also called roughage. It comes from plant sources such as whole grains, pulses, fruits and vegetables. Roughage does not give our body any nutrient, but it adds bulk to the food and helps the digestive system get rid of undigested waste.

## Worked example

Imagine a student named Rahul who eats a traditional meal of rice, dal, and a vegetable curry. The rice provides carbohydrates for instant energy. The dal provides proteins for growth and repair. The vegetable curry provides vitamins and minerals to protect his body from diseases. By eating this balanced meal, Rahul gets energy, growth support, and disease protection all at once.

## Key points

- Carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals, water, and dietary fibre are all components of food.
- Carbohydrates and fats are energy-giving foods.
- Proteins are body-building foods that help in growth and repair.
- Vitamins and minerals protect our body from various diseases.
- A balanced diet includes a variety of foods to ensure all components are present.
