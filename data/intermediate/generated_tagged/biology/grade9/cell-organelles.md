# Cell Organelles

Every living cell is surrounded by a membrane that keeps its contents separate from the outside environment [S1]. Large and complex cells, like those in plants and animals, need many different chemical activities to survive. To keep these activities from interfering with each other, the cell uses tiny, membrane-bound structures called organelles [S1]. This internal organization is one of the main features that distinguishes eukaryotic cells from prokaryotic cells.

## The Cytoplasm and Internal Structure

When you look at a cell under a microscope, you will see three main features in almost every cell: the plasma membrane, the nucleus, and the cytoplasm [S2]. The cytoplasm is the fluid content inside the plasma membrane [S4]. It takes up very little stain in temporary mounts of cells, such as onion peel or human cheek cells [S4]. The cytoplasm contains many specialised cell organelles, and each of these performs a specific function for the cell [S4]. 

The significance of having internal membranes is highlighted by the example of viruses. Viruses lack any membranes and therefore do not show characteristics of life until they enter a living body and use its cell machinery to multiply [S4].

## The Nucleus

We have talked about the nucleus in a previous section [S1]. The nucleus is one of the most important components of the cell. It is a membrane-bound organelle that houses the cell's genetic material and controls its activities [S2].

## The Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi Apparatus

Some important examples of cell organelles which we will discuss now are the endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus [S1]. They are important because they carry out some very crucial functions in cells [S1].

## Mitochondria

Mitochondria are another key organelle found in eukaryotic cells. They are responsible for carrying out very crucial functions in cells [S1]. 

## Lysosomes

Lysosomes are also important cell organelles. They are membrane-bound structures that help carry out very crucial functions in cells [S1].

## Plastids

Plastids are present only in plant cells [S3]. There are two types of plastids – chromoplasts (coloured plastids) and leucoplasts (white or colourless plastids) [S3]. Chromoplasts containing the pigment chlorophyll are known as chloroplasts [S3]. Chloroplasts are important for photosynthesis in plants [S3]. Chloroplasts also contain various yellow or orange pigments in addition to chlorophyll [S3]. Leucoplasts are primarily organelles in which materials such as starch, oils and protein granules are stored [S3].

The internal organisation of the Chloroplast consists of numerous membrane layers embedded in a material called the stroma [S3]. These are similar to mitochondria in external structure [S3]. Like the mitochondria, plastids also have their own DNA and ribosomes [S3].

## Vacuoles

Vacuoles are also present in cells and are membrane-bound structures that help carry out specific functions [S1].

## Ribosomes

Ribosomes are also important cell organelles. They are membrane-bound structures that help carry out specific functions [S1].

## Worked Example

Imagine you are looking at a cell under a microscope. You notice a large region enclosed by the cell membrane that takes up very little stain. This is the cytoplasm, and it contains many specialised cell organelles [S4]. If you were to look at a plant cell, you might also see plastids, which are present only in plant cells [S3].

## Key points
- Every cell has a membrane around it to keep its own contents separate from the external environment [S1].
- Large and complex cells, including cells from multicellular organisms, need a lot of chemical activities to support their complicated structure and function [S1].
- To keep these activities of different kinds separate from each other, these cells use membrane-bound little structures (or ‘organelles’) within themselves [S1].
- The cytoplasm is the fluid content inside the plasma membrane, and it contains many specialised cell organelles [S4].
- Plastids are present only in plant cells, and there are two types: chromoplasts and leucoplasts [S3].
- Chloroplasts are important for photosynthesis in plants [S3].
- Like the mitochondria, plastids also have their own DNA and ribosomes [S3].
- In prokaryotes, beside the absence of a defined nuclear region, the membrane-bound cell organelles are also absent [S4].
- On the other hand, the eukaryotic cells have nuclear membrane as well as membrane-enclosed organelles [S4].
- Some of these organelles are visible only with an electron microscope [S1].
- We have talked about the nucleus in a previous section. Some important examples of cell organelles which we will discuss now are: endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, mitochondria and plastids [S1].
- They are important because they carry out some very crucial functions in cells [S1].
- If we study a cell under a microscope, we would come across three features in almost every cell; plasma membrane, nucleus and cytoplasm [S2].
- All activities inside the cell and interactions of the cell with its environment are possible due to these features [S2].
- When we look at the temporary mounts of onion peel as well as human cheek cells, we can see a large region of each cell enclosed by the cell membrane. This region takes up very little stain. It is called the cytoplasm [S4].
- Cell organelles are enclosed by membranes [S4].
- The significance of membranes can be illustrated with the example of viruses. Viruses lack any membranes and hence do not show characteristics of life until they enter a living body and use its cell machinery to multiply [S4].
