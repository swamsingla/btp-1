# Square Roots

Square roots are the inverse operation of squaring a number. If you know the area of a square, finding the square root gives you the length of its side. This is a very useful skill when you are working with geometry, measuring areas, or solving equations.

## What is a Square Root?

A square root of a number is a value that, when multiplied by itself, gives the original number. [S1] For example, the area of a square is 49 sq. cm. The length of its side is 7 cm because $7 \times 7 = 49$. We call 7 the square root of 49. In general, if $y = x^{2}$, then $x$ is the square root of $y$. [S1]

Every perfect square has two integer square roots. One is positive and the other is negative. [S1] For example, $8^{2} = 64$ and $(-8)^{2} = 64$. The square root of a number is denoted by the symbol $\sqrt{}$. Thus, $\sqrt{64} = \pm 8$ and $\sqrt{100} = \pm 10$. [S1] Note that $\sqrt{8^2} = \pm 8$ and $\sqrt{10^2} = \pm 10$. In general, $\sqrt{n^2} = \pm n$. [S1] In this chapter, we shall only consider the positive square root. [S1]

## Finding Square Roots by Prime Factorisation

We know that a perfect square is obtained by multiplying an integer by itself. [S1] Looking at a number's prime factorisation can help us determine whether it is a perfect square. [S1] If we can divide the prime factors of a number into two equal groups, then the product of the prime factors in either group combine to form the square root. [S1]

We can write the prime factors in pairs. [S1] If all the prime factors can be paired up perfectly, the number is a perfect square.

Consider the number 324. [S1] Its prime factorisation is $2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3$. [S1] We can group these factors into pairs. This gives $324 = (2 \times 3 \times 3) \times (2 \times 3 \times 3)$. [S1] This shows that 324 is a perfect square. Therefore, $324 = (2 \times 3 \times 3)^{2} = 18^{2}$. [S1] The square root of 324 is 18.

Now consider the number 156. [S1] The prime factorisation of 156 is $2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 13$. [S1] We cannot pair up these factors perfectly. Therefore, 156 is not a perfect square. [S1]

## Finding Square Roots by Estimation

For larger numbers, prime factorisation can be difficult. [S1] We can estimate the square root by looking at the closest perfect squares we are familiar with. [S1] For example, to find $\sqrt{1936}$, we can reason as follows. First, 1936 is between 1600 ($40^{2}$) and 2500 ($50^{2}$). [S1] So, 40 is less than $\sqrt{1936}$ which is less than 50. [S1]

Second, the last digit of 1936 is 6. [S1] So, the last digit of the square root must either be 4 or 6. [S1] It can be 44 or 46. [S1]

Third, we can calculate $45^{2}$ to compare it with 1936. [S1] We can write $45^{2}$ as $(40 + 5)(40 + 5) = 40^{2} + 2 \times 40 \times 5 + 5^{2} = 1600 + 400 + 25 = 2025$. [S1]

Fourth, 2025 is greater than 1936. [S1] So, 40 is less than $\sqrt{1936}$ which is less than 45. [S1]

From the observation in the second point, we can guess and then verify that $\sqrt{1936}$ is 44. Check: $44 \times 44 = 1936$. [S1]

## When the Number Is Not a Perfect Square

Suppose we want $\sqrt{250}$. Since 250 is not a perfect square, we can only estimate. We know that $100 < 250 < 400$, and $\sqrt{100} = 10$ and $\sqrt{400} = 20$, so $10 < \sqrt{250} < 20$. To get closer, we use $15^{2} = 225$ and $16^{2} = 256$. Therefore $15 < \sqrt{250} < 16$. Since 256 is much closer to 250 than 225 is, $\sqrt{250}$ is approximately 16, though a little less than 16.

## Worked example

Let us find the square root of 1156 using prime factorisation. [S1] First, we find the prime factors of 1156. [S1] We can divide 1156 by 2 to get 578. [S1] Dividing by 2 again gives 289. [S1] The number 289 is $17 \times 17$. [S1] So, the prime factorisation of 1156 is $2 \times 2 \times 17 \times 17$. [S1]

We can pair up these factors. [S1] This gives $1156 = (2 \times 17) \times (2 \times 17)$. [S1] This shows that 1156 is a perfect square. [S1] Therefore, $1156 = 34^{2}$. [S1] The square root of 1156 is 34.

## Key points
* A square root of a number is a value that, when multiplied by itself, gives the original number.
* The square root of a number is denoted by the symbol $\sqrt{}$.
* A perfect square is a number whose prime factors can be grouped into two equal groups.
* If a number ends with 2, 3, 7, or 8, then we can definitely say that it is not a square. [S1]
* We can estimate the square root of a larger perfect square by looking at the closest perfect squares we are familiar with. [S1]
