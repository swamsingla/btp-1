# Area of Parallelogram

A parallelogram is a four-sided shape where both pairs of opposite sides run parallel to each other [S1]. You may already know how to find the area of a rectangle, which is a special type of parallelogram. The area of any parallelogram is found by multiplying its base by its height. This rule is very useful in everyday life, such as when you need to measure the size of a slanted garden plot or a piece of cloth that is cut in a slanted shape.

## Finding the Base and the Height

To use the formula for the area of a parallelogram, you must first identify the base and the height. The base is simply any one of the four sides of the parallelogram. The height is the perpendicular distance between the base and the opposite side. It is important to remember that the height is not the length of the slanted side unless the parallelogram is a rectangle. Once you choose a base, the height is measured to the side opposite that base, and it meets the base at a right angle.

## The Area Formula

The formula for the area of a parallelogram is given by:
$$ \text{Area} = \text{base} \times \text{height} $$
This formula means that you take the length of the base and multiply it by the height. The result will give you the area in square units, such as square metres or square centimetres.

This formula agrees with what you know about triangles. A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles [S1], so each triangle has half the area of the parallelogram. Each of these triangles has the same base and the same height as the parallelogram. Using the area of a triangle, $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$, the two triangles together give $2 \times \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \text{base} \times \text{height}$.

## Worked Example

Let us look at a practical example to understand how to use the formula. Imagine a parallelogram where the base is $8$ cm and the height is $5$ cm. To find the area, you multiply the base by the height.
$$ \text{Area} = 8 \times 5 = 40 $$
So, the area of this parallelogram is $40$ square cm.

Now take a parallelogram with a base of $10$ cm, a height of $6$ cm and a slanted side of $7$ cm. The area uses the height, not the slanted side:
$$ \text{Area} = 10 \times 6 = 60 $$
So the area is $60$ square cm. Multiplying $10 \times 7 = 70$ would be wrong, because $7$ cm is the slanted side and not the height.

The formula can also be used the other way round. If a parallelogram has an area of $72$ square cm and a base of $9$ cm, then
$$ \text{height} = \frac{\text{Area}}{\text{base}} = \frac{72}{9} = 8 $$
So its height is $8$ cm.

## Key points
- A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel [S1].
- The area of a parallelogram is calculated using the formula: Area = base × height.
- The base is any one of the four sides of the parallelogram.
- The height is the perpendicular distance between the base and the opposite side.
- The area is always expressed in square units.
