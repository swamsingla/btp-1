# Negative and Zero Exponents

Earlier in this chapter you learned about powers and exponents. You know that $5^4$ means 5 multiplied by itself 4 times, which equals 625 [S2]. You also know how to use the laws of exponents to multiply and divide powers when the exponents are counting numbers. However, what happens when you need to divide a smaller power by a larger power, or when you divide a number by itself? This page explores the answers to these questions, introducing you to zero and negative exponents.

## The Rule for Zero Exponents

You already know that $x^a \times x^b = x^{a+b}$ and $x^a \div x^b = x^{a-b}$ [S3]. But what happens when the exponents are equal? If you divide $2^4$ by $2^4$, you get 1, because the top and bottom cancel out. Using the law of exponents, $2^4 \div 2^4 = 2^{4-4} = 2^0$. This means that $2^0$ must equal 1. This rule holds true for any non-zero base $x$ and any counting number $a$, so $x^a \div x^a = x^0 = 1$, where $x \neq 0$ [S3].

## The Rule for Negative Exponents

What happens if you divide $2^4$ by $2^5$? If you expand it out, you get $\frac{2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2}{2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2}$, which simplifies to $\frac{1}{2}$, or $\frac{1}{2^1}$. Using the law of exponents, $2^4 \div 2^5 = 2^{4-5} = 2^{-1}$ [S3]. Therefore, $2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}$. The same logic applies to any negative exponent: $10^{-3} = \frac{1}{10^3}$, $7^{-2} = \frac{1}{7^2}$, and $2^{-6} = \frac{1}{2^6}$ [S3]. In general, for any non-zero base $n$ and positive integer $a$, $n^{-a} = \frac{1}{n^a}$. The rule also works in reverse: $10^3 = \frac{1}{10^{-3}}$ and $7^2 = \frac{1}{7^{-2}}$ [S3].

## Standard Form and Scientific Notation

As numbers get very large or very small, writing them out in full becomes difficult. For example, the initial thickness of a sheet of paper might be $0.001$ cm [S2]. The thickness doubles with each fold, so if you fold it 7 times, the thickness becomes $0.001 \times 2^7 = 0.128$ cm [S2]. Standard form, or scientific notation, helps us write these numbers easily. We write $0.001$ as $1 \times 10^{-3}$. This is because $10^{-3} = \frac{1}{10^3} = \frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$ [S3]. So, the thickness after 7 folds is $1 \times 10^{-3} \times 2^7$ cm, which is $1.28 \times 10^{-1}$ cm in standard form.

## Worked Example

Let's apply these rules to a problem. Suppose we need to simplify the expression $2^3 \times 2^{-5}$. Using the law of exponents, we add the exponents: $2^{3 + (-5)} = 2^{-2}$. Since we have a negative exponent, we convert it to a positive exponent by taking the reciprocal: $2^{-2} = \frac{1}{2^2} = \frac{1}{4}$ [S3].

## Key points
- Any non-zero number raised to the power of zero equals 1: $x^0 = 1$, where $x \neq 0$ [S3].
- A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the base raised to the positive exponent: $n^{-a} = \frac{1}{n^a}$, where $n \neq 0$ [S3].
- Standard form uses powers of 10 to write very large or very small numbers easily [S2].
- The laws of exponents still apply when using zero and negative exponents [S3].
