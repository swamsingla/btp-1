# Range

Data is like a story waiting to be read. Well-organised data can tell interesting stories, such as how the heights of students in different grades differ from one school to another [S1]. To understand such data, we need ways to describe how spread out the numbers are. The range is one such tool. It is the difference between the highest and lowest values, giving us a quick idea of the spread of the data.

## Finding the Highest and Lowest Values

To calculate the range, we first need to identify the two most extreme values in our set of data. The highest value, also known as the maximum, is the largest number in the dataset. The lowest value, or the minimum, is the smallest number in the dataset. Once we have these two numbers, we can find the range by subtracting the minimum from the maximum. This tells us how much the data is stretched out.

Suppose a group of five Grade 8 boys has the following heights: $110$ cm, $114$ cm, $118$ cm, $121$ cm and $125$ cm. The tallest boy is $125$ cm and the shortest is $110$ cm. The difference between these two values is $125 - 110 = 15$ cm. This $15$ cm is the range for that group of boys. It tells us that there is a $15$ cm spread in their heights.

## Why the Range Matters

The range gives us a quick snapshot of the variability in the data. A small range means that the values are clustered closely together, while a large range means the values are spread out over a wider interval. For example, dot plots of the heights of boys and girls in the same grades at two different schools can show a considerable difference in heights [S1]. Finding the range of each group is one simple way to describe how spread out its heights are.

The range depends only on the two extreme values. If one value is much larger or much smaller than the rest, the range becomes large even when most of the values are close together. The range does not tell us how the values in between are arranged.

## Using the Range in Everyday Situations

The concept of range is not limited to heights. It applies to any set of numerical data, such as weights, test scores, or temperatures recorded over a week. To find it, subtract the lowest value from the highest value.

For example, if the highest temperature recorded in a week was $35$ degrees Celsius and the lowest was $15$ degrees Celsius, the range would be $35 - 15 = 20$ degrees Celsius. This tells us that the temperature varied by $20$ degrees over that week.

## Worked Example

Suppose we have the following test scores for a class of students: $45$, $55$, $60$, $70$, $80$, $85$, and $90$. To find the range, we first identify the highest and lowest values. The highest value is $90$, and the lowest value is $45$.

The range is $90 - 45 = 45$. This tells us that the test scores are spread out over $45$ points. Now compare this with another class whose scores are $60$, $62$, $63$, $65$, $66$, $68$, and $70$. Its range is $70 - 60 = 10$. This smaller range shows that the scores in the second class are much more tightly clustered together.

If one more student in the second class scored $100$, the highest value would become $100$ and the range would become $100 - 60 = 40$, even though the other scores did not change. This shows how a single extreme value can change the range a great deal.

## Key points

*   The range is calculated by subtracting the minimum value from the maximum value in a dataset.
*   It measures the spread of the data, showing how far apart the highest and lowest values are.
*   A small range indicates that the data points are close to each other, while a large range indicates a wide spread.
*   The range depends only on the two extreme values, so one very high or very low value can change it a great deal.
*   It can be applied to any set of numerical data, such as heights, weights, or test scores.
