# Inverse Proportion

Inverse proportion is a relationship between two quantities where, when one quantity changes by a factor $n$, the other changes by the inverse factor $\frac{1}{n}$. For example, when one is doubled, the other is halved [S1]. You may already know about direct proportion, where both quantities change by the same factor. In inverse proportion, the product of the two quantities stays the same [S1]. This concept is very useful when solving real-world problems involving time, speed, and work.

## The Rule of Inverse Proportion

When two quantities $x$ and $y$ vary in inverse proportion, their product is a constant [S1]. We write this as $xy = k$, where $k$ is the constant [S1]. This means if you multiply the values of $x$ and $y$, you always get the same number [S1]. For example, if $x$ is the speed of a vehicle and $y$ is the time taken to travel, the product $xy$ gives the distance [S1]. Since the distance between two places does not change, the product remains constant [S1].

## How to Check for Inverse Proportion

To check if two quantities are in inverse proportion, you can look at their values [S1]. If the product $x_1 y_1$ is equal to $x_2 y_2$, then they are inversely proportional [S1]. You can also use the ratio rule [S1]. If $x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2 = k$, then $\frac{x_1}{x_2} = \frac{y_2}{y_1}$ [S1]. This means the ratio of the first quantities is equal to the ratio of the second quantities, but reversed [S1].

## Real-World Examples

Consider the travel time between Lucknow and Kanpur [S1]. The distance is fixed [S1]. If you travel by motorcycle at a speed of $30\mathrm{km / h}$, it takes 3 hours [S1]. If you travel by car at $60\mathrm{km / h}$, it takes 1.5 hours [S1]. The speed has doubled, and the time has halved [S1]. Cycling at $15\mathrm{km / h}$ takes 6 hours and walking at $5\mathrm{km / h}$ takes 18 hours: the speed is 3 times as great and the time is 3 times less. In every case the product of speed and time is 90 km, which is the distance [S1].

## Another Example

20 workers take 4 days to lay a road. How many days will 10 workers take to lay the same road? Fewer workers need more days, by the same factor, so the quantities are inversely proportional and $x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2$. Thus $20 \times 4 = 10 \times y_2$, so $y_2 = \frac{20 \times 4}{10} = 8$. It will take 8 days. Notice that when the number of workers was halved, the number of days doubled.

## Worked Example

A factory machine produces 1200 items in 5 hours. If the machine's speed is doubled, how many hours will it take to produce the same number of items?

Let $x$ be the speed of the machine in items per hour and $y$ be the time taken in hours. The number of items produced is constant, so $xy = k$.
Initially, $x_1 = \frac{1200}{5} = 240$ items per hour and $y_1 = 5$ hours, so $k = 240 \times 5 = 1200$.
When the speed is doubled, $x_2 = 2 \times 240 = 480$ items per hour.
So, $480 \times y_2 = 1200$.
$y_2 = \frac{1200}{480} = 2.5$.
Thus, it will take 2.5 hours to produce 1200 items.

## Key points

*   Two quantities $x$ and $y$ vary in inverse proportion if $xy = k$, where $k$ is a constant [S1].
*   If $x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2 = k$, then $\frac{x_1}{x_2} = \frac{y_2}{y_1}$ [S1].
*   If one quantity changes by a factor $n$, the other changes by the factor $\frac{1}{n}$. For example, halving the workers doubles the days [S1].
*   The product of the two quantities remains constant in inverse proportion [S1].
*   This concept is used to solve problems involving speed, time, and distance [S1].
