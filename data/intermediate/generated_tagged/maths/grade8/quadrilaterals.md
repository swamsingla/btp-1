# Quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is simply a polygon that has exactly four sides. Drawing a diagonal splits any quadrilateral into two triangles, each with angle sum $180^{\circ}$, so the sum of its four interior angles is always $360^{\circ}$ [S2]. This fact is useful when you need to find a missing angle. You may already know that the interior angles of a triangle add up to $180^{\circ}$. We will build on this knowledge to understand different types of four-sided shapes.

## The Parallelogram

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral where both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. Because of this parallel structure, the opposite sides of a parallelogram are always equal in length [S2]. The adjacent angles of a parallelogram always add up to $180^{\circ}$ because they are interior angles on the same side of a transversal crossing two parallel lines [S2]. Furthermore, the opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal to each other [S2]. The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, meaning they intersect exactly at their midpoints [S2].

## The Rhombus

A rhombus is a quadrilateral in which all four sides have the same length [S1]. Because its opposite sides are equal, a rhombus is also a parallelogram, and it inherits all the properties of a parallelogram [S1]. In a rhombus, the adjacent angles add up to $180^{\circ}$, and the opposite angles are equal [S1]. The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other, and they also bisect the angles of the rhombus [S1]. Additionally, the diagonals of a rhombus intersect each other at an angle of $90^{\circ}$ [S1].

## The Rectangle

A rectangle is a quadrilateral in which all four angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$ [S2]. Since all its angles are $90^{\circ}$, adjacent angles add up to $180^{\circ}$, so the opposite sides are parallel and a rectangle is also a parallelogram [S2]. Being a parallelogram, its opposite sides are equal. The diagonals of a rectangle are equal in length and they bisect each other. Conversely, a quadrilateral whose diagonals are equal and bisect each other is a rectangle [S2].

## The Square

A square is a special quadrilateral that is both a rectangle and a rhombus [S1]. Because it is a rectangle, all four of its angles are $90^{\circ}$ [S1]. Because it is a rhombus, all four of its sides have the same length [S1]. The diagonals of a square are equal in length, they bisect each other, and they intersect at an angle of $90^{\circ}$ [S1, S2].

## Worked example

Imagine a rhombus named ABCD where angle A is $50^{\circ}$. Since opposite angles in a rhombus are equal, angle C is also $50^{\circ}$ [S1]. Because adjacent angles in a rhombus add up to $180^{\circ}$, angle B is $180 - 50 = 130^{\circ}$ [S1]. Since opposite angles are equal, angle D is also $130^{\circ}$.

## Key points

*   The sum of the interior angles of any quadrilateral is $360^{\circ}$ [S2].
*   In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal and parallel, and opposite angles are equal [S2].
*   In a rhombus, all four sides are equal, and adjacent angles add up to $180^{\circ}$ [S1].
*   In a rectangle, all four angles are $90^{\circ}$, and the diagonals are equal [S2].
*   A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus, meaning it has four equal sides and four equal angles [S1].
