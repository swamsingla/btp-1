# Direct and Inverse Variation

When two quantities change, they can do so in different ways. Sometimes they change together, and sometimes they move in opposite directions. Understanding how they change helps us solve real-world problems. In this page, we will explore direct variation and inverse variation, which are rules that describe how two quantities relate to each other.

## Direct Variation

Direct variation happens when two quantities both change by the same factor, so their quotient stays the same. If one quantity doubles, the other also doubles, and if one is halved, the other is also halved. We can write this relationship as $\frac{y}{x} = k$, that is, $y = kx$, where $x$ and $y$ are the two quantities and $k$ is a constant. This means that $y$ is always equal to $x$ multiplied by the same fixed number.

A very useful tool for solving direct proportion problems is the rule of three. When two ratios are proportional, i.e., when $a:b::c:d$, then $d = \frac{bc}{a}$. [S1] We call such proportions direct proportions. [S1] We use this understanding to find the value of the fourth quantity $(d)$, when the value of three quantities $(a, b,$ and $c)$ are given. [S1]

Consider a practical scenario involving construction work. If 5 workers can move 4500 bricks in a day, how many workers are needed to move 18000 bricks in a day? We can represent this as a statement of proportionality: 4500 : 18000 :: 5 : x. Using the rule of three, we can find the value of x by $x = \frac{18000 \times 5}{4500} = 20$. [S1] Thus, the number of workers needed are 20. [S1]

## Inverse Variation

Inverse variation is the opposite of direct variation. In this case, when one quantity changes by a factor $n$, the other changes by the inverse factor $\frac{1}{n}$: if one increases, the other decreases, and if one decreases, the other increases. We can write this relationship using the equation $xy = k$, where $x$ and $y$ are the two quantities and $k$ is a constant.

Let us look at a real-world example involving travel. Puneeth's father went from Lucknow to Kanpur in 3 hours by riding his motorcycle at a speed of $30\mathrm{km / h}$. [S1] If he takes a car instead and drives at $60\mathrm{km / h}$, how long will it take him to reach Kanpur? [S1] Can we write this as the statement of proportionality 30 : 60 :: 3 : x? No. That statement would give $x = \frac{60 \times 3}{30} = 6$ hours, which is longer than 3 hours, yet a faster car must take less time. [S1] The travel time decreases as the speed increases, so this is not a direct proportion. Since speed times time is the distance, which does not change, $30 \times 3 = 60 \times x$, so $x = 1.5$ hours. [S1]

The following table shows the time taken to travel from Lucknow to Kanpur using different modes of transport:

|  | Walk | Bicycle | Motorcycle | Car |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed (km/h) | 5 | 15 | 30 | 60 |
| Time (in hours) | 18 | 6 | 3 | 1.5 |

From this table, we can see that when the speed increases, the time taken to travel the same distance decreases. [S1] Going by bicycle is 3 times faster than walking (15 ÷ 5). [S1] The speed has increased 3 times. [S1] The travel time has decreased 3 times too (18 ÷ 6). [S1] The speed has increased by the same factor by which the travel time has decreased. [S1] Since both quantities, speed and time, change by the same factor, they are proportional. But they change in opposite directions, or inversely. Such proportions are called inverse proportions. [S1]

From the table, we can see that the product of speed and time are the same for all modes of transport, namely 90 km. [S1] Two quantities $x$ and $y$ vary in inverse proportion if there exists a relation of the type $xy = k$, where $k$ is a constant. [S1] In the previous example, if $x$ represents the speed and $y$ represents the time taken, then $k$ is the distance between Lucknow and Kanpur, which remains constant. [S1]

## Identifying Variation from Data

To identify whether two quantities vary directly or inversely, we can look at a table of values. For direct variation, the ratio of $y$ to $x$ is always the same. For inverse variation, the product of $x$ and $y$ is always the same.

If quantities $x$ and $y$ are inversely proportional, and $x_{1}$ and $x_{2}$ are values of $x$ that have corresponding $y$ values $y_{1}$ and $y_{2}$, then $x_{1} y_{1} = x_{2} y_{2} = k$ (some constant). [S1] From these we can see that $\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}} = \frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}$. [S1]

Let us check if this is true for other modes of transport, such as walking and by car. Let us use $x$ to represent speed and $y$ to represent time.

$x_{1} = 5, x_{2} = 60, y_{1} = 18, y_{2} = 1.5.$

$\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}} = \frac{5}{60} = 0.083333...$

$\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}} = \frac{1.5}{18} = 0.083333...$

The ratios are equal, confirming that speed and time are inversely proportional.

## Worked Example

Let us apply the rule for inverse variation to a new problem. Suppose a factory machine produces 1200 items in 5 hours. If the machine's speed is doubled, how long will it take to produce the same number of items?

First, we find the constant $k$. The speed is $\frac{1200}{5} = 240$ items per hour, so $k = 240 \times 5 = 1200$ is constant. If the speed is doubled, the time taken will be halved. So, the new time taken will be $\frac{5}{2} = 2.5$ hours.

Alternatively, we can use the formula $\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}} = \frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}$. If the speed is doubled, then $x_{2} = 2x_{1}$. We can write $\frac{x_{1}}{2x_{1}} = \frac{y_{2}}{5}$. This simplifies to $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{y_{2}}{5}$. Solving for $y_{2}$, we get $y_{2} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5$ hours.

## Key points

- Direct variation: both quantities change by the same factor, and $y = kx$ (the quotient $\frac{y}{x}$ is constant).
- Inverse variation: when one quantity changes by a factor $n$, the other changes by $\frac{1}{n}$, and $xy = k$.
- The rule of three is used to solve direct proportion problems: if $a:b::c:d$, then $d = \frac{bc}{a}$.
- For inverse proportions, the ratio of the first quantities is equal to the ratio of the second quantities in reverse: $\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}} = \frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}$.
- The constant $k$ is the product of the two quantities in an inverse proportion, and it remains the same for all values.
