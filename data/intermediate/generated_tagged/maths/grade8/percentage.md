# Percentage

You might have heard statements like, “Mega Sale—up to 50% off!” or “Hiya scored 83% in her board exams” [S1]. The symbol ‘%’ is read as per cent. The word ‘per cent’ is derived from the Latin phrase ‘per centum’, meaning ‘by the hundred’ or ‘out of hundred’ [S1]. Therefore, 25 per cent (25%) means 25 out of every 100. Percentages are simply fractions where the denominator is 100. For instance, $20\% = \frac{20}{100} = \frac{1}{5}$ [S1].

## Expressing Fractions as Percentages

To express a fraction as a percentage, we need to find its equivalent fraction with 100 as the denominator. We can also multiply the fraction by 100 [S1]. 

Consider the fraction $\frac{3}{4}$. To express it as a percentage, we find an equivalent fraction with a denominator of 100:
$$
\frac {3}{4} = \frac {3 \times 2 5}{4 \times 2 5} = \frac {7 5}{1 0 0} = 7 5 \%
$$
Alternatively, we can set up the equation $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{x}{100}$ and solve for $x$:
$$
x = \frac {3}{4} \times 1 0 0 = 7 5
$$
Thus, $\frac{3}{4}$ can be expressed as $75\%$ [S1].

## Percentage of Some Quantity

To find a percentage of a specific quantity, we multiply the fraction by the value. For example, if Madhav ate 95 g of biscuits that are 35% sugar, the amount of sugar he ate is:
$$
\frac {3 5}{1 0 0} \times 9 5 = 3 3. 2 5 \text{ g}
$$
We can also use a proportional relationship: $\frac{35}{100} = \frac{s}{95}$, which gives $s = 33.25$ g [S2]. 

## Expressing Percentages as Fractions

The reverse also works. Since a percentage is a fraction, $24\%$ is the same as $\frac{24}{100}$. We can write other equivalent forms of it: $\frac{24}{100} = \frac{12}{50} = \frac{6}{25}$. In general, a percentage $z\%$ can be written as any fraction equivalent to $\frac{z}{100}$.

## Why Use Percentages?

Percentages make it easy to compare. Eesha scored 42 marks out of 50 in English and 70 marks out of 80 in Science. The maximum marks are different, so the raw scores are hard to compare. Converting both to percentages gives the English score as $\frac{42}{50} \times 100 = 84\%$ and the Science score as $\frac{70}{80} \times 100 = 87.5\%$. The Science score is higher, so Eesha did better in Science.

## The FDP Trio — Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages

Percentages, fractions, and decimals are interchangeable. For example, $50\%$ of a value can be found by multiplying the value by $\frac{1}{2}$ or by $0.5$ [S2]. Similarly, to find $10\%$ of a quantity, we multiply the quantity by $0.1$.

## Worked example

A student scored $\frac{4}{5}$ of the total marks in a test. To find out what percentage this is, we multiply the fraction by 100:
$$
\frac {4}{5} \times 1 0 0 = 8 0
$$
So, the student scored $80\%$ of the total marks.

## Key points

*   A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100: $x\% = \frac{x}{100}$.
*   To express a fraction as a percentage, multiply the fraction by 100.
*   To find a percentage of a quantity, multiply the quantity by the percentage expressed as a fraction (e.g., $\frac{y}{100}$).
*   Percentages, fractions, and decimals are equivalent and can be used interchangeably.
*   Mental calculations can be simplified by knowing common conversions, such as $25\%$ being equivalent to $\frac{1}{4}$ or $0.25$.
