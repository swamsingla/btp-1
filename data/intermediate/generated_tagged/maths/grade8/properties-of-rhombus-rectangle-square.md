# Properties of Rhombus, Rectangle and Square

A rhombus, rectangle, and square are all special types of quadrilaterals. They belong to a family of shapes known as parallelograms, which means their opposite sides are parallel to each other. Understanding how these shapes behave is important because they appear often in real life, from the tiles on your floor to the frames of doors and windows. In this section, we will look at the unique properties of these three shapes, focusing on their sides, angles, and diagonals.

## What Makes a Rectangle Special?

A rectangle is a quadrilateral in which the angles are all right angles, which is $90^{\circ}$, and the opposite sides are of equal length [S1]. This definition tells us exactly what a rectangle is, but we can also describe it in other ways. For instance, we can define a rectangle as a quadrilateral whose diagonals are equal and bisect each other [S1].

The diagonals of a rectangle have a very special relationship. They always have the same length. This means if you measure one diagonal, the other one will be exactly the same size. Furthermore, these diagonals always intersect at their midpoints. When two lines cross at their midpoints, we say that they bisect each other [S1].

Because the angles are all $90^{\circ}$, the opposite sides of a rectangle are also parallel to each other. We can prove this by using the fact that if the interior angles on the same side of a transversal add up to $180^{\circ}$, the two lines are parallel, and here $90^{\circ} + 90^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}$ [S1].

## The Square: A Special Rectangle

A square is a quadrilateral in which all the angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$, and all the sides are of equal length [S1]. This means a square is a special kind of rectangle. Every square is also a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square [S1].

We can show this relationship using a Venn diagram. The set of all squares is represented as a smaller circle inside the larger circle of all rectangles. This is because a square meets all the conditions of a rectangle, but it has one extra condition: all its sides must be equal [S1].

## Properties of a Rhombus

A rhombus is a quadrilateral in which all the sides are of equal length. Like a rectangle, a rhombus is also a type of parallelogram. However, the angles of a rhombus are not necessarily $90^{\circ}$. The sides are all equal, but the angles can be acute or obtuse. Since a rhombus is a parallelogram, its adjacent angles add up to $180^{\circ}$ and its opposite angles are equal. Its diagonals also bisect its angles. Unlike a rectangle's diagonals, the diagonals of a rhombus are not equal in general.

The diagonals of a rhombus have a special property. They bisect each other at right angles. This means they cross at their midpoints, and the angle between them is $90^{\circ}$ [S1].

## Diagonal Properties of a Square

A square has the properties of both a rectangle and a rhombus. Therefore, its diagonals are equal in length and they bisect each other. Additionally, the diagonals of a square bisect each other at right angles [S1].

This means that if you draw the diagonals of a square, they will divide the square into four smaller, identical triangles. These triangles are congruent, which means they have the same shape and size. The diagonals of a square also bisect its angles, so each $90^{\circ}$ angle is split into two $45^{\circ}$ angles.
