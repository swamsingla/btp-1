# Polynomials in One Variable

A variable is denoted by a symbol that can take any real value, and we use letters such as $x$, $y$ and $z$ to denote variables [S1]. Expressions like $2x$, $3x$, $-x$ and $-\frac{1}{2}x$ are all of the form (a constant) $\times$ $x$ [S1]. If we do not know what the constant is, we write it as $a$, $b$, $c$ and so on, so the expression will be $ax$, say [S1]. The values of constants remain the same throughout a particular situation, but the value of a variable can keep changing [S1].

Consider a square of side $x$ units. Its perimeter is $4x$ units, so as the length of the side varies, the perimeter varies [S1]. Its area is $x \times x = x^2$ square units [S1]. Expressions such as $2x$, $x^2 + 2x$ and $x^3 - x^2 + 4x + 7$ have only whole numbers as the exponents of the variable, and expressions of this form are called polynomials in one variable [S1].

## Definition and Terms

In the polynomial $x^3 - x^2 + 4x + 7$ the variable is $x$, so it is a polynomial in $x$ [S1]. Similarly, $3y^2 + 5y$ is a polynomial in the variable $y$, and $t^2 + 4$ is a polynomial in the variable $t$ [S1]. If the variable is $x$, we may denote the polynomial by $p(x)$, $q(x)$, $r(x)$ and so on, for example $p(x) = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$ and $q(x) = x^3 - 1$ [S1].

In the polynomial $x^2 + 2x$, the expressions $x^2$ and $2x$ are called the terms of the polynomial [S1]. The polynomial $3y^2 + 5y + 7$ has three terms, namely $3y^2$, $5y$ and $7$ [S1].

Each term of a polynomial has a coefficient [S1]. In $-x^3 + 4x^2 + 7x - 2$, the coefficient of $x^3$ is $-1$, the coefficient of $x^2$ is 4, the coefficient of $x$ is 7, and $-2$ is the coefficient of $x^0$, because $x^0 = 1$ [S1]. In $x^2 - x + 7$, the coefficient of $x$ is $-1$ [S1].

## Types of Polynomials

A polynomial can have any finite number of terms [S1]. For instance, $x^{150} + x^{149} + \ldots + x^2 + x + 1$ is a polynomial with 151 terms [S1]. Polynomials are named by their number of terms [S1]:

*   A polynomial with only one term is called a monomial ("mono" means "one"). Examples are $2x$, $2$, $5x^3$, $-5x^2$, $y$ and $u^4$.
*   A polynomial with only two terms is called a binomial ("bi" means "two"). Examples are $x + 1$, $x^2 - x$, $y^9 + 1$ and $u^{15} - u^2$.
*   A polynomial with only three terms is called a trinomial ("tri" means "three"). Examples are $x + x^2 + \pi$, $u + u^2 - 2$, $\sqrt{2} + x - x^2$ and $y^4 + y + 5$.

## Degree of a Polynomial

The highest power of the variable in a polynomial is called the degree of the polynomial [S1]. In $p(x) = 3x^7 - 4x^6 + x + 9$ the term with the highest power of $x$ is $3x^7$, so the degree is 7 [S1]. In $q(y) = 5y^6 - 4y^2 - 6$ the term with the highest power of $y$ is $5y^6$, so the degree is 6 [S1]. The degree of a non-zero constant polynomial is zero [S1].

A polynomial of degree one is called a linear polynomial, for example $4x + 5$, $2y$ and $3 - u$ [S1]. A linear polynomial in $x$ can have at most two terms, and it is of the form $ax + b$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants and $a \neq 0$ [S1].

A polynomial of degree two is called a quadratic polynomial, for example $5 - y^2$, $4y + 5y^2$ and $6 - y - y^2$ [S1]. A quadratic polynomial in $x$ has at most 3 terms, and it is of the form $ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a \neq 0$ and $a$, $b$, $c$ are constants [S1].

A polynomial of degree three is called a cubic polynomial, for example $4x^3$, $2x^3 + 1$ and $2x^3 + 4x^2 + 6x + 7$ [S1]. A cubic polynomial in one variable can have at most 4 terms, and it is of the form $ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d$, where $a \neq 0$ and $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$ are constants [S1].

## Constant and Zero Polynomials

The numbers 2, $-5$, 7 and so on are examples of constant polynomials [S1]. The constant polynomial 0 is called the zero polynomial [S1]. The degree of the zero polynomial is not defined [S1].

## Expressions That Are Not Polynomials

Not every algebraic expression is a polynomial. We can write $x + \frac{1}{x} = x + x^{-1}$, and the exponent of the second term, $-1$, is not a whole number, so this expression is not a polynomial [S1]. Again, $\sqrt{x} + 3$ can be written as $x^{\frac{1}{2}} + 3$, where the exponent of $x$ is $\frac{1}{2}$, which is not a whole number, so it is not a polynomial either [S1].

## General Form

A polynomial in one variable $x$ of degree $n$ is an expression of the form $a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0$, where $a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ are constants and $a_n \neq 0$ [S1]. If all the constants $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n$ are zero, we get the zero polynomial, which is denoted by 0 [S1].

## Worked Example

Find the degree of each of the polynomials $x^5 - x^4 + 3$, $2 - y^2 - y^3 + 2y^8$ and $2$ [S1].

*   In $x^5 - x^4 + 3$ the highest power of the variable is 5, so the degree is 5.
*   In $2 - y^2 - y^3 + 2y^8$ the highest power of the variable is 8, so the degree is 8.
*   The only term in $2$ is 2, which can be written as $2x^0$. The exponent of $x$ is 0, so the degree is 0.

## Key points

*   A polynomial in one variable is an expression in which the variable has only whole numbers as exponents [S1].
*   The parts of a polynomial are called its terms, and each term has a coefficient [S1].
*   Polynomials with one, two and three terms are called monomials, binomials and trinomials [S1].
*   The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable [S1].
*   Polynomials of degree one, two and three are called linear, quadratic and cubic polynomials [S1].
*   The degree of a non-zero constant polynomial is zero, and the degree of the zero polynomial is not defined [S1].
