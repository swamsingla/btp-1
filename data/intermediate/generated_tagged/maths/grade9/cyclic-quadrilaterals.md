# Cyclic Quadrilaterals

A cyclic quadrilateral is a special type of four-sided shape where every single corner touches the edge of a single circle [S1]. You can think of it like a table perfectly placed so that all four legs rest exactly on the edge of a round rug. This special connection to a circle gives the shape a very neat property regarding its angles that you can use to solve many geometry problems.

## Defining a Cyclic Quadrilateral

To understand the rules of a cyclic quadrilateral, you must first know what it is. A quadrilateral ABCD is called cyclic if all the four vertices of it lie on a circle [S1]. This means that if you draw a circle and pick any four distinct points on its circumference, connecting them in order creates a cyclic quadrilateral. You can verify this by drawing several circles of different radii and taking four points on each of them [S1].

## The Opposite Angles Property

The most important feature of a cyclic quadrilateral involves its opposite angles. If you measure the opposite angles of several different cyclic quadrilaterals and add them together, you will find a consistent result [S1]. The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is $180^{\circ}$ [S1]. This means that $\angle A + \angle C = 180^{\circ}$ and $\angle B + \angle D = 180^{\circ}$, neglecting the small error that might occur during physical measurement [S1].

This rule is highly useful for finding missing angles when you know the other angles in the shape.

## The Converse Theorem

Just as there is a rule for cyclic quadrilaterals, there is a converse theorem that works in reverse. If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is $180^{\circ}$, the quadrilateral is cyclic [S1]. This theorem allows you to prove that a four-sided shape can be drawn inside a circle just by measuring its angles and seeing if they add up to a straight line.

## Applying the Property in Problems

You can use the opposite angles property to solve various geometry questions. For instance, if you know three of the angles in a cyclic quadrilateral, you can easily find the fourth one. Let us say you have a cyclic quadrilateral where one angle is $100^{\circ}$. Since the opposite angle must add up to $180^{\circ}$, you can immediately calculate the missing angle by subtracting $100^{\circ}$ from $180^{\circ}$, which gives you $80^{\circ}$.

## Worked example

Here is an example of how to use the rules for a cyclic quadrilateral. Consider a cyclic quadrilateral where the angle at vertex A is $110^{\circ}$ and the angle at vertex B is $70^{\circ}$. You need to find the angle at vertex C.

First, we know that the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is $180^{\circ}$ [S1]. The opposite angle to A is C, so we can write the equation $\angle A + \angle C = 180^{\circ}$ [S1]. Substituting the known value, we get $110^{\circ} + \angle C = 180^{\circ}$. To find the value of $\angle C$, we subtract $110^{\circ}$ from $180^{\circ}$, which gives us $\angle C = 70^{\circ}$.

## Key points

*   A quadrilateral is cyclic if all four vertices lie on a circle [S1].
*   The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is $180^{\circ}$ [S1].
*   If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is $180^{\circ}$, the quadrilateral is cyclic [S1].
*   You can use the $180^{\circ}$ rule to find a missing angle in a cyclic quadrilateral [S1].
*   The converse theorem allows you to prove a shape is cyclic by checking its angle measurements [S1].
