# Arc and Chord Properties

In this section, we explore how chords and arcs interact inside a circle. Understanding these properties helps us measure angles and lengths without needing a protractor or a ruler every time. We will look at how the length of a chord relates to the angle it creates at the centre, and how the shortest path from the centre to a chord always cuts it in half.

## The Angle Subtended by a Chord

When we talk about the angle subtended by a chord, we are looking at the angle formed by drawing lines from the endpoints of the chord to another point. If we join the endpoints of a chord to the centre of the circle, we create an angle at the centre. This angle is called the angle subtended by the chord at the centre. We can also draw lines from the endpoints of the chord to any other point on the circle to form angles at the circumference.

There is a direct relationship between the length of a chord and the angle it subtends at the centre. If we draw a longer chord, it will create a larger angle at the centre. Conversely, shorter chords create smaller angles. For instance, if we take two equal chords, they will always subtend equal angles at the centre.

**Example:**
Imagine a circle with centre $O$. Let us draw two equal chords, $AB$ and $CD$. The angle $\angle AOB$ is the angle subtended by chord $AB$ at the centre. Similarly, $\angle COD$ is the angle subtended by chord $CD$ at the centre. Because $AB = CD$, the angle $\angle AOB$ is exactly equal to the angle $\angle COD$.

## Equal Chords and Equal Angles

A very useful rule in geometry is that equal chords always subtend equal angles at the centre. This means that if two chords in a circle have the exact same length, the angles they form at the centre are also the same size. We can prove this using the SSS rule for triangle congruence, where the two sides are radii of the circle and the base is the equal chord.

The converse is also true. If two chords subtend equal angles at the centre, then those chords must be equal in length.

**Example:**
Suppose we have a circle with centre $O$ and two chords $AB$ and $CD$. We are given that $\angle AOB = \angle COD$. We can conclude that the lengths of the chords are equal, so $AB = CD$.

## Perpendicular from the Centre Bisects a Chord

Another important property involves the distance from the centre of a circle to a chord. The shortest distance from the centre to a chord is always the perpendicular line dropped from the centre to the chord. This perpendicular line does not just meet the chord; it cuts it exactly in half.

**Example:**
Consider a chord $AB$ in a circle with centre $O$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. If we join $O$ to $M$, we get the line segment $OM$. The line segment $OM$ is perpendicular to $AB$. This means that the angle $\angle OMA$ and the angle $\angle OMB$ are both $90^{\circ}$.

## Equal Chords and Their Distances

The length of a chord is closely related to its distance from the centre. If we draw two chords of equal length, the perpendicular distances from the centre to these chords will be exactly the same. This is true for any circle, and it also applies to congruent circles.

The converse is also true. If two chords are at the same distance from the centre, they must be equal in length.

**Example:**
Imagine a circle with centre $O$. We draw two chords $AB$ and $CD$ such that the perpendicular distance from $O$ to $AB$ is equal to the perpendicular distance from $O$ to $CD$. We can conclude that the lengths of the chords are equal, so $AB = CD$.

## Worked Example

Let us apply these rules to a slightly more complex problem. Suppose we have a circle with centre $O$ and two chords $AB$ and $CD$ that intersect at a point $E$ inside the circle. Let us say that a diameter $PQ$ passes through $E$ and makes equal angles with the chords, such that $\angle AEQ = \angle DEQ$. We want to prove that the chords $AB$ and $CD$ are equal.

To solve this, we draw perpendiculars from the centre $O$ to the chords. Let $OL$ be the perpendicular to $AB$ and $OM$ be the perpendicular to $CD$. Because $\angle AEQ = \angle DEQ$, the angles $\angle LEO$ and $\angle MEO$ are equal. Also, the angles $\angle LOE$ and $\angle MOE$ are equal. Since $EO$ is a common side to both triangles $OLE$ and $OME$, we can prove that these two triangles are congruent. From this congruence, we can conclude that the perpendicular distances $OL$ and $OM$ are equal. Since equal chords are equidistant from the centre, the chords $AB$ and $CD$ must be equal.

## Key points

- A chord subtends an angle at the centre of the circle.
- Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.
- If two chords subtend equal angles at the centre, they are equal in length.
- The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
- Equal chords of a circle are equidistant from the centre.
- Chords equidistant from the centre of a circle are equal in length.
