# Properties of Parallelogram

You may already know that a parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides. In Grade 8, you learned that opposite sides, opposite angles, and adjacent angles have specific relationships within this shape. This page builds on that foundation by exploring the deeper properties of the diagonals and the converse theorems, which allow you to prove that a given quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

## Diagonals Bisect Each Other

One of the most important properties of a parallelogram is how its diagonals interact. If you draw a parallelogram and measure the segments of its diagonals, you will find that they cut each other exactly in half.

Theorem 8.6 states that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. This means the point where the two diagonals intersect is the midpoint of both diagonals. If the diagonals intersect at a point $O$, then $OA = OC$ and $OB = OD$ [S1].

The converse of this theorem is also true. Theorem 8.7 states that if the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, then it is a parallelogram [S1]. To prove this, you can use the Side-Side-Side congruence rule to show that the triangles formed by the diagonals are congruent, which in turn proves that the opposite sides are parallel.

## Opposite Sides are Equal

Another fundamental property is that the opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal in length. Theorem 8.2 states that in a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal [S1].

You can prove this by drawing a diagonal, which divides the parallelogram into two congruent triangles. Since the triangles are congruent, their corresponding sides must be equal [S1].

The converse of this property is also true. Theorem 8.3 states that if each pair of opposite sides of a quadrilateral is equal, then it is a parallelogram [S1].

## Opposite Angles are Equal

Similarly, the opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal. Theorem 8.4 states that in a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal [S1].

The converse of this property is also true. Theorem 8.5 states that if in a quadrilateral, each pair of opposite angles is equal, then it is a parallelogram [S1].

## Diagonal Divides into Congruent Triangles

A key property of parallelograms is that a diagonal divides it into two congruent triangles. Theorem 8.1 states that a diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles [S1].

Proof: Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram and $AC$ be a diagonal. The diagonal $AC$ divides the parallelogram into two triangles, namely $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta CDA$. Since $BC \parallel AD$ and $AC$ is a transversal, $\angle BCA = \angle DAC$ (alternate angles). Also, since $AB \parallel DC$ and $AC$ is a transversal, $\angle BAC = \angle DCA$ (alternate angles). And $AC = CA$ (common). So, $\Delta ABC \cong \Delta CDA$ (ASA rule) [S1].

## Worked example

Consider a parallelogram $ABCD$ where the length of side $AB$ is $8$ cm and the length of side $AD$ is $6$ cm. The diagonal $AC$ intersects the other diagonal $BD$ at point $O$.

Since opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal, we know that $AB = DC = 8$ cm and $AD = BC = 6$ cm [S1].

Since the diagonals bisect each other, we know that $OA = OC$ and $OB = OD$ [S1].

If the length of diagonal $AC$ is $10$ cm, then the length of $OA$ and $OC$ is $5$ cm each.

## Key points

*   A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
*   A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles [S1].
*   In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal [S1].
*   In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal [S1].
*   The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other [S1].
*   If each pair of opposite sides of a quadrilateral is equal, then it is a parallelogram [S1].
*   If each pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is equal, then it is a parallelogram [S1].
*   If the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, then it is a parallelogram [S1].
