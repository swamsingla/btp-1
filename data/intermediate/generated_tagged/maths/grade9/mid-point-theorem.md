# Mid-Point Theorem

The Mid-Point Theorem is a powerful result about triangles. It tells us exactly what happens when we connect the middle of two sides. This rule is extremely useful because it lets us find unknown lengths and prove that lines are parallel without measuring them. If you know the length of the base of a triangle, you immediately know the length of the line joining the midpoints of the other two sides.

## The Rule

In any triangle, if you take the midpoint of two sides and join them, the new line segment is parallel to the third side and exactly half its length. For example, in triangle $ABC$, if $E$ and $F$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$, then the line segment $EF$ is parallel to the base $BC$, and $EF = \frac{1}{2} BC$ [S1]. 

This relationship is very useful in geometry. It means that a line drawn through the midpoint of one side, parallel to another side, will automatically bisect the third side [S1]. 

## Proof

You can prove this theorem by extending the sides of the triangle and using congruent triangles. Consider triangle $ABC$ where $E$ and $F$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$. Now, extend the side $AC$ to a point $D$ such that $CD = AB$, and join $D$ to $B$ [S1]. 

By the SAS congruence rule, triangle $AEF$ is congruent to triangle $CDF$. Because they are congruent, their corresponding sides are equal, so $EF = DF$. Also, $BE = AE = DC$. Since $BE = DC$ and $BE \parallel DC$, the quadrilateral $BCDE$ is a parallelogram. A key property of a parallelogram is that its diagonals bisect each other, which means $BC$ and $DE$ cut each other in half. Therefore, $EF$ is exactly half of $BC$.

## Worked Example

Let us apply this theorem to a specific problem. Imagine a triangular plot of land $ABC$ where $D$ and $E$ are the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $BC$ respectively. A surveyor wants to know the length of the fence connecting $D$ to $E$, and also wants to know if this fence is parallel to the base $AC$ [S1].

Because $D$ and $E$ are the midpoints of the sides of the triangle, we can apply the Mid-Point Theorem. The theorem states that the line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side [S1]. Therefore, the fence $DE$ is parallel to the base $AC$. 

Furthermore, the theorem states that this joining line is exactly half the length of the third side. If the base $AC$ measures $20$ metres, then the fence $DE$ must measure exactly half of that. So, $DE = \frac{1}{2} \times 20 = 10$ metres. The surveyor can now confidently tell the landowner that the fence is $10$ metres long and runs perfectly parallel to the river.

## Key points

*   The line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.
*   The length of this joining line segment is exactly half the length of the third side.
*   The converse is also true: a line drawn through the midpoint of one side, parallel to another side, bisects the third side.
*   If you join the midpoints of all three sides of a triangle, you divide it into four smaller, congruent triangles.
*   You can use this theorem to prove that three parallel lines cut off equal intercepts on any transversal.
