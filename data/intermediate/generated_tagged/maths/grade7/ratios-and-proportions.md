# Ratios and Proportions

Ratios and proportions are tools we use to compare two quantities. A ratio tells us how much of one thing there is for every amount of another thing. Proportions help us check if two different ratios actually describe the same relationship. These ideas are useful in many everyday situations, from mixing ingredients in the kitchen to reading a map.

## What is a Ratio

A ratio compares two quantities by dividing one by the other. We write a ratio using a colon between two numbers. For example, if you have 60 apples and 40 oranges, the ratio of apples to oranges is $60:40$ [S1]. 

In a ratio written as $a:b$, the first number $a$ represents the units of the first quantity. The second number $b$ represents the units of the second quantity. So, for every 60 units of the first quantity, there are 40 units of the second quantity [S1].

## Ratios in Their Simplest Form

Sometimes ratios use large numbers. We can make them easier to read by reducing them to their simplest form. We do this by dividing both terms by their HCF. The HCF is the Highest Common Factor, which is the largest number that divides both terms exactly.

Let us take the ratio $60:40$. The HCF of 60 and 40 is 20. If we divide both terms by 20, we get $3:2$ [S2]. This is the simplest form of the ratio. 

Let us look at another ratio, $90:60$. The HCF of 90 and 60 is 30. Dividing both terms by 30 gives us $3:2$ again [S2]. 

## Proportional Ratios

When two ratios are the same in their simplest forms, we say that the ratios are in proportion. This means the two ratios are proportional. We use the double colon symbol $::$ to show that two ratios are proportional [S2].

For example, $60:40::30:20$ shows that the ratio $60:40$ and the ratio $30:20$ are proportional [S2]. We can also write $60:40::90:60$. 

On the other hand, if we compare $60:40$ to $40:20$, the simplest forms are $3:2$ and $2:1$. These are not the same, so the ratios are not proportional [S2].

## Maps and Ratios

You can find ratios in everyday objects like maps. Maps usually have a ratio printed in the corner. This ratio is called a Representative Fraction. It shows the relationship between a distance on the map and the actual distance on the ground.

For example, a map might have a ratio of $1:60,00,000$ [S3]. This means that 1 unit on the map equals 60,00,000 units in the real world. If the map uses centimetres, then 1 cm on the map equals 60,00,000 cm in reality.

To understand this better, we can convert 60,00,000 cm to kilometres. Since there are 100,000 cm in a kilometre, 60,00,000 cm is equal to 60 km [S3].

## Worked example

Let us use these ideas to solve a problem. Imagine you are looking at a map. The scale of the map is $1:60,00,000$. On the map, the distance between Bengaluru and Chennai is 5 cm. 

First, we find the actual distance in centimetres. We multiply the map distance by the scale factor: $5 \times 60,00,000 = 3,00,00,000$ cm. 

Now, we convert this to kilometres. We divide by 1,00,000: $3,00,00,000 \div 1,00,000 = 300$ km. 

So, the geographical distance between Bengaluru and Chennai is 300 km [S3].

## Key points

* A ratio compares two quantities using a colon, like $a:b$ [S1].
* We can reduce a ratio to its simplest form by dividing both terms by their HCF [S2].
* When two ratios have the same simplest form, they are in proportion [S2].
* We write a proportion using the double colon symbol, like $a:b::c:d$ [S2].
* A Representative Fraction on a map shows the ratio between the map distance and the actual distance [S3].
