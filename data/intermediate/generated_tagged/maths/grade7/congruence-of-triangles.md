# Congruence of Triangles

Congruence of triangles is a fundamental concept in geometry that deals with figures having the exact same size and shape. When two triangles are congruent, they are identical in every way, meaning their corresponding sides and angles match perfectly. This concept is highly useful in everyday life, such as when carpenters or engineers need to create identical triangular frames, cutouts, or structural supports for buildings and bridges.

## SSS Condition

The Side-Side-Side (SSS) condition states that if two triangles have the same sidelengths, then they are congruent [S1]. Meera and Rabia discovered this while trying to replicate a triangular frame. They measured the sides to be 40 cm, 60 cm, and 80 cm. Meera correctly pointed out that they did not need to measure the angles, since the three sidelengths alone should be enough to construct a triangle congruent to the original. To test whether this is really true, imagine a smaller triangle with sidelengths 4 cm, 6 cm, and 8 cm instead, which is easier to draw on a page. If three given sidelengths always force a unique shape and size, then every triangle built from these three lengths should turn out identical to every other [S1].

Rabia initially worried that drawing circles from the endpoints of a 6 cm line segment with radii of 4 cm and 8 cm would create two different triangles, $\Delta$ABE and $\Delta$ABF. However, these two triangles are actually congruent [S1]. This means that any two triangles with the exact same three sidelengths will always be identical in shape and size.

## Corresponding Parts

When two triangles are congruent, they have corresponding vertices, sides, and angles that fit exactly over each other when overlapped [S1]. For example, if $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta XYZ$ are congruent, we write $\Delta ABC \cong \Delta XYZ$. This notation means that vertex A corresponds to vertex X, vertex B to vertex Y, and vertex C to vertex Z. 

Because of this correspondence, the equal sides overlap perfectly, and the equal angles are also identical. Specifically, $\angle A = \angle X$, $\angle B = \angle Y$, and $\angle C = \angle Z$ [S1]. It is important to keep the vertices in the correct order when writing the congruence statement; for instance, writing $\Delta ACB \cong \Delta XYZ$ would be incorrect because it mismatches the corresponding sides [S1].

## RHS Condition

The Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side (RHS) condition is another way to establish congruence. This rule applies specifically to right-angled triangles. For instance, in an isosceles triangle where the two equal sides are 5 cm and the base is 8 cm, drawing an altitude from the apex to the base creates two smaller triangles. These smaller triangles have a common side, equal hypotenuses, and both contain a right angle. Therefore, they satisfy the RHS condition and are congruent [S2].

This property is extremely useful for proving other geometric facts. For example, in an equilateral triangle, all sides are equal. By drawing an altitude, we split the triangle into two right-angled triangles. Since the hypotenuses are equal, the common side is equal, and the angles formed by the altitude are both 90 degrees, the two resulting triangles are congruent by the RHS condition [S2].

## Congruence in Real Life

Congruent triangles are not just an abstract mathematical idea; they are visible in the world around us. Architects and designers use congruent triangles to create stable and aesthetically pleasing structures. The World-famous Louvre Museum in Paris, the Egyptian Pyramid of Giza, and the dome designs of various buildings all rely on the principles of congruence [S2]. 

Even in everyday art forms like Rangoli designs, congruent triangles are used to create symmetrical and beautiful patterns. The Rabindra Setu, also known as the Howrah Bridge in Kolkata, also features structural elements based on congruent triangles [S2].

## Worked example

Consider two triangles, $\Delta ABD$ and $\Delta CDB$, which are formed inside a rectangle. In a rectangle, the opposite sides are equal in length. Therefore, the side AB is equal to CD, and the side AD is equal to CB [S1]. The two triangles also share the side BD. Since all three corresponding sides are equal, the SSS condition is satisfied. This confirms that $\Delta ABD$ and $\Delta CDB$ are congruent [S1].

## Key points

- Congruence means two triangles have the exact same size and shape, with all corresponding sides and angles matching.
- The SSS condition states that if three sides of one triangle are equal to three sides of another, the triangles are congruent.
- The RHS condition proves that two right-angled triangles are congruent if their hypotenuses and one corresponding side are equal.
- When writing a congruence statement, the order of the vertices must match the corresponding parts exactly.
- Congruent triangles are widely used in architecture, engineering, and art.
