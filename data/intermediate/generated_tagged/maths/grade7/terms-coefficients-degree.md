# Terms, Coefficients and Degree

Algebraic expressions are powerful tools that help us describe patterns and relationships in a concise manner [S1]. When we write an expression, it is made up of smaller pieces called terms. Understanding these pieces is the first step to mastering algebra.

## What is a Term?

A term is a single number, a single variable, or a product of numbers and variables added together with plus or minus signs. In an expression like $2a - b$, there are two terms. The first term is $2a$, and the second term is $-b$ [S1]. Each term is separated by a plus or minus sign.

## What is a Numerical Coefficient?

A numerical coefficient is the number that multiplies a variable in a term. In the term $2a$, the numerical coefficient is $2$ [S1]. It tells us how many of that variable we have. If a term is just a number, like $5$ or $-8$, it has no variable attached to it and is called a constant term rather than being assigned a numerical coefficient.

## What is the Degree of an Expression?

The degree of a term is the sum of the powers of its variables. The degree of an expression is the highest degree among all its terms. For example, in the expression $3 + 2y + y^2$, the degrees of the terms are $0$, $1$, and $2$ respectively. Therefore, the degree of the expression is $2$ [S1].

## Worked example

Consider the expression $3 + 2y + y^2$. The terms are $3$, $2y$, and $y^2$. The numerical coefficient of $y^2$ is $1$. The degree of the term $2y$ is $1$. The degree of the expression $3 + 2y + y^2$ is $2$ because $2$ is the highest degree among its terms [S1].

## Key points

*   A term is a single number or a product of numbers and variables separated by plus or minus signs [S1].
*   A numerical coefficient is the number multiplying a variable in a term [S1].
*   The degree of a term is the sum of the powers of its variables, and the degree of an expression is the highest degree of its terms [S1].
*   The expression $2a - b$ has two terms, $2a$ and $-b$ [S1].
*   Algebraic expressions are used to describe patterns and relationships in a concise and elegant manner [S1].
