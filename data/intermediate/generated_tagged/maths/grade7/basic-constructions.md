# Basic Geometric Constructions

A geometric construction draws a figure exactly, using only an unmarked ruler and a compass. The ruler draws straight lines and the compass carries a length from one place to another, so nothing has to be measured or guessed. You may already know how to use two arcs to find a point at a given distance from two other points. The same idea gives three constructions that are used again and again: cutting a line segment into two equal parts at a right angle, cutting an angle into two equal parts, and making an angle of exactly $60^\circ$.

## Perpendicular Bisector

A division of a line segment, or any geometrical quantity, into two identical parts is called bisection. [S1] A line that bisects a given line and is perpendicular to it, is called the perpendicular bisector. [S1]

We can construct a perpendicular bisector using the simplest geometric tools — an unmarked ruler and a compass. [S1] The method rests on one fact about a line segment $XY$: joining any two points, one above $XY$ and one below, that are each at equal distances from $X$ and $Y$ gives the perpendicular bisector of $XY$. [S1]

1. Taking some fixed radius, from $X$ and then $Y$, construct two sufficiently long arcs above $XY$. Name the point where the arcs meet as $A$. [S1]
2. Using the same radius, from $X$ and then $Y$, construct two sufficiently long arcs below $XY$. Name the point where the arcs meet as $B$. [S1]
3. $AB$ is the required perpendicular bisector. [S1]

Any point that is of equal distance from the two endpoints of a given line segment lies on its perpendicular bisector. [S1] This property can be used to construct the perpendicular bisector using a ruler and compass. [S1]

## Angle Bisection

An angle can be bisected and copied using the congruence properties of triangles. [S4]

To bisect an angle whose vertex is $O$, follow these steps: [S2]
1. Mark a point $A$ on one arm of the angle and a point $B$ on the other arm such that $OA = OB$. [S2]
2. Choosing any sufficiently long radius, cut arcs from $A$ and $B$, keeping the radius same. Mark the point of intersection as $C$. [S2]
3. $OC$ bisects $\angle AOB$. [S2]

## Constructing a $60^\circ$ Angle

A $60^\circ$ angle can be constructed using a ruler and a compass by constructing an equilateral triangle. [S3]

Suppose we need a $60^\circ$ angle at point $A$ on a line segment $AX$. [S3]
Step 1: Construct an arc with centre $A$ and any radius. [S3] Name the point where this arc cuts $AX$ as $B$.
Step 2: With the same radius, cut another arc from $B$ that meets the first arc. Let $C$ be the point at which the arcs meet. [S3]

The lengths $AB$, $AC$ and $BC$ are all equal to the radius, so $ABC$ is an equilateral triangle and $\angle CAX = 60^\circ$. [S3]

## Related Constructions

The method of constructing the perpendicular bisector can be modified to draw a $90^\circ$ angle at any point on a line using only a ruler and compass. [S4]

A $45^\circ$ angle can be constructed by first constructing a $90^\circ$ angle and then bisecting it. [S2]

A $30^\circ$ angle can be constructed by bisecting a $60^\circ$ angle. [S3]
A $15^\circ$ angle can be constructed by bisecting a $30^\circ$ angle. [S3]

## Worked example

Let us construct a $30^\circ$ angle at a point $A$ on a line $AX$, by making a $60^\circ$ angle and then bisecting it.

Step 1: With centre $A$ and any radius, draw an arc that cuts $AX$. Name the point where it cuts $AX$ as $B$.
Step 2: With the same radius and centre $B$, cut the first arc. Name the point where the arcs meet as $C$. Now $\angle CAB = 60^\circ$.
Step 3: The points $B$ and $C$ are already at the same distance from $A$, so they can be used to bisect the angle. Choose any sufficiently long radius, cut an arc from $B$ and an arc from $C$ keeping the radius the same, and name the point where these arcs meet as $D$.
Step 4: Join $A$ and $D$.

The line $AD$ bisects $\angle CAB$, so $\angle DAB$ is half of $60^\circ$, which is $30^\circ$.

## Key points

*   A division of a line segment, or any geometrical quantity, into two identical parts is called bisection. [S4]
*   Any point that is of equal distance from the two endpoints of a given line segment lies on its perpendicular bisector. [S4]
*   The method of constructing the perpendicular bisector can be modified to draw a $90^\circ$ angle at any point on a line using only a ruler and compass. [S4]
*   An angle can be bisected and copied using the congruence properties of triangles. [S4]
*   A $60^\circ$ angle can be constructed using a ruler and a compass by constructing an equilateral triangle. [S4]
