# Similar Triangles

Two triangles are called similar if they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size[S1]. When you look at a photo of a tall building and then at the actual building, or when you look at a small drawing of a map and the real map, the triangles formed by their corners are similar[S1]. The angles in both triangles are exactly the same[S1], and the lengths of their corresponding sides are in the same proportion or ratio[S1]. Understanding similar triangles is useful because if you know the measurements of one triangle, you can find the missing measurements of a similar triangle by using a scale factor[S1].

## Corresponding Parts

When two triangles are similar, their corresponding vertices, sides, and angles are related in a specific way[S1]. Corresponding vertices are the corners that match up when the triangles are placed on top of each other. Corresponding sides are the sides that lie next to each other between these matching corners, and corresponding angles are the angles at these matching corners[S1]. If one triangle has angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$, and another similar triangle has a $30^\circ$ angle, then the other two angles in the second triangle must also be $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$[S1]. This is because similar triangles maintain the exact same angles[S1].

## Proportional Sides

The most important feature of similar triangles is that their corresponding sides are proportional[S1]. This means that if you divide the length of a side in the first triangle by the length of the matching side in the second triangle, you will get the exact same number for all three sides[S1]. This constant ratio is called the scale factor[S1]. For example, if a triangle has sides of lengths 3, 4, and 5, and a similar triangle has sides of lengths 6, 8, and 10, the ratio of the second triangle's sides to the first triangle's sides is 2 for all three sides[S1]. Therefore, the scale factor is 2[S1].

## AA, SAS, and SSS Similarity

You do not need to measure all the sides and all the angles to prove that two triangles are similar[S1]. There are three main criteria, or rules, that you can use[S1]. The first is the AA, or Angle-Angle, criterion[S1]. If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar[S1]. The second is the SAS, or Side-Angle-Side, criterion[S1]. If two sides of one triangle are proportional to two sides of another triangle, and the angle included between those sides is equal, then the triangles are similar[S1]. The third is the SSS, or Side-Side-Side, criterion[S1]. If all three sides of one triangle are proportional to the three sides of another triangle, the triangles are similar[S1].

## Worked example

Let us look at a practical example[S1]. Imagine you are making a small model of a triangular garden bed. The actual garden bed has side lengths of 10 metres, 12 metres, and 15 metres[S1]. You want to make a model that is exactly one-fifth the size of the actual bed. To find the side lengths of the model, you multiply each side length of the actual bed by the scale factor of $\frac{1}{5}$[S1]. The first side will be $10 \times \frac{1}{5} = 2$ metres, the second side will be $12 \times \frac{1}{5} = 2.4$ metres, and the third side will be $15 \times \frac{1}{5} = 3$ metres[S1]. Because the sides are in the same proportion[S1], the model triangle is similar to the actual triangle[S1].

## Key points
- Similar triangles have the same shape, meaning their corresponding angles are equal[S1].
- The corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional to each other[S1].
- The AA, SAS, and SSS criteria are used to prove that two triangles are similar[S1].
- The ratio of the lengths of corresponding sides is called the scale factor[S1].
- Corresponding parts are the matching vertices, sides, and angles between the two triangles[S1].
