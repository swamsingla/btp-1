# Place Value and Indian Numeration

Place value is the value of a digit based on where it sits in a number. It matters because it is the foundation for reading, writing, and comparing large numbers. Without knowing place value, a number like 5000 would look exactly the same as 500, making it impossible to tell them apart.

## The Power of Ten

The Indian numeration system is special because it is built on the number 10. In this system, each place value is 10 times bigger than the place value to its right. This means that 10 ones make 1 ten, 10 tens make 1 hundred, and 10 hundreds make 1 thousand [S1]. This pattern continues as we move to larger numbers.

This relationship also applies to smaller numbers. When we need to measure something more accurately, we split a unit into 10 equal parts to get one-tenth. Splitting one-tenth into 10 equal parts gives one-hundredth. Each place value to the right of the decimal point is 10 times smaller than the one to its left [S1].

## The Indian System of Numeration

The Indian system groups digits from right to left in a 3-2-2-2 pattern. This grouping uses the units, tens, and hundreds places first, then moves to thousands, lakhs, and crores [S2]. A lakh is written as 1 followed by 5 zeroes, and a crore is written as 1 followed by 7 zeroes [S2].

A lakh is a hundred times a thousand. A crore is a hundred times a lakh, and an arab is a hundred times a crore [S2].

## The International System

The International system, also called the American system, groups digits uniformly from right to left in a 3-3-3-3 pattern. This system uses units, thousands, millions, and billions [S2].

Here is how the two systems compare:
- 1 lakh is written as 100,000 in the International system, which is called one hundred thousand.
- 1 crore is written as 10,000,000 in the International system, which is called ten million.
- 1 arab, or one hundred crores, is written as 1,000,000,000 in the International system, which is called one billion [S2].

## Reading and Writing Large Numbers

To read a large number, we must first place the commas correctly. In the Indian system, we place commas to group the digits in a 3-2-2-2 pattern from right to left [S2].

For example, the number 9876501234 is read as 9,87,65,01,234 in the Indian system. This number is read as 9 arab 87 crore 65 lakhs 1 thousand and 234 [S2].

In the International system, the same number is written with commas in a 3-3-3-3 pattern. This gives us 9,876,501,234. This number is read as 9 billion 876 million 501 thousand 234 [S2].

## Worked Example

Let's write the number 50,00,000 in both systems and read it.

In the Indian system, we place commas in a 3-2-2-2 pattern. This gives us 50,00,000. This number is read as 50 lakhs [S2].

In the International system, we place commas in a 3-3-3-3 pattern. This gives us 5,000,000. This number is read as 5 million. So 50 lakhs and 5 million are two names for the same number; only the grouping of the digits is different.

## Key points

- Place value is the value of a digit based on its position in a number.
- The Indian numeration system is built on the number 10, where each place value is 10 times bigger than the one to its right.
- The Indian system groups digits in a 3-2-2-2 pattern from right to left.
- The International system groups digits in a 3-3-3-3 pattern from right to left.
- 1 lakh is 100,000, and 1 crore is 10,000,000.
