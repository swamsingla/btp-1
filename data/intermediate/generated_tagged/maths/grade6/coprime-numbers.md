# Co-prime Numbers

Co-prime numbers are pairs of numbers that do not share any common factor except 1. In other words, their Highest Common Factor (HCF) is exactly 1. This property is very useful in mathematics and helps us understand the relationship between two different numbers, especially when we are looking for their least common multiple (LCM).

## The Treasure Finding Game

Imagine you are playing a treasure finding game. In this game, two numbers hold the treasures. A character named Jumpy can only get the treasures if he can reach both numbers using the exact same jump size. However, there is a strict rule: a jump size of 1 is not allowed.

If the treasures are placed on 12 and 26, Jumpy can easily reach both. For example, if he chooses a jump size of 2, he will land exactly on both 12 and 26. Therefore, this pair is not safe.

On the other hand, if the treasures are placed on 4 and 9, Jumpy cannot reach both numbers using any jump size other than 1. Because a jump of 1 is forbidden, this pair is completely safe.

## What Makes a Pair Safe?

A safe pair is a pair of numbers that do not have any common factor other than 1. Two numbers are said to be **co-prime** to each other if they have no common factor other than 1.

For instance, the numbers 15 and 39 share a common factor of 3. Since they have a common factor greater than 1, they are not co-prime. However, the numbers 4 and 9 only share the factor 1, so they are co-prime.

## Co-prime Pairs and the Least Common Multiple

When we look at the least common multiple (LCM) of two numbers, the co-prime property becomes very clear. The LCM of two numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of both.

Sometimes, the first common multiple of a pair of numbers is exactly the same as the product of the two numbers. This happens specifically when the two numbers are co-prime. For example, if we take the co-prime numbers 4 and 9, their product is $4 \times 9 = 36$. The least common multiple of 4 and 9 is indeed 36.

At other times, the first common multiple is less than the product of the two numbers. This occurs when the numbers are not co-prime. For example, if we take 15 and 39, their product is $15 \times 39 = 585$. However, their least common multiple is 195, which is less than 585, because they share a common factor.

## Co-prime Art and Thread Patterns

The concept of co-prime numbers can also be seen in visual patterns, such as thread art. Imagine you have a board with a certain number of pegs. You take a thread and tie it to every peg that is a specific number of gaps away.

If the number of pegs and the thread-gap are co-prime, the thread will eventually touch every single peg on the board. If they are not co-prime, the thread will only touch a few of the pegs and leave the rest untouched.

For example, if you have 12 pegs and a thread-gap of 4, the thread will only touch 3 pegs. But if you have 13 pegs and a thread-gap of 3, the thread is co-prime with the number of pegs, and it will eventually touch all 13 pegs. This happens because the numbers share no common factors other than 1.

## Worked example

Let us look at a specific example to see how to identify co-prime numbers.

Consider the pair of numbers 18 and 29. To check if they are co-prime, we need to find their common factors. The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 18. The factors of 29 are 1 and 29, because 29 is a prime number.

Since the only common factor between 18 and 29 is 1, they are co-prime numbers.

Now consider the pair 20 and 55. The factors of 20 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, and 20. The factors of 55 are 1, 5, 11, and 55.

Here, the numbers share two common factors: 1 and 5. Because they have a common factor other than 1, they are not co-prime.

## Key points

*   Two numbers are co-prime if their Highest Common Factor (HCF) is 1.
*   Co-prime numbers do not share any common factor other than 1.
*   If two numbers are co-prime, their least common multiple (LCM) is equal to their product.
*   If two numbers are not co-prime, their LCM is less than their product.
*   Any prime number is co-prime with any other number that is not a multiple of it.
