# Types of Angles and Angle Pairs

Angles are everywhere. They are formed whenever two straight lines or rays meet at a single point, called the vertex [S1]. You can see them when you open a door, fold a piece of paper, or look at the hands of a clock. Understanding the different types of angles and how they relate to each other is an important part of geometry. It helps you describe the shapes of objects around you and solve everyday problems.

## Types of Angles

Angles are measured in degrees, which tell us how wide the angle is. A full circle contains $360^{\circ}$, and a straight line contains $180^{\circ}$ [S2]. By comparing an angle to these familiar sizes, we can give it a name.

An **acute angle** is any angle that is smaller than a right angle [S1]. This means its measure is greater than $0^{\circ}$ but less than $90^{\circ}$ [S1]. Imagine the sharp point of a pencil or the narrow opening of a pair of scissors; both are acute angles.

A **right angle** measures exactly $90^{\circ}$ [S1]. It is exactly half of a straight angle [S1][S2]. You can easily spot a right angle because it looks like the corner of a square or the letter 'L'. When two lines meet at a right angle, they are called perpendicular lines [S2].

An **obtuse angle** is larger than a right angle but smaller than a straight angle [S1]. Its measure is greater than $90^{\circ}$ and less than $180^{\circ}$ [S1]. Think of the wide, open mouth of a crocodile; this opening is an obtuse angle.

A **straight angle** measures exactly $180^{\circ}$ [S1]. It forms a perfectly straight line, like a flat road or the edge of a ruler.

Finally, a **reflex angle** is larger than a straight angle but smaller than a full circle [S1]. Its measure is greater than $180^{\circ}$ and less than $360^{\circ}$ [S1]. If you turn a doorknob almost all the way around but stop just before you reach the starting point, the space you turned through is a reflex angle.

## Complementary and Supplementary Angle Pairs

Sometimes, two angles work together to make a specific, important total. We call these pairs complementary or supplementary angles.

**Complementary angles** are two angles whose measures add up to exactly $90^{\circ}$ [S2]. For example, if one angle is $30^{\circ}$, the other angle must be $60^{\circ}$ to be complementary [S2]. Together, they form a perfect right angle [S2].

**Supplementary angles** are two angles whose measures add up to exactly $180^{\circ}$ [S2]. For example, if one angle is $120^{\circ}$, the other angle must be $60^{\circ}$ to be supplementary [S2]. Together, they form a perfectly straight line [S2].

## Worked example

Let us apply these rules to a practical problem. Imagine you are drawing a design for a rangoli. All the lines start from the same centre point. The first line makes a $70^{\circ}$ angle with the second line. You then draw a third line on the far side of the second, so that it makes a $20^{\circ}$ angle with the second line.

Because the second line lies between the first and the third, the angle from the first line round to the third is the sum of the two: $70^{\circ} + 20^{\circ} = 90^{\circ}$. So the first and third lines form a right angle. This adding only works when the middle line sits between the other two; if the third line were drawn on the same side as the first, the angle between them would be the difference instead, $70^{\circ} - 20^{\circ} = 50^{\circ}$.

Keeping the lines in the same order, suppose a fourth line is drawn making a $110^{\circ}$ angle with the second line, again on the far side from the first. Then the angle from the first line round to the fourth is $70^{\circ} + 110^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}$, which is a straight angle, so the first and fourth lines lie flat along one straight line.

## Key points

- An acute angle is less than $90^{\circ}$ [S1].
- A right angle is exactly $90^{\circ}$ [S1].
- An obtuse angle is greater than $90^{\circ}$ but less than $180^{\circ}$ [S1].
- A straight angle is exactly $180^{\circ}$ [S1].
- A reflex angle is greater than $180^{\circ}$ but less than $360^{\circ}$ [S1].
- Complementary angles add up to $90^{\circ}$ [S2].
- Supplementary angles add up to $180^{\circ}$ [S2].
