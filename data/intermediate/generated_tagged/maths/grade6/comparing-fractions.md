# Comparing and Ordering Fractions

Comparing fractions is a skill you use every day, from deciding which piece of a chocolate bar is bigger to checking if you have more or less pocket money than a friend [S1]. When two fractions have the same denominator, you can look at the numerators and immediately know which is larger. But what happens when the denominators are different, like $\frac{4}{5}$ and $\frac{7}{9}$? You cannot compare them directly because the pieces are different sizes. To figure out which is bigger, you have to make the pieces the same size first.

## Using Common Denominators

The easiest way to compare fractions is to rewrite them so they share the same denominator. A common denominator is simply a number that both of the original denominators divide into evenly [S1]. Once the fractions have the same denominator, they are measured in the same fractional units, which makes comparing them as simple as comparing whole numbers.

Let us look at an example. We want to compare $\frac{4}{5}$ and $\frac{7}{9}$ [S1]. We need to find a common denominator for 5 and 9. We can multiply 5 by 9 to get 45. Now, we rewrite both fractions so they have 45 as the denominator.

For the first fraction, we multiply the top and the bottom by 9: [S1]
$$ \frac{4 \times 9}{5 \times 9} = \frac{36}{45} $$

For the second fraction, we multiply the top and the bottom by 5: [S1]
$$ \frac{7 \times 5}{9 \times 5} = \frac{35}{45} $$

Now we can compare $\frac{36}{45}$ and $\frac{35}{45}$. Since 36 is greater than 35, we know that $\frac{36}{45}$ is greater than $\frac{35}{45}$ [S1]. This means the original fraction $\frac{4}{5}$ is greater than $\frac{7}{9}$ [S1].

Let us try another pair: $\frac{7}{9}$ and $\frac{17}{21}$ [S1]. The least common multiple of 9 and 21 is 63. We convert both fractions to have 63 as the denominator [S1].

$$ \frac{7}{9} = \frac{7 \times 7}{9 \times 7} = \frac{49}{63} $$

$$ \frac{17}{21} = \frac{17 \times 3}{21 \times 3} = \frac{51}{63} $$

Comparing the numerators, 49 is less than 51 [S1]. Therefore, $\frac{7}{9}$ is less than $\frac{17}{21}$ [S1].

## The Cross-Multiplication Shortcut

If you do not want to find the least common denominator, you can use a quick trick called cross-multiplication. You multiply the numerator of the first fraction by the denominator of the second fraction, and you multiply the numerator of the second fraction by the denominator of the first fraction. The larger product tells you which fraction is greater [S1].

Using the first example, we compare $\frac{4}{5}$ and $\frac{7}{9}$ [S1]. We multiply 4 by 9 to get 36, and we multiply 7 by 5 to get 35 [S1]. Since 36 is greater than 35, we know that $\frac{4}{5}$ is greater than $\frac{7}{9}$ [S1][S1].

Using the second example, we compare $\frac{7}{9}$ and $\frac{17}{21}$ [S1]. We multiply 7 by 21 to get 147, and we multiply 17 by 9 to get 153 [S1]. Since 147 is less than 153, we know that $\frac{7}{9}$ is less than $\frac{17}{21}$ [S1][S1].

## Step-by-Step Summary

To compare the sizes of two or more fractions, follow these two simple steps: [S1]

**Step 1:** Change the given fractions to equivalent fractions so that they all are expressed with the same denominator or same fractional unit.

**Step 2:** Now, compare the equivalent fractions by simply comparing the numerators, i.e., the number of fractional units each has [S1].

The same two steps order three or more fractions at once. Take $\frac{7}{9}$, $\frac{4}{5}$ and $\frac{17}{21}$. A common denominator for 9, 5 and 21 is 315. Converting each one gives $\frac{7}{9} = \frac{245}{315}$, $\frac{4}{5} = \frac{252}{315}$ and $\frac{17}{21} = \frac{255}{315}$. Comparing the numerators, $245 < 252 < 255$, so in order from smallest to largest the fractions are $\frac{7}{9}$, $\frac{4}{5}$, $\frac{17}{21}$.

## Worked example

Let us compare $\frac{5}{6}$ and $\frac{4}{5}$ using the common denominator method. The denominators are 6 and 5. We can use 30 as a common denominator [S1].

We convert the first fraction: [S1]
$$ \frac{5 \times 5}{6 \times 5} = \frac{25}{30} $$

We convert the second fraction: [S1]
$$ \frac{4 \times 6}{5 \times 6} = \frac{24}{30} $$

Now we compare the numerators [S1]. Since 25 is greater than 24, we know that $\frac{25}{30}$ is greater than $\frac{24}{30}$ [S1]. Therefore, $\frac{5}{6}$ is greater than $\frac{4}{5}$ [S1].

## Key points

*   Fractions with the same denominator are easy to compare because you only need to look at the numerators [S1].
*   To compare fractions with different denominators, you must first find a common denominator or use cross-multiplication [S1].
*   The fraction with the larger numerator is always the larger fraction, provided the denominators are the same [S1].
*   Cross-multiplication works because you are essentially finding the common denominator without doing the full calculation [S1].
*   Ordering fractions means arranging them from smallest to largest or from largest to smallest.
