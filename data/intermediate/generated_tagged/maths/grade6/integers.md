# Integers

You already know how to count using whole numbers like 1, 2, 3, and 4 [S1]. You also know about zero, which represents nothing [S1], but did you know there are numbers that are less than zero? These are called negative numbers [S4], and together with the positive numbers and zero, they make up a special group of numbers called integers [S4]. Understanding integers is very useful. For example, we use them to describe temperatures below freezing, to track money in a bank account, and to count floors in a building that are below ground level [S1].

## The Number Line

You may have already seen a number line [S1]. Usually, it starts at zero and goes to the right with the counting numbers 1, 2, 3, and so on [S1]. But integers include numbers to the left of zero as well [S4]. These numbers are negative [S1]. The full list of integers looks like this: ..., -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ... [S4]. Zero is special because it is neither a positive nor a negative number [S2][S4]. We do not put a plus or minus sign in front of it [S2].

## Positive and Negative Numbers

A number with a plus sign in front is called a positive number [S2]. A number with a minus sign in front is called a negative number [S2]. For example, +5 is a positive number and -5 is a negative number [S2]. In everyday life, you can think of positive numbers as amounts you gain, and negative numbers as amounts you lose [S3].

## The Building of Fun

Imagine a tall building called the 'Building of Fun' [S2]. The ground floor of this building is called the 'Welcome Hall' and it is Floor 0 [S2]. If you want to go up one floor to the Food Court, you press the 'plus' button on the lift once [S2]. We write this movement as +1 [S2]. The Food Court is on Floor +1 [S2]. If you want to go up two floors to the Art Centre, you press the 'plus' button twice [S2]. We write this as +2, and the Art Centre is on Floor +2 [S2].

Now, what about the floors below the ground? To go down one floor to the Toy Store, you press the 'minus' button once [S2]. We write this movement as -1 [S2]. The Toy Store is on Floor -1 [S2]. To go down two floors to the Video Games shop, you press the 'minus' button twice [S2]. We write this as -2, and the Video Games shop is on Floor -2 [S2].

## Addition and Movement

We can use addition to keep track of our movement in the lift [S2]. The rule is: Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor [S2]. For example, if you start at the Food Court, which is Floor +1, and you press the 'plus' button twice (which is a movement of +2), you will reach Floor +3 [S2]. We can write this as $(+1)+(+2)=+3$ [S2].

## Credits and Debits

Integers are also used in banking [S3]. Suppose you open a bank account with ₹100 [S3]. This is a credit, so your balance starts at +₹100 [S3]. The next day, you make ₹60 at your job and deposit it [S3]. This is another credit [S3]. Your new balance is ₹160 [S3].

Then, you pay your electric bill of ₹30 [S3]. This is a debit, which we can think of as a negative number [S3]. Your bank balance is now ₹130 [S3].

The next day, you make a major purchase for ₹150 [S3]. This is another debit [S3]. Your bank balance is now -₹20 [S3]. This means you are in debt [S3]. Some banks do allow your account balance to become negative temporarily [S3].

The next day, you make ₹200 at your business [S3]. Your new balance is ₹180 [S3].

## Worked example

Let's apply the rules for adding integers [S4]. Suppose you are at Floor -2 [S2]. You press the 'plus' button three times to go up to Floor +1 [S2]. We can write this as $(-2)+(+3)=+1$ [S2].

Now, let's try another one [S2]. Suppose you are at Floor +5 [S2]. You press the 'minus' button four times to go down to Floor +1 [S2]. We can write this as $(+5)+(-4)=+1$ [S2].

## Key points

- Integers are the numbers ..., -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ... [S4]
- A positive number has a plus sign in front of it and a negative number has a minus sign [S2]
- Zero is neither positive nor negative [S2][S4]
- We use addition to keep track of movement: Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor [S2]
- Credits are positive numbers and debits are negative numbers [S3]
