# Ratios and Proportions

Comparing quantities is a part of everyday life. You might compare the number of boys and girls in your class, or the distance you walk to school. When we compare two quantities, we can use a ratio. A proportion is simply an equality of two ratios. Understanding these ideas helps you solve many real-world problems, from adjusting recipes to sharing pocket money.

## What is a Ratio?

A ratio is a way to compare two quantities by using division. The two quantities must be counted or measured in the same units, so that dividing one by the other makes sense: a count of fruits against another count of fruits, or a length in centimetres against another length in centimetres. For example, if you have 5 apples and 10 oranges, both are counts of fruit, so you can compare them by writing the ratio. The ratio of apples to oranges is 5 to 10. We can write this ratio in three ways: using a colon, as "to", or as a fraction.

When you write a ratio as a fraction, you can simplify it just like a normal fraction. The ratio 5 to 10 simplifies to $\frac{1}{2}$. This means for every 1 apple, there are 2 oranges.

## Equivalent Ratios

Sometimes, we need to compare quantities that are not the same size. We can do this by making the ratios equivalent. Equivalent ratios are ratios that have the same value, even if the numbers are different.

To find an equivalent ratio, you multiply or divide both sides of the ratio by the same number. For example, the ratio 2 to 3 is equivalent to 4 to 6. This is because you multiplied both 2 and 3 by 2.

## What is a Proportion?

A proportion is an equation that states that two ratios are equal. For example, if you know that 2 to 3 is the same as 4 to 6, you can write this as a proportion.

The proportion $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6}$ is true because both sides simplify to the same value. Proportions are very useful. They help us find unknown values. If you know three numbers in a proportion, you can always find the fourth.

## Worked Example

Let's look at an example of how to use a proportion to find an unknown value. Imagine you are making lemonade. You know that 3 cups of water make 12 glasses of lemonade. You want to know how many glasses of lemonade you can make with 8 cups of water.

First, set up the proportion. The ratio of cups of water to glasses of lemonade is $\frac{3}{12}$. You want to find the number of glasses, let's call it $x$, that you can make with 8 cups of water. The ratio of 8 cups of water to $x$ glasses of lemonade is $\frac{8}{x}$.

Set up the proportion:
$$ \frac{3}{12} = \frac{8}{x} $$

To solve for $x$, you can cross-multiply. This means you multiply the numerator of the first fraction by the denominator of the second fraction, and set it equal to the product of the denominator of the first fraction and the numerator of the second fraction.

$$ 3 \times x = 12 \times 8 $$
$$ 3x = 96 $$

Now, divide both sides by 3 to find $x$:
$$ x = \frac{96}{3} $$
$$ x = 32 $$

So, 8 cups of water will make 32 glasses of lemonade.

## Key points

- A ratio compares two quantities using division, and the two must be measured in the same units for the comparison to mean anything.
- Equivalent ratios are ratios that have the same value, even if the numbers are different.
- A proportion is an equation that states that two ratios are equal.
- You can find an unknown value in a proportion by cross-multiplying and solving the resulting equation.
- Always check your answer by substituting it back into the proportion to ensure the ratios are equal.
