# Natural Numbers

Natural numbers are the counting numbers starting from 1 and going on forever: 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on. They are the most basic numbers we use in everyday life to count objects, measure quantities, and organize information. You have been using them since you first learned to count, and they form the foundation for all the more advanced mathematics you will study in the coming years.

## What Are Natural Numbers?

Natural numbers are the numbers we use for counting. They start at 1 and continue infinitely. You can think of them as the numbers on a number line that go to the right without ever stopping. The set of natural numbers is written as $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, \dots$ [S1].

For example, if you have 5 apples, the number 5 is a natural number. If you have 10 marbles, the number 10 is a natural number. Even very large numbers like 1,00,000 (one lakh) are natural numbers.

## Special Types of Natural Numbers

Not all natural numbers are the same. Some have special properties that make them unique. Let us look at a few important types.

**Prime Numbers**

A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. For example, 43 is a prime number. If we look at a box of numbers like 9, 16, 25, and 43, we can see that 43 is special because it is the only prime number in that set [S1].

Other examples of prime numbers are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13. The number 2 is the only even prime number. All other even numbers are divisible by 2, so they have more than two factors.

**Square Numbers**

A square number is a natural number that can be expressed as the product of two equal integers. For example, 9 is a square number because $3 \times 3 = 9$. Similarly, 16 is a square number because $4 \times 4 = 16$, and 25 is a square number because $5 \times 5 = 25$ [S1].

Square numbers are special because they represent the area of a square with equal side lengths. For instance, if a square has a side length of 4 units, its area is $4 \times 4 = 16$ square units.

**Multiples**

A multiple of a number is the product of that number and an integer. For example, 16 is a multiple of 4 because $4 \times 4 = 16$. In a box of numbers, 16 can be special because it is the only even number and also the only multiple of 4 [S1].

Similarly, 9 is a multiple of 3 because $3 \times 3 = 9$. 25 is a multiple of 5 because $5 \times 5 = 25$.

**Even and Odd Numbers**

Even numbers are natural numbers that are divisible by 2. They end in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8. For example, 16 is an even number because it can be divided by 2 to give 8 [S1].

Odd numbers are natural numbers that are not divisible by 2. They end in 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9. For example, 43 is an odd number.

## Using Natural Numbers to Represent Information

Natural numbers can be used to represent information in real-world situations. For example, children standing in a line can use numbers to describe their surroundings. Each child can say a number based on how many taller children are standing next to them. A child says '1' if there is only one taller child standing next to them. A child says '2' if both the children standing next to them are taller. A child says '0' if neither of the children standing next to them are taller [S2].

This is an example of how natural numbers can be used to describe a situation. The numbers 0, 1, and 2 are used to represent the number of taller neighbours a child has.

## Adding and Subtracting Large Natural Numbers

As you work with larger natural numbers, you will need to add and subtract them. For example, you might need to add two 5-digit numbers to get another 5-digit number. An example of this is $12,350 + 24,545 = 36,895$ [S3].

Similarly, you might need to subtract two 5-digit numbers to get another 5-digit number. An example of this is $48,952 - 24,547 = 24,405$ [S3].

When adding and subtracting large numbers, it is important to align the digits correctly and carry or borrow as needed.

## Worked Example

Let us work through an example of adding two large natural numbers. Suppose we want to add $39,800$ and $15,000$. We can break this down as follows:

$39,800 + 15,000 = 54,800$

We can also use the method of breaking down the numbers into smaller parts. For example, we can break down $39,800$ into $30,000 + 9,000 + 800$. Then we can add $15,000$ to this as follows:

$39,800 + 15,000 = 30,000 + 9,000 + 800 + 15,000$
$= 30,000 + 15,000 + 9,000 + 800$
$= 45,000 + 9,000 + 800$
$= 54,000 + 800$
$= 54,800$

This method can be useful when adding large numbers mentally.

## Key points

* Natural numbers are the counting numbers starting from 1.
* Prime numbers are natural numbers greater than 1 with exactly two factors.
* Square numbers are natural numbers that can be expressed as the product of two equal integers.
* Multiples of a number are the products of that number and an integer.
* Even numbers are divisible by 2, while odd numbers are not.
* Natural numbers can be used to represent information in real-world situations.
* Adding and subtracting large natural numbers requires careful alignment of digits.
