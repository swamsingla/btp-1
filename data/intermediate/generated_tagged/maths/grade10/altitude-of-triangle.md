
# Altitudes of a Triangle

An altitude of a triangle is a straight line drawn from a vertex of the triangle that meets the opposite side at a right angle. In other words, it is the perpendicular distance from a corner to the base. Every triangle has exactly three altitudes because it has three vertices and three opposite sides. These altitudes are very important because they help us find the area of the triangle and reveal special meeting points inside the shape.

## The Orthocentre

When you draw all three altitudes of a triangle, they always meet at a single point inside the triangle. This special meeting point is called the orthocentre. The orthocentre is one of the most important centers in a triangle, just like the center of a circle. The position of the orthocentre depends on the type of triangle. In an acute triangle, the orthocentre lies inside the triangle. In a right-angled triangle, the orthocentre is exactly at the vertex where the right angle is located. In an obtuse triangle, the orthocentre lies outside the triangle.

## How to Draw an Altitude

To draw an altitude, you need a ruler and a compass. First, take the vertex where you want the altitude to start. Then, draw a straight line from this vertex to the opposite side. The rule is that this line must meet the opposite side at a 90-degree angle. This means the line must be perfectly perpendicular to the base. If the base is a horizontal line, the altitude will be a vertical line. You can check this by using a protractor or by ensuring the line forms a perfect L-shape with the base.

## Why Altitudes Matter

Altitudes are not just lines; they are measurements of height. The length of an altitude tells you how tall the triangle is from a specific corner to the opposite side. This height is used in the basic area formula for a triangle, which is half the base multiplied by the height. Without the altitude, you cannot easily find the area of a scalene triangle where the height is not given directly.

## Worked example

Let us consider a triangular plot of land with side lengths of 13 meters, 14 meters, and 15 meters. We want to find the area of this plot. We do not know the height directly, but we can use Heron's formula to find it. First, we calculate the semi-perimeter, which is half the perimeter of the triangle [S1]. The perimeter is 13 + 14 + 15 = 42 meters. Therefore, the semi-perimeter is 21 meters. 

Next, we use Heron's formula to find the area [S1]. The formula is the square root of the semi-perimeter multiplied by the differences between the semi-perimeter and each side. This gives us the square root of 21 multiplied by (21 - 13), (21 - 14), and (21 - 15). This simplifies to the square root of 21 multiplied by 8, 7, and 6. The product is 7056, and the square root of 7056 is 84. So, the area of the plot is 84 square meters.

Now, let us find the altitude. We know the area is 84 square meters and the base is 14 meters. The area formula is half times base times height. So, 84 equals half times 14 times the height. This means 84 equals 7 times the height. Therefore, the height is 12 meters. This altitude is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the base of 14 meters.

## Key points

- An altitude is a perpendicular line from a vertex to the opposite side.
- The three altitudes of a triangle always meet at a single point called the orthocentre.
- In a right-angled triangle, the orthocentre is at the vertex of the right angle.
- The length of an altitude is used to calculate the area of a triangle.
- Heron's formula can be used to find the area when the altitude is unknown, which helps find the altitude [S1].
