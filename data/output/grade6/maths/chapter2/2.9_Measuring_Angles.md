## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Defining Degrees  
> **A degree** is a unit used to measure angles. It is defined by dividing a full circle into **360 equal parts**, where each part represents **1 degree (1°)**. This method allows us to express the measure of any angle as a number of these 1° units.  

#### How It Works:  
- A **full turn** (a complete circle) is **360°** because it contains **360 equal 1° parts**.  
- A **straight angle** (like a straight line) is **half of a full turn**, so it measures **180°**.  
- A **right angle** (like the corner of a square) is **one-fourth of a full turn**, so it measures **90°**.  

### 3.2 Calculating Angle Measures  
> **The measure of an angle** is the number of **1° unit parts** it contains. For example:  
- If an angle contains **30 such units**, its measure is **30°**.  
- If an angle contains **90 units**, its measure is **90°**.  

#### Historical Context: Why 360?  
> **A full turn is 360°** because ancient civilizations found this number convenient. The **Rigveda** (an ancient Indian text) mentions a wheel with **360 spokes**, and many ancient calendars used **360 days** in a year. Additionally, **360 is divisible by many numbers** (like 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, etc.), making it ideal for splitting circles into equal parts.  

### 3.3 Special Angle Measures  
> **Straight Angle**: **180°** (forms a straight line).  
> **Right Angle**: **90°** (forms a perfect L-shape).  
> **Acute Angle**: Less than **90°**.  
> **Obtuse Angle**: Greater than **90°** but less than **180°**.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Question**: If an angle contains **45 units** of 1°, what is its measure?  
**Solution**:  
- Since each unit is **1°**, the total measure is simply the number of units.  
- Therefore, the angle measures **45°**.  

### Example 2:  
**Question**: What is the measure of a full turn?  
**Solution**:  
- A full turn is **360°** by definition.  
- Answer: **360°**.  

### Example 3:  
**Question**: If a straight angle is split into two equal parts, what is the measure of each part?  
**Solution**:  
- A straight angle is **180°**.  
- Dividing it into two equal parts:

$$ \text{Each part} = \frac{180^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ

$$  
- Answer: **90°**.  

### Example 4:  
**Question**: A right angle is **90°**, and three right angles make up what fraction of a full turn?  
**Solution**:  
- Three right angles:

$$ 3 \times 90^\circ = 270^\circ

$$  
- Fraction of a full turn:

$$ \frac{270^\circ}{360^\circ} = \frac{3}{4}

$$  
- Answer: **$\frac{3}{4} $** of a full turn.  

### Example 5:  
**Question**: If an angle measures **120°**, how many **1° units** does it contain?  
**Solution**:  
- The measure directly equals the number of units.  
- Answer: **120 units**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
1. **Problem**: If an angle contains **60 units** of 1°, what is its measure?  
   **Answer**:  
   - Since each unit is **1°**, the measure is **60°**.  

2. **Problem**: What is the measure of a straight angle?  
   **Answer**:  
   - A straight angle is **180°**.  

### 🟡 Medium  
3. **Problem**: If a full turn is **360°**, what fraction of a full turn is a right angle?  
   **Answer**:  
   - A right angle is **90°**, so:

$$ \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} = \frac{1}{4}

$$  
   - Answer: **$\frac{1}{4} $** of a full turn.  

4. **Problem**: An angle is **150°**. How many **1° units** does it contain?  
   **Answer**:  
   - The measure directly equals the number of units.  
   - Answer: **150 units**.  

### 🔴 Hard  
5. **Problem**: If an angle measures **270°**, how many **right angles** does it contain?  
   **Answer**:  
   - One right angle = **90°**.  
   - Total right angles:

$$ \frac{270^\circ}{90^\circ} = 3

$$  
   - Answer: **3 right angles**.  

---  

## 6. Summary  
- **Degrees** are units used to measure angles, with **1°** representing **1/360** of a full circle.  
- A **full turn** = **360°**, a **straight angle** = **180°**, and a **right angle** = **90°**.  
- To find an angle’s measure, count the number of **1° units** it contains.  
- The number **360** is historically significant because it divides evenly by many numbers, making calculations easier.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|------------|  
| $ 1^\circ = \frac{1}{360} $ of a full circle | Definition of 1 degree |  
| Full Turn = $ 360^\circ $ | Measure of a complete circle |  
| Straight Angle = $ 180^\circ $ | Measure of a straight line |  
| Right Angle = $ 90^\circ $ | Measure of a right angle |  
| Angle Measure = Number of $ 1^\circ $ Units | General formula for angle measure |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Confusing degrees with radians**: Always remember that **degrees** are used here, not radians (which are a different unit).  
2. **Miscounting units**: When estimating angles, double-check your count of **1° units** to avoid errors.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Use a protractor** for accurate measurements during exams.  
- **Label your answers** clearly with the **° symbol** to indicate degrees.  

### Memory Aid  
**"360 Days, 360 Degrees"**: Remember that **360** is linked to both **days in a year** and **degrees in a circle**!
