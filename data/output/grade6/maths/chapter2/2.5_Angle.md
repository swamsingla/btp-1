## 2. Introduction  
**Have you ever noticed how doors swing open or how a clock hand moves?** These movements create angles! In this topic, you’ll learn how angles are formed, named, and compared. By the end, you’ll be able to identify angles in real-life situations and understand how their size relates to rotation. This builds on your earlier study of rays (Topic 2.4) and prepares you for exploring special types of angles (Topic 2.8).  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of an Angle  
> An **angle** is formed when **two rays** share a **common starting point**. The shared point is called the **vertex**, and the rays are called the **arms** of the angle.  

**Example**: In Figure 2.8, rays **BD** and **BE** form an angle with vertex **B**.  

### 3.2 Naming Angles  
> To name an angle, use **three letters**:  
- The **first and third letters** represent points on the arms.  
- The **middle letter** is the **vertex**.  

**Symbols**:  
- Angles are written as **∠ABC** or **∠CBA**, where **B** is the vertex.  
- The symbol **∠** replaces the word “angle.”  

### 3.3 Size of an Angle  
> The **size of an angle** depends on the **amount of rotation** between its arms.  
- More rotation means a **larger angle**.  
- Less rotation means a **smaller angle**.  

**Visualizing Rotation**:  
- Imagine opening a book (Figure 2.8). The wider the opening, the greater the angle.  
- Example: Case 1 (small opening) vs. Case 6 (fully open)—Case 6 has a larger angle.  

### 3.4 Real-Life Applications  
Angles are everywhere! Consider:  
- **Compasses/Dividers**: Arms rotate around a central vertex.  
- **Scissors**: Blades form an angle when opened.  
- **Spectacles/Wallets**: Edges create angles at their corners.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Identify the vertex and arms of ∠PQR**.  
**Solution**:  
- **Vertex**: Point **Q**  
- **Arms**: Rays **QP** and **QR**  

### Example 2:  
**Name the angle with vertex **A** and arms **AB** and **AC**.  
**Solution**:  
- The angle is named **∠BAC** or **∠CAB**.  

### Example 3:  
**Compare the sizes of angles in Figures 2.8 (Case 1) and 2.8 (Case 2)**.  
**Solution**:  
- Case 2 requires more rotation than Case 1.  
- Therefore, the angle in **Case 2 is larger**.  

### Example 4:  
**Draw an angle with vertex **O** and arms **OA** and **OB**. Label it appropriately.**  
**Solution**:  
- Draw point **O**.  
- Extend two rays from **O** to points **A** and **B**.  
- Label the angle as **∠AOB** or **∠BOA**.  

### Example 5:  
**Explain why ∠XYZ cannot be named as ∠YZX**.  
**Solution**:  
- In **∠XYZ**, **Y** is the vertex.  
- In **∠YZX**, **Z** is the vertex.  
- Since the vertex must be the **middle letter**, **∠YZX** is incorrect.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**: Name the angle with vertex **M** and arms **MP** and **MQ**.  
**Answer**:  
- The angle is **∠PMQ** or **∠MQP**.  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2**: Compare the angles formed by opening a door slightly versus opening it wide. Which is larger?  
**Answer**:  
- The wider opening creates a **larger angle** due to more rotation.  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3**: A pair of scissors forms an angle. If the blades are closed completely, what is the measure of the angle?  
**Answer**:  
- When blades are closed, the angle is **0°** (no rotation).  

---

## 6. Summary  
- An **angle** is formed by two **rays** sharing a **common vertex**.  
- Angles are named using **three letters**, with the **vertex in the center** (e.g., ∠ABC).  
- The **size of an angle** depends on the **rotation** between its arms.  
- Real-life examples include **doors, clocks, and scissors**.  
- **Key Terms**: Vertex, arms, rotation, naming conventions.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| ∠ABC | Angle with vertex at **B** |  
| ∠DEF | Angle with vertex at **E** |  
| Rotation Amount = Angle Size | Larger rotation = Larger angle |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ **Error**: Confusing vertex with arm labels.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Always place the vertex in the **middle** of the angle name (e.g., ∠ABC ≠ ∠ACB).  

### Exam Tips  
- ✔️ Practice drawing angles with different rotations to visualize size differences.  
- ✔️ Use real-life objects (like books or scissors) to reinforce concepts.  

### Memory Aid  
**Rhyme**: *"Vertex in the middle, arms on either side—rotate to find the angle size!"*
