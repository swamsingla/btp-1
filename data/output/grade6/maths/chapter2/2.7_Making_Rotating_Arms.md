# 2.7 Making Rotating Arms

> **Grade 6** | **Chapter 2: Lines and Angles**

---

## 1. Prerequisites  
Before diving into this topic, you should already understand:  
- **Ray**: A straight line starting at a point and extending infinitely in one direction (e.g., the beam of a flashlight).  
- **Angle**: The figure formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex).  
- **Comparing Angles**: Using methods like superimposition or estimation to determine which angle is larger or smaller.  

---  

## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how clock hands move smoothly or how doors open and close? Today, we’ll explore **rotating arms**—a hands-on way to visualize angles! By building and testing rotating arms, you’ll discover how angles affect whether they fit through a slit. This activity will help you master comparing angles and understanding their measures.  

---  

## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 What Are Rotating Arms?  
Rotating arms are tools made from paper straws and a paper clip. They allow you to create and manipulate angles physically. Here’s how to make one:  
1. **Insert** two paper straws into the paper clip’s arms.  
2. **Rotate** the straws to form different angles.  

> **Definition**: A *rotating arm* consists of two rigid rods connected at a pivot point, enabling them to form various angles.  

### 3.2 How Slits Work With Rotating Arms  
When testing a rotating arm against a slit:  
- **If the slit angle is equal to the arm’s angle**, the arm fits perfectly.  
- **If the slit angle is larger or smaller**, the arm won’t fit.  

> **Property**: The ability of a rotating arm to pass through a slit **depends solely on the angle between its arms**, not their lengths (as long as the arms are short enough).  

### 3.3 Superimposition for Comparison  
To arrange rotating arms by angle size:  
1. **Compare** angles using superimposition (placing one arm over another).  
2. **Arrange** them from smallest to largest.  

> **Tip**: Always keep the arms stationary while comparing to avoid measurement errors.  

### 3.4 Real-Life Analogy  
Imagine a door hinge: the angle between the door and frame determines how easily the door opens. Similarly, rotating arms show how angles control movement and fit.

## 4. Solved Examples  
### Example 1: Identifying Matching Angles  
**Problem**: A slit has an angle of $60^\circ $. Which rotating arm will fit through it?  
**Solution**:  
1. Measure the angle of each rotating arm.  
2. Compare it with $ 60^\circ $.  
3. The arm with an angle **equal to $ 60^\circ $** will pass through the slit.  

### Example 2: Predicting Fit Without Measurement  
**Problem**: If a rotating arm forms a right angle ($ 90^\circ $), will it fit through a slit with an angle of $ 85^\circ $?  
**Solution**:  
1. Since $ 85^\circ < 90^\circ $, the slit is narrower than the arm’s angle.  
2. Therefore, the arm **won’t fit** through the slit.  

### Example 3: Adjusting Angles  
**Problem**: A rotating arm has an angle of $ 120^\circ $. How can you reduce it to $ 90^\circ $?  
**Solution**:  
1. Rotate one straw closer to the other.  
2. Measure the new angle.  
3. Verify it matches $ 90^\circ $ using a protractor (if available).

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**:  
1. A rotating arm has an angle of $ 45^\circ $. Will it fit through a slit with an angle of $ 45^\circ $? Explain why.  

🟡 **Medium**:  
2. If a slit has an angle of $ 135^\circ $, which rotating arms (with angles $ 120^\circ $, $ 140^\circ $, $ 135^\circ $) will fit through it?  

🔴 **Hard**:  
3. Create three rotating arms with angles $ 30^\circ $, $ 60^\circ $, and $ 90^\circ $. Arrange them in ascending order by comparing them visually.  

**Answers**:  
1. **Yes**, because the slit and arm angles are equal.  
2. Only the $ 135^\circ $ arm fits.  
3. Order: $ 30^\circ < 60^\circ < 90^\circ $.  

---  

## 6. Summary  
- **Rotating arms** help visualize angles by allowing physical manipulation.  
- Angles are compared using **superimposition** or estimation.  
- A rotating arm fits through a slit **only if its angle matches the slit’s angle**.  
- The **length of the arms does not affect fitting**, only the angle.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $\theta_{\text{arm}} = \theta_{\text{slit}} $ | Condition for a rotating arm to fit through a slit. |  
| $\theta_{\text{arm}} > \theta_{\text{slit}} $ | Arm does not fit. |  
| $\theta_{\text{arm}} < \theta_{\text{slit}} $ | Arm does not fit. |

## 8. Student Corner  
### Common Mistakes  
- **Confusing angle size with arm length**: Remember, only the angle matters, not how long the straws are!  
- **Misaligning arms during comparison**: Ensure both arms are aligned properly when using superimposition.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Angle Match, Fit Perfect!"** – If the angles match, the arm goes through the slit.
