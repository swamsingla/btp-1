## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Defining Key Terms  
- **Angle**: A figure formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint called the **vertex**.  
- **Protractor**: A tool with markings from $ 0^\circ $ to $ 180^\circ $ used to measure or draw angles.  
- **Vertex**: The common endpoint of the two arms of an angle.  
- **Arms**: The two rays forming the sides of an angle.  

> **Definition**: To draw an angle, you fix one arm as a reference (base) and rotate the second arm to the desired measurement.  

### 3.2 Step-by-Step Process  
Follow these steps to draw an angle like $ 30^\circ $:  
1. **Draw the base arm**: Use a ruler to sketch a straight line (e.g., $ IN $).  
2. **Position the protractor**: Place the center of the protractor on the vertex (e.g., $ I $) and align the base arm with the $ 0^\circ $ mark.  
3. **Mark the desired measurement**: Locate the $ 30^\circ $ mark on the protractor and draw a small arc. Label this point (e.g., $ T $).  
4. **Complete the angle**: Join the vertex ($ I $) to the marked point ($ T $) with a ruler.  

> **Tip**: Always double-check the alignment of the protractor to avoid errors.  

### 3.3 Games to Reinforce Learning  
#### **Game 1: Angle Guessing Challenge**  
- One team secretly creates an angle (e.g., $ 49^\circ $) using a protractor.  
- The opposing team guesses the measure without tools. Points equal the difference between their guess and the actual measure.  

#### **Game 2: Draw It Without a Protractor**  
- A team announces an angle (e.g., $ 34^\circ $). A player draws it freehand while teammates give verbal feedback.  
- The drawn angle is checked with a protractor, and points are awarded based on accuracy.  

> **Why These Games Work**: They build spatial intuition and confidence in estimating angles.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Drawing a $ 90^\circ $ Angle  
**Problem**: Draw a $ 90^\circ $ angle named $\angle ABC $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Draw arm $ BC $ horizontally.  
2. Align the protractor’s center at $ B $ and the baseline with $ BC $.  
3. Mark the $ 90^\circ $ point and label it $ A $.  
4. Join $ B $ to $ A $ to form $\angle ABC $.

$$
\angle ABC = 90^\circ

$$  

### Example 2: Constructing a $120^\circ $ Angle  
**Problem**: Create a $ 120^\circ $ angle with vertex $ O $ and arms $ OP $ and $ OQ $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Draw $ OP $ as the base.  
2. Place the protractor’s center at $ O $ and align $ OP $ with $ 0^\circ $.  
3. Count $ 120^\circ $ on the outer scale and mark $ Q $.  
4. Connect $ O $ to $ Q $ to complete the angle.

$$
\angle POQ = 120^\circ

$$  

### Example 3: Drawing a $45^\circ $ Angle  
**Problem**: Draw $\angle XYZ = 45^\circ $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Sketch $ YZ $ as the base.  
2. Position the protractor at $ Y $ and align $ YZ $ with $ 0^\circ $.  
3. Mark the $ 45^\circ $ point and label it $ X $.  
4. Join $ Y $ to $ X $ to form the angle.

$$
\angle XYZ = 45^\circ

$$

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy: Basic Construction  
**Problem 1**: Draw a $60^\circ $ angle named $\angle DEF $.  
**Answer**: Follow the steps outlined in Example 1, replacing $ ABC $ with $ DEF $ and $ 90^\circ $ with $ 60^\circ $.  

### 🟡 Medium: Estimation and Accuracy  
**Problem 2**: Estimate the measure of $\angle PQR $ drawn freehand. Then, measure it with a protractor. What is the difference between your estimate and the actual measure?  
**Answer**: Estimate first, then use the protractor. Calculate $|\text{Estimated Measure} - \text{Actual Measure}|$.  

### 🔴 Hard: Creative Application  
**Problem 3**: Design a triangle with angles $ 30^\circ $, $ 60^\circ $, and $ 90^\circ $. Draw each angle separately and label them.  
**Answer**: Draw each angle step-by-step, ensuring the sum of angles equals $ 180^\circ $. Verify with a protractor.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Misalignment**: Placing the protractor incorrectly leads to wrong measurements.  
   **Fix**: Ensure the protractor’s center is exactly on the vertex and the baseline matches the arm.  
2. **Confusing Inner/Outer Scales**: Reading the wrong side of the protractor.  
   **Fix**: Align the base arm with $ 0^\circ $ and use the corresponding scale.  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice drawing angles of varying sizes to improve speed and accuracy.  
- Always verify your work with a protractor during exams.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Protractors: Point, Line, Read!"**  
- **Point**: Place the protractor’s center on the vertex.  
- **Line**: Align the base arm with the $ 0^\circ $ mark.  
- **Read**: Find the desired degree and mark it.
