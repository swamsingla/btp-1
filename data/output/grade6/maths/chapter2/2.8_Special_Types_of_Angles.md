## 1. Prerequisites  
Before diving into special types of angles, you should already understand:  
- **Rays**: A ray is a straight path that starts at a point and extends infinitely in one direction.  
- **Angles**: An angle is formed when two rays share a common endpoint (called the vertex).  
- **Measuring angles**: You should be familiar with basic tools like protractors to measure angles.  

These foundational concepts will help you grasp how special angles like straight and right angles are defined and measured.

## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever noticed how doors swing open smoothly or how corners of a room form perfect squares? These observations are linked to **angles**—specifically, **special types of angles** that play a vital role in geometry! In this topic, you’ll explore **straight angles**, **right angles**, and **perpendicular lines**. You’ll also learn how to identify and construct these angles using hands-on activities like folding paper or analyzing classroom objects. Let’s uncover the secrets behind these fascinating shapes!

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Straight Angle  
> A **straight angle** is formed when two rays point in exactly opposite directions, forming a straight line. It measures **180°** and is equivalent to **half of a full turn** (which is 360°).  

**Visualize it**: Imagine opening a book completely flat—the cover forms a straight angle. Mathematically, a straight angle can be represented as ∠AOB, where points A, O, and B lie in a straight line, with O as the vertex.  

**Key Property**: A straight angle is divided into two equal parts by a ray, resulting in two **right angles**.  

---

### 3.2 Right Angle  
> A **right angle** is exactly **one-half of a straight angle**, measuring **90°**. It resembles the shape of an “L” and is often denoted by a small square at the vertex.  

**Activity Insight**: The source text describes how folding a paper so that one side overlaps the other creates a **right angle**. This happens because the fold acts as a bisector of the straight angle, splitting it into two equal 90° angles.  

**Important Note**: Two lines that intersect at a **right angle** are called **perpendicular lines**. For example, the edges of a book or the corners of a window frame are perpendicular.  

---

### 3.3 Perpendicular Lines  
> **Perpendicular lines** are lines that intersect at a **right angle (90°)**. They are fundamental in geometry and appear in everyday objects like rulers, floor tiles, and door frames.  

**Symbol Representation**: Perpendicular lines are denoted by the symbol ⊥. For instance, if line AB is perpendicular to line CD, we write AB ⊥ CD.  

**Real-Life Example**: When you stand a pencil upright on a flat surface, the pencil and the surface form perpendicular lines.  

---

### 3.4 Relationship Between Straight and Right Angles  
> A **straight angle (180°)** contains **two right angles (90° + 90°)**. This relationship is demonstrated in the source text’s activity: folding a paper to divide a straight angle into two equal halves results in two right angles.  

**Mathematical Proof**:  
1. Let ∠AOB be a straight angle (∠AOB = 180°).  
2. Fold the paper so that OA overlaps OB, creating a crease OC.  
3. This fold ensures ∠AOC = ∠COB.  
4. Since ∠AOC + ∠COB = ∠AOB = 180°, each angle must be 90°.  
Thus, ∠AOC = ∠COB = 90°, proving they are right angles.  

---

### 3.5 Identifying Angles in Real Life  
**Think About It**:  
- How many right angles do you see in your classroom? Look for corners of desks, walls, or windows.  
- Can you find a straight angle by opening a door completely?  

**Did You Know?** The term “right angle” comes from the Latin *angulus rectus*, meaning “upright angle,” reflecting its association with vertical and horizontal lines.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Problem**: If a straight angle is divided into three equal parts, what is the measure of each part?  

**Solution**:  
1. A straight angle measures 180°.  
2. Dividing it into three equal parts:

$$
   \text{Each part} = \frac{180^\circ}{3} = 60^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: Each part measures **60°**.  

---

### Example 2:  
**Problem**: A right angle is split into two equal angles. What is the measure of each new angle?  

**Solution**:  
1. A right angle measures 90°.  
2. Splitting it into two equal parts:

$$
   \text{Each part} = \frac{90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: Each new angle measures **45°**.  

---

### Example 3:  
**Problem**: If two lines intersect at a right angle, what is the sum of all four angles formed?  

**Solution**:  
1. When two lines intersect at a right angle, they form **four angles**: two pairs of vertical angles.  
2. Each pair sums to 90° + 90° = 180°.  
3. Total sum:

$$
   180^\circ + 180^\circ = 360^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: The total sum is **360°**.  

---

### Example 4:  
**Problem**: A clock shows 3:00. What type of angle is formed between the hour and minute hands?  

**Solution**:  
1. At 3:00, the hour hand points to 3, and the minute hand points to 12.  
2. The angle between them is calculated as:

$$
   \text{Angle} = 3 \times 30^\circ = 90^\circ \quad (\text{since each hour represents } 30^\circ)

$$  
3. A 90° angle is a **right angle**.  
**Answer**: The angle is a **right angle**.  

---

### Example 5:  
**Problem**: If ∠ABC is a straight angle and BD bisects ∠ABC, what is the measure of ∠ABD?  

**Solution**:  
1. Given ∠ABC = 180°.  
2. Since BD bisects ∠ABC, it splits it into two equal angles:

$$
   \angle ABD = \angle DBC = \frac{180^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: ∠ABD measures **90°**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
1. A straight angle is divided into four equal parts. What is the measure of each part?  
2. If two lines intersect at a right angle, how many right angles are formed?  

### 🟡 Medium  
3. A right angle is divided into three equal parts. What is the measure of each part?  
4. If ∠XYZ is a straight angle and YW bisects ∠XYZ, what is the measure of ∠XYW?  

### 🔴 Hard  
5. A clock shows 12:15. What type of angle is formed between the hour and minute hands? Justify your answer.  

---

### Answers:  

#### Problem 1:  
**Step-by-Step Solution**:  
1. A straight angle = 180°.  
2. Divide by 4:

$$
   \frac{180^\circ}{4} = 45^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: Each part measures **45°**.  

#### Problem 2:  
**Step-by-Step Solution**:  
1. When two lines intersect at a right angle, they form **four angles**: two pairs of vertically opposite angles.  
2. Each pair equals 90°, so total right angles = 4.  
**Answer**: There are **4 right angles**.  

#### Problem 3:  
**Step-by-Step Solution**:  
1. A right angle = 90°.  
2. Divide by 3:

$$
   \frac{90^\circ}{3} = 30^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: Each part measures **30°**.  

#### Problem 4:  
**Step-by-Step Solution**:  
1. ∠XYZ = 180°.  
2. Bisected by YW:

$$
   \angle XYW = \frac{180^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ

$$  
**Answer**: ∠XYW measures **90°**.  

#### Problem 5:  
**Step-by-Step Solution**:  
1. At 12:15, the minute hand points to 3 (90° from 12).  
2. The hour hand moves 0.5° per minute:

$$
   15 \times 0.5^\circ = 7.5^\circ \quad (\text{from 12 to 12:15})

$$  
3. Angle between hands:

$$
   90^\circ - 7.5^\circ = 82.5^\circ

$$  
4. Since 82.5° < 90°, it’s an **acute angle**.  
**Answer**: The angle is **acute**.

## 6. Summary  
- A **straight angle** measures **180°** and is formed by two opposite rays.  
- A **right angle** measures **90°** and resembles an "L".  
- **Perpendicular lines** intersect at a right angle (90°).  
- A straight angle contains **two right angles**.  
- Use **folding** or **tools** to construct and measure these angles.  
- Real-world examples include door hinges, window frames, and clocks.

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Straight angle} = 180^\circ $ | A straight angle is half of a full turn. |  
| $ \text{Right angle} = 90^\circ $ | A right angle is half of a straight angle. |  
| $ \text{Sum of angles around a point} = 360^\circ $ | When multiple angles meet at a point. |  
| $ \text{Vertical angles} = \text{Equal in measure} $ | Opposite angles formed by intersecting lines. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Confusing acute and obtuse angles**: Always check if the angle is less than 90° (acute), equal to 90° (right), or greater than 90° but less than 180° (obtuse).  
2. **Misidentifying perpendicular lines**: Ensure the angle between lines is exactly 90°, not close to it.  

### Exam Tips  
- Draw diagrams to visualize angles and their relationships.  
- Practice folding paper to reinforce the concept of bisecting angles.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Right, Straight, and Perpendicular"**:  
- Remember that a **right angle** is 90°, a **straight angle** is 180°, and **perpendicular lines** meet at 90°. Think of the letters **R**, **S**, and **P** to recall these values!
