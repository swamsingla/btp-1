## Introduction
Imagine you're trying to find the best route to school. You need to know the direction you're heading, just like Vidya did when opening her notebook. In this topic, we'll explore special types of angles that help you navigate through directions and shapes. We'll discover what makes an angle acute, right, obtuse, or straight, and how to identify them.

## Explanation
An angle is formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint. There are four types of angles:

*   **Straight Angle**: Two rays that lie in a straight line.
*   **Right Angle**: Half of a straight angle, formed by two rays that meet at a single point.
*   **Acute Angle**: Less than a right angle, often sharp.
*   **Obtuse Angle**: Greater than a right angle, often blunt.

$$

\text{A right angle}=\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ radians}

$$
### Key Terms:
-   **Ray**: A line extending infinitely in one direction from a single point.
-   **Acute Angle**: An angle whose measure is between 0° and 90°.
-   **Obtuse Angle**: An angle whose measure is greater than 90° but less than 180°.
-   **Right Angle**: An angle whose measure is 90°.

### Important Formulas:
-

$$

\text{Angle Sum Property}:\quad \angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ

$$
-

$$

\text{Complementary Angles}: m\angle A + m\angle B = 90^\circ

$$
-

$$

\text{Supplementary Angles}: m\angle A + m\angle B = 180^\circ

$$
