## 1. Prerequisites  
Before diving into comparing angles, ensure you understand:  
- **Definition of a ray**: A ray has one endpoint and extends infinitely in one direction.  
- **Basic angle components**: An angle consists of a **vertex** (common endpoint) and two **arms** (rays).  
- **Visualizing rotation**: Recognize that angles are formed by rotating one arm relative to the other.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Superimposition Method  
> **Superimposition** is the process of placing one angle over another to compare their sizes.  
To compare two angles:  
1. Align their **vertices** (corners) by overlapping them.  
2. Match one **arm** of each angle.  
3. Observe the second arm:  
   - If it lies **inside** the other angle, the first angle is smaller.  
   - If it lies **outside**, the first angle is larger.  

**Example**:  
If ∠PQR overlaps ∠ABC with matching vertices and arms, and the second arm of ∠PQR extends beyond ∠ABC’s second arm, then ∠PQR > ∠ABC.  

### 3.2 Equal Angles  
> **Equal angles** have the same measure, meaning their arms rotate by the same amount.  
When two angles are superimposed:  
- Their **vertices** and **arms** completely overlap.  
- The **rotation** needed to align one arm with the other is identical.  

**Example**:  
∠AOB and ∠XOY are equal because OA ↔ OX and OB ↔ OY. This means the rotation from OA to OB matches the rotation from OX to OY.  

### 3.3 Practical Applications  
Superimposition isn’t limited to geometry! Think of:  
- Folding paper to create symmetrical shapes.  
- Comparing the spread of a crane’s wings or a fan’s blades.  
- Adjusting door hinges to match angles for perfect alignment.  

**Tip**: Use tracing paper to overlay angles when comparing them visually.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Compare ∠XYZ and ∠PQR using superimposition.  
**Solution**:  
1. Place the vertex of ∠XYZ over ∠PQR’s vertex.  
2. Align one arm (e.g., YZ) with PQ.  
3. Check if the second arm (XY) lies inside or outside PQR’s second arm (QR).  
   - If XY lies **inside** QR, ∠XYZ < ∠PQR.  
   - If XY lies **outside**, ∠XYZ > ∠PQR.  

### Example 2:  
Are ∠AOB and ∠XOY equal?  
**Solution**:  
1. Superimpose ∠AOB and ∠XOY by aligning O with O.  
2. Match OA with OX.  
3. Check if OB aligns with OY.  
   - If OB ↔ OY, the angles are equal.  
   - If not, one is larger.  

### Example 3:  
Which angle is larger: ∠XOB or ∠XOC?  
**Solution**:  
1. Share the common vertex X.  
2. Compare arms OB and OC.  
   - If OC is further rotated from XO than OB, ∠XOC > ∠XOB.  

### Example 4:  
Compare ∠AOB and ∠XOY using tracing paper.  
**Solution**:  
1. Trace ∠AOB onto tracing paper.  
2. Overlay the traced angle on ∠XOY.  
3. If the arms match perfectly, they’re equal. If not, adjust accordingly.  

### Example 5:  
Why is ∠XOY greater than ∠AOB?  
**Solution**:  
1. Visualize the rotation: ∠XOY requires more turn from XO to OY than ∠AOB does from AO to BO.  
2. Thus, ∠XOY > ∠AOB due to greater rotation.

## 6. Summary  
- **Superimposition** helps compare angles by overlapping their vertices and arms.  
- **Equal angles** have matching arms and vertices when overlapped.  
- Rotation determines angle size: more rotation = larger angle.  
- Always align the **vertex** first, then match one **arm** before comparing the second.  
- Real-world applications include paper folding and machinery design.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| ∠A > ∠B | If arm of ∠A lies outside ∠B after superimposition |  
| ∠A = ∠B | If all arms and vertices match during superimposition |  
| Rotation measure | Determines angle size (more rotation = larger angle) |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ **Misaligning the vertex**: Ensure vertices are matched before comparing arms.  
- ❌ **Ignoring rotation direction**: Always measure rotation from one arm to the other.  

### Exam Tips  
- ✅ Use tracing paper for accurate comparisons.  
- ✅ Label all angles clearly (e.g., ∠AOB) to avoid confusion.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Vertex First, Arm Next"**:  
1. Align the **vertex**.  
2. Match one **arm**.  
3. Compare the second **arm** to determine size.
