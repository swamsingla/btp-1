## 2. Introduction  
Ever wondered why clocks have numbers from 1 to 12? Or why athletes adjust their body angles during a jump? Understanding **types of angles** helps us describe the world around us!  

In this topic, you’ll learn to **classify angles** based on their **measures** (how big or small they are). You’ll discover four special types of angles: **acute**, **right**, **obtuse**, and **reflex**. By the end, you’ll be able to identify these angles in diagrams and real-life situations!  

Let’s start by exploring what makes each angle unique!  

---  

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definitions  
> **An acute angle** is an angle that is **smaller than a right angle** (90°). Its measure lies **between 0° and 90°**.  
> **A right angle** is exactly **90°**.  
> **An obtuse angle** is larger than a right angle but **less than a straight angle** (180°). Its measure ranges from **90° to 180°**.  
> **A reflex angle** is **greater than a straight angle** (180°) but **less than a full circle** (360°).  

### 3.2 Visualizing Angles  
Imagine a clock face:  
- The angle between the hour and minute hands at **3:00** is a **right angle** (90°).  
- At **1:00**, the angle is **30°** (acute).  
- At **7:00**, the angle is **150°** (obtuse).  
- If the hands overlap near **10:00**, the smaller angle is **60°**, but the larger angle (going the other way) is **300°** (reflex).  

### 3.3 Classifying Angles  
To classify an angle:  
1. Measure its degree using a protractor.  
2. Compare the measure to the thresholds:  
   - **0° < x < 90°** → Acute  
   - **x = 90°** → Right  
   - **90° < x < 180°** → Obtuse  
   - **180° < x < 360°** → Reflex  

### 3.4 Example Diagrams  
From the source text:  
- **Acute angles**: 40°, 50°, 75° (e.g., ∠PQR = 40°).  
- **Obtuse angles**: 130°, 110° (e.g., ∠TSR = 130°).  
- **Reflex angles**: 240°, 300° (e.g., ∠SPA = 240°).  

Remember: The **largest angle** in a diagram is often the reflex angle!

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Identifying Angles  
**Question**: Classify the following angles:  
a. 35°  
b. 90°  
c. 120°  
d. 270°  

**Solution**:  
a. $35^\circ $ is **acute** because $ 0^\circ < 35^\circ < 90^\circ $.  
b. $ 90^\circ $ is a **right angle**.  
c. $ 120^\circ $ is **obtuse** since $ 90^\circ < 120^\circ < 180^\circ $.  
d. $ 270^\circ $ is **reflex** because $ 180^\circ < 270^\circ < 360^\circ $.  

### Example 2: Using a Protractor  
**Question**: Measure ∠PTQ in the diagram and classify it.  

**Solution**:  
1. Place the protractor’s center at vertex T.  
2. Align one arm of the angle (∠PTQ) with the baseline of the protractor.  
3. Read the measure where the other arm intersects the scale: $ \angle PTQ = 110^\circ $.  
4. Since $ 90^\circ < 110^\circ < 180^\circ $, it is an **obtuse angle**.  

### Example 3: Finding Missing Angles  
**Question**: In the diagram, ∠TER = 80°, and ∠REB is a straight angle (180°). Find ∠BET.  

**Solution**:  
1. A straight angle means $ \angle REB = 180^\circ $.  
2. Subtract ∠TER from ∠REB:

$$
   \angle BET = \angle REB - \angle TER = 180^\circ - 80^\circ = 100^\circ

$$  
3. Since $90^\circ < 100^\circ < 180^\circ $, ∠BET is **obtuse**.  

### Example 4: Reflex Angles  
**Question**: Calculate the reflex angle at vertex S if the non-reflex angle is 100°.  

**Solution**:  
1. A full circle is $ 360^\circ $.  
2. Subtract the non-reflex angle:

$$
   \text{Reflex angle} = 360^\circ - 100^\circ = 260^\circ

$$  
3. Verify: $180^\circ < 260^\circ < 360^\circ $, so it is a **reflex angle**.  

### Example 5: Real-Life Application  
**Question**: A pizza slice has a central angle of 75°. Is this an acute, obtuse, or reflex angle?  

**Solution**:  
- $ 75^\circ $ is **acute** because it is less than 90°.  
- This matches the shape of a thin pizza slice!

## 6. Summary  
- **Acute angles**: $ 0^\circ < x < 90^\circ $  
- **Right angles**: $ x = 90^\circ $  
- **Obtuse angles**: $ 90^\circ < x < 180^\circ $  
- **Reflex angles**: $ 180^\circ < x < 360^\circ $  
- Always measure angles using a protractor to classify them accurately.  
- Remember: A **straight angle** is $ 180^\circ $, and a **full rotation** is $ 360^\circ $.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ 0^\circ < x < 90^\circ $ | Acute angle |  
| $ x = 90^\circ $ | Right angle |  
| $ 90^\circ < x < 180^\circ $ | Obtuse angle |  
| $ 180^\circ < x < 360^\circ $ | Reflex angle |  
| $ x + y = 360^\circ $ | Sum of a reflex angle and its non-reflex counterpart |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing acute and obtuse angles:  
  - **Fix**: Draw both angles side-by-side and compare their sizes.  
- ❌ Misreading reflex angles:  
  - **Fix**: Always check if the angle is **greater than 180°** but **less than 360°**.  

### Exam Tips  
- ✅ Use a protractor carefully—ensure the center aligns with the vertex.  
- ✅ Double-check calculations when subtracting angles (e.g., reflex angles).  

### Memory Aid  
**AROAR** (Acute, Right, Obtuse, Reflex, All Round):  
- A = Acute (less than 90°)  
- R = Right (exactly 90°)  
- O = Obtuse (between 90° and 180°)  
- A = Reflex (between 180° and 360°)  
- R = Round (full circle, 360°)!
