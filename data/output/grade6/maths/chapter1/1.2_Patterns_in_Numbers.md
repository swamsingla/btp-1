## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Number Sequences  
A **number sequence** is a list of numbers arranged in a specific order according to a defined rule. Each number in the sequence is called a **term**. For example, the sequence of counting numbers follows the rule: *add 1 to the previous term*.  

> **Definition**: A **sequence** is an ordered collection of numbers where each term is generated by applying a specific rule.  

### 3.2 Types of Number Sequences  
From **Table 1** in the source text, we encounter several common number sequences:  

#### 3.2.1 Counting Numbers  
Sequence: $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, ...$  
**Rule**: Start at 1 and add 1 repeatedly.

$$
\text{Term}_n = n

$$  

#### 3.2.2 Odd Numbers  
Sequence: $1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, ...$  
**Rule**: Start at 1 and add 2 repeatedly.

$$
\text{Term}_n = 2n - 1

$$  

#### 3.2.3 Even Numbers  
Sequence: $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, ...$  
**Rule**: Start at 2 and add 2 repeatedly.

$$
\text{Term}_n = 2n

$$  

#### 3.2.4 Triangular Numbers  
Sequence: $1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, ...$  
**Rule**: Sum of the first $ n $ natural numbers.

$$
\text{Term}_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}

$$  

#### 3.2.5 Square Numbers  
Sequence: $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, ...$  
**Rule**: Squares of natural numbers.

$$
\text{Term}_n = n^2

$$  

#### 3.2.6 Cube Numbers  
Sequence: $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, ...$  
**Rule**: Cubes of natural numbers.

$$
\text{Term}_n = n^3

$$  

#### 3.2.7 Virahānka Numbers  
Sequence: $1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, ...$  
**Rule**: Fibonacci-like sequence starting with 1 and 2.

$$
\text{Term}_n = \text{Term}_{n-1} + \text{Term}_{n-2}

$$  

#### 3.2.8 Powers of 2  
Sequence: $1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, ...$  
**Rule**: Multiply the previous term by 2.

$$
\text{Term}_n = 2^{n-1}

$$  

#### 3.2.9 Powers of 3  
Sequence: $1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729, ...$  
**Rule**: Multiply the previous term by 3.

$$
\text{Term}_n = 3^{n-1}

$$  

### 3.3 Identifying Patterns  
To find the rule of a sequence:  
1. Look for differences between consecutive terms.  
2. Check if terms are multiples of a number.  
3. Observe geometric relationships (e.g., squares, cubes).  

**Tip**: Always write down the terms and look for repetition or progression.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Extending the Sequence of Even Numbers  
**Problem**: Continue the sequence $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ...$ with the next three terms.  
**Solution**:  
- Rule: Add 2 to the previous term.  
- Next term: $ 10 + 2 = 12 $  
- Following term: $ 12 + 2 = 14 $  
- Final term: $ 14 + 2 = 16 $  
**Answer**: $ 12, 14, 16 $  

### Example 2: Finding the 5th Term in Square Numbers  
**Problem**: Find the 5th term in the sequence $ 1, 4, 9, 16, ...$.  
**Solution**:  
- Rule: $\text{Term}_n = n^2 $  
- Substitute $ n = 5 $:

$$
\text{Term}_5 = 5^2 = 25

$$  
**Answer**: $25 $  

### Example 3: Generating the Next Three Terms in Virahānka Numbers  
**Problem**: Extend the sequence $ 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ...$ with the next three terms.  
**Solution**:  
- Rule: Each term is the sum of the two preceding terms.  
- Next term: $ 13 + 8 = 21 $  
- Following term: $ 21 + 13 = 34 $  
- Final term: $ 34 + 21 = 55 $  
**Answer**: $ 21, 34, 55 $  

### Example 4: Identifying the Pattern in Powers of 3  
**Problem**: Determine the rule for the sequence $ 1, 3, 9, 27, 81, ...$.  
**Solution**:  
- Observe that each term is multiplied by 3.  
- General rule: $\text{Term}_n = 3^{n-1} $  
**Answer**: Multiplying by 3 repeatedly; $\text{Term}_n = 3^{n-1} $  

### Example 5: Calculating the 7th Term in Triangular Numbers  
**Problem**: Find the 7th term in the sequence $ 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, ...$.  
**Solution**:  
- Rule: $\text{Term}_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2} $  
- Substitute $ n = 7 $:

$$
\text{Term}_7 = \frac{7 \times 8}{2} = 28

$$  
**Answer**: $28 $

## 6. Summary  
- **Number sequences** follow specific rules to generate terms.  
- Common sequences include counting numbers, odd/even numbers, triangular numbers, square/cube numbers, and powers.  
- Rules for generating terms often involve addition, multiplication, or geometric progression.  
- To extend a sequence, apply the identified rule consistently.  
- Mathematical formulas like $\text{Term}_n = n^2 $ (squares) or $\text{Term}_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2} $ (triangular numbers) help calculate specific terms.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Confusing square and cube numbers**: Remember, squares are $ n^2 $ while cubes are $ n^3 $.  
   - *Correction*: Use exponents correctly (e.g., $ 4^2 = 16 $, $ 4^3 = 64 $).  
2. **Misapplying rules**: For Fibonacci sequences, always add the two previous terms.  
   - *Correction*: Verify each term by checking the rule.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Practice identifying patterns**: Write out sequences and test different rules.  
- **Use formulas strategically**: Memorize key formulas for quick calculations.  

### Memory Aid  
**Rhyme for triangular numbers**: *"Triangular terms, sum them up, one plus two, three, four, five, six—multiply n by n plus one, divide by two!"*
