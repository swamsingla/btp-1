## Patterns in Mathematics

### 1.2 Patterns in Numbers

Are you aware of the Fibonacci Sequence? This ancient series has fascinated mathematicians for centuries. Did you know that the ratio of any two adjacent numbers in the Fibonacci sequence approaches the golden ratio (approximately 1.61803398875)? The Fibonacci sequence starts with 0 and 1, and each subsequent number is the sum of the previous two. Let's dive deeper into patterns in numbers!

**What is Mathematics?**

In our previous lesson, we explored what mathematics is and how it is used in various aspects of our lives. Now, let's delve into one of the fundamental areas of mathematics: patterns in numbers. We'll discover the beauty and importance of recognizing and analyzing patterns in numerical sequences.

**Explanation**

A **number sequence** is a series of numbers arranged in a specific order, such as counting numbers, odd numbers, even numbers, or even more complex sequences like triangular numbers or powers of 2. These sequences can exhibit various characteristics, including arithmetic progressions, geometric progressions, or even more intricate relationships between the numbers.

One of the most famous sequences is the Fibonacci sequence, which consists of numbers whose values are the sum of the two preceding ones, usually starting with 0 and 1:

$$

0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13,...


$$

Another example of a number sequence is the sequence of perfect squares:

$$

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49,...


$$

These sequences can be classified based on their properties. For instance, a **sequence with constant differences** is called an **arithmetic progression**, while a sequence with constant ratios between successive terms is called a **geometric progression**.

There are several ways to identify patterns in number sequences:

- **Arithmetic Progression**: A sequence with a common difference between consecutive terms.
- **Geometric Progression**: A sequence with a common ratio between consecutive terms.
- **Fibonacci Sequence**: A sequence where each term is the sum of the two preceding terms, usually starting with 0 and 1.
- **Perfect Squares**: A sequence of numbers obtained by squaring integers, starting from 1.

**Solved Examples**

### Example 1: Identifying Arithmetic Progression

Identify the pattern in the sequence:

$$

2, 5, 8, 11, 14,...


$$

Solution:

To determine if this is an arithmetic progression, calculate the difference between consecutive terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_1 &= 5 - 2 \\
&= 3\\
d_2 &= 8 - 5 \\
&= 3\\
d_3 &= 11 - 8 \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}


$$

Since the differences are constant, this is an arithmetic progression with a common difference of 3.

### Example 2: Identifying Geometric Progression

Identify the pattern in the sequence:

$$

1, 3, 9, 27, 81,...


$$

Solution:

To determine if this is a geometric progression, find the ratio between consecutive terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1 &= \frac{3}{1} \\
&= 3\\
r_2 &= \frac{9}{3} \\
&= 3\\
r_3 &= \frac{27}{9} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}


$$

Since the ratios are constant, this is a geometric progression with a common ratio of 3.

### Example 3: Finding the nth Term

Find the 5th term in the Fibonacci sequence:

$$

0, 1, 1, 2, 3,...


$$

Solution:

Using the definition of the Fibonacci sequence, where each term is the sum of the two preceding terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1 &= 0\\
F_2 &= 1\\
F_3 &= F_1 + F_2 = 0 + 1 = 1\\
F_4 &= F_2 + F_3 = 1 + 1 = 2\\
F_5 &= F_3 + F_4 = 1 + 2 = 3\\
\end{aligned}


$$
