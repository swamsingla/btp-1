## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Defining Key Terms  
**Number Sequence**: A list of numbers arranged in a specific order, often following a rule.  
**Triangular Numbers**: Numbers that can form an equilateral triangle when dots are arranged.  
**Square Numbers**: Numbers that can form a perfect square when dots are arranged.  
**Cube Numbers**: Numbers that can form a cube when dots are stacked in three dimensions.  
**Hexagonal Numbers**: Numbers that can form a hexagon shape with dots.  

> **Definition**: Triangular numbers are formed by adding consecutive natural numbers. For example, the third triangular number is $1 + 2 + 3 = 6 $.  

> **Definition**: Square numbers are the squares of whole numbers. For example, $ 4 = 2^2 $, $ 9 = 3^2 $, and so on.  

> **Definition**: Cube numbers are the cubes of whole numbers. For example, $ 8 = 2^3 $, $ 27 = 3^3 $, and so on.  

### 3.2 Visualising Sequences  
#### **Triangular Numbers**  
These numbers represent dots arranged in a triangle. The nth triangular number is calculated as:

$$

T_n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}

$$  
**Example**:  
- $T_1 = 1 $ (1 dot)  
- $ T_2 = 1 + 2 = 3 $ (2 rows of dots)  
- $ T_3 = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 $  

#### **Square Numbers**  
These numbers form a square grid. The nth square number is:

$$

S_n = n^2

$$  
**Example**:  
- $S_1 = 1^2 = 1 $  
- $ S_2 = 2^2 = 4 $  
- $ S_3 = 3^2 = 9 $  

#### **Cube Numbers**  
These numbers form a 3D cube. The nth cube number is:

$$

C_n = n^3

$$  
**Example**:  
- $C_1 = 1^3 = 1 $  
- $ C_2 = 2^3 = 8 $  
- $ C_3 = 3^3 = 27 $  

#### **Hexagonal Numbers**  
These numbers form a hexagon. The nth hexagonal number is:

$$

H_n = n(2n - 1)

$$  
**Example**:  
- $H_1 = 1(2 \cdot 1 - 1) = 1 $  
- $ H_2 = 2(2 \cdot 2 - 1) = 6 $  
- $ H_3 = 3(2 \cdot 3 - 1) = 15 $  

### 3.3 Special Case: Numbers That Belong to Multiple Categories  
Some numbers appear in multiple sequences. For example, **36** is both a triangular number and a square number:  
- As a triangular number: $ T_8 = \frac{8 \times 9}{2} = 36 $  
- As a square number: $ S_6 = 6^2 = 36 $  

This demonstrates how numbers can have **multiple meanings** depending on the context.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Drawing the Next Figure in a Sequence  
**Question**: Complete the triangular number sequence visually.  
**Solution**:  
- First 3 triangular numbers:  
  - Row 1: 1 dot  
  - Row 2: 1 + 2 = 3 dots  
  - Row 3: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 dots  
- Next figure (Row 4): 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = **10 dots**  

### Example 2: Identifying Square Numbers  
**Question**: Is 25 a square number?  
**Solution**:  
- Calculate $ \sqrt{25} = 5 $.  
- Since 5 is a whole number, **25 is a square number**.  

### Example 3: Calculating Cube Numbers  
**Question**: Find the 4th cube number.  
**Solution**:  
- $ C_4 = 4^3 = 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64 $.  

### Example 4: Verifying Hexagonal Numbers  
**Question**: Is 15 a hexagonal number?  
**Solution**:  
- Plug into the formula: $ H_n = n(2n - 1) $  
- Solve $ n(2n - 1) = 15 $:  
  - Try $ n=3 $: $ 3(6 - 1) = 3 \times 5 = 15 $.  
- **Yes, 15 is the 3rd hexagonal number**.  

### Example 5: Explaining Dual Representations  
**Question**: Why is 36 both triangular and square?  
**Solution**:  
- **Triangular**: $ T_8 = \frac{8 \times 9}{2} = 36 $.  
- **Square**: $ S_6 = 6^2 = 36 $.  
- Hence, **36 can be arranged in both a triangle and a square**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**:  
1. Draw the next figure in the square number sequence (1, 4, 9, ...).  
2. Is 16 a square number? Explain.  

🟡 **Medium**:  
3. Calculate the 5th triangular number.  
4. Identify which of the following is a cube number: 8, 12, 27, 30.  

🔴 **Hard**:  
5. Prove that 36 is both triangular and square.  

**Answers**:  
1. Next square number: $ S_5 = 5^2 = 25 $ (draw 5x5 dots).  
2. Yes, $ \sqrt{16} = 4 $, so 16 is $ 4^2 $.  
3. $ T_5 = \frac{5 \times 6}{2} = 15 $.  
4. **27** is $ 3^3 $, so it’s a cube number.  
5. **Proof**:  
   - **Triangular**: $ T_8 = \frac{8 \times 9}{2} = 36 $.  
   - **Square**: $ S_6 = 6^2 = 36 $.  
   - Therefore, 36 satisfies both conditions.

## 6. Summary  
- **Triangular numbers** are formed by summing consecutive integers.  
- **Square numbers** are squares of whole numbers.  
- **Cube numbers** are cubes of whole numbers.  
- **Hexagonal numbers** follow the formula $ H_n = n(2n - 1) $.  
- Some numbers, like 36, belong to multiple sequences.  
- Visualising sequences helps uncover hidden patterns in numbers.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing triangular and square numbers (e.g., mistaking 6 as a square number).  
  ✅ Fix: Remember $ S_n = n^2 $, so 6 isn’t a perfect square.  
- ❌ Forgetting to calculate the **next term** in a sequence.  
  ✅ Fix: Always apply the rule (e.g., add 2 for even numbers).  

### Exam Tips  
- **Sketch diagrams** for sequences to avoid calculation errors.  
- **Memorize the formulas** for triangular, square, and cube numbers.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Triangle, Square, Cube – TSC"**  
- **T**riangle: Sum of numbers (e.g., 1+2+3=6).  
- **S**quare: Multiply number by itself (e.g., 4×4=16).  
- **C**ube: Multiply number thrice (e.g., 3×3×3=27).
