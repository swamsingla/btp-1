## **What is Mathematics?**

Can you think of a time when you noticed a pattern while solving a puzzle or playing a game? Or maybe you've seen a pattern in the arrangement of tiles on a puzzle or the score in a favorite sports team? Mathematics is all about recognizing and understanding these patterns, and using them to make predictions, create models, and solve problems!

In this topic, we'll delve into the world of patterns in mathematics, exploring what makes up mathematics, how we recognize patterns, and how we use them to describe and analyze the world around us. By the end of this topic, you'll understand the importance of patterns in mathematics and be able to identify and apply them in various contexts.

This topic builds upon your existing knowledge of numbers and basic mathematical operations, so let's dive right in!

### > **Definition:** > **A Pattern** is a repeated sequence of objects, colors, shapes, or values that repeat over time or space. > **An Equation** is a statement that expresses the equality of two mathematical expressions.

## **Explanation**

Mathematics is, in large part, the search for patterns, and for the explanations as to why those patterns exist. Such patterns indeed exist all around us — in nature, in our homes and schools, and in the motion of the sun, moon, and stars. They occur in everything that we do and see, from shopping and cooking, to throwing a ball and playing games, to understanding weather patterns and using technology.

There are several key aspects of mathematics that involve patterns:

- **Number Patterns**: These are sequences of numbers that exhibit a regular repetition, such as the sequence 2, 4, 8, 16,...
- **Shape Patterns**: These are arrangements of shapes that repeat, such as the pattern of squares in a chessboard or the repeating design of a leaf.
- **Algebraic Patterns**: These involve relationships between variables and constants, often expressed through equations.

To understand these patterns, we need to identify the underlying structure and relationship between the elements. This involves developing skills such as observation, deduction, and logical reasoning.

### > **Key Concepts:**

- **Pattern recognition**: The ability to identify regularities and repetitions in data.
- **Sequence**: A series of events or data points arranged in a particular order.
- **Series**: A sum of a sequence of numbers or quantities.

### > **Formulas and Theorems:**

- **\*The Arithmetic Mean-Geometric Mean Inequality (AM-GM)**: For non-negative real numbers \(a*1, a_2,..., a_n\), the inequality holds: $\left(\prod*{i=1}^{n} a_i\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} \geq \frac{a_1 + a_2 +... + a_n}{n} $.
- **\*The Summation Notation**: $\sum\_{i=1}^{n} a_i $ represents the sum of the terms $ a_i $ from $ i=1 $ to $ n $.

## **Solved Examples**

### Example 1:

Find the next number in the sequence: 2, 4, 8, 16,?

**Solution:**

1.  Identify the pattern: Each term is twice the previous term.
2.  Apply the pattern: The next term is 32.

### Example 2:

Given the equation $ x^2 + 7x + 12 = 0 $, factor the quadratic expression.

**Solution:**

1.  Factor the quadratic expression by finding two numbers whose product is 12 and whose sum is 7. These numbers are 3 and 4.
2.  Rewrite the equation as $ (x+3)(x+4)=0 $
3.  Set each factor equal to zero and solve for x: $ x=-3 $ or $ x=-4 $

### Example 3:

Determine whether the sequence 1, 2, 4, 8,... is arithmetic or geometric.

**Solution:**

1.  Check if the difference between consecutive terms is constant: 2-1=1, 4-2=2, 8-4=4. The differences are increasing, indicating it's not an arithmetic sequence.
2.  Check if the ratio between consecutive terms is constant: 2/1=2, 4/2=2, 8/4=2. The ratios are constant, indicating it's a geometric sequence.
