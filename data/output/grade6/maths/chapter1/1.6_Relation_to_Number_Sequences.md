## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever noticed how shapes and numbers are connected? In this lesson, we’ll explore how **shape sequences** like regular polygons, stacked squares, and fractals like the Koch Snowflake are deeply tied to **number sequences**! By understanding these relationships, you’ll gain new insights into both geometry and arithmetic.  

---

## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 Key Terms and Definitions  
- **Number Sequence**: A list of numbers arranged in a specific pattern (e.g., counting numbers: $3, 4, 5, 6, \dots $).  
- **Shape Sequence**: A series of shapes that follow a geometric pattern (e.g., regular polygons increasing in sides).  
- **Regular Polygon**: A polygon with all sides and angles equal (e.g., triangle, square, pentagon).  

### 3.2 Example: Regular Polygons and Counting Numbers  
> The number of **sides** in a regular polygon sequence corresponds to the **counting numbers** starting at 3:

$$
3, 4, 5, 6, 7, \dots

$$  
These are called:  
- Triangle (3 sides)  
- Quadrilateral (4 sides)  
- Pentagon (5 sides)  
- Hexagon (6 sides), and so on.  

**Why does this happen?**  
Each regular polygon has sides equal to its name’s prefix. For instance, a "pentagon" has five sides because "penta-" means five. This direct link makes the side count a straightforward **number sequence**.  

### 3.3 Other Shape Sequences  
#### 3.3.1 Stacked Squares and Triangles  
- **Stacked Squares**: Each figure adds a layer of squares. The number of small squares forms a **triangular number sequence**:

$$
  1, 3, 6, 10, \dots

$$  
- **Stacked Triangles**: Similarly, the number of small triangles follows a **triangular number sequence**.  

#### 3.3.2 Koch Snowflake  
- The **Koch Snowflake** starts with a triangle and iteratively replaces each line segment with a "speed bump."  
- The number of **line segments** in each iteration forms the sequence:

$$
  3, 12, 48, 192, \dots

$$  
  This is a **geometric sequence** where each term is multiplied by 4:

$$
  a_n = 3 \times 4^{n-1}

$$

## 4. Solved Examples  
### Example 1: Regular Polygon Side Count  
**Question**: What is the number of sides in the 7th shape of the regular polygon sequence?  
**Solution**:  
The sequence is $3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, \dots $. The 7th term is **9**.  
Thus, the 7th shape is a **nonagon** (9-sided polygon).  

### Example 2: Koch Snowflake Line Segments  
**Question**: Find the number of line segments in the 3rd iteration of the Koch Snowflake.  
**Solution**:  
Using the formula $ a_n = 3 \times 4^{n-1} $:

$$
a_3 = 3 \times 4^{2} = 3 \times 16 = 48

$$  
**Answer**: 48 line segments.  

### Example 3: Stacked Squares  
**Question**: How many small squares are in the 4th shape of the stacked squares sequence?  
**Solution**:  
Triangular numbers: $1, 3, 6, 10, \dots $. The 4th term is **10**.  
**Answer**: 10 small squares.

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**:  
1. Identify the number sequence formed by the sides of regular polygons.  
2. What is the 5th term in the Koch Snowflake line segment sequence?  

🟡 **Medium**:  
3. How many small triangles are in the 3rd iteration of the stacked triangles sequence?  
4. Explain why the number of corners in a regular polygon equals its number of sides.  

🔴 **Hard**:  
5. Derive the formula for the number of line segments in the Koch Snowflake sequence.  

**Answers**:  
1. **Counting numbers starting at 3**: $ 3, 4, 5, 6, \dots $.  
2. $ a_5 = 3 \times 4^{4} = 3 \times 256 = 768 $.  
3. Triangular number sequence: $ 1, 3, 6, 10, \dots $. 3rd term is **6**.  
4. In regular polygons, each corner corresponds to a vertex where sides meet. Since sides and angles are equal, corners = sides.  
5. General formula: $ a_n = 3 \times 4^{n-1} $.  

---

## 6. Summary  
- **Shape sequences** often mirror **number sequences** (e.g., regular polygons → counting numbers).  
- The Koch Snowflake’s line segments form a geometric sequence: $ 3, 12, 48, \dots $.  
- Stacked squares/triangles follow **triangular number sequences**: $ 1, 3, 6, 10, \dots $.  
- Understanding these links helps analyze patterns in both geometry and arithmetic.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ a_n = 3 \times 4^{n-1} $ | Koch Snowflake line segments |  
| $ T_n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} $ | Triangular numbers |  
| $ a_n = n $ | Counting numbers (starting at 3 for regular polygons) |  

---

## 8. Student Corner  
**Common Mistakes**:  
- Confusing the number of **sides** and **corners** in polygons (they are always equal!).  
- Miscounting triangles in stacked figures (use the triangular number formula).  

**Memory Aid**:  
Remember the Koch Snowflake’s rule: "Start with 3, multiply by 4 each time!"
