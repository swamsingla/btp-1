## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever noticed how tiles in a floor or patterns on a wall repeat in a predictable way? This is a **pattern in shapes**!  
In this topic, we’ll explore **shape sequences**—patterns made by arranging shapes in a specific order. We’ll learn how to recognize rules governing these sequences and predict the next shape in the series.  
This builds on your knowledge of number patterns and introduces how shapes can follow similar logic. By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to identify and create your own shape patterns!  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 What Are Shape Sequences?  
A **shape sequence** is a series of shapes arranged according to a specific rule. These shapes can be in **one dimension (1D)** (like lines), **two dimensions (2D)** (like polygons), or **three dimensions (3D)** (like cubes).  

> **Definition**: A shape sequence is a pattern formed by repeating or changing shapes in a consistent manner.  

### 3.2 Types of Shape Sequences  
From **Table 3**, we see various shape sequences:  
- **Stacked Triangles**: Triangles arranged in layers.  
- **Complete Graphs**: Graphs with nodes connected by edges.  
- **Koch Snowflake**: A fractal pattern created by iteratively adding smaller triangles.  
- **Regular Polygons**: Shapes with equal sides and angles (e.g., triangles, squares, pentagons).  

> **Property**: Regular polygons have **equal sides and angles**, while irregular polygons do not.  

### 3.3 Identifying Patterns in Shapes  
To recognize a pattern, look for changes in:  
- **Number of sides** (e.g., triangles → quadrilaterals → pentagons).  
- **Size or orientation** (e.g., growing triangles).  
- **Arrangement** (e.g., alternating shapes).  

> **Tip**: Draw the sequence step-by-step to spot the rule.  

### 3.4 Example: Polygon Side Count  
Consider the sequence: Triangle (3 sides) → Quadrilateral (4 sides) → Pentagon (5 sides).  
**Rule**: The number of sides increases by 1 each time.  

> **Formula**: If the first term has $ n $ sides, the $ k^{th} $ term has $ n + (k - 1) $ sides.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### **Example 1:**  
Identify the rule for the sequence: Triangle → Square → Pentagon → Hexagon.  
**Solution:**  
- Triangle has 3 sides.  
- Square has 4 sides.  
- Pentagon has 5 sides.  
- Hexagon has 6 sides.  
**Rule**: Each shape has **one more side than the previous**.  
**Next shape**: Heptagon (7 sides).  

### **Example 2:**  
Draw the next shape in the sequence:

$$
\text{Line segment} \rightarrow \text{Square} \rightarrow \text{Cube}

$$  
**Solution:**  
- Line segment (1D) → Square (2D) → Cube (3D).  
**Pattern**: Dimensions increase by 1 each step.  
**Next shape**: A 4D hypercube (though not visible in our world!).  

### **Example 3:**  
Analyze the Koch Snowflake sequence.  
**Step 1**: Start with an equilateral triangle.  
**Step 2**: Replace each side with four segments, forming a smaller triangle.  
**Step 3**: Repeat the process indefinitely.  
**Rule**: Each iteration adds more sides, creating a fractal pattern.  

### **Example 4:**  
Given the sequence: K2 → K3 → K4 → K5, determine the next term.  
**Solution:**  
- K2 is a graph with 2 vertices.  
- K3 has 3 vertices.  
- K4 has 4 vertices.  
- K5 has 5 vertices.  
**Rule**: The subscript represents the number of vertices.  
**Next term**: K6 (6 vertices).  

### **Example 5:**  
Find the missing shape in the sequence:

$$
\text{Circle} \rightarrow \text{Square} \rightarrow \_\_ \rightarrow \text{Hexagon}

$$  
**Solution:**  
- Circle (0 sides) → Square (4 sides) → Pentagon (5 sides) → Hexagon (6 sides).  
**Missing shape**: Pentagon.

## 6. Summary  
- **Shape sequences** follow rules like increasing sides, dimensions, or arrangements.  
- **Regular polygons** have equal sides and angles; **irregular** ones do not.  
- **Fractals** like the Koch Snowflake grow infinitely by repeating patterns.  
- **Key Rule**: The $ k^{th} $ term in a side-count sequence has $ n + (k - 1) $ sides.  
- **Patterns in shapes** connect to number sequences via visual and numerical rules.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|

| $ \text{Vertices in } K_n $ | $ n $ |  
| $ \text{Dimensions} $ | Increases by 1 in sequences like line → square → cube |

## 8. Student Corner  

### **Common Mistakes**  
- ❌ Miscounting sides in polygons (e.g., confusing pentagon with hexagon).  
- ❌ Assuming all shapes in a sequence are regular.  
- ✅ Fix: Use a ruler to count sides or label each vertex.  

### **Exam Tips**  
- **Always draw** the sequence step-by-step to visualize the pattern.  
- **Look for incremental changes** in size, sides, or arrangement.
