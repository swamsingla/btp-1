## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 Definition of Supercells  
A **supercell** is a number that is **greater than all its adjacent cells**. Adjacent cells are those directly next to it: **left, right, top, and bottom**. For example:  
- $ 626 $ is a supercell because it’s larger than $ 577 $ (left) and $ 345 $ (bottom).  
- $ 200 $ is **not** a supercell because it’s smaller than $ 577 $ (top).  

> **Math Tip**: To find a supercell, compare a number to its neighbors using symbols like $>$ (greater than) and $<$ (less than).  

### 3.2 Rules for Identifying Supercells  
- Only **one direction** matters per cell (e.g., up, down, left, right).  
- Diagonals **do not count** as adjacent.  
- A cell can’t be a supercell if it has **equal values** in any neighbor.  

### 3.3 Special Cases  
- **Largest number in a table**: It **always** becomes a supercell unless surrounded by equal numbers.  
- **Smallest number**: It **can never** be a supercell because it’s smaller than all others.  

---

## 4. Solved Examples  
### Example 1: Identify Supercells in a Grid  
Consider this 3×3 grid:

$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
45 & 30 & 25 \\
\hline
35 & 50 & 40 \\
\hline
20 & 42 & 60 \\
\hline
\end{array}

$$  
**Step 1**: Compare each number to its neighbors.  
- $50 $ (center): Neighbors are $ 45 $, $ 30 $, $ 35 $, $ 42 $, and $ 40 $.  
  - $ 50 > 45 $, $ 50 > 30 $, $ 50 > 35 $, $ 50 > 42 $, $ 50 > 40 $ → ✅ SuperCell  
- $ 60 $ (bottom-right): Neighbors are $ 40 $ (left) and $ 42 $ (top).  
  - $ 60 > 40 $ and $ 60 > 42 $ → ✅ SuperCell  

**Answer**: Supercells are $ 50 $ and $ 60 $.  

---  

### Example 2: Maximize Supercells  
Goal: Create a table with **maximum supercells** using numbers $ 100–1000 $ (no repeats).  
**Strategy**:  
1. Place the **largest number** in the center.  
2. Surround it with **smaller numbers** to maximize comparisons.  

Example grid:

$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
100 & 200 & 300 \\
\hline
400 & 900 & 500 \\
\hline
600 & 700 & 800 \\
\hline
\end{array}

$$  
**Analysis**:  
- $900 $ (center) is a supercell.  
- $ 600 $ (top-left) has neighbors $ 100 $ (right) and $ 400 $ (below).  
  - $ 600 > 100 $ and $ 600 > 400 $ → ✅ SuperCell  
- Similarly, $ 700 $ and $ 800 $ may also qualify.  

**Answer**: Maximum supercells achievable = 4 (depending on arrangements).

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**:  
1. Which number in this grid is a supercell?

$$
   \begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
   \hline
   10 & 20 & 30 \\
   \hline
   40 & 50 & 60 \\
   \hline
   70 & 80 & 90 \\
   \hline
   \end{array}

$$  
**Answer**: $90 $ is a supercell (largest number).  

🟡 **Medium**:  
2. Design a 2×2 grid with **three supercells** using numbers $ 100–200 $.  
**Answer**:

$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|}
\hline
150 & 120 \\
\hline
130 & 180 \\
\hline
\end{array}

$$  
- $180 $ (bottom-right) is a supercell.  
- $ 150 $ (top-left) is greater than $ 120 $ (right) and $ 130 $ (below) → ✅ SuperCell  

🔴 **Hard**:  
3. Is it possible to create a table with **zero supercells**? Explain.  
**Answer**: No. At least one number (the largest) will always be greater than its neighbors, making it a supercell.  

---

## 6. Summary  
- A **supercell** is a number greater than all its adjacent cells.  
- The **largest number** in a table is always a supercell.  
- The **smallest number** cannot be a supercell.  
- To maximize supercells, place large numbers strategically.

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ a > b $ | $ a $ is greater than $ b $ |  
| $ a < b $ | $ a $ is smaller than $ b $ |  
| $ a = b $ | $ a $ and $ b $ are equal |
