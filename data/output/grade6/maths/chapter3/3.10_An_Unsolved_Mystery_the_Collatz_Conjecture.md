## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 The Collatz Rule  
The Collatz Conjecture involves a simple rule:  
- **If the number is even**, divide it by 2.  
- **If the number is odd**, multiply it by 3 and add 1.  
Repeat this process until you reach the number **1**.  

For example:  
Start with **5**:  
$ 5 $ (odd) → $ 3 \times 5 + 1 = 16 $ → $ 16 $ (even) → $ 16 \div 2 = 8 $ → $ 8 \div 2 = 4 $ → $ 4 \div 2 = 2 $ → $ 2 \div 2 = 1 $.  

> **Did You Know?** The conjecture was proposed in 1937 by Lothar Collatz, but even today, no one has proven it works for *every* whole number!  

### 3.2 Example Sequences  
The source text shows sequences like:  
**12, 6, 3, 10, 5, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1**  
Each number follows the rule:  
- Even numbers are halved (e.g., $ 12 \rightarrow 6 $)  
- Odd numbers become $ 3n + 1 $ (e.g., $ 3 \rightarrow 10 $)  

All sequences eventually reach **1**, but this hasn’t been proven for *all* numbers.

## 4. Solved Examples  
### 4.1 Example 1: Starting with 7  
Step 1: $ 7 $ (odd) → $ 3 \times 7 + 1 = 22 $  
Step 2: $ 22 $ (even) → $ 22 \div 2 = 11 $  
Step 3: $ 11 $ (odd) → $ 3 \times 11 + 1 = 34 $  
Step 4: $ 34 \rightarrow 17 $ → $ 17 \rightarrow 52 $ → $ 52 \rightarrow 26 $ → $ 26 \rightarrow 13 $ → $ 13 \rightarrow 40 $ → $ 40 \rightarrow 20 $ → $ 20 \rightarrow 10 $ → $ 10 \rightarrow 5 $ → $ 5 \rightarrow 16 $ → $ 16 \rightarrow 8 $ → $ 8 \rightarrow 4 $ → $ 4 \rightarrow 2 $ → $ 2 \rightarrow 1 $.  

**Answer:** Yes, the sequence reaches 1!  

### 4.2 Example 2: Starting with 15  
Step 1: $ 15 $ (odd) → $ 3 \times 15 + 1 = 46 $  
Step 2: $ 46 \rightarrow 23 $ → $ 23 \rightarrow 70 $ → $ 70 \rightarrow 35 $ → $ 35 \rightarrow 106 $ → $ 106 \rightarrow 53 $ → $ 53 \rightarrow 160 $ → $ 160 \rightarrow 80 $ → $ 80 \rightarrow 40 $ → $ 40 \rightarrow 20 $ → $ 20 \rightarrow 10 $ → $ 10 \rightarrow 5 $ → $ 5 \rightarrow 16 $ → ... → $ 1 $.  

**Answer:** The sequence reaches 1!
