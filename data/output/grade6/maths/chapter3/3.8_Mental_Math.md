## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Understanding Mental Math  
**Mental math** refers to performing calculations in your head without writing them down. It relies on recognizing patterns, breaking numbers into manageable parts, and using shortcuts.  

> **Definition**: Mental math is the ability to perform arithmetic operations quickly and accurately using only your mind.  

For example, instead of calculating $12,350 + 24,545 $ step-by-step, you can round numbers to simplify:

$$
12,350 + 24,545 \approx 12,000 + 24,000 = 36,000

$$  
Then adjust for the extra $350 + 545 = 895 $, giving a total of $ 36,895 $. This method saves time while improving accuracy.  

### 3.2 Using Middle Numbers to Create Larger Ones  
The source text shows how numbers in the middle column can be combined (added or subtracted) to form larger numbers on the sides. For instance:

$$
38,800 = 25,000 + (400 \times 2) + 13,000

$$  
Here, the middle number $400 $ is multiplied by 2 and added to other numbers to reach the target.  

> **Property**: Any number in the middle can be reused multiple times to form new numbers through addition or subtraction.  

### 3.3 Adding and Subtracting Large Numbers Mentally  
When working with large numbers like 5-digit values, break them into smaller components. For example:

$$
39,800 = 40,000 - 800 + 300 + 300

$$  
Start with $40,000 $, subtract $ 800 $ to get $ 39,200 $, then add $ 300 + 300 = 600 $, resulting in $ 39,800 $.  

### 3.4 Exploring Number Combinations  
The source asks whether numbers like $ 1,000 $, $ 14,000 $, or $ 16,000 $ can be formed using middle numbers. Let’s explore this:  
- To make $ 1,000 $: Combine $ 1,500 - 500 $ (if $ 500 $ is available).  
- To make $ 14,000 $: Use $ 12,000 + 2,000 $ (if $ 2,000 $ exists).  

> **Tip**: Look for combinations that match the target number by adjusting smaller parts (e.g., adding or subtracting chunks like $ 1,000 $, $ 2,000 $, etc.).  

### 3.5 Solving Figure-it-Out Problems

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ **Error**: Forgetting to carry over digits in addition.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Break numbers into chunks (e.g., $ 12,350 = 12,000 + 350 $).  
- ❌ **Error**: Assuming all $ 5 $-digit sums are $ 5 $-digits.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Remember, $ 99,999 + 99,999 = 199,998 $ (a 6-digit number).  

### Exam Tips  
- **Tip 1**: Practice mental math daily with small challenges (e.g., “What’s $ 25,000 + 3,500 $?”).  
- **Tip 2**: Use estimation to check if your answer is reasonable.  

### Memory Aid  
**Chunk & Check**: Split numbers into smaller parts (e.g., $ 12,350 = 12,000 + 350 $), solve each part, then combine.
