## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Palindromic Numbers  
> A **palindromic number** is a number that reads the same forward and backward. For example, $66 $, $ 848 $, and $ 797 $ are palindromes because reversing their digits gives the same number.  

**Key Vocabulary**:  
- **Palindrome**: A sequence (like a number or word) that reads the same backward as forward.  
- **Digits**: Individual symbols (0–9) used to write numbers.  

### 3.2 Reverse-and-Add Process  
> To explore palindromic patterns, try this activity:  
1. Start with a **2-digit number** (e.g., $ 23 $).  
2. Reverse its digits to get $ 32 $.  
3. Add the original number and its reverse: $ 23 + 32 = 55 $.  
4. If the result is a palindrome, stop. If not, repeat the process with the new number.  

**Example**: Starting with $ 89 $:

$$
89 + 98 = 187 \quad (\text{not a palindrome})  
187 + 781 = 968 \quad (\text{not a palindrome})  
968 + 869 = 1837 \quad (\text{not a palindrome})

$$  
Continue until you reach a palindrome or determine if it’s impossible.  

### 3.3 Creating 3-Digit Palindromes  
> Using digits $1 $, $ 2 $, and $ 3 $, create all possible 3-digit palindromes. A 3-digit palindrome has the form $ ABA $, where $ A $ and $ B $ are digits.  

**Step-by-step**:  
1. Choose the first and last digit ($ A $): $ 1 $, $ 2 $, or $ 3 $.  
2. Choose the middle digit ($ B $): $ 1 $, $ 2 $, or $ 3 $.  
3. Combine as $ ABA $.  

**Result**:  
- $ 111 $, $ 121 $, $ 131 $  
- $ 212 $, $ 222 $, $ 232 $  
- $ 313 $, $ 323 $, $ 333 $  

### 3.4 Solving the Puzzle  
> Solve this riddle:  
- I am a **5-digit palindrome**.  
- I am an **odd number**.  
- My **‘t’ digit** is double my **‘u’ digit**.  
- My **‘h’ digit** is double my **‘t’ digit**.  
- Who am I?  

**Clues Breakdown**:  
- Let the number be $ T H U T H $ (since it’s a palindrome).  
- Odd number ⇒ Last digit (**H**) must be $ 1 $, $ 3 $, $ 5 $, $ 7 $, or $ 9 $.  
- From the clues:  
  - $ T = 2 \times U $  
  - $ H = 2 \times T $  

**Solving**:  
1. Since $ H = 2T $, and $ H $ must be odd, $ T $ must be $ \frac{H}{2} $.  
2. Test $ H = 1 $: $ T = 0.5 $ → Invalid (digits are integers).  
3. Test $ H = 3 $: $ T = 1.5 $ → Invalid.  
4. Test $ H = 5 $: $ T = 2.5 $ → Invalid.  
5. Test $ H = 7 $: $ T = 3.5 $ → Invalid.  
6. Test $ H = 9 $: $ T = 4.5 $ → Invalid.  

Wait! This suggests no solution exists. But the puzzle says there **is** one. Let’s re-express the number as $ T H U T H $, where $ T $ and $ H $ are digits. Since $ T = 2U $, $ U $ must be ≤ 4 (so $ T $ ≤ 8). Let’s test $ U = 1 $:  
- $ T = 2 \times 1 = 2 $  
- $ H = 2 \times T = 4 $  
- Number: $ 2 4 1 2 4 $ → $ 24124 $, which is **even** (ends with 4). Not valid.  

Try $ U = 2 $:  
- $ T = 4 $, $ H = 8 $ → Number: $ 4 8 2 4 8 $ → $ 48248 $, which is even. Still invalid.  

Hmm… contradiction! Maybe the puzzle uses different digit positions? Let’s assume the number is written as $ T H U H T $ instead (common in puzzles). Repeating the logic:  
- $ T = 2U $, $ H = 2T $  
- $ H = 4U $, and $ H $ must be odd ⇒ $ U = 1 $, $ H = 4 $ (invalid). No solution? Or perhaps the puzzle has a typo? Regardless, this shows how logical deduction helps uncover patterns!

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Generating 3-Digit Palindromes  
**Question**: List all 3-digit palindromes using digits $ 1 $, $ 2 $, and $ 3 $.  

**Solution**:  
- First digit ($ A $) can be $ 1 $, $ 2 $, or $ 3 $.  
- Middle digit ($ B $) can be $ 1 $, $ 2 $, or $ 3 $.  
- Resulting palindromes:

$$
  111,\ 121,\ 131,\ 212,\ 222,\ 232,\ 313,\ 323,\ 333

$$  

### Example 2: Reverse-and-Add Activity  
**Question**: Apply the reverse-and-add process to $ 87 $. How many steps does it take to reach a palindrome?  

**Solution**:  
1. $ 87 + 78 = 165 $ (Not a palindrome)  
2. $ 165 + 561 = 726 $ (Not a palindrome)  
3. $ 726 + 627 = 1353 $ (Not a palindrome)  
4. $ 1353 + 3531 = 4884 $ (Palindrome!)  

✅ It took **4 steps** to reach $ 4884 $.  

### Example 3: Solving the Puzzle  
**Question**: Find a 5-digit palindrome satisfying:  
- Odd number  
- ‘t’ digit = 2 × ‘u’ digit  
- ‘h’ digit = 2 × ‘t’ digit  

**Solution**:  
Assume the number is $ T H U T H $.  
From the clues:  
1. $ T = 2U $  
2. $ H = 2T = 4U $  
3. Since $ H $ must be odd, $ 4U $ is odd ⇒ Contradiction.  

No solution exists under these constraints. However, if the number is written as $ T H U H T $, the same logic applies. Thus, the puzzle may have no valid answer or require reinterpretation.  

### Example 4: Check if $ 121 $ is a Palindrome  
**Question**: Is $ 121 $ a palindrome?  

**Solution**:  
- Reverse $ 121 $: $ 121 $  
- Since the reversed number equals the original, $ 121 $ is a palindrome. ✅

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**: List all 3-digit palindromes using digits $ 1 $, $ 2 $, and $ 3 $.  

**Answer**:

$$
111,\ 121,\ 131,\ 212,\ 222,\ 232,\ 313,\ 323,\ 333

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2**: Apply the reverse-and-add process to $ 97 $. How many steps to reach a palindrome?  

**Answer**:  
1. $ 97 + 79 = 176 $  
2. $ 176 + 671 = 847 $  
3. $ 847 + 748 = 1595 $  
4. $ 1595 + 5951 = 7546 $  
5. $ 7546 + 6457 = 14003 $  
6. $ 14003 + 30041 = 44044 $ ✅  

It took **6 steps** to reach $ 44044 $.  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3**: Solve the puzzle:  
- I am a 5-digit palindrome.  
- I am an odd number.  
- My ‘t’ digit is double my ‘u’ digit.  
- My ‘h’ digit is triple my ‘t’ digit.  
- Who am I?  

**Answer**:  
Let the number be $ T H U T H $.  
1. $ T = 2U $  
2. $ H = 3T = 6U $  
Since $ H $ must be a single digit, $ 6U \leq 9 $ ⇒ $ U = 1 $, $ T = 2 $, $ H = 6 $.  
Number: $ 2\ 6\ 1\ 2\ 6 $ → $ 26126 $, which is odd? Wait, ends with 6 ⇒ even! Contradiction.  

If the number is $ T H U H T $:  
1. $ T = 2U $, $ H = 3T = 6U $  
Same issue. Hence, no solution exists under these constraints.

## 6. Summary  
- A **palindromic number** reads the same forward and backward (e.g., $ 66 $, $ 848 $).  
- The **reverse-and-add** process creates palindromes by reversing a number and adding it repeatedly.  
- Some numbers never become palindromes, making this an open mystery.  
- Puzzles like "Who am I?" use logical deductions to solve for unknown digits.  
- Palindromes appear in patterns, games, and even nature (e.g., DNA sequences).

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Palindrome Condition} $ | A number is a palindrome if it equals its reverse (e.g., $ 121 = \text{reverse}(121) $). |  
| $ \text{Reverse-and-Add Steps} $ | Start with a number $ N $, reverse it to get $ R $, add $ N + R $. Repeat until a palindrome is found. |  
| $ \text{Digit Relationships} $ | Use algebra to express digit constraints (e.g., $ T = 2U $, $ H = 2T $). |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing palindromes with regular numbers (e.g., thinking $ 123 $ is a palindrome).  
- ❌ Skipping steps in reverse-and-add (always check each addition!).  
- ❌ Assuming all numbers eventually form palindromes (some may never do so).  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice generating palindromes using digits and small numbers.  
- Use scratch paper to track steps in reverse-and-add problems.
