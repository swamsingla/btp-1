## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Game Rules and Winning Strategy  
**Winning Strategy**: A plan that ensures you win no matter what your opponent does.  
**Turn-Based Game**: A game where players take turns performing actions.  

#### Game #1: Reach 21  
**Rules**:  
1. Player 1 says a number between 1 and 3.  
2. Players alternate adding 1, 2, or 3 to the previous number.  
3. The player who says **21** wins.  

**Winning Pattern**:  
To always win, aim to make the total increase by **4** each round. This means:  
- If your opponent adds $ x $, you add $ 4 - x $.  
- By doing this, you’ll eventually force your opponent to say **21**.  

> **Example**: If you start with 1, your opponent adds 2 (total = 3), you add 1 (total = 4). Repeat this until the total reaches 21.  

#### Game #2: Reach 99  
**Rules**:  
1. Player 1 says a number between 1 and 10.  
2. Players alternate adding 1–10 to the previous number.  
3. The player who says **99** wins.  

**Winning Pattern**:  
Here, the goal is to control the total increase so that you’re the one to say 99. The key is to **force your opponent into positions where they can’t avoid losing**.  

> **Strategy Tip**: If you can make the total increase by **11** each round, you’ll always win. For instance, if your opponent adds $ y $, you add $ 11 - y $.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Game #1 – Winning Move  
If you are the first player, what number should you say to guarantee a win?  

**Solution**:  
To force the opponent into a losing position, start by saying **1**. This creates a cycle where you control the total increase by 4 each round.  

### Example 2: Game #2 – Target 99  
Suppose the first player says 10. What should the second player add to maintain the winning strategy?  

**Solution**:  
To control the total increase by 11, the second player should add $ 11 - 10 = 1 $. This keeps the total increasing by 11 each round.  

### Example 3: Custom Game Rule  
Design a game where players add 1–5 to reach 25. What is the winning strategy?  

**Solution**:  
The target is 25, and the maximum allowed addition is 5. To control the total increase, aim for a cycle of **6** (since $ 5 + 1 = 6 $). If your opponent adds $ x $, you add $ 6 - x $. This forces the opponent to say 25.

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**:  
1. In Game #1, if you start with 2, what number should your opponent add to disrupt your strategy?  
2. If the target in Game #2 is changed to 55, what is the new winning increment?  

🟡 **Medium**:  
3. Design a custom game where players add 1–4 to reach 20. What is the winning strategy?  
4. If you are the second player in Game #1 and the first player says 2, what number should you add to take control?  

🔴 **Hard**:  
5. Suppose in Game #2, the first player says 7. What number should you add to ensure you win?  

**Answers**:  
1. Opponent should add 1 to disrupt the 4-cycle.  
2. New winning increment = $ 10 $ (since $ 5 + 5 = 10 $).  
3. Win by controlling the total increase by 5 (e.g., if opponent adds $ x $, add $ 5 - x $).  
4. Add 2 to make the total increase by 4 (start with 2 → opponent adds 1 → you add 2).  
5. Add $ 11 - 7 = 4 $ to maintain the 11-cycle.

## 6. Summary  
- **Game #1 (Reach 21)**: Control the total increase by 4 to force a win.  
- **Game #2 (Reach 99)**: Control the total increase by 11 to dominate.  
- **Custom Games**: Adjust the winning increment based on the target and allowed range.  
- Always analyze the **modular relationship** between the target and allowed moves.

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Winning Increment} = \text{Allowed Max} + 1 $ | For games where players add 1–N to reach a target. |  
| $ \text{Total Increase per Round} = \text{Target} / \text{Increment Cycles} $ | Determines the strategic cycle. |  
| $ \text{Opponent Move} + \text{Your Move} = \text{Winning Increment} $ | Ensures controlled progression. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes

- **Mistake 2**: Not accounting for the **modular relationship** between the target and allowed moves.
