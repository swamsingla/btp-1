### Introduction

Have you ever tried to measure the length of something using a ruler, but ended up getting confused because you didn't know how to hold it correctly? Or perhaps you've wondered why some measuring devices are made of flexible materials? In this topic, we'll dive into the correct way of measuring lengths and explore why certain methods work better than others.

By the end of this topic, you'll understand how to properly use a ruler, identify common mistakes, and apply the correct techniques to measure lengths accurately.

### Explanation

#### Key Terms:

*   **Scale**: A device used to measure lengths
*   **Unit of measurement**: The standard unit of length used to express a measurement (e.g., meter, centimeter, millimeter)

> Theorem: To ensure accurate measurements, always place the scale in contact with the object along its length and have your eye level with the zero mark of the scale.

#### Important Formulas:

$$

\text{Length} = \text{Reading at one end} - \text{Reading at the other end}

$$

When using a scale with a broken end, find another full mark and subtract its reading from the reading at the other end.

#### Visual Description:

Imagine holding a ruler horizontally and ensuring your eye is aligned with the zero mark. This ensures accuracy and prevents errors.

#### Step-by-Step Guide:

1.  Place the scale in contact with the object along its length.
2.  Position your eye level with the zero mark on the scale.
3.  Read the marks at both ends of the scale.
4.  Subtract the readings to find the length of the object.

#### Example 1:

Suppose you're trying to measure the length of a pencil using a 30-cm scale. You read 10 cm at one end and 20 cm at the other end.

Solution:

Length of pencil = Reading at one end - Reading at the other end
= 10 cm - 20 cm
= -10 cm (or -20 + 10 = 10 cm)

The negative sign indicates the direction of measurement.

#### Example 2:

Using a 50-metre scale to measure the height of a building:

Solution:

Height of building = Reading at top - Reading at bottom
= 40 metres - 10 metres
= 30 metres

#### Example 3:

Measuring the circumference of a circle using a flexible measuring tape:

Solution:

Circumference = π × Diameter
= 22/7 × 14 cm
≈ 44 cm

#### Practice Problems

1.  Measure the length of a pencil using a 10-cm scale and a ruler. (🟢 Easy)
2.  Find the length of a 20-meter scale with a broken end using the method described in the theory. (🟡 Medium)
3.  Calculate the area of a rectangle with a length of 12 meters and a width of 8 meters. (🔴 Hard)

#### Answers

1.  10 cm
2.  19.2 meters
3.  Area = Length × Width
= 12 m × 8 m
= 96 square meters
