## **Correct Way of Measuring Length**

### Introduction

Have you ever tried to measure the length of something without knowing how to do it correctly? Maybe you've had to estimate the length of an object by stretching out a piece of string or using a ruler. But did you know there's a right way to measure length?

In this section, we'll learn about the correct way to measure length using a ruler or scale. We'll cover the basics of measurement, including how to place the scale, read the marks, and deal with broken or missing marks.

**What will you learn?**

* The correct way to measure length using a ruler or scale
* How to identify and interpret measurement units
* Strategies for dealing with broken or missing marks on the scale
* Tips for accurate measurement

This topic builds upon what you learned in previous chapters on measurement and motion, so let's dive in!

### Explanation

#### Key Concepts

To measure length accurately, we need to understand a few key concepts:

* **Ruler or Scale**: A tool used to measure length.
* **Unit of Measurement**: The standard unit of length, such as meter (m), centimeter (cm), or millimeter (mm).
* **Zero Point**: The starting point on the scale where the measurement begins.

#### Theorem: The Zero Point

> When measuring length, the zero point on the scale is the reference point for all subsequent measurements.

When taking a measurement, ensure the zero point is placed on the object being measured. This ensures accuracy and consistency.

#### Placing the Scale

To place the scale correctly:

1. Align the scale with the object being measured.
2. Make sure the zero point is on the object.
3. Read the measurement from the scale, ensuring the zero point remains aligned.

#### Reading the Scale

When reading the scale, always:

* Place your eye directly above the tip of the object being measured.
* Take note of the unit of measurement (e.g., meters, centimeters, millimeters).
* Record the measurement as a number followed by the unit.

#### Dealing with Broken or Missing Marks

If the ends of the scale are broken or the zero point is unclear, don't worry! Here are some strategies:

* Use a full mark on the scale instead of the broken or unclear mark.
* Subtract the reading of the broken or unclear mark from the reading of the full mark.

### Solved Examples

**Example 1:** Measure the length of a pencil using a 1 cm scale.

1. Place the scale on the pencil, ensuring the zero point is on the pencil.
2. Read the measurement from the scale.

The scale shows 7.2 cm. Write the measurement as 7.2 cm.

**Example 2:** Measure the length of a bookshelf using a 10 cm scale.

1. Place the scale on the top edge of the bookshelf, ensuring the zero point is on the shelf.
2. Read the measurement from the scale.

The scale shows 25.5 cm. Write the measurement as 25.5 cm.

**Example 3:** Measure the length of a toy car using a 0.5 cm scale.

1. Place the scale on the base of the toy car, ensuring the zero point is on the car.
2. Read the measurement from the scale.

The scale shows 12.2 cm. Write the measurement as 12.2 cm.
