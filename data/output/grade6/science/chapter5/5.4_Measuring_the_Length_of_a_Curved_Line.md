## Measuring the Length of a Curved Line

### Introduction

Anish and his parents want to hang string lights on the arches of their house for a celebration. But, the arches are curved, making it difficult to measure the exact length of string needed. In this topic, you'll learn how to measure the length of a curved line using different methods.

### Explanation

A curved line is a line that bends and curves in a non-straight path. When measuring the length of a curved line, we need to consider the shape and size of the curve. There are two common ways to measure the length of a curved line:

*   Using a flexible measuring tape or a thread, as shown in Fig. 5.8.
*   By dividing the curve into smaller straight segments and adding up their lengths.

To calculate the total length, we can use the formula:

$$

L = L_1 + L_2 +... + L_n

$$

where $L_i $ is the length of each segment.

For example, let's say we have a curve that can be divided into three equal segments, each of length $ 10 $ units. The total length of the curve would be:

$$

L = 10 + 10 + 10 = 30

$$

Alternatively, we can also use the Pythagorean theorem to find the length of a curved line. For instance, if we know the radius of a circle and the angle subtended by the arc, we can calculate the length using the formula:

$$

L = r\theta

$$

where $r $ is the radius and $\theta $ is the angle in radians.

### Key Terms

*   **Length**: the distance between two points along a curve.
*   **Segment**: a part of a curve that can be measured separately.
*   **Radius**: the distance from the center of a circle to its edge.

### Solved Example

A gardener wants to water a circular garden with a radius of $ 5 $ meters. She has a hose that can cover a length of $ 20 $ meters. How long should she stretch her hose to reach the entire garden?

Solution:

The circumference of the circular garden is given by:

$$

C = 2\pi r = 2 \times \pi \times 5 = 10\pi

$$

Since the hose can cover a length of $20 $ meters, the gardener needs to stretch it twice to cover the entire garden:

$$

L = C \times 2 = 10\pi \times 2 = 20\pi

$$

So, the gardener should stretch her hose by $20\pi $ meters.
