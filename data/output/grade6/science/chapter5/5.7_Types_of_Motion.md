## Types of Motion
### Importance Score: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Do objects always move along a straight line? No! But what about circular motion, oscillatory motion, or even motion that moves back and forth? In this section, we'll explore the different types of motion that occur in our everyday lives.

### Introduction

Have you ever watched the Republic Day parade and noticed how the students marched in a straight line? Or seen an eraser being dropped from a certain height and observed its motion? But did you think about whether its motion was linear or something else?

In this section, we'll delve into the three main types of motion: linear, circular, and oscillatory motion. We'll discover how these motions differ and when they occur.

### Explanation

#### What is Motion?

Motion refers to the change in position of an object over time. There are several types of motion, but we'll focus on three primary ones:

*   **Linear Motion**: An object moves in a straight line, either towards or away from a fixed point.
*   **Circular Motion**: An object moves along a circular path, returning to its starting point.
*   **Oscillatory Motion**: An object moves back and forth about a fixed point, often with a repeated pattern.

#### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Linear Motion** | An object moves in a straight line. |
| **Circular Motion** | An object moves along a circular path. |
| **Oscillatory Motion** | An object moves back and forth about a fixed point. |

#### Theorem: Periodicity

An object in circular or oscillatory motion repeats its path after a fixed interval of time. This means that the object returns to its original position and starts moving in the opposite direction.

#### Formula: Distance traveled

The distance traveled by an object can be calculated using the formula:

d = v × t

where d is the distance, v is the velocity, and t is the time.

#### Property: Uniform Velocity

An object moving with uniform velocity maintains a constant speed and direction.

#### Example 1: Linear Motion

A car travels from city A to city B at a constant speed of 60 km/h. If the distance between the two cities is 240 km, how long will it take to cover this distance?

**Solution:**

Let's denote the time taken to cover the distance as 't'. Using the formula d = v × t, we get:

240 km = 60 km/h × t

Dividing both sides by 60 km/h gives us:

t = 4 hours

Therefore, it will take the car 4 hours to cover the distance between city A and city B.

#### Example 2: Circular Motion

A merry-go-round rotates at a constant angular velocity of 2 revolutions per minute. If the circumference of the merry-go-round is 10 meters, what is its linear velocity?

**Solution:**

First, we need to find the linear velocity (v) using the formula v = ωr, where ω is the angular velocity (2 revolutions/minute) and r is the radius (half of the circumference):

v = 2 rev/min × π × 5 m

v ≈ 31.4 m/s

So, the linear velocity of the merry-go-round is approximately 31.4 meters per second.

#### Example 3: Oscillatory Motion

A pendulum oscillates at a frequency of 2 Hz. If the amplitude of the pendulum is 20 cm, what is the period of the oscillation?

**Solution:**

Using the formula T = 1 / f, where T is the period and f is the frequency:

T = 1 / 2 Hz

T = 0.5 seconds

Therefore, the period of the pendulum is 0.5 seconds.

### Solved Examples

**Example 1:** A child throws a ball upwards with an initial velocity of 20 m/s. If air resistance is negligible, how far will the ball travel before hitting the ground?

**Solution:**

Since air resistance is negligible, we can assume the ball's acceleration due to gravity is 9.8 m/s². Using the equation s = ut + ½at², where s is the displacement, u is the initial velocity, t is the time, and a is the acceleration, we can calculate the distance traveled:

s = 20 m/s × 0 s + ½ × 9.8 m/s² × t²

As the ball hits the ground, the final velocity is 0 m/s. Using the equation v = u + at, we can find the time taken:

0 m/s = 20 m/s + (-9.8 m/s²) × t

t ≈ 2.04 s

Substituting this value of t into the equation for s, we get:

s ≈ 40.08 m

So, the ball will travel approximately 40.08 meters before hitting the ground.

**Example 2:** A car accelerates from rest to a top speed of 30 m/s in 10 seconds. What is its average velocity during this time?

**Solution:**

Using the equation v = u + at, where v is the final velocity, u is the initial velocity (0 m/s), a is the acceleration, and t is the time, we can find the average velocity:

v = 0 m/s + (30 m/s / 10 s) = 3 m/s

So, the average velocity of the car during this time is 3 m/s.

**Example 3:** A person walks in a circular path with a radius of 20 meters. If they complete 3 laps in 10 minutes, what is their average linear velocity?

**Solution:**

First, we need to find the total distance traveled:

Total distance = 3 laps × 2πr = 3 × 2 × 3.14 × 20 m = 376.8 m

Next, we can find the time taken:

Time = Total distance / Speed

We know the speed is constant, so we can use the formula v = (d / t) to find the speed:

Speed = 376.8 m / 600 s ≈ 0.628 m/s

Now, we can find the average velocity:

Average velocity = Speed × Time

Average velocity = 0.628 m/s × 10 min = 6.28 m/s

So, the person's average linear velocity is approximately 6.28 meters per second.

### Summary

*   Circular motion occurs when an object moves along a circular path.
*   Oscillatory motion occurs when an object moves back and forth about a fixed point.
