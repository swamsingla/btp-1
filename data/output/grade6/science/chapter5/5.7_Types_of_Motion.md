### Types of Motion

#### Introduction

Have you ever watched a merry-go-round spinning round and round, or seen a ball bouncing back and forth? What type of motion did these objects exhibit? In this topic, we'll explore three fascinating types of motion: Linear, Circular, and Oscillatory motion. We'll discover how they differ and provide examples to help you understand each better.

#### Explanation

##### Definition of Motion

Motion is the change in position of an object over time. There are several types of motion, which can be classified based on their paths.

*   **Linear Motion**: An object moves along a straight line.
*   **Circular Motion**: An object moves along a circular path.
*   **Oscillatory Motion**: An object moves to and fro about a fixed position.

These types of motion are characterized by different properties.

*   **Periodicity**: An object repeats its path after a fixed interval of time.
*   **Perpendicularity**: The direction of motion is perpendicular to the direction of force applied.

The following theorem explains periodic motion:

> A body executing periodic motion has its velocity and acceleration constantly changing in magnitude and direction.

- **Key Terms**

- **Linear Motion**: The motion of an object along a straight line.
- **Circular Motion**: The motion of an object along a circular path.
- **Oscillatory Motion**: The motion of an object to and fro about a fixed position.

- **Formulae**

>

$$

v=\frac{\text{distance traveled}}{\text{time taken}}

$$

>

$$

a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}

$$

#### Solved Examples

1.  A boy throws a stone upwards with an initial velocity of 10 m/s. Assuming negligible air resistance, find the maximum height reached by the stone.
    1.  Draw a diagram to represent the situation.
    2.  Use the equation of motion to find the time taken for the stone to reach its maximum height.
    3.  Find the maximum height reached by the stone.

$$
    s=v_{0}t+\frac{1}{2}at^{2}

$$

    Given: $v_{0}=10 m/s, $ $ a=-10 m/s^{2}, $

    Substituting values in the above equation,

$$
    s=10t-\frac{1}{2}\times 10t^{2}\\
    =10t-5t^{2}

$$


    Now, for maximum height,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{ds}{dt}&=0\\
    10-10t&=0\\
    t&=1\sec
    \end{aligned}

$$


    Putting $t=1 $ in the equation of motion, we get

$$
    s=10\times 1-5(1)^{2}\\
    =10-5\\
    =5m

$$

2.  A car travels from station A to B at a uniform speed of 40 km/h. After reaching the midpoint, the driver accelerates uniformly to 60 km/h. Find the distance between A and B.
    1.  First, calculate the distance covered in the first half of the journey at 40 km/h.
    2.  Next, find the time taken to cover the second half of the journey at 60 km/h.
    3.  Finally, add the distances covered in both halves of the journey.

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=v_{0}t+\frac{1}{2}at^{2}\\
    &=v_{0}t+\frac{1}{2}(a-v_{0})t^{2}\\
    &=\left(v_{0}+\frac{a-v_{0}}{2}\right)t\\
    &=\left(\frac{(v_{0}+a)}{2}\right)t
    \end{aligned}

$$


    Given: $v_{0}=40 km/h, $ $ a=20 km/h, $ $ d=?$

 

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=\left(\frac{(40+20)}{2}\right)\times t\\
    &=30\times t
    \end{aligned}

$$


    For first half of the journey,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=v_{0}t\\
    &=40t
    \end{aligned}

$$


    And for second half of the journey,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=\frac{1}{2}at^{2}\\
    &=\frac{1}{2}\times 20t^{2}\\
    &=10t^{2}
    \end{aligned}

$$


    Adding the distances covered in both halves of the journey,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=40t+10t^{2}\\
    &=10(4+t)\\
    &=10(4+1)\\
    &=50km
    \end{aligned}

$$

3.  A train traveling from station A to station B takes 12 hours to cover a distance of 180 km. If it maintains a uniform speed, find the distance between A and C, given that the train travels at a uniform speed of 15 km/h for the first 3 hours and then increases its speed to 25 km/h for the remaining 9 hours.

    1.  First, calculate the total distance covered during the entire journey.
    2.  Next, find the distance covered in the first 3 hours.
    3.  Then, find the distance covered in the next 9 hours.
    4.  Finally, subtract the distance covered in the first 3 hours from the total distance to find the distance between A and C.

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=v_{0}t\\
    &=15\times 3\\
    &=45km
    \end{aligned}

$$


    And,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=v_{0}t\\
    &=25\times 9\\
    &=225km
    \end{aligned}

$$


    Total distance covered,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=45+225\\
    &=270km
    \end{aligned}

$$


    Distance between A and C,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    d&=d_{AB}-d_{AC}\\
    &=180-45\\
    &=135km
    \end{aligned}

$$


#### Practice Problems

1.  A child rides a bicycle from home to school, covering a distance of 12 km in 36 minutes. The distance between home and school is increasing at a rate of 100 meters per minute. Find the speed of the child. [Easy]

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{speed}&=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}\\
    &=\frac{12\text{ km}}{0.6\text{ hour}}\\
    &=20\text{ m/s}
    \end{aligned}

$$

2.  A car covers a distance of 120 km in 24 hours. At what average speed did it travel? [Medium]

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{average speed}&=\frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}\\
    &=\frac{120\text{ km}}{24\text{ h}}\\
    &=5\text{ km/h}
    \end{aligned}

$$


3.  A boy throws a ball upwards with an initial velocity of 15 m/s. Neglecting air resistance, find the time taken by the ball to reach its maximum height. [Hard]

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    v&=v_{0}-gt\\
    &=0-10t\\
    &=0-10t\\
    &=-10t
    \end{aligned}

$$


    Since final velocity is zero,

$$
    \begin{aligned}
    0&=-10t\\
    t&=0
    \end{aligned}

$$


    However, since the ball reaches the topmost point, it will not stop immediately. So, the actual time taken to reach the maximum height is half of this value.

$$
    t=\frac{1}{2}\times 0\\
    =0\text{ s}

$$


#### Summary

*   **Linear Motion**: The motion of an object along a straight line.
*   **Circular Motion**: The motion of an object along a circular path.
*   **Oscillatory Motion**: The motion of an object to and fro about a fixed position.
*   To find the distance traveled by an object under constant acceleration, use the equation:

$$

s=v_{0}t+\frac{1}{2}at^{2}

$$
*   To find the distance covered by an object under uniform acceleration, use the equation:

$$

d=\frac{1}{2}(v_{0}+a)t

$$

*   Check your signs: Ensure that the signs of velocities, accelerations, and distances are correct.
