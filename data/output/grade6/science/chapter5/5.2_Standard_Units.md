## Standard Units
### Introduction
Do you know how much longer a cricket pitch is compared to a hockey field? Have you ever wondered why we need standard units to compare measurements across different locations?

In this topic, we'll delve into the world of standard units, exploring the International System of Units (SI units) and their importance in measuring length. We'll start by understanding the concept of standard units, their significance, and how they help us compare measurements accurately.

### Explanation
#### What are Standard Units?
Standard units are a set of units used consistently across a region or country to measure various quantities such as length, mass, temperature, and others. The SI system, also known as the International System of Units, is widely adopted globally.

> **Definition of Standard Unit**: A standard unit is a unit of measurement that serves as a reference point for other units.

#### Importance of Standard Units
Standard units enable us to compare measurements across different locations, making it easier to communicate and trade goods. For instance, when buying apples from different farms, knowing that 1 meter is equivalent to 100 centimeters helps ensure consistency in pricing and transactions.

#### Key Terms
* **Metre (m)**: The base unit of length in the SI system.
* **Centimetre (cm)**: A subdivision of the metre, equal to 1/100th of a metre.
* **Millimetre (mm)**: A subdivision of the centimetre, equal to 1/10th of a centimetre.
* **Kilometre (km)**: A large unit of length, equal to 1000 metres.

#### Converting Between Units
To convert between units, we can use the following conversion factors:
* To convert metres to centimetres: multiply by 100
* To convert centimetres to millimetres: multiply by 10
* To convert kilometres to metres: multiply by 1000

#### Why Use Standard Units?
Using standard units simplifies calculations and comparisons, reducing errors and misunderstandings. By adopting a consistent system, we can easily perform calculations and share knowledge across different regions.

#### Visual Description
Imagine a ruler with markings for metres, centimetres, and millimetres. Each mark represents a specific unit of length, allowing us to measure objects accurately.

### Solved Examples
#### Example 1:
A basketball court measures 94 meters in length. Convert this to centimetres.

**Solution:**
First, convert metres to centimetres:
1 metre = 100 centimetres
Multiply the length of the basketball court by 100:
94 m × 100 = 9400 cm

#### Example 2:
A bookshelf is 180 centimetres tall. Convert this to millimetres.

**Solution:**
First, convert centimetres to millimetres:
1 centimetre = 10 millimetres
Multiply the height of the bookshelf by 10:
180 cm × 10 = 1800 mm

#### Example 3:
A car travels 450 kilometres to reach its destination. Convert this to metres.

**Solution:**
Multiply the distance travelled by 1000 (since 1 kilometer = 1000 metres):
450 km × 1000 = 450,000 m

#### Example 4:
A garden measures 75 metres in width. Convert this to centimetres.

**Solution:**
Multiply the width of the garden by 100:
75 m × 100 = 7500 cm

#### Did You Know?
The word "metre" comes from the Greek word "metron," meaning "barleycorn." Barleycorn was an ancient unit of length used to measure the length of barley grains!
