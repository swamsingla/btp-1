## Measurement of Length and Motion (5.1 How do we Measure?)
### Importance Score: 4/5

**Introduction**

Have you ever wondered how people measure the length of things, like tables or rooms? Or perhaps you've used a ruler to measure the length of a pencil or a book? But did you know that different people can have different ways of measuring the same thing?

In this topic, we'll explore how we measure length and motion, and why we need standard units to make comparisons easier.

**Explanation**

When we measure the length of something, we need a common way to express the measurement. Otherwise, our measurements might seem different because of individual differences.

A **unit** is a standard quantity that serves as a basis for comparison. In the case of length, we often use units like meters, centimeters, or inches. But what if someone else has a different way of measuring the same length? We need a universal standard to ensure consistency.

One way to achieve this is by using **body parts**, like the length of a hand, foot, or finger. However, as Deepa's experience shows us, these methods can vary between individuals.

To overcome this issue, we use **standard units** like the meter or foot, which is a fixed length that everyone agrees upon. This allows us to compare measurements easily and accurately.

> Theorem: **Standard Unit Definition**
If two objects have the same length but are measured in different units, then their difference is zero.

Mathematically, this can be represented as:

$$

d = |l_1 - l_2| = 0

$$

where $d $ is the difference in length, $ l_1 $ is the length of object 1, and $ l_2 $ is the length of object 2.

**Properties of Length**

*   **Transitivity**: If $ l_1 < l_2 $ and $ l_2 < l_3 $, then $ l_1 < l_3 $
*   **Additivity**: If $ l_1 + l_2 = l_3 $, then $ l_1 + l_2 $ is equal to $ l_3 $

### Solved Examples

#### Example 1:
Tom and Rohan both want to measure the length of a room using a tape measure. Tom uses a 10-meter tape measure, while Rohan uses a 12-meter tape measure. Which tape measure is longer?

**Solution:**
We need to find the difference in the lengths measured by Tom and Rohan. Since 10 meters is shorter than 12 meters, the 10-meter tape measure is longer.

#### Example 2:
Suppose we want to measure the length of a room that is 20 meters long. If we divide the room into 4 sections, each section being 5 meters long, what is the total length of the room?

**Solution:**
We can add up the lengths of the four sections:

$$ ext{Total Length} = 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20

$$

#### Example 3:
A rectangular garden measures 15 meters in length and 8 meters in width. What is the perimeter of the garden?

**Solution:**
The perimeter of a rectangle is given by the formula:

$$

P = 2(l + w)

$$
where $l $ is the length and $ w $ is the width. Substituting the values, we get:

$$

P = 2(15 + 8) = 46

$$

= 240 km / 60 km/h
