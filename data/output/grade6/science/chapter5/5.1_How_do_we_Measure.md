## Explanation
--------------

When we talk about measuring length, we often think of using rulers or measuring tapes. However, as shown in the story of Deepa, Anish, Padma, Tasneem, and Hardeep, our bodies can also be used to measure length. This is known as a **body part method** of measurement.

For example, a common body part used to measure length is the **handspan**. But as we see in Table 5.1, the number of handspans can vary greatly from person to person. Therefore, it's difficult to use handspans as a standard unit of measurement.

To overcome this challenge, we use **standard units**, such as centimeters, meters, or inches. These units are based on a fixed length that doesn't change from person to person. For instance, 1 meter is always equal to 100 centimeters.

A **unit** is defined as a standard quantity that represents a particular amount. For example, the unit of length is a meter. The **SI unit** (Systeme Internationale) for length is the **meter**.

The **metric system** is a decimal-based system that uses powers of ten. It's widely used in science and everyday applications.

To ensure accurate measurements, we need to use the **correct way of measuring length**. This involves using a ruler or measuring tape that has marked increments of 1 cm, mm, or inch.

Now, let's consider the length of a curved line. We can't simply extend a straightedge to measure it, because the edge would intersect the curve. To solve this problem, we can use the **method of exhaustion**, which involves dividing the curved shape into smaller segments and summing up their lengths.

Another approach is to find the equation of the curve and integrate it to obtain the arc length. This is a fundamental concept in calculus and is essential for solving many problems involving curves and surfaces.

### Important Formulas

> **Theorem 1:** The length of a curve given by the equation y = f(x) over the interval [a, b] can be calculated using the definite integral ∫[a,b] √(1 + (∂f/∂x)^2) dx.

> **Formula for the arc length of a circle:** s = 2πr, where r is the radius of the circle.

> **Unit Conversion Formula:** 1 m = 100 cm = 10000 mm = 393.7018 inches

## Solved Examples
------------------

### Example 1:

Find the length of a rectangular garden that is 10 meters long and 5 meters wide.

#### Solution:

The area of a rectangle is given by length × width. Since the area of the garden is 50 square meters, we can set up the equation:

Area = length × width
50 = 10 × 5

To find the perimeter, we add up the lengths of all sides:

Perimeter = 2(l + w)
= 2(10 + 5)
= 30 meters

### Example 2:

A man is standing 15 meters away from a tree. His friend is standing 10 meters away from him. Find the distance between them.

#### Solution:

We can use the Pythagorean theorem to calculate the distance between the two men:

c^2 = a^2 + b^2
where c is the hypotenuse (distance between men), a is the distance between man 1 and the tree (15 meters), and b is the distance between man 2 and the tree (10 meters).

c^2 = 15^2 + 10^2
c^2 = 225 + 100
c^2 = 325

c = √325
c ≈ 18.03 meters

### Example 3:

A car travels 250 kilometers in 5 hours. Find its average speed.

#### Solution:

Average speed = total distance ÷ time taken
= 250 km ÷ 5 h
= 50 km/h

### Example 4:

A bakery sells 250 loaves of bread per day. Each loaf weighs approximately 0.25 kilograms. What is the total weight of the bread sold?

#### Solution:

Total weight = number of loaves × weight per loaf
= 250 loaves × 0.25 kg/loaf
= 62.5 kg

## Practice Problems
--------------------

🟢 Easy
What is the length of a bookshelf that is 2 meters long and 0.5 meters high?

🟡 Medium
A car travels 200 kilometers in 4 hours. Find its average speed.

🔴 Hard
A boat travels 300 meters downstream and 200 meters upstream. Find its average speed.

## Answers
-----------------

🟢 Easy
Length = 2 m × 0.5 m = 1 m

🟡 Medium
Speed = 200 km ÷ 4 h = 50 km/h

🔴 Hard
To solve this problem, we need to find the average speed of the boat. We can use the formula:

Average speed = (downstream speed + upstream speed) ÷ 2

Since the boat travels 300 meters downstream and 200 meters upstream, we can assume that the water's speed is negligible compared to the boat's speed.

Downstream speed = 300 km ÷ 4 h = 75 km/h
Upstream speed = 200 km ÷ 4 h = 50 km/h

Average speed = (75 + 50) ÷ 2
= 125 km/h

## Summary

### Common Mistakes

*   Using incorrect units (e.g., using feet instead of meters).
*   Failing to convert units correctly (e.g., converting kilometers to meters).
*   Not considering the direction of measurement (e.g., using the wrong sign for the opposite direction).
