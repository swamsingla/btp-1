## **Introduction**

Get ready to explore the world of measurement and motion! In this summary, we'll review the key concepts related to measuring lengths and understanding different types of motion. ## **Explanation**

### Measuring Lengths

Measuring lengths involves converting units and choosing the right tool for the job. We need to know the standard units of measurement, including centimeters, meters, kilometers, and millimeters. For example, 1 kilometer is equal to 1000 meters (

$$

1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ m}

$$ ) and 1 meter is equal to 100 centimeters (

$$

1 \text{ m} = 100 \text{ cm}

$$ ).

When measuring lengths, always choose the most suitable unit. For instance, a coin is best measured in millimeters (mm), while a basketball is better measured in meters (m).

### Types of Motion

There are several types of motion:

* Linear motion: motion in a straight line
* Circular motion: rotation around a central point
* Oscillatory motion: back-and-forth motion

To describe motion, we can use the concept of displacement, velocity, and acceleration. Displacement refers to the change in position, velocity is the rate of change of displacement, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

### Key Formulas

* Distance = Speed x Time (

$$

d = vt

$$ )
* Velocity = Change in Displacement / Time (

$$

v = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}

$$ )

## **Solved Examples**

1. Convert 150 meters to centimeters:

$$

150 \text{ m} = 150 \times 100 \text{ cm} = 15000 \text{ cm}

$$
2. Calculate the velocity of an object traveling 20 meters in 4 seconds:

$$

v = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t} = \frac{20 \text{ m}}{4 \text{ s}} = 5 \text{ m/s}

$$

To summarize, we've learned about measuring lengths, identifying types of motion, and understanding key formulas. Remember to choose the right unit for the job and apply the formulas correctly to solve problems.
