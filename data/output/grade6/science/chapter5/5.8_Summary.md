**Welcome to the world of measurements!**

Do you know how far away your school is from your home? Or how long is a cricket pitch? In this summary, we'll review the key concepts of measurement of length and motion. We'll also explore various ways to measure lengths, discuss different types of motion, and even come up with creative ideas for measuring distances!

### Explanation

#### Key Concepts

* **Standard Units**: We use specific units to measure lengths, such as meters, centimeters, and millimeters.
* **Measurement of Length**: We can measure lengths using various tools, including rulers, measuring tapes, and even our hands!
* **Types of Motion**: There are several types of motion, including linear, circular, and oscillatory motion.

> **Theorem:** An object is in motion if its position changes with respect to a reference point over time.

#### Formulas

* To convert kilometers to centimeters, multiply by 10000 (1 km = 1000 m = 100,000 cm).
* To calculate the perimeter of a rectangle, add twice the length and twice the width (P = 2l + 2w).

#### Example 1: Converting Kilometers to Centimeters

Suppose you want to convert 1.5 kilometers to centimeters.

1. Convert kilometers to meters: 1.5 km × 1000 m/km = 1500 m
2. Convert meters to centimeters: 1500 m × 100 cm/m = 150,000 cm

So, 1.5 kilometers is equal to 150,000 centimeters!

### Solved Examples

**Example 1:** Measure the length of a pencil using a ruler.

1. Place the ruler on top of the pencil, ensuring the zero mark aligns with the pencil's end.
2. Read the length on the ruler.

Answer: Let's say the pencil measures 15 cm.

**Example 2:** Calculate the perimeter of a square with sides of 5 cm.

1. Write down the formula: P = 2(l + w)
2. Substitute values: P = 2(5 + 5)
3. Simplify: P = 20 cm

So, the perimeter is 20 cm!
