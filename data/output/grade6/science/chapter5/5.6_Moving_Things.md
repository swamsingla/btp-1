## 5.6 Moving Things
### Introduction
Have you ever seen a car moving down the road, or a ball bouncing across the playground? What makes them move? In this topic, we'll explore what it means for an object to be in motion, and how we can measure the speed and distance of moving objects.

We've learned about measuring length in previous topics, but now we're going to delve into the world of motion! You might recall that an object's position changes over time if it's moving. But what exactly does that mean?

As we continue our journey through the world of measurement, you'll discover that motion is a fundamental concept that affects many areas of our lives. So, buckle up and get ready to learn!

### Explanation
#### Definition of Motion
An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to the reference point with time. This means that if an object is moving, its location changes as it moves. On the other hand, if an object is not changing its position with respect to the reference point with time, it is said to be at rest.

#### Key Terms
*   **Velocity**: The rate of change of an object's position with respect to time. Mathematically, velocity is represented by the symbol v.
*   **Speed**: The magnitude of velocity, or how fast an object is moving. Speed is always positive, while direction can be either positive or negative.
*   **Displacement**: The shortest distance between an object's initial and final positions. Displacement is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction.

#### Theorem: Average Velocity
The average velocity of an object is calculated by dividing the displacement by the time taken.

$$

v_{avg} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}

$$

where \(v_{avg}\) is the average velocity, \(\Delta x\) is the displacement, and \(\Delta t\) is the time taken.

#### Formula: Speed
The speed of an object is calculated using the formula:

$v = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} $

or

$ v = \frac{x^2}{t} $

where $ v $ is the speed, $ x $ is the distance traveled, and $ t $ is the time taken.

#### Properties of Motion
*   **Constant Velocity**: An object with constant velocity maintains its speed and direction throughout its motion.
*   **Uniform Motion**: An object with uniform motion travels at a constant speed in a straight line.

#### Example 1:
A car travels from city A to city B at a constant speed of 60 km/h. After 2 hours, it covers a distance of 120 km. Find the average velocity of the car.

#### Solution:
To find the average velocity, we divide the displacement (120 km) by the time taken (2 hours):

$$

v_{avg} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{120 \text{ km}}{2 \text{ h}} = 60 \text{ km/h}

$$

Therefore, the average velocity of the car is 60 km/h.

#### Example 2:
A bat and a ball are moving towards each other at speeds of 20 m/s and 30 m/s respectively. If they meet after 10 seconds, what is their relative velocity?

#### Solution:
First, calculate the distance covered by each object:

Distance covered by bat = speed × time
= 20 m/s × 10 s
= 200 m

Distance covered by ball = speed × time
= 30 m/s × 10 s
= 300 m

Since they are moving towards each other, add their distances together to get the total displacement:

Total displacement = 200 m + 300 m = 500 m

Now, calculate the relative velocity by dividing the total displacement by the time taken:

Relative velocity = $\frac{\text{total displacement}}{\text{time}} $
= $\frac{500 \text{ m}}{10 \text{ s}} $
= 50 m/s

### Solved Examples

#### Example 3:
A bike travels from home to school at a speed of 25 m/s. If it takes 15 minutes to cover the distance, what is the time taken in hours?

#### Solution:
First, convert the time from minutes to hours:
Time in hours = $\frac{15 \text{ min}}{60} $
= $\frac{1}{4} $ hour

Now, use the formula to find the distance:
Distance = speed × time
= 25 m/s × $\frac{1}{4} $ hour
= 6.25 m

Alternatively, use the formula to find the time:
Time = distance ÷ speed
= 6.25 m ÷ 25 m/s
= $\frac{1}{4} $ hour

#### Example 4:
A train travels from station A to station B at a speed of 80 km/h. If it takes 3 hours to cover the distance, what is the distance traveled?

#### Solution:
Use the formula to find the distance:
Distance = speed × time
= 80 km/h × 3 h
= 240 km

### Summary
*   **Key Formulas:**
    *

$$

v_{avg} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}

$$
    *

$$

v = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}

$$
    *

$$

v = \frac{x^2}{t}

$$
*   **Important Concepts:**
    *   Constant velocity
    *   Uniform motion
    *   Relative velocity
*   **Tips and Tricks:**
    *   When calculating average velocity, remember to divide the displacement by the time taken.
    *   When calculating relative velocity, add the velocities of the two objects and divide by the sum of their times.
