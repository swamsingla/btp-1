## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Landmark Numbers and Base  
> **Landmark numbers** are the building blocks of a number system. They are created by repeatedly grouping previous numbers in a fixed ratio.  

In the **Egyptian system**, the first landmark number is **1**. To create the next landmark number:  
- Group **10** collections of **1** → **10** becomes the second landmark number.  
- Group **10** collections of **10** → **100** becomes the third landmark number.  
- Continue this pattern: **10 × 10 = 1000**, **10 × 100 = 10,000**, and so on.  

Each landmark number is **10 times** the previous one. This means the **base** of the Egyptian system is **10**.  

> **Base** is the number of unique digits used in a number system. For example, base-10 uses digits 0–9, and base-5 uses digits 0–4.  

### 3.2 Creating a New Number System (Base-5)  
The source text explores what happens if we replace **10** with **5** in the grouping process:  
- Start with **1** as the first landmark number.  
- Group **5** collections of **1** → **5** becomes the second landmark number.  
- Group **5** collections of **5** → **25** becomes the third landmark number.  
- Continue: **5 × 25 = 125**, **5 × 125 = 625**, and so on.  

Now, the landmark numbers are **powers of 5**:

$$
5^0 = 1,\quad 5^1 = 5,\quad 5^2 = 25,\quad 5^3 = 125,\quad 5^4 = 625,\quad 5^5 = 3125

$$  
This creates a **base-5** system, where each digit represents a power of **5**.  

### 3.3 Converting Numbers in Different Bases  
To convert a number into a base-5 system, follow these steps:  
1. Identify the largest landmark number less than or equal to the target number.  
2. Divide the number by the landmark number to find how many full groups fit.  
3. Repeat the process with the remainder until all digits are accounted for.  

For example, let’s convert **143** into base-5:  
- Largest landmark number ≤ 143 is **125** (which is $5^3 $).  
- $ 143 ÷ 125 = 1 $ (with remainder **18**).  
- Next landmark number is **25** ($ 5^2 $): $ 18 ÷ 25 = 0 $ (remainder **18**).  
- Next is **5** ($ 5^1 $): $ 18 ÷ 5 = 3 $ (remainder **3**).  
- Final landmark number is **1** ($ 5^0 $): $ 3 ÷ 1 = 3 $.  

So, **143** in base-5 is **1 0 3 3** (written as $ 1033_5 $).

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Representing 10458 in the Egyptian System  
The Egyptian system uses **base-10** and **landmark numbers** (1, 10, 100, 1000, etc.).  
Breakdown of 10458:  
- $ 10,000 = 10^4 $ → **1 symbol**  
- $ 400 = 4 \times 100 $ → **4 symbols**  
- $ 50 = 5 \times 10 $ → **5 symbols**  
- $ 8 = 8 \times 1 $ → **8 symbols**  

Total symbols: **1 (10,000) + 4 (100) + 5 (10) + 8 (1)**.  

**Solution:**

$$
10458 = 1 \times 10^4 + 4 \times 10^2 + 5 \times 10^1 + 8 \times 10^0

$$  
In the Egyptian system, this would be represented as:

$$
\underbrace{\text{Symbol for }10^4}_{1}\ \underbrace{\text{Symbols for }10^2}_{4}\ \underbrace{\text{Symbols for }10^1}_{5}\ \underbrace{\text{Symbols for }10^0}_{8}

$$  

### Example 2: Converting 143 to Base-5  
As shown in Section 3.3, **143** in base-5 is **1033**.  

**Verification:**

$$
1 \times 5^3 + 0 \times 5^2 + 3 \times 5^1 + 3 \times 5^0 = 125 + 0 + 15 + 3 = 143

$$  
✅ Correct!  

### Example 3: Converting 784 to Egyptian Symbols  
Breakdown of 784:  
- $700 = 7 \times 100 $ → **7 symbols**  
- $ 80 = 8 \times 10 $ → **8 symbols**  
- $ 4 = 4 \times 1 $ → **4 symbols**  

**Solution:**

$$
784 = 7 \times 10^2 + 8 \times 10^1 + 4 \times 10^0

$$  
Representation: **7 (100) + 8 (10) + 4 (1)**.  

### Example 4: Writing 2660 in Egyptian Symbols  
Breakdown of 2660:  
- $2000 = 2 \times 1000 $ → **2 symbols**  
- $ 600 = 6 \times 100 $ → **6 symbols**  
- $ 60 = 6 \times 10 $ → **6 symbols**  

**Solution:**

$$
2660 = 2 \times 10^3 + 6 \times 10^2 + 6 \times 10^1

$$  
Representation: **2 (1000) + 6 (100) + 6 (10)**.  

### Example 5: Converting 70707 to Egyptian Symbols  
Breakdown of 70707:  
- $70,000 = 7 \times 10,000 $ → **7 symbols**  
- $ 700 = 7 \times 100 $ → **7 symbols**  
- $ 7 = 7 \times 1 $ → **7 symbols**  

**Solution:**

$$
70707 = 7 \times 10^4 + 7 \times 10^2 + 7 \times 10^0

$$  
Representation: **7 (10,000) + 7 (100) + 7 (1)**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy: Convert 1111 to Egyptian Symbols  
**Step-by-step:**  
1. Break 1111 into landmark numbers (1, 10, 100, 1000).  
2. Count how many of each landmark number are needed.  

**Answer:**

$$
1111 = 1 \times 10^3 + 1 \times 10^2 + 1 \times 10^1 + 1 \times 10^0

$$  
Representation: **1 (1000) + 1 (100) + 1 (10) + 1 (1)**.  

### 🟡 Medium: Convert 2660 to Base-5  
**Steps:**  
1. Find the largest landmark number ≤ 2660.  
2. Divide and repeat.  

**Answer:**

$$
2660 ÷ 125 = 21\ (\text{remainder } 11)\  
21 ÷ 25 = 0\ (\text{remainder } 21)\  
21 ÷ 5 = 4\ (\text{remainder } 1)\  
1 ÷ 1 = 1

$$  
Final representation: **4 1 0 1** (i.e., $4101_5 $).  

### 🔴 Hard: Convert 70707 to Base-5  
**Steps:**  
1. Use landmark numbers $ 5^0 = 1 $, $ 5^1 = 5 $, ..., up to $ 5^6 = 15625 $.  
2. Perform repeated division.  

**Answer:**

$$
70707 ÷ 15625 = 4\ (\text{remainder } 70707 - 62500 = 8207)\  
8207 ÷ 3125 = 2\ (\text{remainder } 8207 - 6250 = 1957)\  
1957 ÷ 625 = 3\ (\text{remainder } 1957 - 1875 = 82)\  
82 ÷ 125 = 0\ (\text{remainder } 82)\  
82 ÷ 25 = 3\ (\text{remainder } 7)\  
7 ÷ 5 = 1\ (\text{remainder } 2)\  
2 ÷ 1 = 2

$$  
Final representation: **4 2 3 0 3 1 2** (i.e., $4230312_5 $).

## 6. Summary  
- **Base** determines how numbers are grouped (e.g., base-10 vs. base-5).  
- **Landmark numbers** are powers of the base (e.g., $ 10^0, 10^1, ...$ for base-10).  
- The **Egyptian system** uses base-10, while the **base-5 system** groups numbers in fives.  
- To convert a number to a new base: divide by the largest landmark number and record remainders.  
- In base-5, digits range from **0 to 4**, unlike base-10 (digits 0–9).  
- Understanding bases helps explain why numbers are written in different ways across cultures.

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ 10^n $ | Landmark number in base-10 (e.g., $ 10^3 = 1000 $) |  
| $ 5^n $ | Landmark number in base-5 (e.g., $ 5^3 = 125 $) |  
| $ N = a_k \times b^k + a_{k-1} \times b^{k-1} + \dots + a_0 \times b^0 $ | General form of a number in base-$ b $ |  
| $ a_n $ | Digit in position $ n $ of a number (e.g., $ a_2 $ in $ 123_5 $ is 1) |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Mixing Up Bases**: Confusing base-10 and base-5 landmarks (e.g., mistaking $ 5^2 = 25 $ as 100 in base-10).  
   **Correction**: Always clarify whether the base is 10 or 5 before calculations.  
2. **Ignoring Remainders**: Skipping remainders during conversion leads to incorrect results.  
   **Correction**: Record all remainders systematically.  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice converting numbers between bases using small examples first.  
- Check your work by expanding the converted number back to decimal.
