## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 The Origin of Counting  
Humans began counting long before written numbers existed. As early as the **Stone Age**, people needed to track resources like food, animals, and trade goods. They also recorded time-related events, such as lunar cycles and seasonal changes.  

> **Definition**: A *numeral system* is a structured method for representing numbers using symbols or digits.  

### 3.2 Mesopotamian Numerals  
The Mesopotamians (around 4000 years ago) used a **base-60** system, called **sexagesimal**, to represent numbers. Their symbols were wedge-shaped marks carved into clay tablets. For example:  
- $1 $ was represented by a vertical wedge.  
- $ 10 $ was shown by a horizontal wedge.  
This system allowed them to handle large numbers efficiently for trade and astronomy.  

> **Property**: The **base** of a numeral system determines how many unique digits are used. For example, base-10 uses digits $ 0 $–$ 9 $.  

### 3.3 Ancient Indian Contributions  
Ancient Indian mathematicians made groundbreaking advancements. Around 2000 years ago, they developed the **decimal system** (base-10) using digits $ 0 $–$ 9 $. The **Bakhshali manuscript** (circa 3rd century CE) is the earliest known record of using zero as a placeholder.  

> **Definition**: A *digit* is a single symbol used in a numeral system. In base-10, digits are $ 0, 1, 2, ..., 9 $.  

#### Example: Understanding Base-10  
In our modern system:  
- $ 1 $ represents $ 1 $ unit.  
- $ 10 $ represents $ 1 $ group of $ 10 $ units.  
- $ 100 $ represents $ 1 $ group of $ 10 $ groups of $ 10 $ units.  

This hierarchical structure simplifies calculations and communication across cultures.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Identifying Base-10 Digits  
**Question**: Which of the following are valid digits in the base-10 system?  
$ a) $ $ 9 $  
$ b) $ $ 10 $  
$ c) $ $-3 $  
$ d) $ $ 5 $  

**Solution**:  
- $ a) $ Valid: $ 9 $ is a digit in base-10.  
- $ b) $ Invalid: $ 10 $ is not a single digit; it’s a two-digit number.  
- $ c) $ Invalid: Negative numbers are not digits.  
- $ d) $ Valid: $ 5 $ is a digit in base-10.  

Answer: $ a) $ and $ d) $ are valid.  

---  

### Example 2: Converting Mesopotamian Numbers  
**Question**: If the Mesopotamians used base-60, how would they represent $ 60 $?  

**Solution**:  
In base-60, $ 60 $ equals $ 1 $ group of $ 60 $ units. So, they would use one symbol for $ 1 $ (representing $ 60 $) and none for $ 0 $.  

Answer: One symbol for $ 1 $ (base-60).  

---  

### Example 3: Historical Fact Check  
**Question**: Was the concept of zero used in ancient India?  

**Solution**:  
Yes! The **Bakhshali manuscript** (circa 3rd century CE) is the first known evidence of using zero as a placeholder. Later, Aryabhata (circa 499 CE) further refined its use.  

Answer: Yes, zero was used in ancient India.

## 6. Summary  
- Humans began counting in the Stone Age to manage resources and time.  
- The Mesopotamians used a base-60 system for complex calculations.

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Base} = \text{Number of unique digits} $ | Defines the base of a numeral system. |

| $ 10^n $ | Represents powers of 10 in the decimal system. |
