## 2. Introduction  
**Have you ever wondered how something small can become huge with just a few steps?** Imagine taking a thin sheet of paper and folding it repeatedly. Each fold doubles its thickness, but even after just 10 folds, the paper becomes thicker than a meter! This is the magic of *exponential growth*—a powerful concept that shapes our world in surprising ways.  

In this topic, you’ll explore how the thickness of a paper changes when folded multiple times. You’ll discover the rule behind this rapid growth and apply it to solve real-world problems. By the end, you’ll understand why folding a paper just 46 times could theoretically reach the Moon—and whether it’s truly possible!

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 What Is Exponential Growth?  
> **Exponential growth** occurs when a quantity increases by a fixed ratio (or factor) over regular intervals. In the case of folding paper, the thickness doubles with each fold. This means the growth rate accelerates rapidly, making the total amount grow much faster than linear growth.  

#### Key Terms:  
- **Doubling**: Multiplying a quantity by 2.  
- **Exponential growth**: A pattern where the growth rate depends on the current value (not just a fixed amount).  

### 3.2 How Paper Thickness Grows  
When you fold a paper once, its thickness becomes twice the original. After the second fold, it doubles again, and so on. Mathematically, the thickness after $ n $ folds is calculated as:

$$
\text{Final Thickness} = \text{Initial Thickness} \times 2^n

$$  
For example, if the initial thickness is $ 0.001 $ cm, after 10 folds:

$$
0.001 \times 2^{10} = 0.001 \times 1024 = 1.024 \text{ cm}

$$  
This explains why the thickness jumps from less than a centimeter to over a meter after just 10 folds!  

### 3.3 Real-World Implications  
The table below shows how quickly the thickness grows:  

| Fold | Thickness       |  
|------|------------------|  
| 0    | $0.001 $ cm      |  
| 1    | $ 0.002 $ cm      |  
| 2    | $ 0.004 $ cm      |  
| ...  | ...              |  
| 10   | $ 1.024 $ cm      |  
| 17   | $ 131 $ cm        |  
| 26   | $ 670 $ m         |  
| 30   | $ 10.7 $ km       |  
| 46   | $ 7,00,000 $ km   |  

As you can see, the thickness grows from millimeters to kilometers in just a few folds!  

### 3.4 Why Does This Happen?  
Each fold multiplies the thickness by 2. Over time, this repeated multiplication leads to an explosion in size. This is because the growth isn’t just adding a fixed amount—it’s scaling the entire previous value. For instance:  
- After 1 fold: $ 0.001 \times 2 = 0.002 $ cm  
- After 2 folds: $ 0.002 \times 2 = 0.004 $ cm  
- After 3 folds: $ 0.004 \times 2 = 0.008 $ cm  
...and so on.  

This pattern is described by the formula $ 2^n $, where $ n $ is the number of folds.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Problem**: If a paper starts at $ 0.001 $ cm, what is its thickness after 15 folds?  

**Solution**:  
Use the formula $ \text{Final Thickness} = 0.001 \times 2^{15} $.  
Calculate $ 2^{15} $:

$$
2^{10} = 1024,\quad 2^{15} = 2^{10} \times 2^5 = 1024 \times 32 = 32,768

$$  
Multiply by the initial thickness:

$$
0.001 \times 32,768 = 32.768 \text{ cm}

$$  
**Answer**: $ 32.768 $ cm  

### Example 2:  
**Problem**: Compare the thickness of 20 folds versus 25 folds.  

**Solution**:  
- After 20 folds:

$$
0.001 \times 2^{20} = 0.001 \times 1,048,576 = 1,048.576 \text{ cm} \approx 10.49 \text{ m}

$$  
- After 25 folds:

$$
0.001 \times 2^{25} = 0.001 \times 33,554,432 = 33,554.432 \text{ cm} \approx 335.54 \text{ m}

$$  
**Conclusion**: The thickness increases by a factor of $ 2^{5} = 32 $ times from 20 to 25 folds.  

### Example 3:  
**Problem**: How many folds would it take to reach the height of the Burj Khalifa (830 m)?  

**Solution**:  
Set $ 0.001 \times 2^n = 830 $. Solve for $ n $:

$$
2^n = \frac{830}{0.001} = 830,000

$$  
Take logarithm base 2:

$$
n = \log_2(830,000) \approx 19.7

$$  
Since $ n $ must be an integer, it takes **20 folds**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**:  
1. If a paper is folded 5 times, what is its thickness? (Initial thickness = $ 0.001 $ cm)  
2. Calculate the thickness after 12 folds.  

🟡 **Medium**:  
3. How many folds are needed to reach 1 km in thickness?  
4. Compare the thickness after 10 folds and 15 folds.  

🔴 **Hard**:  
5. If the paper were folded 46 times, how far would it reach? (Assume Earth-Moon distance = $ 384,400 $ km)  

**Answers**:  
1. Solution: $ 0.001 \times 2^5 = 0.032 $ cm  
2. Solution: $ 0.001 \times 2^{12} = 4096 $ cm = $ 40.96 $ m  
3. Solution: $ n = \log_2(1000 / 0.001) = \log_2(1,000,000) \approx 19.9 $, so **20 folds**  
4. Solution: 10 folds = $ 1.024 $ cm; 15 folds = $ 32.768 $ cm → 32 times greater  
5. Solution: $ 0.001 \times 2^{46} \approx 700,000 $ km (matches Earth-Moon distance)

## 6. Summary  
- **Exponential growth** occurs when a quantity doubles repeatedly, leading to rapid increases.  
- The formula $ t_n = t_0 \times 2^n $ calculates the thickness after $ n $ folds.  
- Folding a paper 10 times makes it over 1 meter thick; 46 folds can reach the Moon!  
- Doubling every time creates a pattern that grows much faster than linear addition.  
- This concept applies to real-world scenarios like population growth, compound interest, and technological advancements.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mistake**: Assuming linear growth instead of exponential.  
  **Fix**: Remember that each fold multiplies the thickness, not adds a fixed amount.  
- **Mistake**: Confusing $ 2^n $ with $ n \times 2 $.  
  **Fix**: Use the formula $ t_n = t_0 \times 2^n $, not $ t_n = t_0 + n \times 2 $.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Draw tables** to visualize how thickness grows with each fold.  
- **Check units** carefully (cm, m, km) to avoid errors in comparisons.

Remember that each fold doubles the thickness, leading to explosive growth.
