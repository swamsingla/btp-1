## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how a single sheet of paper can become extremely thick after being folded multiple times? Imagine folding a piece of paper 10 times—its thickness grows exponentially! This phenomenon is a real-life example of **exponential notation**, which helps us express large numbers compactly.  

In this topic, you’ll learn:  
- How to write repeated multiplication using **exponential form**  
- The meaning of **base** and **exponent**  
- How to perform operations like multiplication and division with exponents  
- Applications of exponents in everyday situations  

By the end, you’ll be able to convert expressions like $2 \times 2 \times 2 $ into $ 2^3 $ and calculate values like $ 5^4 $ easily!

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Exponential Notation  
> **Exponential notation** is a way to write repeated multiplication of the same number.  
For example:  
- $ 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2^3 $ (read as “2 raised to the power of 3”)  
- $ 5 \times 5 = 5^2 $ (read as “5 squared” or “5 raised to the power of 2”)  

In $ n^a $:  
- **n** is the **base** (the number being multiplied repeatedly)  
- **a** is the **exponent** (how many times the base is multiplied by itself)  

### 3.2 Rules for Exponents  
> **Rule 1**: Any number raised to the power of 1 equals itself.

$$

n^1 = n

$$  
> **Rule 2**: Any non-zero number raised to the power of 0 equals 1.

$$

n^0 = 1 \quad \text{(where } n \neq 0\text{)}

$$  
> **Rule 3**: When multiplying powers with the same base, **add the exponents**.

$$

n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b}

$$  
> **Rule 4**: When dividing powers with the same base, **subtract the exponents**.

$$

\frac{n^a}{n^b} = n^{a-b}

$$  
> **Rule 5**: When raising a power to another power, **multiply the exponents**.

$$

(n^a)^b = n^{a \times b}

$$  

### 3.3 Negative Bases and Exponents  
> **Negative bases** behave differently depending on whether the exponent is even or odd:  
- $(−4)^2 = (−4) \times (−4) = 16 $ (positive result because the product of two negatives is positive)  
- $ (−4)^3 = (−4) \times (−4) \times (−4) = −64 $ (negative result because the product of three negatives is negative)  

> **Zero raised to any positive exponent is zero**:

$$

0^n = 0 \quad \text{(where } n > 0\text{)}

$$  
However, $0^0 $ is undefined.  

### 3.4 Prime Factorization with Exponents  
> To express a number as a product of its prime factors in **exponential form**, break it down into primes and count duplicates.  
For example:

$$

32400 = 2^4 \times 5^2 \times 3^4

$$  
This means:  
- 2 appears 4 times  
- 5 appears 2 times  
- 3 appears 4 times  

### 3.5 Combining Variables and Constants  
> When simplifying expressions with both numbers and variables, apply exponents to each term separately.  
Example:

$$

a \times a \times b \times b = a^2 \times b^2

$$

$$

x \times x \times x \times y \times y = x^3 \times y^2

$$

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Convert Repeated Multiplication to Exponential Form  
**Problem**: Express $4 \times 4 \times 4 $ in exponential form.  
**Solution**:

$$

4 \times 4 \times 4 = 4^3

$$  
So, the exponential form is $4^3 $.  

### Example 2: Simplify an Expression with Multiple Terms  
**Problem**: Simplify $ 2 \times 2 \times a \times a $.  
**Solution**:  
Group the like terms:

$$

2 \times 2 = 2^2

$$

$$

a \times a = a^2

$$  
Combine them:

$$

2^2 \times a^2

$$  
Final Answer: $2^2a^2 $  

### Example 3: Evaluate a Negative Base with an Odd Exponent  
**Problem**: Calculate $ (−3)^5 $.  
**Solution**:  
Multiply $−3 $ five times:

$$
(−3)^5 = (−3) \times (−3) \times (−3) \times (−3) \times (−3)

$$  
Step-by-step:  
- First pair: $(−3) \times (−3) = 9 $  
- Second pair: $ 9 \times (−3) = −27 $  
- Third pair: $−27 \times (−3) = 81 $  
- Final step: $ 81 \times (−3) = −243 $  
Final Answer: $−243 $  

### Example 4: Apply the Rule for Multiplying Powers  
**Problem**: Simplify $ 2^3 \times 2^4 $.  
**Solution**:  
Use the rule $ n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b} $:

$$
2^3 \times 2^4 = 2^{3+4} = 2^7

$$  
Final Answer: $2^7 $  

### Example 5: Prime Factorization in Exponential Form  
**Problem**: Express 32400 as a product of its prime factors in exponential form.  
**Solution**:  
Break down 32400 into prime factors:

$$
32400 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 5 \times 5 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3

$$  
Count duplicates:  
- 2 appears 4 times → $2^4 $  
- 5 appears 2 times → $ 5^2 $  
- 3 appears 4 times → $ 3^4 $  
Combine them:

$$
32400 = 2^4 \times 5^2 \times 3^4

$$  
Final Answer: $2^4 \times 5^2 \times 3^4 $

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
1. Express $ 7 \times 7 \times 7 $ in exponential form.  
2. Simplify $ 5^2 \times 5^3 $.  
3. What is $ (−6)^2 $?  

### 🟡 Medium  
4. Simplify $ a \times a \times b \times b \times b $.  
5. Evaluate $ (−2)^4 \times 3^2 $.  
6. Express 1024 as a power of 2.  

### 🔴 Hard  
7. Simplify $ 2^5 \div 2^2 $.  
8. Find the value of $ (−1)^{56} $.  
9. Express $ 3600 $ as a product of its prime factors in exponential form.  

**Answers:**  
1. $ 7^3 $  
2. $ 5^{2+3} = 5^5 $  
3. $ (-6)^2 = 36 $  
4. $ a^2b^3 $  
5. $ (-2)^4 = 16 $, $ 3^2 = 9 $, $ 16 \times 9 = 144 $  
6. $ 2^{10} $  
7. $ 2^{5-2} = 2^3 = 8 $  
8. $ (-1)^{56} = 1 $ (even exponent)  
9. $ 3600 = 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 $

## 6. Summary  
- **Exponential notation** writes repeated multiplication as $ n^a $, where $ n $ is the base and $ a $ is the exponent.  
- **Rules for exponents**:  
  - $ n^1 = n $  
  - $ n^0 = 1 $ (if $ n \neq 0 $)  
  - $ n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b} $  
  - $\frac{n^a}{n^b} = n^{a-b} $  
  - $ (n^a)^b = n^{a \times b} $  
- **Negative bases**: Even exponents yield positive results; odd exponents yield negative results.  
- **Prime factorization**: Break numbers into primes and express them in exponential form.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ n^a = n \times n \times \dots \times n $ (a times) | Definition of exponential notation |  
| $ n^1 = n $ | Any number to the power of 1 |  
| $ n^0 = 1 $ (for $ n \neq 0 $) | Zero exponent rule |  
| $ n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b} $ | Multiplying powers with the same base |  
| $\frac{n^a}{n^b} = n^{a-b} $ | Dividing powers with the same base |  
| $ (n^a)^b = n^{a \times b} $ | Raising a power to another power |  
| $ 0^n = 0 $ (for $ n > 0 $) | Zero raised to a positive exponent |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing exponents with multiplication: $ 2^3 = 8 $, not $ 2 \times 3 = 6 $  
- ❌ Forgetting that negative bases with even exponents give positive results: $ (−2)^2 = 4 $, not $−4 $  

### Exam Tips  
- Always check if the exponent is even or odd when dealing with negative bases.  
- Practice converting repeated multiplication to exponents regularly to reinforce the concept.  

### Memory Aid  
**"PEMDAS" for exponents**: Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication/Division, Addition/Subtraction. Remember to handle exponents *before* multiplication or division!
