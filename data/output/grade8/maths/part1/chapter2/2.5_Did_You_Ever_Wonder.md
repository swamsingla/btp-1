## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how much money or weight equals a person’s mass? This section explores real-world scenarios like donating jaggery or coins equal to someone’s weight, using **mathematical reasoning** and **estimation**. By the end, you’ll learn how to calculate such values and appreciate ancient practices like *Tulābhāra*!  

---  

## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 Calculating Worth Using Weight and Cost  
To determine the **worth** of donated items, multiply **weight** (in kg) by **price per unit** (e.g., ₹/kg):

$$
\text{Worth} = \text{Weight} \times \text{Price per unit}

$$  
Example:  
- Jaggery worth = $45 \, \text{kg} \times ₹70/\text{kg} = ₹3150 $  
- Wheat worth = $ 50 \, \text{kg} \times ₹50/\text{kg} = ₹2500 $  

### 3.2 Estimating with Coins  
To find how many **coins** equal a person’s weight:

$$
\text{Number of coins} = \frac{\text{Weight in grams}}{\text{Coin weight in grams}}

$$  
For example, if Roxie weighs 45 kg (45,000 g) and 1-rupee coins weigh 5 g each:

$$
\frac{45000}{5} = 9000 \, \text{coins}

$$  
### 3.3 Historical Context  
*Tulābhāra* is an ancient practice where offerings (like jaggery or coins) match a person’s weight. It symbolizes devotion and community support.

## 4. Solved Examples  
### Example 1: Jaggery Donation  
**Problem**: If Roxie’s weight is 45 kg and jaggery costs ₹70/kg, calculate the total worth.  
**Solution**:

$$
\text{Worth} = 45 \times 70 = ₹3150

$$  
**Answer**: ₹3150  

### Example 2: Coin Calculation  
**Problem**: How many 10-rupee notes equal Estu’s weight of 50 kg? Assume each note weighs 10 g.  
**Solution**:  
Convert weight to grams: $50 \, \text{kg} = 50000 \, \text{g} $ 

$$
\frac{50000}{10} = 5000 \, \text{notes}

$$  
**Answer**: 5000 notes  

### Example 3: Estimation Challenge  
**Problem**: Estimate the number of 5-rupee coins needed to equal Roxie’s weight (45 kg).  
**Solution**:  
Guess: Hundreds of thousands (e.g., 1 lakh).  
Calculate: Assume each coin weighs 5 g:

$$
\frac{45000}{5} = 9000 \, \text{coins} (\text{which is } ₹45000)

$$  
**Answer**: ~9000 coins (₹45,000)

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**: If 1-rupee coins weigh 5 g, how many coins equal 30 kg?  
🟡 **Medium**: Calculate the worth of 20 kg wheat at ₹60/kg.  
🔴 **Hard**: Estimate the number of 10-rupee notes needed for a 50 kg weight, assuming each note weighs 10 g.  

**Answers**:  
- **Easy**:

$$
  \frac{30000 \, \text{g}}{5 \, \text{g/coin}} = 6000 \, \text{coins}

$$  
- **Medium**:

$$
  20 \times 60 = ₹1200

$$  
- **Hard**:

$$
  \frac{50000 \, \text{g}}{10 \, \text{g/note}} = 5000 \, \text{notes}

$$

## 6. Summary  
- **Worth = Weight × Price per unit**  
- **Estimation** involves guessing and refining with calculations  
- Ancient *Tulābhāra* connects math to cultural practices  
- Use **approximations** when exact data is missing
