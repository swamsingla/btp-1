## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Cubic Numbers  
A **cubic number** is a number that results from multiplying a number by itself three times. Mathematically, this is written as:

$$
n^3 = n \times n \times n

$$  
For example:  
- $1^3 = 1 \times 1 \times 1 = 1 $  
- $ 2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8 $  
- $ 3^3 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27 $  

> **Perfect cubes** are numbers like 1, 8, 27, 64, etc., which are exact cubes of integers.  

### 3.2 Identifying Perfect Cubes  
To determine if a number is a perfect cube:  
1. Factorize the number into prime factors.  
2. Check if all exponents in the factorization are multiples of 3.  

**Example**: Is 9 a perfect cube?  
- Prime factorization of 9: $ 3^2 $  
- Since the exponent 2 is not divisible by 3, **9 is not a perfect cube**.  

### 3.3 Patterns in Last Digits  
Like square numbers, cubes have predictable last digits. Here’s the pattern:  
| Unit Digit of $ n $ | Last Digit of $ n^3 $ |  
|---------------------|-----------------------|  
| 0                   | 0                     |  
| 1                   | 1                     |  
| 2                   | 8                     |  
| 3                   | 7                     |  
| 4                   | 4                     |  
| 5                   | 5                     |  
| 6                   | 6                     |  
| 7                   | 3                     |  
| 8                   | 2                     |  
| 9                   | 9                     |  

**Tip**: Remember this pattern to quickly guess the last digit of any cube!  

### 3.4 Taxicab Numbers  
The number **1729** is famous because it’s the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways:

$$
1729 = 1^3 + 12^3 = 9^3 + 10^3

$$  
This story connects math to history and highlights the beauty of number theory.  

### 3.5 Cubes of Fractions and Decimals  
Cubes work with non-integers too! For example:  
- $\left(\frac{4}{6}\right)^3 = \frac{4}{6} \times \frac{4}{6} \times \frac{4}{6} = \frac{64}{216} $  
- $ (13.08)^3 = 13.08 \times 13.08 \times 13.08 = 2237.810112 $  
- $ (-6)^3 = (-6) \times (-6) \times (-6) = -216 $  

> Note: Negative numbers raised to odd powers remain negative.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Calculating a Cube  
**Question**: Find $ 7^3 $.  
**Solution**:

$$
7^3 = 7 \times 7 \times 7 = 49 \times 7 = 343

$$  
✅ Answer: $343 $  

### Example 2: Identifying a Perfect Cube  
**Question**: Is 64 a perfect cube?  
**Solution**:  
- Prime factorization of 64: $ 2^6 $  
- Exponent 6 is divisible by 3 → Yes, $ 64 = 4^3 $.  
✅ Answer: Yes, 64 is a perfect cube.  

### Example 3: Using the Last-Digit Pattern  
**Question**: What is the last digit of $ 19^3 $?  
**Solution**:  
- From the table, the last digit of 9³ is 9.  
✅ Answer: 9  

### Example 4: Solving a Taxicab Puzzle  
**Question**: Verify that 1729 is the sum of two cubes in two ways.  
**Solution**:  
1. $ 1^3 + 12^3 = 1 + 1728 = 1729 $  
2. $ 9^3 + 10^3 = 729 + 1000 = 1729 $  
✅ Answer: Verified!  

### Example 5: Cubing a Decimal  
**Question**: Calculate $ (1.5)^3 $.  
**Solution**:

$$
(1.5)^3 = 1.5 \times 1.5 \times 1.5 = 2.25 \times 1.5 = 3.375

$$  
✅ Answer: $3.375 $

## 6. Summary  
- **Cubic numbers** are numbers formed by cubing integers ($ n^3 = n \times n \times n $).  
- **Perfect cubes** include 1, 8, 27, 64, etc.  
- The last digit of a cube follows a fixed pattern.  
- **1729** is the smallest taxicab number.  
- Cubes apply to fractions, decimals, and negative numbers.

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ n^3 = n \times n \times n $ | Definition of a cubic number |  
| $ (-a)^3 = -a^3 $ | Negative numbers cubed remain negative |  
| $ a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2) $ | Sum of cubes identity |  
| $ a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2) $ | Difference of cubes identity |
