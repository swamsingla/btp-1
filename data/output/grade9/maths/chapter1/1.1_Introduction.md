## 2. Introduction  
Imagine walking along a number line, collecting numbers as you go. This journey begins with natural numbers, expands to whole numbers, and eventually includes integers and beyond. In this section, you'll explore how numbers are categorized and introduced to the concept of **rational numbers**, which form a crucial foundation for further studies in number systems.  

---  

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definitions  
**Natural Numbers**: The set of positive integers starting from 1, denoted by $ \mathbb{N} $.  
**Whole Numbers**: The set of natural numbers plus zero, denoted by $ \mathbb{W} $.  
**Integers**: The set of whole numbers extended to include negative numbers, denoted by $ \mathbb{Z} $.  
**Rational Numbers**: A number $ r $ is called rational if it can be expressed as $ \frac{p}{q} $, where $ p $ and $ q $ are integers and $ q \neq 0 $. This set is denoted by $ \mathbb{Q} $.  

> **Important Theorem**: The set of rational numbers $ \mathbb{Q} $ includes all natural numbers $ \mathbb{N} $, whole numbers $ \mathbb{W} $, and integers $ \mathbb{Z} $. This is because any integer $ n $ can be written as $ \frac{n}{1} $, satisfying the condition $ q \neq 0 $.  

### 3.2 Non-Unique Representations  
Rational numbers do not have a unique representation. For instance:

$$
\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{10}{20} = \frac{25}{50}

$$  
These are equivalent fractions, but when representing a rational number $ \frac{p}{q} $, it is assumed that $ p $ and $ q $ share no common factors (i.e., the fraction is in its simplest form).  

### 3.3 Origins of Symbols  
- **$ \mathbb{Z} $**: Derived from the German word *zahlen*, meaning “to count.”  
- **$ \mathbb{Q} $**: Stands for *quotient*, reflecting the division operation central to rational numbers.

## 4. Solved Examples  

**Example 1:**  
Determine whether $ -\frac{3}{5} $ is a rational number.  

**Solution:**  
By definition, a rational number is any number expressible as $ \frac{p}{q} $ where $ p $ and $ q $ are integers and $ q \neq 0 $. Here, $ p = -3 $ and $ q = 5 $, both integers with $ q \neq 0 $. Thus, $ -\frac{3}{5} $ is a rational number.  

**Example 2:**  
Show that $ 5 $ can be represented as a rational number.  

**Solution:**  
Any integer can be written as $ \frac{n}{1} $. For $ n = 5 $, we have:

$$
5 = \frac{5}{1}

$$  
Since $ p = 5 $ and $ q = 1 $ satisfy $ q \neq 0 $, $ 5 $ is a rational number.  

**Example 3:**  
Identify which of the following are rational numbers: $ \sqrt{2}, \frac{1}{3}, -7, 0.333\ldots $.  

**Solution:**  
- $ \sqrt{2} $: Not rational (irrational number, discussed in later sections).  
- $ \frac{1}{3} $: Rational (already in $ \frac{p}{q} $ form).  
- $ -7 $: Rational (can be written as $ \frac{-7}{1} $).  
- $ 0.333\ldots $: Rational (equal to $ \frac{1}{3} $).

**Answers:**  

1. **Answer:** Yes. $ \frac{9}{2} $ is rational because $ p = 9 $, $ q = 2 $ are integers, and $ q \neq 0 $.  
2. **Answer:** $ -4 = \frac{-4}{1} $, so it is a rational number.  
3. **Answer:** Simplify $ \frac{4}{6} $ to $ \frac{2}{3} $ by dividing numerator and denominator by 2. Both fractions represent the same value.  
4. **Answer:** Three examples are $ \frac{5}{8}, \frac{7}{12}, \frac{11}{16} $.  
5. **Answer:** Division by zero is undefined in mathematics. If $ q = 0 $, the expression $ \frac{p}{q} $ becomes meaningless, violating the fundamental properties of rational numbers.

## 6. Summary  
- **Natural Numbers ($ \mathbb{N} $)**: Positive integers starting from 1.  
- **Whole Numbers ($ \mathbb{W} $)**: $ \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\} $.  
- **Integers ($ \mathbb{Z} $)**: Includes negatives, zeros, and positives.  
- **Rational Numbers ($ \mathbb{Q} $)**: Any number expressible as $ \frac{p}{q} $, $ q \neq 0 $.  
- Rational numbers encompass integers, whole numbers, and natural numbers.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ r = \frac{p}{q} $ | Rational number definition, where $ p, q \in \mathbb{Z} $, $ q \neq 0 $. |  
| $ \mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{W} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} $ | Hierarchy of number sets. |  
| $ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} $ | Equivalent fractions if $ ad = bc $. |

## 8. Student Corner  
**Common Mistakes:**  
- Confusing integers with rational numbers (e.g., thinking $ \pi $ is rational).  
- Forgetting that $ q \neq 0 $ in $ \frac{p}{q} $.  

**Memory Aid:**  
Recall that **Q** stands for *quotient*—a hint that rational numbers involve division.  

**Exam Tip:**  
When proving a number is rational, always express it in the form $ \frac{p}{q} $ and confirm $ q \neq 0 $.
