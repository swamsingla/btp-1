## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Factor Theorem  
> **Definition**: If $ p(x) $ is a polynomial of degree $ n > 1 $ and $ a $ is a real number, then:  
> - **(i)** $ x - a $ is a factor of $ p(x) $ **if and only if** $ p(a) = 0 $.  
> - **(ii)** Conversely, if $ x - a $ is a factor of $ p(x) $, then $ p(a) = 0 $.  

**Proof**:  
From the **Remainder Theorem**, any polynomial $ p(x) $ can be expressed as:

$$
p(x) = (x - a)q(x) + p(a)

$$  
where $ q(x) $ is the quotient and $ p(a) $ is the remainder.  
- **Case (i)**: If $ p(a) = 0 $, substituting $ x = a $ gives $ p(a) = (a - a)q(a) + 0 = 0 $. Thus, $ x - a $ divides $ p(x) $ completely, making it a factor.  
- **Case (ii)**: If $ x - a $ is a factor, then $ p(x) = (x - a)g(x) $ for some polynomial $ g(x) $. Substituting $ x = a $ yields $ p(a) = (a - a)g(a) = 0 $.  

### 3.2 Factorisation of Quadratic Polynomials  
To factorise a quadratic polynomial $ ax^2 + bx + c $, follow these steps:  
1. Identify two numbers $ m $ and $ n $ such that:  
   - $ m + n = b $ (coefficient of $ x $)  
   - $ m \cdot n = ac $ (product of leading coefficient and constant term)  
2. Rewrite the middle term $ bx $ as $ mx + nx $.  
3. Group terms and factor by grouping.  

**Example**: Factorising $ 6x^2 + 17x + 5 $:  
- Here, $ a = 6 $, $ b = 17 $, $ c = 5 $.  
- Find $ m $ and $ n $: $ m + n = 17 $, $ m \cdot n = 6 \cdot 5 = 30 $.  
- The pair $ 15 $ and $ 2 $ satisfies these conditions.  
- Rewrite: $ 6x^2 + 15x + 2x + 5 $.  
- Group: $ (6x^2 + 15x) + (2x + 5) = 3x(2x + 5) + 1(2x + 5) $.  
- Final factorisation: $ (3x + 1)(2x + 5) $.  

### 3.3 Relationship Between Factors and Roots  
Every factor of a polynomial corresponds to a root (zero) of the polynomial. For example, if $ x - a $ is a factor of $ p(x) $, then $ x = a $ is a solution to $ p(x) = 0 $. This connects factorisation to the study of polynomial roots (Section 2.3).

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Using the Factor Theorem  
**Problem**: Examine whether $ x + 2 $ is a factor of $ x^3 + 3x^2 + 5x + 6 $.  

**Solution**:  
1. Find the zero of $ x + 2 $: $ x = -2 $.  
2. Substitute $ x = -2 $ into the polynomial:

$$
   (-2)^3 + 3(-2)^2 + 5(-2) + 6 = -8 + 12 - 10 + 6 = 0

$$  
3. Since the result is zero, $ x + 2 $ is a factor by the **Factor Theorem**.  

### Example 2: Finding a Missing Constant  
**Problem**: Find $ k $ such that $ x - 1 $ is a factor of $ 4x^3 + 3x^2 - 4x + k $.  

**Solution**:  
1. Substitute $ x = 1 $ into the polynomial:

$$
   4(1)^3 + 3(1)^2 - 4(1) + k = 4 + 3 - 4 + k = 3 + k

$$  
2. Set equal to zero (since $ x - 1 $ is a factor):

$$
   3 + k = 0 \implies k = -3

$$  

### Example 3: Factorising a Quadratic  
**Problem**: Factorise $ 6x^2 + 17x + 5 $.  

**Solution**:  
1. Identify $ m $ and $ n $: $ m = 15 $, $ n = 2 $.  
2. Split the middle term:

$$
   6x^2 + 15x + 2x + 5

$$  
3. Group and factor:

$$
   (6x^2 + 15x) + (2x + 5) = 3x(2x + 5) + 1(2x + 5) = (3x + 1)(2x + 5)

$$  

### Example 4: Verifying a Linear Factor  
**Problem**: Is $ 2x + 4 $ divisible by $ x + 2 $?  

**Solution**:  
1. Factor $ 2x + 4 $:

$$
   2x + 4 = 2(x + 2)

$$  
2. Since $ x + 2 $ is a factor, the division is exact.  

### Example 5: Applying the Factor Theorem to a Cubic  
**Problem**: Show that $ x - 3 $ is a factor of $ x^3 - 27 $.  

**Solution**:  
1. Substitute $ x = 3 $:

$$
   (3)^3 - 27 = 27 - 27 = 0

$$  
2. By the **Factor Theorem**, $ x - 3 $ is a factor.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**: Check if $ x - 2 $ is a factor of $ x^2 - 4 $.  
**Answer**: Substitute $ x = 2 $: $ 2^2 - 4 = 0 $. Hence, yes.  

**Problem 2**: Factorise $ x^2 + 5x + 6 $.  
**Answer**: Split $ 5x $ into $ 2x + 3x $: $ (x + 2)(x + 3) $.  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 3**: Find $ k $ such that $ x - 2 $ is a factor of $ 2x^2 + kx - 8 $.  
**Answer**: Substitute $ x = 2 $: $ 2(4) + 2k - 8 = 8 + 2k - 8 = 2k = 0 \implies k = 0 $.  

**Problem 4**: Factorise $ 3x^2 + 10x + 8 $.  
**Answer**: Find $ m $ and $ n $: $ 6 $ and $ 4 $. Split $ 10x $ into $ 6x + 4x $:

$$
3x^2 + 6x + 4x + 8 = 3x(x + 2) + 4(x + 2) = (3x + 4)(x + 2)

$$  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 5**: Factorise $ 6x^2 + 19x + 15 $.  
**Answer**: Find $ m $ and $ n $: $ 10 $ and $ 9 $. Split $ 19x $:

$$
6x^2 + 10x + 9x + 15 = 2x(3x + 5) + 3(3x + 5) = (2x + 3)(3x + 5)

$$

## 6. Summary  
- The **Factor Theorem** links factors and roots of polynomials: $ x - a $ is a factor ⇨ $ p(a) = 0 $.  
- To factorise quadratics $ ax^2 + bx + c $:  
  1. Find two numbers $ m $ and $ n $ such that $ m + n = b $ and $ mn = ac $.  
  2. Split the middle term and group.  
- Factorisation simplifies solving equations and analyzing polynomial behavior.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ p(a) = 0 $ ⇨ $ x - a $ is a factor | **Factor Theorem** |  
| $ ax^2 + bx + c = (px + q)(rx + s) $ | General form of factorisation |  
| $ m + n = b $, $ mn = ac $ | Conditions for splitting the middle term |  
| $ (x - a)(x - b) = x^2 - (a + b)x + ab $ | Factoring quadratic with known roots |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Misapplying the Factor Theorem**: Always substitute the **zero** of the factor (e.g., for $ x - a $, use $ x = a $).  
2. **Incorrect splitting of terms**: Ensure the product $ mn = ac $ and sum $ m + n = b $.  

### Exam Tips  
- Use the **Factor Theorem** first to test potential factors before lengthy expansion.  
- For quadratics, prioritize splitting the middle term over trial-and-error factoring.  

### Memory Aid  
**F.O.I.L.**: First, Outer, Inner, Last—helps remember how to multiply binomials during factorisation.
