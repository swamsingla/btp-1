## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how mathematicians simplify complex multiplications or factorizations effortlessly? The secret lies in **algebraic identities**—special equations that hold true for all values of variables. These identities act as shortcuts, allowing us to perform tasks like multiplying large numbers or factoring polynomials without tedious computation.  

In this topic, you’ll explore **five fundamental algebraic identities** and learn how to apply them to:  
- Expand expressions efficiently (e.g., $ (x + 3)^2 $).  
- Factorize quadratic and cubic expressions (e.g., $ x^2 - 9 $).  
- Solve real-world problems involving multiplication and simplification (e.g., calculating $ 105 \times 106 $).  

By mastering these identities, you’ll gain tools to tackle problems faster and with greater confidence!

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definitions and Core Concepts  
An **algebraic identity** is an equation that remains valid for all values of the variables involved. These identities are derived from the distributive law and are universally applicable.  

> **Definition of Algebraic Identity**: An equation that holds true for all values of the variables in it.  

The source text introduces **four key identities**, which are extended to three variables later. Below are their full statements and derivations:  

#### Identity I: Square of a Binomial Sum  
> **Identity I**:

$$

(x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2

$$  
**Proof**:  
Start with $ (x + y)^2 = (x + y)(x + y) $. Apply distributive law:

$$
(x + y)(x + y) = x \cdot x + x \cdot y + y \cdot x + y \cdot y = x^2 + xy + xy + y^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2

$$  

#### Identity II: Square of a Binomial Difference  
> **Identity II**:

$$

(x - y)^2 = x^2 - 2xy + y^2

$$  
**Proof**:  
Use $ (x - y)^2 = (x - y)(x - y) $:

$$
x \cdot x + x \cdot (-y) + (-y) \cdot x + (-y) \cdot (-y) = x^2 - xy - xy + y^2 = x^2 - 2xy + y^2

$$  

#### Identity III: Difference of Squares  
> **Identity III**:

$$

x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)

$$  
**Proof**:  
Multiply the right-hand side:

$$
(x + y)(x - y) = x \cdot x + x \cdot (-y) + y \cdot x + y \cdot (-y) = x^2 - xy + xy - y^2 = x^2 - y^2

$$  

#### Identity IV: Product of Two Binomials  
> **Identity IV**:

$$

(x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab

$$  
**Proof**:  
Expand the left-hand side:

$$
x \cdot x + x \cdot b + a \cdot x + a \cdot b = x^2 + bx + ax + ab = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab

$$  

#### Identity V: Square of a Trinomial  
> **Identity V**:

$$

(x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2zx

$$  
**Proof**:  
Let $ x + y = t $, so $ (x + y + z)^2 = (t + z)^2 $. Apply Identity I:

$$
t^2 + 2tz + z^2 = (x + y)^2 + 2(x + y)z + z^2

$$  
Substitute $ (x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2 $:

$$
x^2 + 2xy + y^2 + 2xz + 2yz + z^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2zx

$$

### 3.2 Applications of Identities  
Algebraic identities are invaluable for:  
- **Simplifying expressions**: Replace lengthy expansions with compact forms (e.g., $ (x + 3)^2 $ instead of $ x^2 + 6x + 9 $).  
- **Factorizing polynomials**: Recognize patterns to rewrite expressions as products (e.g., $ x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3) $).  
- **Computational efficiency**: Perform mental math quickly (e.g., $ 105 \times 106 = 11130 $).  

These identities bridge abstract algebra with practical problem-solving.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Expanding $ (x + 3)^2 $  
**Solution**:  
Apply Identity I:

$$
(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 2(x)(3) + (3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9

$$  

### Example 2: Multiplying $ (x - 3)(x + 5) $  
**Solution**:  
Use Identity IV:

$$
(x - 3)(x + 5) = x^2 + (-3 + 5)x + (-3)(5) = x^2 + 2x - 15

$$  

### Example 3: Evaluating $ 105 \times 106 $  
**Solution**:  
Express numbers as sums:

$$
105 \times 106 = (100 + 5)(100 + 6)

$$  
Apply Identity IV:

$$
(100)^2 + (5 + 6)(100) + (5 \times 6) = 10000 + 1100 + 30 = 11130

$$  

### Example 4: Factorizing $ 49a^2 + 70ab + 25b^2 $  
**Solution**:  
Recognize the pattern of Identity I:

$$
49a^2 = (7a)^2,\quad 25b^2 = (5b)^2,\quad 70ab = 2(7a)(5b)

$$  
Thus:

$$
49a^2 + 70ab + 25b^2 = (7a + 5b)^2 = (7a + 5b)(7a + 5b)

$$  

### Example 5: Simplifying $ (x + y + z)^2 $  
**Solution**:  
Apply Identity V:

$$
(x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2zx

$$

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy: Expand $ (x + 4)^2 $  
**Answer**:  
Use Identity I:

$$
(x + 4)^2 = x^2 + 2(x)(4) + (4)^2 = x^2 + 8x + 16

$$  

### 🟡 Medium: Factorize $ 25x^2 - 16y^2 $  
**Answer**:  
Recognize the difference of squares (Identity III):

$$
25x^2 - 16y^2 = (5x)^2 - (4y)^2 = (5x - 4y)(5x + 4y)

$$  

### 🔴 Hard: Evaluate $ 99 \times 101 $ using an identity  
**Answer**:  
Express numbers as $ (100 - 1)(100 + 1) $:

$$
(100 - 1)(100 + 1) = (100)^2 - (1)^2 = 10000 - 1 = 9999

$$  

### 🟡 Medium: Expand $ (x + 2)(x + 3) $  
**Answer**:  
Use Identity IV:

$$
(x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 + (2 + 3)x + (2 \times 3) = x^2 + 5x + 6

$$  

### 🟢 Easy: Simplify $ (y + x)(y - x) $  
**Answer**:  
Apply Identity III:

$$
(y + x)(y - x) = y^2 - x^2

$$

## 6. Summary  
- **Identity I**: $ (x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2 $  
- **Identity II**: $ (x - y)^2 = x^2 - 2xy + y^2 $  
- **Identity III**: $ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) $  
- **Identity IV**: $ (x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab $  
- **Identity V**: $ (x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2zx $  
- Identities are universal and simplify multiplication/factorization.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Incorrect sign application**: Forgetting the negative sign in Identity II leads to errors like $ (x - y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2 $.  
   **Correction**: Always double-check the middle term’s sign.  
2. **Misidentifying patterns**: Confusing $ x^2 - y^2 $ with $ x^2 + y^2 $.  
   **Correction**: Recall Identity III explicitly applies to differences of squares.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Memorize identities**: Quick recall saves time during exams.  
- **Check for symmetry**: Patterns like $ x^2 + 2xy + y^2 $ often hint at Identity I.  

### Memory Aid  
Recite the identities in order:  
**"Square, Subtract, Multiply, Expand, Trinomial"** (I to V).
