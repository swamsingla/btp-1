## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how to describe patterns like the perimeter of a square or the area of a rectangle mathematically? These patterns often depend on a single variable, such as the side length of a square. Today, we’ll explore **polynomials in one variable**, which are powerful tools for modeling such relationships. By the end of this section, you’ll learn:  
- What makes an expression a polynomial.  
- How to identify terms, coefficients, and degrees in polynomials.  
- Why polynomials are foundational for advanced algebraic concepts.  

This builds on your existing knowledge of variables and expressions, helping you analyze how quantities change systematically.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of a Polynomial  
> **A polynomial in one variable** is an algebraic expression consisting of terms involving powers of a single variable with non-negative integer exponents. The general form is:

$$
a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0

$$  
where $a_n, a_{n-1}, \dots, a_0 $ are constants, and $ n $ is a non-negative integer.  

**Examples from the Source Text**:  
- $ 4x $: Perimeter of a square with side length $ x $.  
- $ x^2 $: Area of a square with side length $ x $.  
- $ x^3 - x^2 + 4x + 7 $: A cubic polynomial in $ x $.  

### 3.2 Terms and Coefficients  
> **Terms** are individual parts of a polynomial separated by addition or subtraction.  
> **Coefficients** are the numerical factors of each term.  

**Key Points**:  
- In $ 3y^2 + 5y + 7 $, the terms are $ 3y^2 $, $ 5y $, and $ 7 $.  
- The coefficient of $ y^2 $ is $ 3 $, the coefficient of $ y $ is $ 5 $, and $ 7 $ is the constant term (coefficient of $ y^0 $).  
- Constants like $ 2 $, $-5 $, or $ 7 $ are also polynomials (degree 0).  

### 3.3 Degree of a Polynomial  
> **The degree** of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable with a non-zero coefficient.  

**Rules**:  
- For $ x^3 - x^2 + 4x + 7 $, the degree is $ 3 $.  
- For $ t^2 + 4 $, the degree is $ 2 $.  
- For a constant polynomial like $ 5 $, the degree is $ 0 $.  

---

## 4. Solved Examples  

### **Example 1:** Identify terms and coefficients in $-x^3 + 4x^2 + 7x - 2 $.  
**Solution:**  
- **Terms**: $-x^3 $, $ 4x^2 $, $ 7x $, and $-2 $.  
- **Coefficients**:  
  - Coefficient of $ x^3 $: $-1 $  
  - Coefficient of $ x^2 $: $ 4 $  
  - Coefficient of $ x $: $ 7 $  
  - Constant term: $-2 $  

### **Example 2:** Determine if $ 3y^2 + 5y^{-1} $ is a polynomial.  
**Solution:**  
- The term $ 5y^{-1} $ has an exponent of $-1 $, which is **not a non-negative integer**.  
- Therefore, **this is not a polynomial**.  

### **Example 3:** Find the degree of $ 2x^5 - 3x^3 + 4 $.  
**Solution:**  
- The highest power of $ x $ is $ 5 $.  
- **Degree**: $ 5 $.  

### **Example 4:** Classify $ 7 $ as a polynomial.  
**Solution:**  
- $ 7 $ can be written as $ 7x^0 $, where the exponent is $ 0 $ (non-negative).  
- **Classification**: A constant polynomial of degree $ 0 $.  

### **Example 5:** Simplify $ 2x + 3x - 4x $ and state its degree.  
**Solution:**  
- Combine like terms: $ (2 + 3 - 4)x = 1x $.  
- Simplified form: $ x $.  
- **Degree**: $ 1 $.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
1. Identify the terms and coefficients in $ 4z^2 - 7z + 1 $.  
2. Is $ x^2 + 3x + 2 $ a polynomial? Justify your answer.  

### 🟡 Medium  
3. Find the degree of $ 5m^4 - 2m^3 + m - 8 $.  
4. Write the polynomial $ 3p + 5p^2 - 2 $ in standard form.  

### 🔴 Hard  
5. Determine whether $ x^{-1} + 2x + 3 $ is a polynomial. Explain why or why not.  

---

### Answers:  

1. **Terms**: $ 4z^2 $, $-7z $, $ 1 $.  
   **Coefficients**: $ 4 $, $-7 $, $ 1 $.  

2. Yes, because all exponents of $ x $ are non-negative integers.  

3. **Degree**: $ 4 $.  

4. Standard form arranges terms from highest to lowest degree:  
   $ 5x^2 + 3x - 2 $.  

5. No, because $ x^{-1} $ has an exponent of $-1 $, which is not allowed in polynomials.  

---

## 6. Summary  
- A **polynomial in one variable** is an expression with non-negative integer exponents.  
- **Terms** are parts of a polynomial separated by $+$ or $-$; **coefficients** are numerical factors.  
- The **degree** is the highest exponent of the variable.  
- Constants like $ 5 $ are polynomials of degree $ 0 $.  
- Examples: $ x^2 $, $ 3y^2 + 5y $, $ t^2 + 4 $.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| General form: $ a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_0 $ | A polynomial in one variable $ x $. |  
| Degree: Highest power of $ x $ in the polynomial | Determines the complexity of the polynomial. |  
| Coefficient of $ x^k $: Numerical factor of the term $ a_k x^k $ | Includes constants (e.g., $-2 $ is the coefficient of $ x^0 $). |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing **constants** (fixed values) with **variables** (changing values).  
  ✅ Correction: Constants remain unchanged in a problem, while variables vary.  
- ❌ Misidentifying the **degree** of a polynomial.  
  ✅ Fix: Always look for the **highest exponent** of the variable.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Focus on definitions**: Clearly distinguish between terms, coefficients, and degrees.  
- **Practice simplification**: Combine like terms to simplify expressions before analyzing them.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Powers are Positive, Exponents are Nice!"**  
(Remember: Polynomials only allow **positive** (including zero) exponents.)
