## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever struggled to find someone’s house just by knowing the street number? Or tried to pinpoint a dot on a paper without precise measurements? This chapter explores how to describe positions using **two reference points**, forming the foundation of **coordinate geometry**. You’ll learn how to use **horizontal and vertical lines** to uniquely identify locations, setting the stage for understanding the Cartesian system.  

---  

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Defining Coordinates  
To locate a point accurately, we need **two independent pieces of information**. These are typically distances measured along **two perpendicular lines** (like a grid).  

> **Definition**: A **coordinate** is a pair of numbers $(x, y) $ that specifies the position of a point relative to two perpendicular lines (axes).  
>  
> **Key Terms**:  
> - **Axes**: The horizontal (**x-axis**) and vertical (**y-axis**) lines used to measure positions.  
> - **Origin**: The point where the axes intersect, denoted as $ (0, 0) $.  
> - **Quadrants**: The four regions formed by the axes.  

### 3.2 Real-World Application  
Consider the example of a house located on **Street 2** and **House 5** (Fig. 3.1):  
- The **first number (2)** corresponds to the **x-coordinate** (horizontal position).  
- The **second number (5)** corresponds to the **y-coordinate** (vertical position).  
Thus, the house’s location is represented as $ (2, 5) $.  

Similarly, a dot placed **5 cm from the left edge** and **9 cm from the bottom** (Fig. 3.2) has coordinates $ (5, 9) $.  

### 3.3 Classroom Activity: Seating Plan  
This activity involves mapping students’ positions in a classroom:  
1. Draw a grid representing desks.  
2. Label each desk with a student’s name.  
3. Use coordinates to identify each student’s seat (e.g., Desk 3, Row 2 → $ (3, 2) $).  

This mirrors how coordinates work in real-life scenarios.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Locating a Point on a Grid  
**Problem**: A library book is stored on Shelf 4 and Rack 7. What are its coordinates?  
**Solution**:  
- The shelf number (4) corresponds to the **x-coordinate**.  
- The rack number (7) corresponds to the **y-coordinate**.  
Thus, the book’s location is $ (4, 7) $.  

### Example 2: Describing a Dot’s Position  
**Problem**: A dot is 3 units right of the origin and 5 units up. What are its coordinates?  
**Solution**:  
- Moving **3 units right** means $ x = 3 $.  
- Moving **5 units up** means $ y = 5 $.  
Therefore, the dot’s coordinates are $ (3, 5) $.  

### Example 3: Interpreting a Map  
**Problem**: On a map, Park A is 2 km east and 1 km north of the city center. What are its coordinates?  
**Solution**:  
- **East** corresponds to the **x-direction**, so $ x = 2 $.  
- **North** corresponds to the **y-direction**, so $ y = 1 $.  
Hence, Park A is located at $ (2, 1) $.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**: A car park has 5 rows and 8 columns. If a car is parked in Row 3, Column 6, what are its coordinates?  
**Answer**:  
- Row number = $ x = 3 $  
- Column number = $ y = 6 $  
Coordinates: $ (3, 6) $  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2**: A bird flies 7 meters west and 4 meters south from the treehouse. What are its coordinates?  
**Answer**:  
- West = negative $ x $-direction → $ x = -7 $  
- South = negative $ y $-direction → $ y = -4 $  
Coordinates: $ (-7, -4) $  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3**: A treasure chest is buried 10 feet from the oak tree (eastward) and 6 feet below ground level (southward). What are its coordinates?  
**Answer**:  
- East = positive $ x $-direction → $ x = 10 $  
- Below ground = negative $ y $-direction → $ y = -6 $  
Coordinates: $ (10, -6) $  

---  

## 6. Summary  
- To locate a point, **two reference points** (distances) are needed.  
- The **x-coordinate** measures horizontal position; the **y-coordinate** measures vertical position.  
- The **origin** $ (0, 0) $ is the starting point of the coordinate system.  
- Coordinates help map positions in grids, maps, and real-world scenarios.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ (x, y) $ | Coordinates of a point |  
| $ (0, 0) $ | Origin (intersection of axes) |  
| Quadrant I | $ x > 0 $, $ y > 0 $ |  
| Quadrant II | $ x < 0 $, $ y > 0 $ |  
| Quadrant III | $ x < 0 $, $ y < 0 $ |  
| Quadrant IV | $ x > 0 $, $ y < 0 $ |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mixing up x and y coordinates**: Always remember $ x $ comes first (horizontal), followed by $ y $ (vertical).  
- **Ignoring negative values**: Directions like "west" or "south" correspond to negative coordinates.  

### Memory Aid  
Think of the coordinate plane as a **grid**:  
- **Rows** = $ x $-coordinates (left-right).  
- **Columns** = $ y $-coordinates (up-down).  

### Exam Tip  
When solving problems, sketch a rough diagram to visualize the position before assigning coordinates.
