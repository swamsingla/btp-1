## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition  
> **Definition**: A *matrix* is an ordered rectangular array of numbers or functions. The individual elements within the matrix are referred to as its **entries**.  

Matrices are typically denoted by capital letters (e.g., $ A $, $ B $), and their entries are organized into **rows** (horizontal arrangements) and **columns** (vertical arrangements). For instance, consider the matrix:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 \\ 
3 & 4 
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
Here, the matrix $ A $ has **2 rows** and **2 columns**. Each entry is identified by its position, such as $ a_{ij} $, where $ i $ denotes the row and $ j $ denotes the column.  

### 3.2 Order of a Matrix  
> **Definition**: The **order** (or dimension) of a matrix is defined as the number of rows multiplied by the number of columns. It is written as $ m \times n $, where $ m $ is the number of rows and $ n $ is the number of columns.  

#### Example:  
The matrix

$$
B = \begin{bmatrix} 
5 & -1 & 0 \\ 
2 & 3 & 4 
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
has **2 rows** and **3 columns**, so its order is $ 2 \times 3 $.  

#### Key Vocabulary:  
- **Row**: A horizontal arrangement of entries.  
- **Column**: A vertical arrangement of entries.  
- **Element/Entry**: An individual value within the matrix.  

### 3.3 Representation Through Real-Life Scenarios  
Consider the scenario involving Radha, Fauzia, and Simran’s notebook and pen counts:

$$
\text{Notebooks: } \begin{bmatrix} 15 & 10 & 8 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{Pens: } \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}

$$  
This can be represented as a single matrix:

$$
M = \begin{bmatrix} 
15 & 10 & 8 \\ 
6 & 2 & 5 
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
Here, the **first row** represents notebook counts, and the **second row** represents pen counts. The **columns** correspond to Radha, Fauzia, and Simran.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Determine the order of the matrix:

$$
C = \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 3 \\ 
4 & 5 & 6 
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
**Solution:**  
- Count the **rows**: There are 2 rows.  
- Count the **columns**: There are 3 columns.  
Thus, the order of matrix $ C $ is $ 2 \times 3 $.  

---

### Example 2:  
Identify the entries $ a_{12} $, $ b_{23} $, and $ c_{31} $ in the following matrices:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 
7 & -3 \\ 
0 & 9 
\end{bmatrix}, \quad 
B = \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 4 \\ 
5 & 6 & 7 \\ 
8 & 9 & 10 
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
**Solution:**  
- For matrix $ A $:  
  - $ a_{12} $ refers to the entry in the 1st row and 2nd column: $ -3 $.  
- For matrix $ B $:  
  - $ b_{23} $ refers to the entry in the 2nd row and 3rd column: $ 7 $.  
  - $ c_{31} $ does not exist because matrix $ B $ has only 3 rows and 3 columns; the index $ c_{31} $ would refer to the 3rd row and 1st column, which is $ 8 $.  

---

### Example 3:  
Construct a matrix representing the following data:  
- Students: Alice, Bob, Charlie  
- Subjects: Math, Science, English  
- Scores:  
  - Alice: 85 (Math), 90 (Science), 88 (English)  
  - Bob: 78 (Math), 82 (Science), 80 (English)  
  - Charlie: 92 (Math), 95 (Science), 93 (English)  

**Solution:**  
Arrange the data into a matrix where rows represent students and columns represent subjects:

$$
S = \begin{bmatrix} 
85 & 90 & 88 \\ 
78 & 82 & 80 \\ 
92 & 95 & 93 
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
This matrix has **3 rows** and **3 columns**, so its order is $ 3 \times 3 $.  

---

### Example 4:  
Explain why the matrix $ D = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} $ is invalid.  
**Solution:**  
- The matrix has **3 rows** and **2 columns**.  
- A valid matrix must have consistent rows and columns across all entries.  
- Since there are 3 rows but only 2 columns, the matrix is **not rectangular** and thus **invalid**.  

---

### Example 5:  
Compare the matrices $ E = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} $ and $ F = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 3 \end{bmatrix} $. Are they equal?  
**Solution:**  
- Both matrices have the same order $ 2 \times 2 $.  
- However, the entries differ: $ e_{22} = 4 $ vs. $ f_{22} = 3 $.  
- **Conclusion**: Matrices $ E $ and $ F $ are **not equal**.

## 6. Summary  
- **Matrix**: An ordered rectangular array of numbers or functions.  
- **Order**: Determined by the number of rows $ m $ and columns $ n $, written as $ m \times n $.  
- **Rows and Columns**: Rows are horizontal, columns are vertical.  
- **Entries**: Individual elements identified by $ a_{ij} $, where $ i $ is the row and $ j $ is the column.  
- **Real-Life Application**: Matrices can model data like inventory counts or employee salaries.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Order of matrix } A = m \times n $ | Where $ m $ = number of rows, $ n $ = number of columns |  
| $ a_{ij} $ | Entry in the $ i^{th} $ row and $ j^{th} $ column |  
| $ \text{Valid matrix condition} $ | Must have consistent rows and columns across all entries |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Confusing rows and columns**: Always remember rows are horizontal, columns are vertical.  
   - **Correction**: Label rows as top-to-bottom and columns as left-to-right.  
2. **Incorrect order notation**: Writing $ 2 \times 3 $ instead of $ 3 \times 2 $ for a matrix with 3 rows and 2 columns.  
   - **Correction**: The order is always $ \text{rows} \times \text{columns} $.  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice constructing matrices from real-life data to reinforce understanding of rows and columns.  
- Verify the consistency of rows and columns before labeling a matrix as valid.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Row Left, Column Right"**:  
- **Row**: Left to right (horizontal).  
- **Column**: Top to bottom (vertical).
