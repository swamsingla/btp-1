## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Order  
> **The order of a matrix** refers to the number of rows and columns it contains. A matrix with $ m $ rows and $ n $ columns is denoted as an $ m \times n $ matrix (read as “m by n”).  

For example:  
- Matrix $ A $ with 3 rows and 2 columns is a **3 × 2 matrix**.  
- Matrix $ B $ with 3 rows and 3 columns is a **3 × 3 matrix**.  
- Matrix $ C $ with 2 rows and 3 columns is a **2 × 3 matrix**.  

### 3.2 Notation and Elements  
An $ m \times n $ matrix is generally written as:

$$
A = [a_{ij}]_{m \times n}, \quad \text{where } 1 \leq i \leq m, \ 1 \leq j \leq n

$$  
- $ a_{ij} $ denotes the element in the **i-th row** and **j-th column**.  
- The **total number of elements** in an $ m \times n $ matrix is $ m \times n $.  

### 3.3 Practical Applications  
Matrices can represent real-world data or geometric figures:  
- **Points in a plane**: A point $ (x, y) $ can be expressed as a **column matrix** $ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} $ or a **row matrix** $ [x, y] $.  
- **Geometric shapes**: Vertices of polygons (e.g., quadrilaterals) can be encoded in matrices. For instance, a quadrilateral with vertices $ A(1, 0), B(3, 2), C(1, 3), D(-1, 2) $ is represented as:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\
0 & 2 & 3 & 2
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
Each column corresponds to a vertex’s coordinates.  

### 3.4 Key Properties  
- **Row vs. Column**: Rows run horizontally, while columns run vertically.  
- **Dimensionality**: The order dictates how matrices interact with other matrices (e.g., multiplication requires matching inner dimensions).  
- **Uniqueness**: The order uniquely identifies a matrix’s structure.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Representing Data in Matrix Form  
**Problem**: Convert the following data into a 3 × 2 matrix:  
| Factory | Men Workers | Women Workers |  
|---------|-------------|---------------|  
| I       | 100         | 80            |  
| II      | 120         | 90            |  
| III     | 150         | 110           |  

**Solution**:  
Arrange the data such that rows correspond to factories and columns to categories (men/women):

$$
A = 
\begin{bmatrix}
100 & 80 \\
120 & 90 \\
150 & 110
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
The entry in the **third row and second column** (i.e., $ a_{32} $) represents **110 women workers in factory III**.  

### Example 2: Determining Possible Orders  
**Problem**: A matrix has 8 elements. What are its possible orders?  

**Solution**:  
If a matrix has $ m \times n $ elements, then $ m \times n = 8 $. Find all pairs of natural numbers $ (m, n) $ such that their product is 8:  
- $ 1 \times 8 $  
- $ 2 \times 4 $  
- $ 4 \times 2 $  
- $ 8 \times 1 $  

Thus, the possible orders are **1 × 8, 2 × 4, 4 × 2, and 8 × 1**.  

### Example 3: Interpreting Matrix Dimensions  
**Problem**: Given matrix $ B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 7 & 3 \end{bmatrix} $, identify its order and total elements.  

**Solution**:  
- **Order**: 2 rows × 2 columns → **2 × 2 matrix**.  
- **Total elements**: $ 2 \times 2 = 4 $.  

### Example 4: Constructing a Matrix  
**Problem**: Create a **3 × 3 matrix** where each element $ a_{ij} = i + j $.  

**Solution**:  
Compute each element:  
- Row 1: $ a_{11} = 1+1 = 2 $, $ a_{12} = 1+2 = 3 $, $ a_{13} = 1+3 = 4 $  
- Row 2: $ a_{21} = 2+1 = 3 $, $ a_{22} = 2+2 = 4 $, $ a_{23} = 2+3 = 5 $  
- Row 3: $ a_{31} = 3+1 = 4 $, $ a_{32} = 3+2 = 5 $, $ a_{33} = 3+3 = 6 $  

Result:

$$
B = 
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 3 & 4 \\
3 & 4 & 5 \\
4 & 5 & 6
\end{bmatrix}

$$

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**:  
1. Identify the order of the matrix $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} $.  
2. How many elements are in a **4 × 5 matrix**?  

🟡 **Medium**:  
3. A matrix has 12 elements. List all possible orders.  
4. Construct a **2 × 3 matrix** where $ a_{ij} = 2i - j $.  

🔴 **Hard**:  
5. Prove that the number of elements in an $ m \times n $ matrix is always $ m \times n $.  

---  

**Answers**:  

1. **Answer**: The matrix is **2 × 2**.  
2. **Answer**: Total elements = $ 4 \times 5 = 20 $.  
3. **Answer**: Possible orders: $ 1 \times 12, 2 \times 6, 3 \times 4, 4 \times 3, 6 \times 2, 12 \times 1 $.  
4. **Answer**:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
2(1)-1 & 2(1)-2 & 2(1)-3 \\
2(2)-1 & 2(2)-2 & 2(2)-3
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & -1 \\
3 & 2 & 1
\end{bmatrix}

$$  
5. **Answer**:  
By definition, an $ m \times n $ matrix has $ m $ rows and $ n $ columns. Each row contains $ n $ elements, so total elements = $ m \times n $. Hence, proved.

## 6. Summary  
- **Order of a matrix**: Denotes the number of rows $ m $ and columns $ n $, written as $ m \times n $.  
- **Elements**: An $ m \times n $ matrix has $ m \times n $ elements.  
- **Notation**: $ A = [a_{ij}]_{m \times n} $ means the element at position $ (i, j) $ is $ a_{ij} $.  
- **Applications**: Matrices can represent points, data tables, and geometric figures.  
- **Key Rule**: To multiply matrices, the number of columns in the first matrix must match the number of rows in the second.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|------------|  
| $ \text{Order} = m \times n $ | Defines the size of a matrix with $ m $ rows and $ n $ columns. |  
| $ \text{Number of elements} = m \times n $ | Total elements in an $ m \times n $ matrix. |  
| $ a_{ij} $ | Element located at the $ i $-th row and $ j $-th column. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing rows and columns: Always recall that the first number in the order is the **number of rows**.  
- ❌ Miscounting elements: Double-check $ m \times n $ calculations for accuracy.  

### Exam Tips  
- ✔️ When unsure about a matrix’s order, count the rows and columns explicitly.  
- ✔️ For application-based questions, visualize the matrix as a grid to avoid mix-ups.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Rows First, Columns Second"** – Think of the order $ m \times n $ as "rows first, then columns" (like arranging books in shelves: rows = shelves, columns = books per shelf).
