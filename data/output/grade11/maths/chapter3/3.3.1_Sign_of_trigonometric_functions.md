## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered why certain trigonometric functions behave differently depending on the direction you rotate from the positive x-axis? This topic explores how the **sign of trigonometric functions** changes based on the **quadrant** in which an angle lies. By analyzing the coordinates of points on the unit circle, we can derive consistent patterns for the signs of sine, cosine, tangent, and their reciprocals across four quadrants.  

This section will teach you:  
- How to determine the sign of trigonometric functions for any given angle.  
- The relationship between quadrant location and function behavior.  
- A systematic way to remember the signs of all six trigonometric functions in each quadrant.  

By mastering this, you’ll gain a deeper intuition for periodicity and symmetry in trigonometry, which is essential for solving equations and analyzing waveforms in higher-level mathematics.  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Unit Circle and Coordinate Representation  
Consider a unit circle centered at the origin with radius $ r = 1 $. Let $ P(a, b) $ be a point on the circle corresponding to an angle $ x $, where $ \angle AOP = x $. The coordinates of $ P $ satisfy $ a = \cos x $ and $ b = \sin x $.  

If the angle is reflected over the x-axis to form $ -x $, the point $ Q $ becomes $ (a, -b) $, implying:

$$
\cos(-x) = \cos x \quad \text{(even function)}

$$

$$
\sin(-x) = -\sin x \quad \text{(odd function)}

$$  
This establishes the **even-odd properties** of cosine and sine, respectively.  

> **Definition**: A function $ f(x) $ is **even** if $ f(-x) = f(x) $ for all $ x $, and **odd** if $ f(-x) = -f(x) $.  

---

### 3.2 Quadrant Analysis and Function Signs  
The unit circle divides into four quadrants, each defined by the signs of $ a $ (cosine) and $ b $ (sine):  

| Quadrant | Angle Range         | $ \cos x $ (a) | $ \sin x $ (b) |  
|----------|---------------------|------------------|------------------|  
| I        | $ 0 < x < \frac{\pi}{2} $ | +                | +                |  
| II       | $ \frac{\pi}{2} < x < \pi $ | -               | +                |  
| III      | $ \pi < x < \frac{3\pi}{2} $ | -              | -                |  
| IV       | $ \frac{3\pi}{2} < x < 2\pi $ | +             | -                |  

From this, we deduce:  
- **Sine** is positive in Quadrants I and II, negative in III and IV.  
- **Cosine** is positive in I and IV, negative in II and III.  
- **Tangent** ($ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $) inherits the sign of the numerator divided by the denominator.  

> **Theorem**: The sign of a trigonometric function depends solely on the quadrant in which the terminal side of the angle lies.  

---

### 3.3 Sign Chart for Trigonometric Functions  
Using the quadrant analysis, we construct the following sign chart:  

| Quadrant | $ \sin x $ | $ \cos x $ | $ \tan x $ | $ \csc x $ | $ \sec x $ | $ \cot x $ |  
|----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|  
| I        | +           | +           | +           | +           | +           | +           |  
| II       | +           | -           | -           | +           | -           | -           |  
| III      | -           | -           | +           | -           | -           | +           |  
| IV       | -           | +           | -           | -           | +           | -           |  

**Proof**:  
- $ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $: The sign of tangent is determined by dividing the signs of sine and cosine.  
- $ \csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x} $, $ \sec x = \frac{1}{\cos x} $, and $ \cot x = \frac{1}{\tan x} $: These reciprocal functions inherit the sign of their respective bases.  

---

### 3.4 Special Cases and Symmetry  
- **Odd Multiples of $ \frac{\pi}{2} $**: At $ x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2} $, sine reaches maximum/minimum values while cosine is zero, leading to undefined tangents and secants.  
- **Periodicity**: Trigonometric functions repeat every $ 2\pi $, but their **signs** depend only on the **reference angle** within the appropriate quadrant.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Determine the sign of $ \sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right) $.**  
**Solution:**  
- $ \frac{5\pi}{6} $ lies in **Quadrant II** ($ \frac{\pi}{2} < \frac{5\pi}{6} < \pi $).  
- From the sign chart, $ \sin x $ is **positive** in Quadrant II.  
**Answer:** Positive.  

### Example 2:  
**Find the sign of $ \cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{4}\right) $.**  
**Solution:**  
- $ \frac{7\pi}{4} $ is equivalent to $ -\frac{\pi}{4} $, lying in **Quadrant IV**.  
- Cosine is **positive** in Quadrant IV.  
**Answer:** Positive.  

### Example 3:  
**Evaluate $ \tan\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\right) $.**  
**Solution:**  
- $ \frac{3\pi}{2} $ is on the **negative y-axis**, where cosine is 0 and sine is -1.  
- $ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $, which is undefined due to division by zero.  
**Answer:** Undefined.  

### Example 4:  
**Determine the sign of $ \cot\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) $.**  
**Solution:**  
- $ \frac{2\pi}{3} $ lies in **Quadrant II**.  
- Cotangent ($ \cot x = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} $) has a **negative** sign in Quadrant II because cosine is negative and sine is positive.  
**Answer:** Negative.  

### Example 5:  
**Which trigonometric functions are positive in Quadrant III?**  
**Solution:**  
- From the sign chart, **tangent** and **cotangent** are positive in Quadrant III.  
**Answer:** Tangent and cotangent.

## 6. Summary  
- **Trigonometric functions change signs based on the quadrant** of the angle.  
- **Sine** is positive in Quadrants I and II, negative in III and IV.  
- **Cosine** is positive in I and IV, negative in II and III.  
- **Tangent** is positive in I and III, negative in II and IV.  
- **Reciprocal functions** (cosecant, secant, cotangent) inherit the signs of their respective base functions.  
- The unit circle provides a geometric foundation for analyzing these sign patterns.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|------------|  
| $ \cos(-x) = \cos x $ | Cosine is an even function |  
| $ \sin(-x) = -\sin x $ | Sine is an odd function |  
| $ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $ | Tangent identity |  
| $ \csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x} $ | Cosecant as reciprocal of sine |  
| $ \sec x = \frac{1}{\cos x} $ | Secant as reciprocal of cosine |  
| $ \cot x = \frac{1}{\tan x} $ | Cotangent as reciprocal of tangent |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Confusing Quadrants**: Mistaking Quadrant III for having both sine and cosine positive.  
   **Correction**: Quadrant III has both sine and cosine negative.  
2. **Ignoring Reciprocals**: Assuming cosecant behaves like sine instead of mirroring its sign.  
   **Correction**: Cosecant’s sign matches sine’s sign.  

### Exam Tips  
- Memorize the sign chart using the phrase **“All Students Take Calculus”** (All: I, Students: II, Take: III, Calculus: IV).  
- Always sketch the unit circle for complex angles to visualize quadrant placement.  

### Memory Aid  
Use the acronym **ASTC** (All Students Take Calculus) to recall the signs:  
- **A**ll (positive) in Quadrant I  
- **S**tudents (positive) in Quadrant II  
- **T**ake (positive) in Quadrant III  
- **C**alculus (positive) in Quadrant IV
