## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how to calculate the sine or cosine of an angle formed by combining two smaller angles? This topic explores exactly that! By leveraging the unit circle and geometric relationships, we derive formulas for $\cos(x \pm y) $, $\sin(x \pm y) $, and related identities. These formulas are essential for simplifying trigonometric expressions and solving real-world problems involving compound angles. Building on your existing knowledge of trigonometric functions, you’ll now learn how to break down complex angles into simpler components.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Fundamental Definitions  
**Trigonometric Function**: A function relating the ratios of sides in a right triangle or coordinates on the unit circle.  
**Unit Circle**: A circle with radius 1 centered at the origin, where angles correspond to coordinates $ (\cos \theta, \sin \theta) $.  

> **Definition**: The **sum and difference formulas** express $\cos(x \pm y) $ and $\sin(x \pm y) $ in terms of $\cos x $, $\cos y $, $\sin x $, and $\sin y $.  

### 3.2 Cosine Addition and Subtraction  
> **Theorem**:

$$
\cos(x + y) = \cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y \quad \text{(1)}

$$

$$
\cos(x - y) = \cos x \cos y + \sin x \sin y \quad \text{(2)}

$$  

**Proof (Cosine Addition)**:  
Consider the unit circle with points $P_1(\cos x, \sin x) $, $ P_2(\cos(x + y), \sin(x + y)) $, and $ P_3(\cos(-y), \sin(-y)) $. Using the distance formula, equate distances $ P_1P_3 $ and $ P_2P_4 $:

$$
[\cos x - \cos(-y)]^2 + [\sin x - \sin(-y)]^2 = [1 - \cos(x + y)]^2 + [0 - \sin(x + y)]^2

$$  
Expanding and simplifying yields:

$$
2 - 2(\cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y) = 2 - 2\cos(x + y)

$$  
Thus, $\cos(x + y) = \cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y $.  

> **Corollary**: Replacing $ y $ with $-y $ in Equation (1) gives Equation (2):

$$
\cos(x - y) = \cos x \cos y + \sin x \sin y

$$  

### 3.3 Sine Addition and Subtraction  
> **Theorem**:

$$
\sin(x + y) = \sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y \quad \text{(3)}

$$

$$
\sin(x - y) = \sin x \cos y - \cos x \sin y \quad \text{(4)}

$$  

**Proof (Sine Addition)**:  
Use the identity $\sin \theta = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) $. Substitute $\theta = x + y $:

$$
\sin(x + y) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - (x + y)\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x - y\right)

$$  
Apply the cosine subtraction formula (Equation 2):

$$
\cos\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) - y\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right)\cos y + \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right)\sin y

$$  
Using $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \sin x $ and $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos x $, this becomes:

$$
\sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y

$$  

### 3.4 Co-Function Identities  
> **Theorem**:

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \sin x \quad \text{(5)}

$$

$$
\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos x \quad \text{(6)}

$$  

**Proof (Identity 5)**: Replace $x $ with $\frac{\pi}{2} $ and $ y $ with $ x $ in Equation (2):

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\cos x + \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\sin x

$$  
Since $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 0 $ and $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1 $, this simplifies to $\sin x $.  

### 3.5 Special Angle Identities  
From Equations (1)-(6), additional identities emerge:  
- $\cos\left(x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = -\sin x $  
- $\sin\left(x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \cos x $  
- $\cos(\pi - x) = -\cos x $  
- $\sin(\pi - x) = \sin x $  
- $\cos(\pi + x) = -\cos x $  
- $\sin(\pi + x) = -\sin x $  

These identities help simplify expressions involving shifts by $\frac{\pi}{2} $ or $\pi $.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Problem**: Find $\cos(75^\circ) $ using the cosine addition formula.  
**Solution**:  
Express $ 75^\circ $ as $ 45^\circ + 30^\circ $:

$$
\cos(75^\circ) = \cos(45^\circ + 30^\circ) = \cos 45^\circ \cos 30^\circ - \sin 45^\circ \sin 30^\circ

$$  
Substitute known values:

$$
\cos 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad \cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \sin 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}

$$

$$
\cos(75^\circ) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}

$$  

### Example 2:  
**Problem**: Simplify $\sin(15^\circ) $ using the sine subtraction formula.  
**Solution**:  
Express $ 15^\circ $ as $ 45^\circ - 30^\circ $:

$$
\sin(15^\circ) = \sin(45^\circ - 30^\circ) = \sin 45^\circ \cos 30^\circ - \cos 45^\circ \sin 30^\circ

$$  
Substitute values:

$$
\sin(15^\circ) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}

$$  

### Example 3:  
**Problem**: Prove $\cos(\pi - x) = -\cos x $.  
**Solution**:  
Use the cosine subtraction formula (Equation 2):

$$
\cos(\pi - x) = \cos \pi \cos x + \sin \pi \sin x

$$  
Substitute $\cos \pi = -1 $ and $\sin \pi = 0 $:

$$
\cos(\pi - x) = (-1)(\cos x) + (0)(\sin x) = -\cos x

$$  

### Example 4:  
**Problem**: Calculate $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) $ using the sine subtraction formula.  
**Solution**:  
Express $\frac{\pi}{12} $ as $\frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{4} $:

$$
\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \sin \frac{\pi}{3} \cos \frac{\pi}{4} - \cos \frac{\pi}{3} \sin \frac{\pi}{4}

$$  
Substitute values:

$$
\sin \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \cos \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad \cos \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \sin \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}

$$

$$
\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}

$$  

### Example 5:  
**Problem**: Verify $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \sin x $.  
**Solution**:  
Use the identity $\cos(a - b) = \cos a \cos b + \sin a \sin b $:

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos \frac{\pi}{2} \cos x + \sin \frac{\pi}{2} \sin x

$$  
Substitute $\cos \frac{\pi}{2} = 0 $ and $\sin \frac{\pi}{2} = 1 $:

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = 0 \cdot \cos x + 1 \cdot \sin x = \sin x

$$

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**: Compute $\cos(15^\circ) $ using the cosine subtraction formula.  
**Problem 2**: Simplify $\sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{12}\right) $ using the sine addition formula.  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 3**: Prove $\sin(\pi + x) = -\sin x $ using the sine addition formula.  
**Problem 4**: Find $\cos(105^\circ) $ using the cosine addition formula.  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 5**: Derive $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + x\right) = -\sin x $ using identities (5) and (6).  

**Answers**:  

#### Problem 1:

$$
\cos(15^\circ) = \cos(45^\circ - 30^\circ) = \cos 45^\circ \cos 30^\circ + \sin 45^\circ \sin 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}

$$  

#### Problem 2:

$$
\sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{12}\right) = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \sin \frac{\pi}{3} \cos \frac{\pi}{4} + \cos \frac{\pi}{3} \sin \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}

$$  

#### Problem 3:

$$
\sin(\pi + x) = \sin \pi \cos x + \cos \pi \sin x = 0 \cdot \cos x + (-1) \cdot \sin x = -\sin x

$$  

#### Problem 4:

$$
\cos(105^\circ) = \cos(60^\circ + 45^\circ) = \cos 60^\circ \cos 45^\circ - \sin 60^\circ \sin 45^\circ = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}(1 - \sqrt{3})}{4}

$$  

#### Problem 5:  
Using identity (6): $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + x\right) = \cos x $. But $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + x\right) = -\sin x $ via the identity $\cos(a + b) = \cos a \cos b - \sin a \sin b $:

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + x\right) = \cos \frac{\pi}{2} \cos x - \sin \frac{\pi}{2} \sin x = 0 \cdot \cos x - 1 \cdot \sin x = -\sin x

$$

## 6. Summary  
- The **cosine addition formula** is $\cos(x + y) = \cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y $.  
- The **sine addition formula** is $\sin(x + y) = \sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y $.  
- **Co-function identities**: $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \sin x $, $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos x $.  
- **Special identities**: $\cos(\pi - x) = -\cos x $, $\sin(\pi + x) = -\sin x $.  
- These formulas are derived using geometric proofs and algebraic manipulation.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing $\cos(x + y) $ and $\sin(x + y) $ signs.  
  ✅ Remember: $\cos(x + y) $ has a minus sign; $\sin(x + y) $ has a plus sign.  
- ❌ Mixing up degrees and radians in calculations.  
  ✅ Always confirm whether angles are in degrees or radians.  

### Exam Tips  
- 🔍 Memorize the core formulas (1)-(4) and their proofs.  
- 📝 Practice applying identities to special angles like $\frac{\pi}{6} $, $\frac{\pi}{4} $, and $\frac{\pi}{3} $.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Cosine Plus, Sine Minus"**:  
- $\cos(x + y) $: $\cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y $  
- $\sin(x + y) $: $\sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y $
