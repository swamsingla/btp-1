In this topic, we explore **how angles are measured** and the **units used to quantify them**—degrees and radians. By the end of this section, you’ll understand:

This builds a foundation for later topics like trigonometric functions and their periodic behavior.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of an Angle  
> An **angle** is formed by rotating a **ray** about its **vertex**, resulting in an **initial side** (starting position) and a **terminal side** (final position). The **direction of rotation** determines whether the angle is **positive** (counterclockwise) or **negative** (clockwise).  

#### Key Terms:  
- **Initial Side**: The starting ray of the angle.  
- **Terminal Side**: The final position of the ray after rotation.  
- **Vertex**: The common endpoint of the initial and terminal sides.  
- **Positive/Negative Angles**: Positive angles are measured counterclockwise; negative angles are measured clockwise.  

### 3.2 Degree Measure  
> A **degree measure** quantifies angles based on fractions of a full circle. One complete revolution corresponds to $ 360^\circ $. Thus, a quarter turn is $ 90^\circ $, half a turn is $ 180^\circ $, and three-quarters is $ 270^\circ $.  

#### Example Conversion:  
- $ 1^\circ = \frac{1}{360} $ of a full circle.  
- $ 360^\circ = 1 $ full rotation.  

### 3.3 Radian Measure  
> A **radian measure** defines an angle as the ratio of the **arc length** ($ s $) to the **radius** ($ r $):

$$
\theta = \frac{s}{r}

$$  
One **radian** is the angle subtended at the center of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius. A full circle contains $ 2\pi $ radians.  

#### Key Relationship:

$$
\text{Full Circle} = 2\pi \text{ radians} = 360^\circ

$$  
Thus, $ 1 \text{ radian} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} $ and $ 1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180} \text{ radians} $.  

### 3.4 Converting Between Degrees and Radians  
To convert between the two systems:  
- **Degrees to Radians**: Multiply by $ \frac{\pi}{180} $.  
- **Radians to Degrees**: Multiply by $ \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} $.  

#### Example:  
Convert $ 90^\circ $ to radians:

$$
90^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{\pi}{2} \text{ radians}

$$  

### 3.5 Mathematical Insights  
- **Radian measure** is preferred in calculus and higher mathematics due to its direct relationship with arc lengths and derivatives.  
- **Degree measure** remains practical in everyday applications (e.g., navigation, construction).

## 4. Solved Examples  

### **Example 1:**  
Convert $ 180^\circ $ to radians.  
**Solution:**  
Use the formula $ \theta_{\text{radians}} = \theta_{\text{degrees}} \times \frac{\pi}{180} $:

$$
180^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \pi \text{ radians}

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ \pi $ radians.  

### **Example 2:**  
Convert $ \frac{\pi}{3} $ radians to degrees.  
**Solution:**  
Use the formula $ \theta_{\text{degrees}} = \theta_{\text{radians}} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} $:

$$
\frac{\pi}{3} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 60^\circ

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ 60^\circ $.  

### **Example 3:**  
Find the angle in radians for a wheel completing 2.5 revolutions.  
**Solution:**  
One revolution = $ 2\pi $ radians.

$$
2.5 \times 2\pi = 5\pi \text{ radians}

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ 5\pi $ radians.  

### **Example 4:**  
Determine the sign of an angle rotated $ 30^\circ $ clockwise.  
**Solution:**  
Clockwise rotation corresponds to **negative** angles.  
✅ Final Answer: Negative angle ($ -30^\circ $).  

### **Example 5:**  
Calculate the angle in radians for a semicircle.  
**Solution:**  
A semicircle spans $ 180^\circ $, which converts to:

$$
180^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \pi \text{ radians}

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ \pi $ radians.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1:** Convert $ 90^\circ $ to radians.  
**Problem 2:** Convert $ \frac{\pi}{4} $ radians to degrees.  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 3:** A car tire rotates 4 times. Express the total angle in radians.  
**Problem 4:** Find the angle in degrees for $ \frac{3\pi}{2} $ radians.  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 5:** If a fan blade completes 1.5 revolutions, calculate the angle in radians and degrees.  

---

### Answers:  

**Problem 1:**

$$
90^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{\pi}{2} \text{ radians}

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ \frac{\pi}{2} $ radians.  

**Problem 2:**

$$
\frac{\pi}{4} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 45^\circ

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ 45^\circ $.  

**Problem 3:**

$$
4 \times 2\pi = 8\pi \text{ radians}

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ 8\pi $ radians.  

**Problem 4:**

$$
\frac{3\pi}{2} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 270^\circ

$$  
✅ Final Answer: $ 270^\circ $.  

**Problem 5:**  
- **Radians:** $ 1.5 \times 2\pi = 3\pi $ radians.  
- **Degrees:** $ 3\pi \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 540^\circ $.  
✅ Final Answers: $ 3\pi $ radians and $ 540^\circ $.

## 6. Summary  
- An **angle** is formed by rotating a ray about its vertex.  
- **Positive angles** are counterclockwise; **negative angles** are clockwise.  
- **Degree measure**: $ 360^\circ = 1 $ full rotation.  
- **Radian measure**: $ 2\pi $ radians = $ 360^\circ $.  
- Conversion formulas:

$$
  \theta_{\text{radians}} = \theta_{\text{degrees}} \times \frac{\pi}{180}, \quad \theta_{\text{degrees}} = \theta_{\text{radians}} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}

$$  
- Applications span physics, engineering, and daily life (e.g., clocks, wheels).

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \theta = \frac{s}{r} $ | Radian measure relates arc length $ s $ and radius $ r $. |  
| $ 360^\circ = 2\pi $ radians | Full circle equivalence. |  
| $ 1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180} $ radians | Conversion factor from degrees to radians. |  
| $ 1 $ radian $ = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} $ | Conversion factor from radians to degrees. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### **Common Mistakes**  
1. **Confusing degrees and radians**: Always check units in problems!  
   - ❌ Mistake: Assuming $ 180^\circ = \pi $ radians without verification.  
   - ✅ Correction: Recall $ 180^\circ = \pi $ radians via the conversion formula.  
2. **Ignoring direction**: Forgetting that clockwise angles are negative.  
   - ❌ Mistake: Labeling $ -90^\circ $ as positive.  
   - ✅ Correction: Apply the sign based on rotation direction.  

### **Exam Tips**  
- Memorize key conversions: $ \pi $ radians = $ 180^\circ $, $ \frac{\pi}{2} $ = $ 90^\circ $, etc.  
- Practice quick conversions during exams to save time.  

### **Memory Aid**  
**"PIE" Mnemonic**:  
- P = **P**i radians = $ 180^\circ $.  
- I = **I**ncrease by $ \frac{\pi}{2} $ for $ 90^\circ $.  
- E = **E**nd with $ \frac{\pi}{4} $ for $ 45^\circ $.
