## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how we measure the rotation of a clock hand or the tilt of a ladder? These questions lead us to the concept of **angle measurement**, which is fundamental in trigonometry. In this topic, we explore the **degree measure** system—a way to quantify angles using degrees, minutes, and seconds. By the end of this section, you’ll master how to express and convert angles in degrees, understand their subdivisions, and interpret standard angular values like $360^\circ $ or $ 180^\circ $. This builds a strong foundation for later topics such as **radian measure** and trigonometric functions.  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Degree Measure  
> **A degree** ($^\circ $) is a unit of angular measurement. One full rotation (a complete circle) is divided into **360 equal parts**, each part being **one degree** ($ 1^\circ $).  

> **Minutes** ($'$) and **seconds** ($''$) are further subdivisions of a degree:  
> - $ 1^\circ = 60'$ (one degree equals sixty minutes)  
> - $ 1' = 60''$ (one minute equals sixty seconds)  

This system allows precise measurement of small angular changes, much like how hours, minutes, and seconds divide time.  

### 3.2 Conversion Between Units  
> To convert degrees to minutes:  
>

$$ \text{Total minutes} = \text{Degrees} \times 60

$$  
> Example: $2^\circ = 2 \times 60 = 120'$  

> To convert minutes to seconds:  
>

$$ \text{Total seconds} = \text{Minutes} \times 60

$$  
> Example: $15' = 15 \times 60 = 900''$  

> To convert seconds to minutes:  
>

$$ \text{Total minutes} = \frac{\text{Seconds}}{60}

$$  
> Example: $180'' = \frac{180}{60} = 3'$  

### 3.3 Standard Angular Values  
The source text provides several examples of angles measured in degrees:  
- $ 360^\circ $: A full rotation (circle)  
- $ 180^\circ $: A straight line (semicircle)  
- $ 270^\circ $: Three-quarters of a circle  
- $ 420^\circ $: One full rotation plus $ 60^\circ $  
- $-30^\circ $: An angle rotated clockwise by $ 30^\circ $  
- $-420^\circ $: One full rotation clockwise plus $ 60^\circ $ clockwise  

These values illustrate how positive and negative angles represent counterclockwise and clockwise rotations, respectively.

## 4. Solved Examples  

**Example 1:** Convert $ 5^\circ $ to minutes.  
**Solution:**

$$
5^\circ = 5 \times 60' = 300'

$$  
**Answer:** $5^\circ = 300'$  

**Example 2:** Convert $ 45'$ to seconds.  
**Solution:**

$$
45' = 45 \times 60'' = 2700''

$$  
**Answer:** $45' = 2700''$  

**Example 3:** Express $ 3^\circ 45'$ in minutes.  
**Solution:**  
Convert $ 3^\circ $ to minutes:

$$
3^\circ = 3 \times 60' = 180'

$$  
Add remaining minutes:

$$
180' + 45' = 225'

$$  
**Answer:** $3^\circ 45' = 225'$  

**Example 4:** Convert $ 2^\circ 30' 15''$ to seconds.  
**Solution:**  
Convert degrees to minutes:

$$
2^\circ = 2 \times 60' = 120'

$$  
Convert total minutes to seconds:

$$
(120' + 30') \times 60'' = 150' \times 60'' = 9000''

$$  
Add remaining seconds:

$$
9000'' + 15'' = 9015''

$$  
**Answer:** $2^\circ 30' 15'' = 9015''$  

**Example 5:** Find the value of $-60^\circ $ in minutes.  
**Solution:**

$$
-60^\circ = -60 \times 60' = -3600'

$$  
**Answer:** $-60^\circ = -3600'$

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**: Convert $ 1^\circ $ to minutes.  
🟡 **Medium**: Express $ 4^\circ 15'$ in seconds.  
🔴 **Hard**: Convert $ 5^\circ 30' 45''$ to seconds.  

**Answers:**  

🟢 **Easy**:

$$
1^\circ = 1 \times 60' = 60'

$$  
**Answer:** $60'$  

🟡 **Medium**:  
Convert $ 4^\circ $ to minutes:

$$
4^\circ = 4 \times 60' = 240'

$$  
Add remaining minutes:

$$
240' + 15' = 255'

$$  
**Answer:** $255'$  

🔴 **Hard**:  
Convert degrees to minutes:

$$
5^\circ = 5 \times 60' = 300'

$$  
Convert total minutes to seconds:

$$
(300' + 30') \times 60'' = 330' \times 60'' = 19800''

$$  
Add remaining seconds:

$$
19800'' + 45'' = 19845''

$$  
**Answer:** $19845''$  

---

## 6. Summary  
- **1 degree** ($ 1^\circ $) equals $ 60 $ minutes ($ 60'$)  
- **1 minute** ($ 1'$) equals $ 60 $ seconds ($ 60''$)  
- Positive angles rotate counterclockwise; negative angles rotate clockwise  
- Conversions: $^\circ \leftrightarrow ' \leftrightarrow ''$ via multiplication/division by $ 60 $  
- Standard angles include $ 360^\circ $ (full circle), $ 180^\circ $ (straight line), etc.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ 1^\circ = 60'$ | Conversion between degrees and minutes |  
| $ 1' = 60''$ | Conversion between minutes and seconds |  
| Total minutes = Degrees × 60 | Convert degrees to minutes |  
| Total seconds = Minutes × 60 | Convert minutes to seconds |  
| Total minutes = Seconds ÷ 60 | Convert seconds to minutes |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mistake**: Confusing minutes and seconds (e.g., treating $ 1'$ as $ 1^\circ $)  
  **Correction**: Remember $ 1^\circ = 60'$ and $ 1' = 60''$. Always check units!  

- **Mistake**: Neglecting negative signs in angles (e.g., interpreting $-30^\circ $ as $ 30^\circ $)  
  **Correction**: Negative angles indicate clockwise rotation. Always retain the sign.  

### Exam Tips  
- When converting between units, write down each step clearly to avoid calculation errors.  
- For mixed units (e.g., $ 2^\circ 30'$), separate degrees, minutes, and seconds before converting.  

### Memory Aid  
Use the acronym **DMS** (Degrees, Minutes, Seconds) to recall the hierarchy of units:  
- **D**egrees → **M**inutes → **S**econds
