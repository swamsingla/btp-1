## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered why some trigonometric functions are undefined at certain angles? For instance, why is $ \tan(90^\circ) $ undefined? This topic explores the **domains** (all possible input values) and **ranges** (output values) of six fundamental trigonometric functions. By analyzing their behavior across quadrants and identifying restrictions, we gain deeper insight into their mathematical nature. This knowledge is crucial for solving equations, graphing functions, and applying trigonometry in physics and engineering.  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definitions  
> **Domain**: The set of all permissible input values (angles) for which a function is defined.  
> **Range**: The set of all output values produced by the function.  

#### 3.1.1 Sine and Cosine Functions  
> **Definition**: The domain of $ y = \sin x $ and $ y = \cos x $ is the set of all real numbers $ \mathbb{R} $. Their range is the closed interval $ [-1, 1] $, meaning $ -1 \leq \sin x \leq 1 $ and $ -1 \leq \cos x \leq 1 $ for all $ x \in \mathbb{R} $.  

#### 3.1.2 Cosecant and Secant Functions  
> **Definition**: The domain of $ y = \csc x $ is $ \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\} $, because $ \csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x} $ and $ \sin x = 0 $ at integer multiples of $ \pi $. Its range is $ (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty) $.  
> **Definition**: The domain of $ y = \sec x $ is $ \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\} $, since $ \sec x = \frac{1}{\cos x} $ and $ \cos x = 0 $ at odd multiples of $ \frac{\pi}{2} $. Its range is also $ (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty) $.  

#### 3.1.3 Tangent and Cotangent Functions  
> **Definition**: The domain of $ y = \tan x $ is $ \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\} $, because $ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $ and $ \cos x = 0 $ at odd multiples of $ \frac{\pi}{2} $. Its range is $ \mathbb{R} $.  
> **Definition**: The domain of $ y = \cot x $ is $ \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\} $, since $ \cot x = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} $ and $ \sin x = 0 $ at integer multiples of $ \pi $. Its range is $ \mathbb{R} $.  

#### 3.1.4 Behavior Across Quadrants  
> **Observation**: As $ x $ varies through quadrants, trigonometric functions exhibit distinct trends:  
- **Sine**: Increases from 0 to 1 in QI, decreases from 1 to 0 in QII, decreases from 0 to -1 in QIII, and increases from -1 to 0 in QIV.  
- **Cosine**: Decreases from 1 to 0 in QI, decreases from 0 to -1 in QII, increases from -1 to 0 in QIII, and increases from 0 to 1 in QIV.  
- **Tangent**: Increases from 0 to $ +\infty $ in QI, decreases from $ -\infty $ to 0 in QII, increases from 0 to $ +\infty $ in QIII, and decreases from $ -\infty $ to 0 in QIV.  
- **Cotangent**: Decreases from $ +\infty $ to 0 in QI, decreases from 0 to $ -\infty $ in QII, decreases from $ +\infty $ to 0 in QIII, and decreases from 0 to $ -\infty $ in QIV.  
- **Secant**: Increases from 1 to $ +\infty $ in QI, decreases from $ -\infty $ to -1 in QII, decreases from -1 to $ -\infty $ in QIII, and decreases from $ +\infty $ to 1 in QIV.  
- **Cosecant**: Decreases from $ +\infty $ to 1 in QI, increases from 1 to $ +\infty $ in QII, increases from $ -\infty $ to -1 in QIII, and decreases from -1 to $ -\infty $ in QIV.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
**Find the domain of $ y = \csc x $.**  
**Solution:**  
Since $ \csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x} $, the denominator $ \sin x $ cannot be zero.

$$
\sin x = 0 \iff x = n\pi, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}

$$  
Thus, the domain is:

$$
\left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\}

$$  

### Example 2:  
**Determine the range of $ y = \sec x $.**  
**Solution:**  
Since $ \sec x = \frac{1}{\cos x} $, and $ \cos x \in [-1, 1] $, the reciprocal $ \sec x $ satisfies:

$$
|\sec x| \geq 1 \implies \sec x \leq -1 \text{ or } \sec x \geq 1

$$  
Hence, the range is:

$$
(-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)

$$  

### Example 3:  
**Verify the domain of $ y = \tan x $ at $ x = \frac{3\pi}{2} $.**  
**Solution:**  
At $ x = \frac{3\pi}{2} $, $ \cos x = 0 $, making $ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $ undefined. Thus, $ \frac{3\pi}{2} $ is excluded from the domain.  

### Example 4:  
**Show that the range of $ y = \cot x $ is all real numbers.**  
**Solution:**  
Let $ y = \cot x = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} $. For any real number $ y $, there exists an $ x $ such that $ \cot x = y $. For example:  
- When $ x \to 0^+ $, $ \cot x \to +\infty $  
- When $ x \to 0^- $, $ \cot x \to -\infty $  
This confirms the range is $ \mathbb{R} $.  

### Example 5:  
**Explain why $ \csc x $ has a range of $ (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty) $.**  
**Solution:**  
From $ \csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x} $, note that $ |\sin x| \leq 1 $ implies $ |\csc x| \geq 1 $. Therefore:

$$
\csc x \leq -1 \quad \text{or} \quad \csc x \geq 1

$$  
This gives the range $ (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty) $.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
1. Find the domain of $ y = \sec x $.  
2. What is the range of $ y = \cos x $?  

### 🟡 Medium  
3. Prove that $ \tan x $ is undefined at $ x = \frac{\pi}{2} $.  
4. Determine the domain of $ y = \cot x $.  

### 🔴 Hard  
5. Show that the range of $ y = \csc x $ excludes the interval $ (-1, 1) $.  

**Answers:**  

1. **Answer:** $ \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\} $  
   **Steps:** Since $ \sec x = \frac{1}{\cos x} $, $ \cos x $ cannot be zero. Solving $ \cos x = 0 $ gives $ x = \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} $.  

2. **Answer:** $ [-1, 1] $  
   **Steps:** From the definition of $ \cos x $, its maximum and minimum values are $ \pm 1 $.  

3. **Answer:** At $ x = \frac{\pi}{2} $, $ \cos x = 0 $, making $ \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} $ undefined.  

4. **Answer:** $ \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x \neq n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z} \right\} $  
   **Steps:** $ \cot x = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} $; $ \sin x = 0 $ at $ x = n\pi $.  

5. **Answer:**  
   **Steps:** Since $ \csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x} $, and $ |\sin x| \leq 1 $, then $ |\csc x| \geq 1 $. Hence, $ \csc x \leq -1 $ or $ \csc x \geq 1 $, excluding $ (-1, 1) $.

## 6. Summary  
- **Sine & Cosine**: Domain $ \mathbb{R} $, Range $ [-1, 1] $  
- **Cosecant & Secant**: Domain excludes $ n\pi $ and $ \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} $, respectively; Range $ (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty) $  
- **Tangent & Cotangent**: Domain excludes $ \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} $ and $ n\pi $, respectively; Range $ \mathbb{R} $  
- Behavior across quadrants determines monotonicity and limits of functions

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing domain exclusions: e.g., mistaking $ \sec x $’s domain as excluding $ n\pi $ instead of $ \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} $.  
- ❌ Incorrect range assumptions: Assuming $ \csc x $’s range is $ [-1, 1] $, ignoring absolute values.  

### Exam Tips  
- 🔍 Always check for vertical asymptotes in graphs to identify domain exclusions.  
- 📊 Use unit circle visuals to recall sign patterns and behavior across quadrants.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Cosecant and Secant: No Zero, Only Extremes!"**  
Remember that $ \csc x $ and $ \sec x $ exclude zeros of $ \sin x $ and $ \cos x $, respectively, and their ranges extend beyond ±1.
