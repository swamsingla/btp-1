Have you ever wondered why a full circle is 360° but also equals $ 2\pi $ radians? This fascinating relationship lies at the heart of trigonometry! In this topic, we explore how to convert **degrees** to **radians** and vice versa. By mastering this skill, you’ll be able to work seamlessly between these two systems of angular measurement, which is critical for solving problems in geometry, physics, and higher-level mathematics.

> $ \pi $ radians = 180°.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Fundamental Conversion Factor  
The relationship between degrees and radians is defined by the equation:  
> $ \pi $ radians = 180°.  

This implies two key conversion formulas:  
> **Radian Measure = $ \frac{\pi}{180} \times $ Degree Measure**  
> **Degree Measure = $ \frac{180}{\pi} \times $ Radian Measure**  

These formulas allow seamless conversion between the two systems. For example:  
- $ 1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180} \approx 0.01746 $ radians.  
- $ 1 $ radian ≈ $ \frac{180}{\pi} \approx 57.2958^\circ $.  

#### 3.2 Common Angle Conversions  
The source text provides a table of standard angles and their equivalent radian measures:  
| Degree | Radian |  
|--------|--------|  
| 30°    | $ \frac{\pi}{6} $ |  
| 45°    | $ \frac{\pi}{4} $ |  
| 60°    | $ \frac{\pi}{3} $ |  
| 90°    | $ \frac{\pi}{2} $ |  
| 180°   | $ \pi $ |  
| 270°   | $ \frac{3\pi}{2} $ |  
| 360°   | $ 2\pi $ |  

These values are essential for trigonometric calculations and will be used extensively in later sections.  

#### 3.3 Notation Conventions  
When angles are written:  
- **Angle θ°** refers to an angle measured in **degrees**.  
- **Angle β** refers to an angle measured in **radians**.  

The term “radian” is often omitted when expressing angles in radians, e.g., $ \pi $ radians is simply written as $ \pi $.  

#### 3.4 Conversion Process  
To convert degrees to radians:  
1. Multiply the degree measure by $ \frac{\pi}{180} $.  
2. Simplify the resulting fraction.  

Example: Convert $ 40^\circ 20' $ to radians.  
- First, convert minutes to degrees: $ 20' = \frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3}^\circ $.  
- Total degrees: $ 40 + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{121}{3}^\circ $.  
- Convert to radians: $ \frac{121}{3} \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{121\pi}{540} $ radians.  

To convert radians to degrees:  
1. Multiply the radian measure by $ \frac{180}{\pi} $.  
2. Simplify the result, converting fractional degrees to minutes and seconds if needed.  

Example: Convert $ 6 $ radians to degrees.  
- Calculation: $ 6 \times \frac{180}{\pi} \approx 343.77^\circ $.  
- Break down: $ 0.77^\circ = 0.77 \times 60' = 46.2' $, and $ 0.2' = 12'' $.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Convert $ 40^\circ 20' $ to radians  
**Solution:**  
- Convert minutes to degrees: $ 20' = \frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3}^\circ $.  
- Total degrees: $ 40 + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{121}{3}^\circ $.  
- Convert to radians:

$$  
  \text{Radians} = \frac{121}{3} \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{121\pi}{540}.

$$  
**Answer:** $ \frac{121\pi}{540} $ radians.  

### Example 2: Convert $ 6 $ radians to degrees  
**Solution:**  
- Use the formula:

$$  
  \text{Degrees} = 6 \times \frac{180}{\pi} \approx 6 \times \frac{180}{3.14} \approx 343.77^\circ.

$$  
- Break down decimal degrees:  
  - $ 0.77^\circ = 0.77 \times 60' = 46.2' $.  
  - $ 0.2' = 0.2 \times 60'' = 12'' $.  
**Answer:** Approximately $ 343^\circ 46' 12'' $.  

### Example 3: Convert $ 90^\circ $ to radians  
**Solution:**  
- Use the formula:

$$  
  \text{Radians} = 90 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{2}.

$$  
**Answer:** $ \frac{\pi}{2} $ radians.  

### Example 4: Convert $ \frac{5\pi}{6} $ radians to degrees  
**Solution:**  
- Use the formula:

$$  
  \text{Degrees} = \frac{5\pi}{6} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{5 \times 180}{6} = 150^\circ.

$$  
**Answer:** $ 150^\circ $.  

### Example 5: Convert $ 120^\circ $ to radians  
**Solution:**  
- Use the formula:

$$  
  \text{Radians} = 120 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{2\pi}{3}.

$$  
**Answer:** $ \frac{2\pi}{3} $ radians.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy:  
1. Convert $ 30^\circ $ to radians.  
2. Convert $ \frac{\pi}{4} $ radians to degrees.  

### 🟡 Medium:  
3. Convert $ 135^\circ $ to radians.  
4. Convert $ 5 $ radians to degrees (use $ \pi \approx 3.14 $).  

### 🔴 Hard:  
5. Convert $ 75^\circ 30' $ to radians.  

---

### Answers:  

1. **Convert $ 30^\circ $ to radians:**

$$  
   \text{Radians} = 30 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{6}.

$$  
   **Answer:** $ \frac{\pi}{6} $ radians.  

2. **Convert $ \frac{\pi}{4} $ radians to degrees:**

$$  
   \text{Degrees} = \frac{\pi}{4} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = 45^\circ.

$$  
   **Answer:** $ 45^\circ $.  

3. **Convert $ 135^\circ $ to radians:**

$$  
   \text{Radians} = 135 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{3\pi}{4}.

$$  
   **Answer:** $ \frac{3\pi}{4} $ radians.  

4. **Convert $ 5 $ radians to degrees:**

$$  
   \text{Degrees} = 5 \times \frac{180}{3.14} \approx 286.62^\circ.

$$  
   **Answer:** Approximately $ 286.62^\circ $.  

5. **Convert $ 75^\circ 30' $ to radians:**  
   - Convert minutes: $ 30' = \frac{30}{60} = 0.5^\circ $.  
   - Total degrees: $ 75 + 0.5 = 75.5^\circ $.  
   - Convert to radians:

$$  
     \text{Radians} = 75.5 \times \frac{\pi}{180} \approx 1.318\pi \approx 4.142 \text{ radians}.

$$  
   **Answer:** Approximately $ 4.142 $ radians.

## 6. Summary  
- The relationship $ \pi $ radians = 180° allows conversion between degrees and radians.  
- To convert degrees to radians: multiply by $ \frac{\pi}{180} $.  
- To convert radians to degrees: multiply by $ \frac{180}{\pi} $.  
- Common angles like $ 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ $ correspond to $ \frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{3} $ radians.  
- Degrees can be converted to minutes and seconds for precision.  
- Applications include calculating arc lengths and solving trigonometric problems.

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Radian Measure} = \frac{\pi}{180} \times \text{Degree Measure} $ | Converts degrees to radians. |  
| $ \text{Degree Measure} = \frac{180}{\pi} \times \text{Radian Measure} $ | Converts radians to degrees. |  
| $ 1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180} \approx 0.01746 $ radians | Approximate value of 1 degree in radians. |  
| $ 1 $ radian ≈ $ \frac{180}{\pi} \approx 57.2958^\circ $ | Approximate value of 1 radian in degrees. |  
| $ \theta = \frac{l}{r} $ | Central angle in radians, where $ l $ is arc length and $ r $ is radius. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mistake:** Confusing degrees and radians during conversion.  
  **Correction:** Always double-check the formula: $ \text{Radians} = \frac{\pi}{180} \times \text{Degrees} $.  
- **Mistake:** Incorrectly converting minutes to degrees (e.g., $ 1' = \frac{1}{60}^\circ $).  
  **Correction:** Remember $ 1' = \frac{1}{60}^\circ $, so $ 20' = \frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3}^\circ $.  

### Exam Tips  
- Memorize the standard angle conversions (e.g., $ 90^\circ = \frac{\pi}{2} $) for quick problem-solving.  
- Practice converting between degrees and radians regularly to build fluency.  

### Memory Aid  
**"PIE" Rule:**  
- **P**i (π) corresponds to **180°**.  
- **I**n radians, **180° = π**.  
- **E**asy to remember: **π radians = 180°**.
