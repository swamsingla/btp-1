## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Ordered Pairs and Cartesian Product  
An **ordered pair** is a pair of elements written in parentheses, where the **first element** comes from one set and the **second element** from another. For example, $ (a, b) $ is an ordered pair where $ a \in P $ and $ b \in Q $.  

> **Definition**: Given two non-empty sets $ P $ and $ Q $, the **Cartesian product** $ P \times Q $ is the set of all ordered pairs $ (p, q) $ such that $ p \in P $ and $ q \in Q $.  
>  
> Mathematically:  
>

$$  
> P \times Q = \{(p, q) \mid p \in P, q \in Q\}  
>

$$  

If either $ P $ or $ Q $ is the **empty set** ($ \emptyset $), then $ P \times Q = \emptyset $, because there are no elements to form pairs.  

---

### 3.2 Example-Based Construction  
Consider two sets:  
- $ A = \{\text{red, blue}\} $ (colors)  
- $ B = \{\text{b, c, s}\} $ (objects: bag, coat, shirt)  

To form the Cartesian product $ A \times B $, pair each element of $ A $ with every element of $ B $:

$$  
A \times B = \{(\text{red}, \text{b}), (\text{red}, \text{c}), (\text{red}, \text{s}), (\text{blue}, \text{b}), (\text{blue}, \text{c}), (\text{blue}, \text{s})\}

$$  

This results in $ 2 \times 3 = 6 $ ordered pairs.  

---

### 3.3 General Rule for Cardinality  
The number of elements in $ P \times Q $ is equal to the product of the number of elements in $ P $ and $ Q $. If $ n(P) = m $ and $ n(Q) = k $, then:

$$  
n(P \times Q) = m \times k

$$  

**Proof**:  
Each element in $ P $ forms $ k $ pairs with elements of $ Q $. With $ m $ elements in $ P $, total pairs = $ m \times k $.  

---

### 3.4 Real-World Applications  
Another example involves license plate codes:  
- $ A = \{\text{DL, MP, KA}\} $ (states)  
- $ B = \{01, 02, 03\} $ (code prefixes)  

The Cartesian product $ A \times B $ yields:

$$  
A \times B = \{(\text{DL}, 01), (\text{DL}, 02), (\text{DL}, 03), \dots, (\text{KA}, 03)\}

$$  

There are $ 3 \times 3 = 9 $ valid codes, demonstrating how Cartesian products model real-world combinatorial problems.  

---

### 3.5 Order Matters  
Ordered pairs are **not commutative**. For example:  
- $ (\text{DL}, 01) \neq (01, \text{DL}) $  
- $ (\text{red}, \text{b}) \neq (\text{b}, \text{red}) $  

This highlights the importance of **order** in defining Cartesian products.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Given $ A = \{1, 2\} $ and $ B = \{3, 4\} $, find $ A \times B $.  

**Solution**:  
Pair each element of $ A $ with each element of $ B $:

$$  
A \times B = \{(1, 3), (1, 4), (2, 3), (2, 4)\}

$$  
Number of elements: $ 2 \times 2 = 4 $.  

---

### Example 2:  
If $ A = \{a\} $ and $ B = \{1, 2, 3\} $, compute $ A \times B $.  

**Solution**:  
Since $ A $ has 1 element and $ B $ has 3 elements:

$$  
A \times B = \{(a, 1), (a, 2), (a, 3)\}

$$  
Total pairs: $ 1 \times 3 = 3 $.  

---

### Example 3:  
Let $ A = \{x, y\} $ and $ B = \{m, n\} $. Verify that $ A \times B \neq B \times A $.  

**Solution**:  
Compute both products:

$$  
A \times B = \{(x, m), (x, n), (y, m), (y, n)\}

$$

$$  
B \times A = \{(m, x), (m, y), (n, x), (n, y)\}

$$  
Clearly, the ordered pairs differ in positions, proving $ A \times B \neq B \times A $.  

---

### Example 4:  
Find $ A \times B $ where $ A = \{1, 2\} $ and $ B = \emptyset $.  

**Solution**:  
If $ B = \emptyset $, then $ A \times B = \emptyset $. There are no elements in $ B $ to pair with elements of $ A $.  

---

### Example 5:  
Calculate $ n(A \times B) $ if $ n(A) = 5 $ and $ n(B) = 4 $.  

**Solution**:  
Using the cardinality rule:

$$  
n(A \times B) = 5 \times 4 = 20

$$

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**:  
Let $ A = \{a, b\} $ and $ B = \{1, 2\} $. Find $ A \times B $.  

**Answer**:

$$  
A \times B = \{(a, 1), (a, 2), (b, 1), (b, 2)\}

$$  

---  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2**:  
If $ A = \{2, 4\} $ and $ B = \{3\} $, compute $ A \times B $ and determine $ n(A \times B) $.  

**Answer**:

$$  
A \times B = \{(2, 3), (4, 3)\} \quad \Rightarrow \quad n(A \times B) = 2

$$  

---  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3**:  
Given $ A = \{x, y, z\} $ and $ B = \{1, 2\} $, prove that $ A \times B $ contains exactly 6 elements.  

**Answer**:  
Each element of $ A $ pairs with both elements of $ B $:  
- $ x $ pairs with 1 and 2 → 2 pairs  
- $ y $ pairs with 1 and 2 → 2 pairs  
- $ z $ pairs with 1 and 2 → 2 pairs  
Total: $ 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 $ pairs.  

---

## 6. Summary  
- The **Cartesian product** $ P \times Q $ consists of all ordered pairs $ (p, q) $ where $ p \in P $ and $ q \in Q $.  
- The **cardinality** of $ P \times Q $ is $ n(P) \times n(Q) $.  
- If either set is empty, $ P \times Q = \emptyset $.  
- Ordered pairs are **not commutative**; $ (a, b) \neq (b, a) $.  
- Cartesian products are used to model real-world scenarios like license plate codes.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ P \times Q = \{(p, q) \mid p \in P, q \in Q\} $ | Definition of Cartesian product |  
| $ n(P \times Q) = n(P) \times n(Q) $ | Cardinality of Cartesian product |  
| $ \text{If } P = \emptyset \text{ or } Q = \emptyset, \text{ then } P \times Q = \emptyset $ | Empty set property |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Confusing order in ordered pairs**: $ (a, b) \neq (b, a) $. Always check the sequence.  
2. **Miscalculating cardinality**: Forgetting to multiply the sizes of the sets.  
3. **Ignoring the empty set case**: If either set is empty, the product is empty.  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice constructing Cartesian products for small sets to reinforce the concept.  
- Memorize the cardinality formula: $ n(A \times B) = n(A) \times n(B) $.  

### Memory Aid  
Use the acronym **C.P.** for Cartesian Product, remembering it’s built from **C**ombining **P**airs.
