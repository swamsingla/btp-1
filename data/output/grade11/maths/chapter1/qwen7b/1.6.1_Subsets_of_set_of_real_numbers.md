### 2. Explanation
 explain the core concept concisely:
- A subset of a set \( A \) is any set \( B \) such that every element of \( B \) is also an element of \( A \). In mathematical notation, this is written as \( B \subseteq A \).
- For the set of real numbers \( \mathbb{R} \), we will consider subsets that are intervals.

#### 2.1 definition of intervals
- An interval is a subset of \( \mathbb{R} \) that consists of all real numbers between two given real numbers \( a \) and \( b \) (where \( a < b \)).
- There are four types of intervals:
  1. **Open interval**: \( (a, b) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a < x < b \} \)
  2. **Closed interval**: \( [a, b] = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a \leq x \leq b \} \)
  3. **Half-open intervals**: \( [a, b) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a \leq x < b \} \)
  4. \( (a, b] = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a < x \leq b \} \)

#### 2.2 Properties of intervals
- Intervals are subsets of \( \mathbb{R} \) and are therefore subject to the subset relation \( \subseteq \).
- For example, \( (a, b) \subseteq [a, b] \) because every element in the open interval \( (a, b) \) is also in the closed interval \( [a, b] \).
