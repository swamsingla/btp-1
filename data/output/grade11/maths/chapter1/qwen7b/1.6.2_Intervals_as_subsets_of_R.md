Intervals as subsets of \(\mathbb{R}\) are fundamental in real analysis and help us describe specific ranges of real numbers. There are four main types of intervals:

1. **Open Interval**: denoted by \((a, b)\), includes all real numbers between \(a\) and \(b\) but exclude \(a\) and \(b\).
   \[
   (a, b) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a < x < b \}
   \]

2. **Closed Interval**: denoted by \([a, b]\), includes all real numbers between \(a\) and \(b\) and also includes \(a\) and \(b\).
   \[
   [a, b] = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a \leq x \leq b \}
   \]

3. **Half-Open Intervals**:
   - **Left Half-Open Interval**: denoted by \([a, b)\), includes all real numbers between \(a\) and \(b\) and includes \(a\) but excludes \(b\).
     \[
     [a, b) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a \leq x < b \}
     \]
   - **Right Half-Open Interval**: denoted by \((a, b]\), includes all real numbers between \(a\) and \(b\) and excludes \(a\) but includes \(b\).
     \[
     (a, b] = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid a < x \leq b \}
     \]

4. **Infinite Intervals**:
   - ** opens to the right**: denoted by \((a, \infty)\), includes all real numbers greater than \(a\).
     \[
     (a, \infty) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x > a \}
     \]
   - **opens to the left**: denoted by \((- \infty, b)\), includes all real numbers less than \(b\).
     \[
     (-\infty, b) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x < b \}
     \]
   - ** opens to both sides**: denoted by \((- \infty, \infty)\), includes all real numbers.
     \[
     (-\infty, \infty) = \mathbb{R}
     \]

Each type of interval has a specific length, except for infinite intervals which have infinite length. The length of a finite interval \((a, b)\), \([a, b]\), \([a, b)\), or \((a, b]\) is given by \(b - a\).

In conclusion, intervals as subsets of \(\mathbb{R}\) provide a systematic way to describe ranges of real numbers. Understanding these intervals is crucial for many areas of mathematics, including calculus, analysis, and probability theory.
