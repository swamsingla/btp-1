## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Multiplication Shortcuts  
**Multiplying by Powers of Ten**:  
When you multiply a number by 10, 100, or 1000, simply add the corresponding number of zeros to the end of the number.  
> **Rule**:

$$
\text{Number} \times 10^n = \text{Number followed by } n \text{ zeros}

$$  
**Example**:

$$
123 \times 10 = 1230 \quad (\text{Add 1 zero})  
123 \times 100 = 12300 \quad (\text{Add 2 zeros})

$$  

### 3.2 Patterns in Special Multiplications  
**Pattern 1: Repeating Digits**  
Multiplying numbers made of repeated digits (like 11, 111, etc.) often creates palindromic results.  
**Example**:

$$
11 \times 11 = 121  
111 \times 111 = 12321  
1111 \times 1111 = 1234321

$$  

**Pattern 2: Multiplying by 66...6**  
Numbers like 66, 666, and 6666 form unique patterns when multiplied by numbers ending in 1.  
**Example**:

$$
66 \times 61 = 3966  
666 \times 661 = 439566  
6666 \times 6661 = 44395566

$$  

**Pattern 3: Multiplying by 3...3**  
Multiplying numbers with repeating 3s by numbers ending in 5 reveals a consistent pattern.  
**Example**:

$$
3 \times 35 = 105  
33 \times 335 = 11055  
333 \times 335 = 1110555

$$  

### 3.3 Digit Count Patterns  
**Observation**: The number of digits in the product depends on the size of the numbers being multiplied.  
- **Two 2-digit numbers**:

$$
  \text{Minimum product}: 10 \times 10 = 100 \quad (\text{3 digits})  
  \text{Maximum product}: 99 \times 99 = 9801 \quad (\text{4 digits})

$$  
  So, the product of two 2-digit numbers can have **3 or 4 digits**.  

- **Three 3-digit numbers**:

$$
  \text{Minimum product}: 100 \times 100 = 10,000 \quad (\text{5 digits})  
  \text{Maximum product}: 999 \times 999 = 998,001 \quad (\text{6 digits})

$$  
  Thus, the product of two 3-digit numbers has **5 or 6 digits**.  

**Did You Know?**  
This pattern helps estimate the number of digits in large products without full calculation!

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Calculate $ 2 \times 1768 \times 50 $.  
**Solution**:  
Group $ 2 \times 50 = 100 $ first:

$$
2 \times 1768 \times 50 = (2 \times 50) \times 1768 = 100 \times 1768 = 176,800

$$  

### Example 2:  
Evaluate $ 72 \times 125 $.  
**Solution**:  
Use the hint $ 125 = \frac{1000}{8} $:

$$
72 \times 125 = 72 \times \left(\frac{1000}{8}\right) = \frac{72 \times 1000}{8} = \frac{72,000}{8} = 9,000

$$  

### Example 3:  
Find the pattern in $ 101 \times 101 $, $ 102 \times 102 $, and $ 103 \times 103 $.  
**Solution**:  
Calculate each product:

$$
101 \times 101 = 10,201  
102 \times 102 = 10,404  
103 \times 103 = 10,609

$$  
**Pattern**: The product increases by 203 each time (e.g., $ 10,201 + 203 = 10,404 $).

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**: Calculate $ 4 \times 250 \times 2 $.  
🟡 **Medium**: Find the missing values in $ 2500 \times 12 = ? $ and $ ? \times ? = 120,000,000 $.  
🔴 **Hard**: Prove that the product of two 3-digit numbers cannot exceed 999,999.  

**Answers**:  
**Easy**:

$$
4 \times 250 \times 2 = (4 \times 2) \times 250 = 8 \times 250 = 2,000

$$  

**Medium**:

$$
2500 \times 12 = 30,000  
\quad \text{and} \quad 10,000 \times 12,000 = 120,000,000

$$  

**Hard**:  
The maximum product of two 3-digit numbers is $ 999 \times 999 = 998,001 $, which is less than 999,999. Hence, the claim holds.

## 6. Summary  
- Multiplying by 10, 100, or 1000 adds zeros to the end of a number.  
- Repeating digits (e.g., 11, 111) create palindromic patterns in products.  
- The number of digits in a product depends on the size of the original numbers.  
- Grouping numbers strategically simplifies calculations (e.g., $ 2 \times 50 = 100 $).  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Number} \times 10^n = \text{Number followed by } n \text{ zeros} $ | Multiplication by powers of ten |  
| $ a \times b = b \times a $ | Commutative property of multiplication |  
| $ (a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c) $ | Associative property of multiplication |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Misplacing Zeros**: Forgetting to add the correct number of zeros when multiplying by 10, 100, etc.  
- **Ignoring Order**: Not grouping numbers effectively (e.g., calculating $ 2 \times 1768 \times 50 $ as $ 2 \times 1768 = 3536 $, then $ 3536 \times 50 $).  

### Memory Aid  
**"Shift Left, Multiply Right"**: When multiplying by 10, shift the decimal point one place to the right. For 100, shift twice.
