## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 Understanding Large Numbers  
This section uses real-life scenarios to compare **large quantities** like lakhs and crores. For instance:  
- **Population of Mumbai**: Over $ 1 $ crore $ 24 $ lakhs ($ 12,400,000 $)  
- **Distance to the Moon**: $ 3,84,400 $ kilometers  

These examples show how **multiplication** and **estimation** help answer big questions.  

### 3.2 Calculating with Large Numbers  
To solve problems like *“Can 1 lakh buses hold Mumbai’s population?”*, follow these steps:  
1. **Assume** a bus holds $ 50 $ people.  
2. Multiply: $ 1 $ lakh buses $\times 50 $ people/bus = $ 50 $ lakh people.  
3. Compare with the actual population ($ 1.24 $ crore people).  

This method breaks down complex problems into simpler stages.

## 4. Solved Examples  
### Example 1: Mumbai Population vs. Buses  
**Question**: If $ 1 $ bus carries $ 50 $ people, can $ 1 $ lakh buses hold Mumbai’s population ($ 1.24 $ crore)?  

**Solution**:  
1. Calculate total capacity:

$$
   1 \text{ lakh buses} \times 50 = 50 \text{ lakh people}

$$  
2. Compare with population:

$$
   50 \text{ lakh} < 1.24 \text{ crore} \quad (\text{since } 1 \text{ crore} = 10 \text{ lakh})

$$  
**Answer**: No, because $50 $ lakh is less than $ 1.24 $ crore.  

### Example 2: Distance to the Moon  
**Question**: If you walk $ 100 $ km/day, how long to reach the Moon ($ 3,84,400 $ km)?  

**Solution**:  
1. Find days needed:

$$
   \frac{3,84,400 \text{ km}}{100 \text{ km/day}} = 3,844 \text{ days}

$$  
2. Convert to years:

$$
   \frac{3,844}{365} \approx 10.5 \text{ years}

$$  
**Answer**: Approximately $10.5 $ years.  

### Example 3: Paper Weight  
**Question**: Does $ 1 $ lakh sheets of paper ($ 5 $ grams each) weigh more than $ 50 $ kg?  

**Solution**:  
1. Total weight:

$$
   1,00,000 \text{ sheets} \times 5 \text{ g/sheet} = 5,00,000 \text{ grams}

$$  
2. Convert to kilograms:

$$
   5,00,000 \text{ g} = 500 \text{ kg}

$$  
**Answer**: Yes, it weighs $500 $ kg, which is much heavier than $ 50 $ kg.

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**:  
**(a)** If $ 250 $ babies are born every minute, how many are born in $ 1 $ hour?  
**(b)** If you count $ 1 $ coin per second, how many coins can you count in $ 1 $ hour?  

🟡 **Medium**:  
**(c)** Can you lift $ 1 $ lakh sheets of paper weighing $ 5 $ grams each? (Assume your strength limit is $ 50 $ kg)  

🔴 **Hard**:  
**(d)** Strike out $ 10 $ digits from $ 12345123451234512345 $ to make the largest possible number.  

**Answers**:  
**(a)**

$$
250 \text{ babies/min} \times 60 \text{ min/hour} = 15,000 \text{ babies/hour}

$$  
**(b)**

$$
1 \text{ coin/sec} \times 3600 \text{ sec/hour} = 3,600 \text{ coins/hour}

$$  
**(c)**  
Total weight:

$$
1,00,000 \text{ sheets} \times 5 \text{ g} = 500,000 \text{ g} = 500 \text{ kg} > 50 \text{ kg}

$$  
**Answer**: No, it’s too heavy.  
**(d)** Remove the smallest digits first: $987654321 $.
