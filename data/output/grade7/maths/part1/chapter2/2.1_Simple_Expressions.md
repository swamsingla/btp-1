## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 What is an Arithmetic Expression?  
An **arithmetic expression** is a combination of numbers and operations (like +, −, ×, ÷) that represents a calculation. For example:  
- $ 13 + 2 $ means "13 plus 2"  
- $ 20 - 4 $ means "20 minus 4"  

> **Definition**: An arithmetic expression is a mathematical phrase that combines numbers and operations to represent a calculation. Its **value** is the result after performing the operations.  

### 3.2 Evaluating Expressions  
To evaluate an expression, perform the operations in the correct order. For instance:  
- $ 5 \times 25 $ evaluates to $ 125 $ because multiplying 5 and 25 gives 125.  
- $ 10 + 2 $ evaluates to $ 12 $.  

> **Property**: The value of an arithmetic expression depends on the numbers used and the operations applied. Different expressions can have the same value, such as $ 10 + 2 = 12 $ and $ 15 - 3 = 12 $.  

### 3.3 Comparing Expressions  
Expressions can be compared by evaluating their values and using comparison symbols:  
- $ =$ means "equal to"  
- $ > $ means "greater than"  
- $ < $ means "less than"  

**Example**:  
Compare $ 10 + 2 $ and $ 7 + 1 $:  
- $ 10 + 2 = 12 $  
- $ 7 + 1 = 8 $  
Since $ 12 > 8 $, we write $ 10 + 2 > 7 + 1 $.  

> **Rule**: When comparing expressions, always evaluate their values first before applying the comparison symbol.  

### 3.4 Real-Life Applications  
Arithmetic expressions are everywhere! For example:  
- **Mallika’s Lunch Cost**: If she spends ₹25 daily, her weekly cost is $ 5 \times 25 = 125 $.  
- **Marble Comparison**: If Raja has $ 1023 + 125 $ marbles and Joy has $ 1022 + 128 $, we can compare them without calculating the full totals.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Writing Expressions  
**Problem**: Mallika spends ₹25 every day for lunch at school. Write the expression for the total amount she spends on lunch in a week (Monday to Friday).  

**Solution**:  
- She eats lunch 5 days a week.  
- Total cost = $ 5 \times 25 $.  
- $ 5 \times 25 = 125 $.  
So, the expression is $ 5 \times 25 $, and its value is ₹125.  

---

### Example 2: Comparing Expressions  
**Problem**: Which is greater, $ 1023 + 125 $ or $ 1022 + 128 $?  

**Solution**:  
- Analyze the differences:  
  - Raja starts with 1 more marble than Joy ($ 1023 $ vs. $ 1022 $).  
  - Joy gains 3 more marbles than Raja ($ 128 $ vs. $ 125 $).  
- Net effect: Joy ends up with 2 more marbles.  
Thus, $ 1023 + 125 < 1022 + 128 $.  

---

### Example 3: Finding Missing Values  
**Problem**: Fill in the blank: $ 13 + 4 = \_\_ + 6 $.  

**Solution**:  
- Left side: $ 13 + 4 = 17 $.  
- Right side: $ x + 6 = 17 $.  
- Solve for $ x $: $ x = 17 - 6 = 11 $.  
Answer: $ 13 + 4 = 11 + 6 $.  

---

### Example 4: Arranging Expressions  
**Problem**: Arrange these expressions in ascending order:  
(a) $ 67 - 19 $  
(b) $ 67 - 20 $  
(c) $ 35 + 25 $  
(d) $ 5 \times 11 $  
(e) $ 120 \div 3 $  

**Solution**:  
- Calculate each value:  
  - (a): $ 67 - 19 = 48 $  
  - (b): $ 67 - 20 = 47 $  
  - (c): $ 35 + 25 = 60 $  
  - (d): $ 5 \times 11 = 55 $  
  - (e): $ 120 \div 3 = 40 $  
- Order from smallest to largest:  
  $ 47 $ (b) < $ 40 $ (e) < $ 48 $ (a) < $ 55 $ (d) < $ 60 $ (c).  

---

### Example 5: Comparing Subtractions  
**Problem**: Which is greater, $ 113 - 25 $ or $ 112 - 24 $?  

**Solution**:  
- Calculate each value:  
  - $ 113 - 25 = 88 $  
  - $ 112 - 24 = 88 $  
- Both expressions equal $ 88 $.  
Answer: They are equal, so $ 113 - 25 = 112 - 24 $.

## 6. Summary  
- **Arithmetic expressions** combine numbers and operations to represent calculations.  
- The **value** of an expression is found by performing the operations.  
- Use $ =$, $ > $, and $ < $ to compare expressions based on their values.  
- Different expressions can yield the same value (e.g., $ 10 + 2 = 12 $, $ 15 - 3 = 12 $).  
- Always evaluate expressions completely before comparing them.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ a + b = c $ | Addition expression |  
| $ a - b = c $ | Subtraction expression |  
| $ a \times b = c $ | Multiplication expression |  
| $ a \div b = c $ | Division expression |  
| $ a = b $ | Equality: $ a $ and $ b $ have the same value |  
| $ a > b $ | Greater than: $ a $'s value is larger than $ b $'s |  
| $ a < b $ | Less than: $ a $'s value is smaller than $ b $'s |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Forgetting to evaluate expressions completely**:  
   ❌ Mistake: Comparing $ 10 + 2 $ and $ 7 + 1 $ without calculating values.  
   ✅ Fix: Always compute the values first.  

2. **Misplacing numbers in comparisons**:  
   ❌ Mistake: Writing $ 10 + 2 > 7 + 1 $ as $ 12 < 8 $.  
   ✅ Fix: Double-check calculations and apply the correct inequality sign.  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice evaluating expressions quickly and accurately.  
- Use scratch paper to write down intermediate steps during exams.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Balance the Scale"**: Think of the equals sign (=) as a scale. Both sides must balance (have the same value) for the expression to be true.
