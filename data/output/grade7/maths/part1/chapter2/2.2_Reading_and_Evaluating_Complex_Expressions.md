## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered why a sentence like “Shalini sat next to a friend with toys” can mean two different things depending on where the comma is placed? Just like punctuation clarifies meaning in language, **brackets and rules** help avoid confusion in math!  

In this topic, you’ll learn how to **evaluate complex arithmetic expressions** correctly by following the **order of operations** and using **brackets** to clarify priorities. You’ll see how small changes like adding brackets can completely alter the result of an expression. This skill is essential for solving real-world problems involving money, measurements, and more!  

---  

## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 Understanding Ambiguity in Expressions  
When an expression lacks context or brackets, multiple interpretations are possible. For example:  
- **Expression**: $30 + 5 \times 4 $.  
  - **Wrong interpretation**: Add $ 30 + 5 $ first, then multiply by $ 4 $: $ (30 + 5) \times 4 = 140 $.  
  - **Correct interpretation**: Multiply $ 5 \times 4 $ first, then add $ 30 $: $ 30 + (5 \times 4) = 50 $.  

> **Definition**: An *expression* is a combination of numbers, operators, and variables that represents a mathematical operation. Without clear rules, ambiguity arises.  

### 3.2 Role of Brackets  
**Brackets** ($ ($, $) $) force a specific order of operations. They tell you to **calculate the value inside them first**, regardless of usual precedence rules.  

> **Rule**: When evaluating an expression with brackets, solve the operations **inside the brackets first**, followed by the rest.  

#### Example:  
For $ 30 + (5 \times 4) $:  
1. Calculate inside the brackets: $ 5 \times 4 = 20 $.  
2. Add to $ 30 $: $ 30 + 20 = 50 $.  

### 3.3 Order of Operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS)  
If **no brackets** are present, follow these rules:  
1. **Parentheses/Brackets** → Solve inside them first.  
2. **Exponents/Orders** → Not discussed here, but relevant in later topics.  
3. **Multiplication/Division** → Left to right.  
4. **Addition/Subtraction** → Left to right.  

> **Note**: In this topic, focus on **multiplication/division** taking precedence over **addition/subtraction**.  

### 3.4 Terms in an Expression  
An **expression** consists of **terms** separated by operators. For example:  
- In $ 30 + (5 \times 4) $, the terms are $ 30 $ and $ 5 \times 4 $.  
- Brackets group terms together, making their evaluation explicit.

## 4. Solved Examples  
### **Example 1:**  
Evaluate $ 20 + 3 \times 5 $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Identify multiplication first: $ 3 \times 5 = 15 $.  
2. Add to $ 20 $: $ 20 + 15 = 35 $.  
**Answer**: $ 35 $.  

### **Example 2:**  
Evaluate $ (10 + 2) \times 3 $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Solve inside the brackets: $ 10 + 2 = 12 $.  
2. Multiply by $ 3 $: $ 12 \times 3 = 36 $.  
**Answer**: $ 36 $.  

### **Example 3:**  
Evaluate $ 8 + 4 \times (6 - 2) $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Solve inside the innermost brackets: $ 6 - 2 = 4 $.  
2. Multiply $ 4 \times 4 = 16 $.  
3. Add to $ 8 $: $ 8 + 16 = 24 $.  
**Answer**: $ 24 $.  

### **Example 4:**  
Irfan buys a biscuit pack for ₹15 and a toor dal packet for ₹56. He pays ₹100. Write an expression for his change.  
**Solution**:  
1. Total cost: $ 15 + 56 = 71 $.  
2. Change: $ 100 - (15 + 56) = 100 - 71 = 29 $.  
**Expression**: $ 100 - (15 + 56) $  
**Answer**: ₹29.  

### **Example 5:**  
Evaluate $ 6 \times (2 + 3) - 4 $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Solve inside the brackets: $ 2 + 3 = 5 $.  
2. Multiply: $ 6 \times 5 = 30 $.  
3. Subtract $ 4 $: $ 30 - 4 = 26 $.  
**Answer**: $ 26 $.

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**: Evaluate $ 12 + 3 \times 2 $.  
🟡 **Medium**: Evaluate $ 10 + (5 \times 2) - 4 $.  
🔴 **Hard**: Evaluate $ 8 \times (3 + (4 - 1)) $.  

### Answers:  
**Easy**:  
1. Multiply $ 3 \times 2 = 6 $.  
2. Add to $ 12 $: $ 12 + 6 = 18 $.  
**Answer**: $ 18 $.  

**Medium**:  
1. Solve inside brackets: $ 5 \times 2 = 10 $.  
2. Add to $ 10 $: $ 10 + 10 = 20 $.  
3. Subtract $ 4 $: $ 20 - 4 = 16 $.  
**Answer**: $ 16 $.  

**Hard**:  
1. Solve innermost brackets: $ 4 - 1 = 3 $.  
2. Add to $ 3 $: $ 3 + 3 = 6 $.  
3. Multiply by $ 8 $: $ 8 \times 6 = 48 $.  
**Answer**: $ 48 $.  

---  

## 6. Summary  
- **Brackets** force specific calculations first.  
- **Multiplication/division** takes precedence over **addition/subtraction**.  
- Always evaluate **inside brackets first**, even if no parentheses are present.  
- Misplacing brackets can drastically change results (e.g., $ 30 + 5 \times 4 $ vs. $ (30 + 5) \times 4 $).  
- Apply the **order of operations** step-by-step to avoid errors.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ a + (b \times c) $ | Multiply first, then add. |  
| $ (a + b) \times c $ | Add first, then multiply. |  
| $ a + b \times c $ | Follow multiplication before addition. |  
| $ a \times (b + c) $ | Parentheses override default order. |  

---  

## 8. Student Corner  
### **Common Mistakes**  
- ❌ Doing addition before multiplication without brackets.  
  ✅ Fix: Always check for brackets and apply the correct order.  
- ❌ Skipping steps in multi-bracket expressions.  
  ✅ Fix: Solve innermost brackets first, working outward.  

### **Exam Tips**  
- Underline or highlight brackets in expressions to track evaluation order.  
- Rewrite expressions step-by-step to avoid mental calculation errors.  

### **Memory Aid**  
Use **BEDMAS** to remember the order:  
**B**rackets, **E**xponents, **D**ivision/Multiplication, **A**ddition/Subtraction.
