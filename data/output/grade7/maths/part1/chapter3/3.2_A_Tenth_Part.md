## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Tenths  
**Tenths** are parts of a whole divided into 10 equal segments. One tenth is written as $ \frac{1}{10} $.  

> **Definition**: A tenth is a fraction where the denominator is 10. It represents one part out of ten equal parts of a whole.  

For example:  
- $ \frac{1}{10} $ means **one-tenth**  
- $ \frac{4}{10} $ means **four-tenths**  
- $ \frac{10}{10} $ equals **one whole unit**  

### 3.2 Converting Mixed Numbers to Tenths  
When a measurement includes a whole number and tenths, it can be expressed as a single fraction with denominator 10.  

#### Example:  
The length of a pencil is $ 3 \frac{4}{10} $ units.  
This means:  
- $ 3 \times 1 = 3 $ units  
- $ 4 \times \frac{1}{10} = \frac{4}{10} $ units  
Total: $ 3 + \frac{4}{10} = \frac{34}{10} $ units  

> **Rule**: To convert a mixed number to tenths, multiply the whole number by 10 and add the numerator of the fraction.

$$
\text{Mixed number } a \frac{b}{10} = \frac{(a \times 10) + b}{10}

$$  

### 3.3 Adding Tenths  
When adding measurements with tenths, combine the whole numbers and tenths separately.  

#### Example:  
Add $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ and $ 3 \frac{6}{10} $:  
1. Whole numbers: $ 2 + 3 = 5 $  
2. Tenths: $ \frac{7}{10} + \frac{6}{10} = \frac{13}{10} $  
3. Simplify $ \frac{13}{10} $:  
   - $ \frac{10}{10} = 1 $ unit  
   - Remaining: $ \frac{3}{10} $  
Total: $ 5 + 1 + \frac{3}{10} = 6 \frac{3}{10} $  

> **Property**: When the sum of tenths exceeds 10, convert the excess to whole units.  

### 3.4 Reading Measurements  
Measurements with tenths can be read in two ways:  
1. **Mixed number form**: e.g., $ 4 \frac{8}{10} $ ("four and eight-tenths")  
2. **Improper fraction form**: e.g., $ \frac{48}{10} $ ("forty-eight tenths")  

> **Tip**: Always check the unit scale in diagrams to avoid confusion between different measurement systems.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Converting a Mixed Number to Tenths  
Convert $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ to an improper fraction.  

**Solution**:  
1. Multiply the whole number by 10: $ 2 \times 10 = 20 $  
2. Add the numerator: $ 20 + 7 = 27 $  
3. Write as a fraction: $ \frac{27}{10} $  

Answer: $ \frac{27}{10} $ units  

---

### Example 2: Adding Tenths  
Add $ 5 \frac{2}{10} $ and $ 3 \frac{9}{10} $.  

**Solution**:  
1. Whole numbers: $ 5 + 3 = 8 $  
2. Tenths: $ \frac{2}{10} + \frac{9}{10} = \frac{11}{10} $  
3. Simplify $ \frac{11}{10} $:  
   - $ \frac{10}{10} = 1 $ unit  
   - Remaining: $ \frac{1}{10} $  
Total: $ 8 + 1 + \frac{1}{10} = 9 \frac{1}{10} $  

Answer: $ 9 \frac{1}{10} $ units  

---

### Example 3: Subtracting Tenths  
Subtract $ 6 \frac{7}{10} $ from $ 12 \frac{4}{10} $.  

**Solution**:  
1. Whole numbers: $ 12 - 6 = 6 $  
2. Tenths: $ \frac{4}{10} - \frac{7}{10} = -\frac{3}{10} $  
3. Adjust borrowing:  
   - Borrow 1 unit from the whole number: $ 6 - 1 = 5 $  
   - Convert borrowed unit to tenths: $ 1 = \frac{10}{10} $  
   - New tenths calculation: $ \frac{10}{10} + \frac{4}{10} - \frac{7}{10} = \frac{7}{10} $  
Total: $ 5 + \frac{7}{10} = 5 \frac{7}{10} $  

Answer: $ 5 \frac{7}{10} $ units  

---

### Example 4: Comparing Lengths  
Arrange these lengths in increasing order:  
(a) $ \frac{9}{10} $  
(b) $ 1 \frac{7}{10} $  
(c) $ \frac{130}{10} $  
(d) $ 13 \frac{1}{10} $  
(e) $ 10 \frac{5}{10} $  
(f) $ 7 \frac{6}{10} $  
(g) $ 6 \frac{7}{10} $  

**Solution**:  
Convert all to improper fractions or decimals for comparison:  
- (a) $ \frac{9}{10} = 0.9 $  
- (b) $ 1 \frac{7}{10} = 1.7 $  
- (c) $ \frac{130}{10} = 13.0 $  
- (d) $ 13 \frac{1}{10} = 13.1 $  
- (e) $ 10 \frac{5}{10} = 10.5 $  
- (f) $ 7 \frac{6}{10} = 7.6 $  
- (g) $ 6 \frac{7}{10} = 6.7 $  

Order from smallest to largest:  
**(a)** $ 0.9 $ → **(g)** $ 6.7 $ → **(f)** $ 7.6 $ → **(b)** $ 1.7 $ → **(e)** $ 10.5 $ → **(d)** $ 13.1 $ → **(c)** $ 13.0 $  

Answer: (a) < (g) < (f) < (b) < (e) < (d) < (c)  

---

### Example 5: Real-Life Application  
Sonu’s lower arm is $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ units, and his upper arm is $ 3 \frac{6}{10} $ units. What is the total length?  

**Solution**:  
1. Add whole numbers: $ 2 + 3 = 5 $  
2. Add tenths: $ \frac{7}{10} + \frac{6}{10} = \frac{13}{10} $  
3. Simplify $ \frac{13}{10} $:  
   - $ \frac{10}{10} = 1 $ unit  
   - Remaining: $ \frac{3}{10} $  
Total: $ 5 + 1 + \frac{3}{10} = 6 \frac{3}{10} $  

Answer: $ 6 \frac{3}{10} $ units

## 6. Summary  
- **Tenths** are parts of a whole divided into 10 equal segments.  
- $ \frac{1}{10} $ is one-tenth, $ \frac{4}{10} $ is four-tenths.  
- To convert a mixed number to tenths: $ a \frac{b}{10} = \frac{(a \times 10) + b}{10} $.  
- When adding or subtracting tenths, handle whole numbers and fractions separately.  
- Improper fractions can be simplified to mixed numbers if needed.  
- Tenths allow precise measurements beyond whole units.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ a \frac{b}{10} = \frac{(a \times 10) + b}{10} $ | Converts mixed numbers to improper fractions |  
| $ \frac{a}{10} + \frac{b}{10} = \frac{a + b}{10} $ | Adds tenths |  
| $ \frac{a}{10} - \frac{b}{10} = \frac{a - b}{10} $ | Subtracts tenths |  
| $ \frac{a}{10} = a \div 10 $ | Converts tenths to decimal form |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing tenths with hundredths (e.g., $ \frac{1}{10} $ vs. $ \frac{1}{100} $).  
  ✅ Fix: Remember $ \frac{1}{10} = 0.1 $, while $ \frac{1}{100} = 0.01 $.  

- ❌ Forgetting to borrow when subtracting tenths exceeding 10.  
  ✅ Fix: Always convert 10 tenths to 1 whole unit before subtraction.  

### Exam Tips  
- ✔️ Practice converting mixed numbers to improper fractions regularly.  
- ✔️ Use visual aids (like rulers or number lines) to visualize tenths.  

### Memory Aid  
**"TENTH" = "Ten Equal Parts"**  
Imagine dividing a cake into 10 slices. Each slice is a tenth!
