## 2. Introduction  
Imagine folding a piece of paper in half—it becomes shorter, right? Now imagine doing this again! How would you measure such tiny lengths? This is where **hundredths** come into play.  

In this topic, you’ll learn how to divide a unit into **100 equal parts**, called **hundredths**, and how they connect to **tenths** (which you studied earlier). By the end, you’ll be able to compare, convert, and work with measurements like $ 4 \frac{45}{100} $ or $ 3.27 $. Let’s explore!  

**Connect to Prior Knowledge**: Remember how we divided a unit into **tenths** ($ \frac{1}{10} $)? Today, we’ll go even smaller!

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of a Hundredth  
A **hundredth** is **one part out of 100 equal parts** of a unit.  
> **Definition**: A hundredth is represented as $ \frac{1}{100} $ of a unit.  

**Visualizing Hundredths**:  
If you split each **tenth** ($ \frac{1}{10} $) into **10 smaller parts**, each part is $ \frac{1}{100} $ of the original unit.

$$
\text{Each } \frac{1}{10} \div 10 = \frac{1}{100}

$$  
So, **10 hundredths make 1 tenth**, and **100 hundredths make 1 whole unit**.  

### 3.2 Converting Tenths to Hundredths  
To convert **tenths** to **hundredths**, multiply the numerator by 10.  
> **Rule**: $ \frac{a}{10} = \frac{10a}{100} $  

**Example**:

$$
\frac{1}{10} = \frac{10}{100}, \quad \frac{3}{10} = \frac{30}{100}

$$  
This means $ 0.3 $ (three tenths) is equivalent to $ 0.30 $ (thirty hundredths).  

### 3.3 Reading and Writing Hundredths  
Numbers with hundredths are written in **decimal form** (e.g., $ 4.45 $) or **fractional form** (e.g., $ 4 \frac{45}{100} $).  
> **Reading Rule**:  
- The **whole number** part is read first.  
- The **digits after the decimal point** are read as "and x hundredths".  

**Example**:

$$
4.45 = \text{"Four and forty-five hundredths"}

$$  

### 3.4 Adding and Subtracting Hundredths  
When adding or subtracting, align the **decimal points** vertically.  
**Steps**:  
1. Convert all numbers to **like denominators** (same decimal places).  
2. Perform the operation.  
3. Simplify if necessary.  

**Example**:  
Add $ 1.23 $ and $ 0.45 $:

$$
\begin{align*}
1.23 \\
+0.45 \\
\hline
1.68 \\
\end{align*}

$$  

### 3.5 Practical Applications  
**Real-Life Example**:  
A ribbon measures $ 2 \frac{1}{10} $ meters. If you cut it into **10 equal pieces**, each piece is:

$$
\frac{1}{10} \times 2.1 = 0.21 \text{ meters} = 21 \text{ cm}

$$  
This shows how hundredths help in precise measurements.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Converting Tenths to Hundredths  
Convert $ \frac{7}{10} $ to hundredths.  
**Solution**:

$$
\frac{7}{10} = \frac{7 \times 10}{10 \times 10} = \frac{70}{100}

$$  
✅ Answer: $ \frac{70}{100} $ or $ 0.70 $  

### Example 2: Reading a Decimal  
Read $ 5.67 $ in words.  
**Solution**:

$$
5.67 = \text{"Five and sixty-seven hundredths"}

$$  
✅ Answer: Five and sixty-seven hundredths  

### Example 3: Adding Decimals  
Add $ 3.12 $ and $ 2.45 $.  
**Solution**:

$$
\begin{align*}
3.12 \\
+2.45 \\
\hline
5.57 \\
\end{align*}

$$  
✅ Answer: $ 5.57 $  

### Example 4: Comparing Lengths  
Which is longer: $ 4 \frac{3}{10} $ or $ 4 \frac{35}{100} $?  
**Solution**:  
Convert both to hundredths:  
- $ 4 \frac{3}{10} = 4 \frac{30}{100} $  
- $ 4 \frac{35}{100} $ is larger because $ 35 > 30 $.  
✅ Answer: $ 4 \frac{35}{100} $ is longer  

### Example 5: Mixed Operations  
Subtract $ 1.25 $ from $ 2.75 $.  
**Solution**:

$$
\begin{align*}
2.75 \\
-1.25 \\
\hline
1.50 \\
\end{align*}

$$  
✅ Answer: $ 1.50 $

## 6. Summary  
- A **hundredth** is $ \frac{1}{100} $ of a unit.  
- $ \frac{1}{10} = \frac{10}{100} $, so 1 tenth = 10 hundredths.  
- Decimals like $ 0.45 $ mean 45 hundredths.  
- To add/subtract, align decimals and perform operations.  
- Compare lengths by converting to the same denominator.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|------------|  
| $ \frac{a}{10} = \frac{10a}{100} $ | Converting tenths to hundredths |  
| $ \frac{a}{100} $ | Represents a hundredth |  
| $ \text{Decimal} = \text{Whole number} + \frac{\text{Digits after decimal}}{100} $ | Reading decimals |  
| $ \text{Sum} = \text{Align decimals and add} $ | Adding decimals |  
| $ \text{Difference} = \text{Align decimals and subtract} $ | Subtracting decimals |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mistake**: Confusing tenths and hundredths (e.g., $ \frac{1}{10} $ vs. $ \frac{1}{100} $).  
  **Fix**: Remember: 1 tenth = 10 hundredths.  
- **Mistake**: Misaligning decimals during addition/subtraction.  
  **Fix**: Always line up the decimal points vertically.  

### Exam Tips  
- Practice converting between tenths and hundredths frequently.  
- Use visual aids (like number lines) to compare decimal values.  

### Memory Aid  
**Rhyme**: "Tenths to hundredths, multiply by ten—just like a game!"
