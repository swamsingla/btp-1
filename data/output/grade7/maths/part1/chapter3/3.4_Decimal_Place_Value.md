## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Decimal Place Value  
Decimal place value extends the place value system to **numbers less than one**. It uses powers of 10 to represent smaller units, starting with tenths ($\frac{1}{10} $), hundredths ($\frac{1}{100} $), and so on.  
> **Each digit in a decimal number has a place value that depends on its position relative to the decimal point.**  

### 3.2 Structure of the Decimal System  
The decimal system is based on grouping units into **10 equal parts**. Here’s how it works:  
- **One unit** divided into 10 parts → **Tenths** ($\frac{1}{10} $)  
- Each tenth divided into 10 parts → **Hundredths** ($\frac{1}{100} $)  
- Each hundredth divided into 10 parts → **Thousandths** ($\frac{1}{1000} $)  
This pattern continues indefinitely, allowing us to represent infinitely small quantities.  

### 3.3 Relationship Between Place Values  
Every place value is **10 times larger** than the one to its right and **10 times smaller** than the one to its left.  
For example:  
- $ 1 $ tenth ($ 0.1 $) is $ 10 $ times larger than $ 1 $ hundredth ($ 0.01 $).  
- $ 1 $ hundredth ($ 0.01 $) is $ 10 $ times smaller than $ 1 $ tenth ($ 0.1 $).  

### 3.4 Extending the Place Value Chart  
The place value chart now includes positions to the **right of the decimal point**:  
| Position       | Name         | Value        |  
|----------------|--------------|--------------|  
| Hundreds       | Hundreds     | $ 100 $       |  
| Tens           | Tens         | $ 10 $        |  
| Ones           | Ones         | $ 1 $         |  
| Tenths         | Tenths       | $\frac{1}{10} $ |  
| Hundredths     | Hundredths   | $\frac{1}{100} $ |  
| Thousandths    | Thousandths  | $\frac{1}{1000} $ |  
This system ensures consistency in representing both large and small quantities.  

### 3.5 Why Split Into 10 Parts?  
The Indian place value system prioritizes **base 10**, making it easier to split units into 10 parts repeatedly. For example:  
- $ 1 $ unit → $ 10 $ tenths → $ 100 $ hundredths → $ 1000 $ thousandths  
This avoids confusion compared to splitting into other numbers (e.g., 4 or 5 parts), which would complicate calculations.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Identifying Place Values  
**Question**: Identify the place value of each digit in the number $ 2.345 $.  
**Solution**:  
- Digit **2**: Ones place ($ 2 \times 1 = 2 $)  
- Digit **3**: Tenths place ($ 3 \times \frac{1}{10} = 0.3 $)  
- Digit **4**: Hundredths place ($ 4 \times \frac{1}{100} = 0.04 $)  
- Digit **5**: Thousandths place ($ 5 \times \frac{1}{1000} = 0.005 $)  

### Example 2: Converting Fractions to Decimals  
**Question**: Convert $\frac{7}{100} $ to a decimal.  
**Solution**:  
- Since the denominator is $ 100 $, move the decimal point two places left in $ 7 $:

$$
  \frac{7}{100} = 0.07

$$  

### Example 3: Comparing Decimals  
**Question**: Which is greater, $0.45 $ or $ 0.405 $?  
**Solution**:  
1. Compare the tenths place: Both have $ 4 $.  
2. Compare the hundredths place: $ 5 $ vs. $ 0 $.  
3. Since $ 5 > 0 $, $ 0.45 > 0.405 $.  

### Example 4: Adding Decimals  
**Question**: Add $ 1.23 + 0.456 $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Align decimals vertically:  
   ```
     1.230  
   + 0.456  
   --------  
   ```  
2. Add column by column:  
   - Thousandths: $ 0 + 6 = 6 $  
   - Hundredths: $ 3 + 5 = 8 $  
   - Tenths: $ 2 + 4 = 6 $  
   - Ones: $ 1 + 0 = 1 $  
3. Result: $ 1.686 $  

### Example 5: Multiplying by Powers of 10  
**Question**: Multiply $ 0.07 $ by $ 100 $.  
**Solution**:  
- Moving the decimal two places right:

$$
  0.07 \times 100 = 7

$$

## 6. Summary  
- **Decimal place value** extends the place value system to numbers less than one.  
- Positions to the right of the decimal point represent **tenths, hundredths, thousandths**, etc.  
- Each place value is **10 times larger** than the next.  
- Decimal systems are based on **base 10**, simplifying calculations.  
- Converting fractions to decimals involves moving the decimal point according to the denominator.

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula                          | Description                              |  
|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------|  
| $ \text{Tenths} = \frac{1}{10} $ | One-tenth of a unit                      |  
| $ \text{Hundredths} = \frac{1}{100} $ | One-hundredth of a unit                 |  
| $ \text{Thousandths} = \frac{1}{1000} $ | One-thousandth of a unit                |  
| $ x \times 10^n $              | Moves decimal point $ n $ places right    |  
| $ x \div 10^n $                | Moves decimal point $ n $ places left     |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mistake**: Confusing tenths and hundredths (e.g., writing $ 0.1 $ as $ 0.01 $).  
  **Correction**: Count the number of decimal places: $ 0.1 $ has 1 place, $ 0.01 $ has 2.  

### Exam Tips  
- Always align decimals vertically during addition/subtraction.  
- When multiplying/dividing by powers of 10, count the number of zeros in the multiplier/divisor.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Left to Right, Ten Times More"**:  
- Move the decimal **left** when dividing by 10, **right** when multiplying.
