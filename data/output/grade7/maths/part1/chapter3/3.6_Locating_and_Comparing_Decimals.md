## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Understanding Decimal Placement  
**Decimal numbers** are used to represent parts of a whole, often smaller than 1 unit. For example, 1.4 means **1 unit and 4 tenths**. To visualize this:  
- Divide the gap between 1 and 2 into **10 equal parts**.  
- Each part represents **0.1 (one tenth)**.  
- So, 1.4 is **4 parts** away from 1, lying between 1 and 2.  

> **Tip**: Think of a ruler marked in millimeters. Each small tick represents a tenth of a centimeter.  

### 3.2 Locating Decimals on a Number Line  
To plot a decimal like **1.4** on a number line:  
1. Draw a straight line and mark the integer endpoints (e.g., 1 and 2).  
2. Divide the segment between 1 and 2 into **10 equal parts**.  
3. Count **4 parts** from 1 to reach 1.4.  

**Example**:  
If the number line has marks at 1.0, 1.1, ..., 1.9, then **1.4** is the fourth mark after 1.  

### 3.3 The Zero Dilemma  
Sometimes, people worry that adding zeros to decimals changes their value. However, **trailing zeros** don’t affect the magnitude of a decimal. For instance:  
- **0.2**, **0.20**, and **0.200** all represent **2 tenths**.  
- But **0.2**, **0.02**, and **0.002** are different because they represent **tenths, hundredths, and thousandths**, respectively.  

> **Important Rule**: Trailing zeros after the decimal point **don’t change the value** of the number.  

### 3.4 Comparing Decimals  
To compare decimals, examine them **digit by digit** from left to right:  
1. Compare the **units** place.  
2. If they’re equal, move to the **tenths** place.  
3. Continue to **hundredths**, **thousandths**, etc., until a difference is found.  

**Example**:  
Compare **6.456** and **6.465**:  
- Both have **6 units**.  
- Tenths place: **4 vs. 4** → equal.  
- Hundredths place: **5 vs. 6** → **6.465 is larger**.  

### 3.5 Identifying Equivalent Decimals  
Some decimals look different but are actually equal. For example:  
- **4.5** = **4.50** = **4.500** (all represent 4 units and 5 tenths).  
- **0.405** ≠ **4.05** because of the position of the decimal point.  

> **Rule**: Add or remove trailing zeros after the decimal point to match precision, but never before the decimal point.  

### 3.6 Visualizing Decimals with Magnification  
Decimals can be visualized by **magnifying segments** of a number line. For example:  
- To locate **4.185**, first focus on the segment between **4.1** and **4.2**.  
- Magnify further to show the **hundredths** place (**1.8**) and then the **thousandths** place (**5**).  

> **Visualize**: Imagine zooming in on a map to pinpoint a location.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Locating 1.4 on a Number Line  
**Problem**: Plot **1.4** on a number line between 1 and 2.  
**Solution**:  
1. Draw a number line with marks at **1.0, 1.1, 1.2, ..., 1.9**.  
2. Count **4 ticks** from 1.0 to reach **1.4**.  
3. Shade or label the point at **1.4**.  

### Example 2: Comparing 0.2 and 0.02  
**Problem**: Which is larger, **0.2** or **0.02**?  
**Solution**:  
1. Compare the **tenths** place: **2 vs. 0**.  
2. **0.2** has **2 tenths**, while **0.02** has **0 tenths**.  
3. Therefore, **0.2 > 0.02**.  

### Example 3: Identifying Equivalent Decimals  
**Problem**: Are **4.5** and **4.50** equal?  
**Solution**:  
1. **4.5** = **4 units + 5 tenths**.  
2. **4.50** = **4 units + 5 tenths + 0 hundredths**.  
3. Since trailing zeros don’t change the value, **4.5 = 4.50**.  

### Example 4: Comparing 6.456 and 6.465  
**Problem**: Is **6.456 < 6.465**?  
**Solution**:  
1. Compare units: **6 = 6** → equal.  
2. Compare tenths: **4 = 4** → equal.  
3. Compare hundredths: **5 < 6** → **6.456 < 6.465**.  

### Example 5: Finding Missing Decimal on a Number Line  
**Problem**: Identify the decimal represented by the box labeled **‘?’** in Figure (b).  
**Given**:  
- First segment: **4.1** to **4.2**.  
- Second segment: **4.18** to **4.19**.  
- Third segment: **4.185**.  

**Solution**:  
1. The third segment shows **4.185**.  
2. The missing number **‘?’** is **4.185**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy: Locate Decimals on a Number Line  
**Problem 1**: Plot **2.7** on a number line between 2 and 3.  
**Answer**: Divide the segment between 2 and 3 into 10 parts. Count 7 parts from 2 to reach 2.7.  

**Problem 2**: Which is larger, **0.3** or **0.03**?  
**Answer**: **0.3 > 0.03** (tenths place: 3 vs. 0).  

### 🟡 Medium: Compare Decimals  
**Problem 3**: Compare **5.23** and **5.203**.  
**Answer**:  
- Units: 5 = 5.  
- Tenths: 2 = 2.  
- Hundredths: 3 > 0 → **5.23 > 5.203**.  

**Problem 4**: Identify the decimal at position **‘c’** in the diagram.  
**Diagram**:  
- Between **7.0** and **7.5**, with labels **a**, **b**, **c**.  
- **b** is **7.5**.  
**Answer**: If **b** is 7.5 (midpoint), then **a** is **7.0**, **c** is **7.5**. Wait—this seems conflicting. Recheck the diagram logic.  

### 🔴 Hard: Multi-Step Comparison  
**Problem 5**: Determine which is larger: **3.1415** or **3.142**.  
**Answer**:  
- Units: 3 = 3.  
- Tenths: 1 = 1.  
- Hundredths: 4 = 4.  
- Thousandths: **1 < 2** → **3.1415 < 3.142**.

## 6. Summary  
- **Decimal numbers** represent parts of a whole (e.g., 1.4 = 1 unit + 4 tenths).  
- **Trailing zeros** after the decimal point do not change the value (e.g., 0.2 = 0.20 = 0.200).  
- To **locate decimals** on a number line, divide the unit segment into equal parts.  
- To **compare decimals**, examine digits from left to right until a difference is found.  
- **Magnification** helps visualize decimals with higher precision (e.g., 4.185).

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ **Misplacing decimal points**: Forgetting that 0.02 is much smaller than 0.2.  
- ❌ **Ignoring leading zeros**: Thinking 0.05 is equal to 0.5.  
- ❌ **Confusing equivalent decimals**: Believing 0.200 is different from 0.2.  

### Exam Tips  
- ✅ Always draw a number line for visualization, especially when comparing decimals.  
- ✅ Compare digits step by step, starting from the highest place value.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Left to Right, Digit by Digit"** – When comparing decimals, always check digits from left to right (units, tenths, hundredths, etc.).
