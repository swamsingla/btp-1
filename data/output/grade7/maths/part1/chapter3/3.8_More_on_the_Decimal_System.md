## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Understanding Decimal Errors  
> **Definition**: A decimal error occurs when a number is misread, miswritten, or incorrectly converted, leading to significant discrepancies in measurements or calculations.  

**Examples from Real Life**:  
- **Air Canada Fuel Miscalculation (1983)**: Ground staff loaded 22,300 **pounds** of fuel instead of **kilograms**, causing a dangerous situation.  
- **Medication Dosage Errors**: Misreading 0.05 mg as 0.5 mg can result in overdosing.  

**Key Takeaway**: Always verify units and check the position of the decimal point!  

---

### 3.2 Deceptive Decimal Notation  
> **Definition**: Decimal notation can appear similar to fractions but often represents different quantities depending on context.  

**Example 1: Time Calculation**  
A message says: “The bus will reach the station 4.5 hours post noon.”  
- **Step 1**: Recognize that 0.5 hours = $ \frac{1}{2} $ hour.  
- **Step 2**: Convert $ \frac{1}{2} $ hour to minutes: $ 60 \text{ min} \times \frac{1}{2} = 30 \text{ min} $.  
- **Conclusion**: The bus arrives at **4:30 p.m.** (not 4:05 p.m. or 4:50 p.m.).  

**Example 2: Cricket Overs**  
“Overs left: 5.5” means **5 full overs and 5 balls** (since 1 over = 6 balls).  
- **Step 1**: Split 5.5 into whole and fractional parts: $ 5 + 0.5 $.  
- **Step 2**: Convert $ 0.5 \times 6 = 3 $ balls.  
- **Conclusion**: Total overs remaining = 5 overs and 3 balls.  

**Key Rule**: Context determines whether a decimal represents a fraction or a combination of units.  

---

### 3.3 Historical Context of Decimals  
> **Definition**: Decimal fractions (fractions with denominators like $ \frac{1}{10}, \frac{1}{100}, \frac{1}{1000} $) were used by ancient Indian mathematicians like Aryabhata and Brahmagupta.  

**Why It Matters**:  
- Decimal systems simplify calculations compared to fractions.  
- They enable precise measurements in fields like astronomy, trade, and engineering.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Unit Conversion Error  
**Problem**: A carpenter is told to build a door 2.5 ft wide, but interprets it as 2 ft 5 inches. What is the difference in width?  

**Solution**:  
1. Convert 2.5 ft to inches:  
   $ 2.5 \text{ ft} \times 12 \text{ in/ft} = 30 \text{ in} $.  
2. Calculate the carpenter’s mistake:  
   $ 2 \text{ ft } 5 \text{ in} = (2 \times 12) + 5 = 29 \text{ in} $.  
3. Difference:  
   $ 30 \text{ in} - 29 \text{ in} = 1 \text{ inch} $.  

**Answer**: The door is **1 inch too narrow**.  

---

### Example 2: Fuel Load Calculation  
**Problem**: Air Canada mistakenly loaded 22,300 pounds instead of kilograms. How many kilograms is this?  

**Solution**:  
1. Use the conversion factor: $ 1 \text{ lb} = 0.453 \text{ kg} $.  
2. Multiply:  
   $ 22,300 \text{ lb} \times 0.453 \text{ kg/lb} = 10,101.9 \text{ kg} $.  
3. Compare to the intended load (assuming 22,300 kg):  
   $ 22,300 \text{ kg} - 10,101.9 \text{ kg} = 12,198.1 \text{ kg} $ less.  

**Answer**: The plane was missing **~12,198 kg** of fuel.  

---

### Example 3: Medication Dosage  
**Problem**: A patient is prescribed 0.05 mg of a drug, but the nurse administers 0.5 mg. How much excess medicine was given?  

**Solution**:  
1. Find the ratio:  
   $ \frac{0.5}{0.05} = 10 $.  
2. Conclusion: The dose was **10 times higher** than prescribed.  

**Answer**: Excess medicine: **10 times the intended dose**.

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**:  
**Problem 1**: Convert 0.5 hours to minutes.  
**Problem 2**: If 1 pound = 0.453 kg, find the weight of 10 pounds in kilograms.  

🟡 **Medium**:  
**Problem 3**: A bus departs at 10.25 a.m. and takes 2.5 hours. What time does it arrive?  
**Problem 4**: A recipe requires 0.25 liters of milk. How many milliliters is that?  

🔴 **Hard**:  
**Problem 5**: A doctor prescribes 0.025 mg of a drug. A nurse reads it as 0.25 mg. How many times greater is the nurse’s dose?  

---

**Answers**:  

**Problem 1**:  
$ 0.5 \text{ h} \times 60 \text{ min/h} = 30 \text{ min} $.  
**Answer**: **30 minutes**.  

**Problem 2**:  
$ 10 \text{ lb} \times 0.453 \text{ kg/lb} = 4.53 \text{ kg} $.  
**Answer**: **4.53 kg**.  

**Problem 3**:  
$ 10.25 \text{ a.m.} + 2.5 \text{ h} = 12.75 \text{ p.m.} = 12:45 p.m. $.  
**Answer**: **12:45 p.m.**  

**Problem 4**:  
$ 0.25 \text{ L} \times 1000 \text{ mL/L} = 250 \text{ mL} $.  
**Answer**: **250 mL**.  

**Problem 5**:  
$ \frac{0.25}{0.025} = 10 $.  
**Answer**: **10 times greater**.

## 6. Summary  
- **Decimal errors** can cause catastrophic outcomes (e.g., financial losses, flight accidents).  
- **Context matters**: 0.5 hours ≠ 0.5 seconds; 5.5 overs ≠ 5.5 balls.  
- **Convert units carefully**: Always match the units in calculations.  
- **Double-check readings**: Misplacing the decimal point changes meaning entirely.  
- **Historical significance**: Ancient Indians pioneered decimal fractions.

|--------|-------------|

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Misreading decimal points**: E.g., 0.05 vs. 0.5.  
   → **Correction**: Count decimal places carefully.  
2. **Ignoring units**: Mixing pounds and kilograms.  
   → **Correction**: Always convert to the same unit before calculation.  

### Exam Tips  
- Underline the decimal point in numbers to avoid misreading.  
- Verify unit conversions during problem-solving.  

### Memory Aid  
**"D.P. Check"**:  
Decimals & Points = **Double-check** every calculation involving decimals!
