## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Why Smaller Units Matter  
When measuring objects, sometimes **whole units** aren’t enough. For example, if a screw is slightly longer or shorter than another, even a tiny difference matters. This is where **smaller units** come in.  

> **Definition**: When we need more precision, we divide a standard unit (like a centimeter) into **equal smaller parts**. These parts allow us to measure objects with greater accuracy.  

**Example from the Source Text**:  
The screws in the story looked almost the same, but their lengths differed by a fraction of a centimeter. To measure this difference, Sonu used a scale divided into **tenths** (1/10) of a centimeter. This showed how **dividing units into smaller parts** enables precise comparisons.  

### 3.2 Tenths and Hundredths  
- **Tenths** are **one-tenth** of a unit (1/10).  
- **Hundredths** are **one-hundredth** of a unit (1/100).  

> **Definition**: A **tenth** is represented as $ \frac{1}{10} $, and a **hundredth** is $ \frac{1}{100} $. When combined with whole numbers, they form **decimals**, like $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ cm or $ 3 \frac{2}{10} $ cm.  

### 3.3 Reading Decimal Measurements  
To read a measurement like $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ cm:  
1. Start at the whole number (**2 cm**).  
2. Count **seven parts** of $ \frac{1}{10} $ cm (each part is 1 mm, since 1 cm = 10 mm).  

> **Explanation**: The total length is $ 2 + \frac{7}{10} = 2.7 $ cm. This method works for any decimal measurement.  

### 3.4 Units of Measurement  
Units like centimeters, meters, and grams are often divided into smaller parts for precision. For instance:  
- 1 meter = 10 decimeters (dm) = 100 centimeters (cm).  
- 1 centimeter = 10 millimeters (mm).  

This division allows us to measure objects that are **not whole units**.  

### 3.5 Locating Decimals on a Scale  
On a ruler, each centimeter is marked with **small ticks** representing tenths. For example:  
- The tick at $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ cm is **7 mm** from the 2 cm mark.  

> **Tip**: Always count the number of ticks between whole numbers to locate decimals accurately.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Converting Mixed Numbers to Decimals  
Convert $ 3 \frac{2}{10} $ cm to a decimal.  

**Solution**:  
1. Write the whole number as is: 3.  
2. Add the fraction: $ 3 + \frac{2}{10} = 3.2 $ cm.  

**Answer**: $ 3.2 $ cm  

### Example 2: Measuring Objects  
If a pencil measures $ 1 \frac{5}{10} $ cm, how many millimeters is that?  

**Solution**:  
1. Remember: $ 1 $ cm = $ 10 $ mm.  
2. Convert $ \frac{5}{10} $ cm to mm: $ \frac{5}{10} \times 10 = 5 $ mm.  
3. Total: $ 1 $ cm + $ 5 $ mm = $ 15 $ mm.  

**Answer**: $ 15 $ mm  

### Example 3: Comparing Decimals  
Compare $ 2 \frac{7}{10} $ cm and $ 3 \frac{2}{10} $ cm. Which is larger?  

**Solution**:  
1. Convert both to decimals: $ 2.7 $ cm vs. $ 3.2 $ cm.  
2. Compare whole numbers: $ 2 < 3 $.  
3. Conclusion: $ 3.2 $ cm is larger.  

**Answer**: $ 3.2 $ cm is larger.  

### Example 4: Adding Decimals  
Add $ 1 \frac{4}{10} $ cm and $ 2 \frac{6}{10} $ cm.  

**Solution**:  
1. Convert to decimals: $ 1.4 $ cm + $ 2.6 $ cm.  
2. Add: $ 1.4 + 2.6 = 4.0 $ cm.  

**Answer**: $ 4.0 $ cm  

### Example 5: Subtracting Decimals  
Subtract $ 1 \frac{3}{10} $ cm from $ 3 \frac{5}{10} $ cm.  

**Solution**:  
1. Convert to decimals: $ 3.5 $ cm – $ 1.3 $ cm.  
2. Subtract: $ 3.5 - 1.3 = 2.2 $ cm.  

**Answer**: $ 2.2 $ cm

## 6. Summary  
- **Smaller units** (like tenths and hundredths) improve measurement accuracy.  
- $ \frac{1}{10} $ is a **tenth**, and $ \frac{1}{100} $ is a **hundredth**.  
- Decimals combine whole numbers and fractions, e.g., $ 2 \frac{7}{10} = 2.7 $.  
- Rulers are divided into **tenths** (1/10 cm) for precise readings.  
- You can **add** and **subtract** decimals like regular numbers.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \text{Whole number} + \frac{\text{Part}}{10} $ | Converts mixed numbers to decimals (e.g., $ 2 + \frac{7}{10} = 2.7 $). |  
| $ \text{Decimal} \times 10 $ | Converts tenths to millimeters (e.g., $ 0.5 $ cm = $ 5 $ mm). |  
| $ \text{Decimal}_1 + \text{Decimal}_2 $ | Adds decimals (e.g., $ 2.3 + 1.7 = 4.0 $). |  
| $ \text{Decimal}_1 - \text{Decimal}_2 $ | Subtracts decimals (e.g., $ 3.5 - 1.3 = 2.2 $). |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ **Misreading ticks**: Confusing the number of ticks with the actual value.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Count the ticks carefully and match them to the scale markings.  
- ❌ **Ignoring zeros**: Writing $ 2.7 $ as $ 2.70 $ or vice versa.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Ensure decimals are written correctly (e.g., $ 2.7 = 2.70 $).  

### Exam Tips  
- Always **check the scale** for divisions (tenths/hundredths) before measuring.  
- Practice **converting between units** (e.g., cm to mm) to avoid confusion.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Tenths are big, hundredths are small; 10 parts make a tenth, 100 parts make a whole!"**
