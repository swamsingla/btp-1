## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how the roots of a polynomial relate to its graph? This topic explores the **geometrical meaning of the zeroes of a polynomial**, linking algebraic solutions to visual interpretations. By studying this, you’ll discover how the roots of a polynomial appear on its graph—specifically, where the graph crosses the x-axis. This bridges abstract algebra with concrete visuals, helping you visualize solutions to equations like $ y = ax + b $ or $ y = ax^2 + bx + c $.  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Linear Polynomials: Zeros and Graphs  
> **Definition**: A **zero of a polynomial** is a real number $ k $ such that $ p(k) = 0 $.  

For a **linear polynomial** $ ax + b $, where $ a \neq 0 $:  
- The graph of $ y = ax + b $ is a **straight line**.  
- The **zero** of the polynomial is the **x-coordinate** where the line crosses the x-axis.  

**Proof**:  
To find the zero, solve $ ax + b = 0 $.

$$  
ax + b = 0 \implies x = -\frac{b}{a}

$$  
Thus, the graph intersects the x-axis at $ \left( -\frac{b}{a}, 0 \right) $.  

**Example**: For $ y = 2x + 3 $, the zero is $ x = -\frac{3}{2} $, so the graph crosses the x-axis at $ (-1.5, 0) $.  

---

### 3.2 Quadratic Polynomials: Zeros and Parabolas  
> **Definition**: A **quadratic polynomial** is of the form $ ax^2 + bx + c $, where $ a \neq 0 $.  

The graph of $ y = ax^2 + bx + c $ is a **parabola**, which opens **upward** if $ a > 0 $ or **downward** if $ a < 0 $.  

**Geometrical Meaning of Zeroes**:  
- The **zeros** of a quadratic polynomial are the **x-coordinates** where the parabola intersects the x-axis.  
- These correspond to the **real roots** of the equation $ ax^2 + bx + c = 0 $.  

**Example**: Consider $ y = x^2 - 3x - 4 $.  
- Solving $ x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0 $:

$$  
  x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{(-3)^2 - 4(1)(-4)}}{2(1)} = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{25}}{2} = \frac{3 \pm 5}{2}

$$  
  So, $ x = 4 $ or $ x = -1 $.  
- The graph intersects the x-axis at $ (-1, 0) $ and $ (4, 0) $.  

---

### 3.3 General Case for Any Polynomial  
For a polynomial $ p(x) $:  
- The **number of zeroes** corresponds to the **maximum number of times** the graph can intersect the x-axis.  
- A **linear polynomial** always has **one zero**.  
- A **quadratic polynomial** can have **two zeroes**, **one zero**, or **no real zeroes** depending on the discriminant $ D = b^2 - 4ac $.  

**Discriminant Rule**:  
- If $ D > 0 $: Two distinct real zeroes (graph crosses x-axis twice).  
- If $ D = 0 $: One real zero (graph touches x-axis once).  
- If $ D < 0 $: No real zeroes (graph does not cross x-axis).

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Find the zero of $ y = -3x + 6 $ and interpret it geometrically.  

**Solution**:  
Set $ y = 0 $:

$$  
-3x + 6 = 0 \implies x = \frac{6}{3} = 2

$$  
The graph of $ y = -3x + 6 $ intersects the x-axis at $ (2, 0) $.  

---

### Example 2:  
Graph $ y = x^2 - 4 $ and determine its zeroes.  

**Solution**:  
Factorize:

$$  
y = x^2 - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)

$$  
Zeroes: $ x = 2 $ and $ x = -2 $.  
Graph: A parabola opening upward, crossing the x-axis at $ (2, 0) $ and $ (-2, 0) $.  

---

### Example 3:  
Determine the number of zeroes for $ y = x^2 + 2x + 5 $.  

**Solution**:  
Compute discriminant $ D = b^2 - 4ac = (2)^2 - 4(1)(5) = 4 - 20 = -16 $.  
Since $ D < 0 $, the quadratic has **no real zeroes**. The graph does not intersect the x-axis.  

---

### Example 4:  
Sketch the graph of $ y = -2x^2 + 4x $ and identify its zeroes.  

**Solution**:  
Factorize:

$$  
y = -2x(x - 2)

$$  
Zeroes: $ x = 0 $ and $ x = 2 $.  
Graph: A downward-opening parabola (since $ a = -2 < 0 $) crossing the x-axis at $ (0, 0) $ and $ (2, 0) $.  

---

### Example 5:  
Explain the geometrical meaning of the zero $ x = 3 $ for $ y = 2x - 6 $.  

**Solution**:  
Substitute $ x = 3 $: $ y = 2(3) - 6 = 0 $. The graph crosses the x-axis at $ (3, 0) $, confirming $ x = 3 $ is a zero.

## 6. Summary  
- The **zero** of a polynomial is the x-coordinate where its graph intersects the x-axis.  
- A **linear polynomial** $ ax + b $ has exactly one zero at $ x = -\frac{b}{a} $.  
- A **quadratic polynomial** $ ax^2 + bx + c $ may have two, one, or no real zeroes depending on the discriminant $ D = b^2 - 4ac $.  
- The graph of a quadratic polynomial is a **parabola** opening upward ($ a > 0 $) or downward ($ a < 0 $).  
- The **discriminant** determines the nature of the zeroes:  
  - $ D > 0 $: Two distinct real zeroes.  
  - $ D = 0 $: One real zero (repeated root).  
  - $ D < 0 $: No real zeroes.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ x = -\frac{b}{a} $ | Zero of linear polynomial $ ax + b $. |  
| $ D = b^2 - 4ac $ | Discriminant of quadratic polynomial $ ax^2 + bx + c $. |  
| $ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a} $ | Roots of quadratic equation $ ax^2 + bx + c = 0 $. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ Confusing **zeroes** with **y-intercepts**: The y-intercept occurs at $ x = 0 $, while zeroes are x-values where $ y = 0 $.  
- ❌ Incorrectly assuming all quadratics have two zeroes: Remember $ D < 0 $ results in no real zeroes.  

### Exam Tips  
- Always sketch the graph mentally for quadratics to determine the number of zeroes.  
- Verify calculations for discriminant to avoid misclassifying zeroes.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Zeros are where the graph hits the ground!"**  
Think of the x-axis as the ground; zeroes are the points where the graph touches or crosses it.
