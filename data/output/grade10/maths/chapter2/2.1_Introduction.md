## 2. Introduction  
Polynomials are fundamental building blocks in algebra, appearing in everyday scenarios like calculating areas, modeling motion, and analyzing data. This section introduces the basics of polynomials, including their classification by degree and the concept of zeroes. By the end, you’ll grasp how to identify and evaluate polynomials and understand the significance of their zeroes.  

---  

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definitions and Types of Polynomials  
A **polynomial** is an expression consisting of variables and coefficients combined using addition, subtraction, and multiplication. The **degree** of a polynomial is the highest exponent of the variable. For example:  
- $ 4x + 2 $: Degree 1 (**linear**)  
- $ 2y^2 - 3y + 4 $: Degree 2 (**quadratic**)  
- $ 5x^3 - 4x^2 + x - 1 $: Degree 3 (**cubic**)  

> **Definition**: A **linear polynomial** is of the form $ ax + b $, where $ a \neq 0 $.  
> **Definition**: A **quadratic polynomial** is of the form $ ax^2 + bx + c $, where $ a \neq 0 $.  
> **Definition**: A **cubic polynomial** is of the form $ ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d $, where $ a \neq 0 $.  

### 3.2 Evaluating Polynomials  
To evaluate a polynomial at a specific value of $ x $, substitute the value into the expression. For instance, for $ p(x) = x^2 - 3x - 4 $:  
- $ p(2) = 2^2 - 3(2) - 4 = 4 - 6 - 4 = -6 $  
- $ p(4) = 4^2 - 3(4) - 4 = 16 - 12 - 4 = 0 $  

> **Property**: The result of substituting $ x = k $ into a polynomial $ p(x) $ is called $ p(k) $.  

### 3.3 Zeroes of Polynomials  
A **zero** of a polynomial $ p(x) $ is a value $ k $ such that $ p(k) = 0 $. For example, for $ p(x) = x^2 - 3x - 4 $, the zeroes are $ x = -1 $ and $ x = 4 $ since $ p(-1) = 0 $ and $ p(4) = 0 $.  

> **Theorem**: If $ k $ is a zero of $ p(x) = ax + b $, then $ k = -\frac{b}{a} $.  
> **Proof**:  
> Given $ p(k) = 0 $, substitute into $ ax + b = 0 $:  
> $ ak + b = 0 \Rightarrow ak = -b \Rightarrow k = -\frac{b}{a} $.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Identifying Polynomial Type  
Determine the type (linear, quadratic, cubic) of the polynomial $ p(x) = 3x^2 - 5x + 7 $.  
**Solution**:  
- The highest exponent of $ x $ is 2.  
- Since the degree is 2, it is a **quadratic polynomial**.  

### Example 2: Finding the Value of a Polynomial  
Evaluate $ q(y) = 2y^3 - y + 1 $ at $ y = -1 $.  
**Solution**:

$$
q(-1) = 2(-1)^3 - (-1) + 1 = 2(-1) + 1 + 1 = -2 + 1 + 1 = 0

$$  
Thus, $q(-1) = 0 $.  

### Example 3: Solving for Zeros of a Linear Polynomial  
Find the zero of $ r(z) = 4z - 8 $.  
**Solution**:  
Set $ r(z) = 0 $:

$$
4z - 8 = 0 \Rightarrow 4z = 8 \Rightarrow z = \frac{8}{4} = 2

$$  
The zero is $z = 2 $.  

### Example 4: Verifying Zeroes of a Quadratic Polynomial  
Check if $ x = 3 $ is a zero of $ s(x) = x^2 - 5x + 6 $.  
**Solution**:  
Substitute $ x = 3 $:

$$
s(3) = 3^2 - 5(3) + 6 = 9 - 15 + 6 = 0

$$  
Since $s(3) = 0 $, $ x = 3 $ is indeed a zero.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1**: Identify the type of the polynomial $ t(x) = 7x^4 - 3x^2 + 2 $.  
**Answer**: The degree is 4, so it is a **quartic polynomial** (not covered in this section but serves as a review).  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2**: Evaluate $ u(w) = w^3 - 2w^2 + 4 $ at $ w = 2 $.  
**Answer**:

$$
u(2) = (2)^3 - 2(2)^2 + 4 = 8 - 8 + 4 = 4

$$  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3**: Find the zeroes of $v(m) = 3m^2 - 12m + 9 $.  
**Answer**:  
Factor the equation:

$$
3m^2 - 12m + 9 = 0 \Rightarrow m^2 - 4m + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow (m - 1)(m - 3) = 0

$$  
Thus, the zeroes are $m = 1 $ and $ m = 3 $.  

---  

## 6. Summary  
- A **polynomial** is an expression with variables and coefficients.  
- The **degree** determines its type: linear (degree 1), quadratic (degree 2), cubic (degree 3).  
- To evaluate a polynomial at $ x = k $, substitute $ k $ into the expression.  
- A **zero** of a polynomial is a value $ k $ where $ p(k) = 0 $.  
- For linear polynomials $ ax + b $, the zero is $ k = -\frac{b}{a} $.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ p(k) = 0 $ | Definition of a zero of a polynomial |  
| $ k = -\frac{b}{a} $ | Zero of a linear polynomial $ ax + b $ |  
| Degree of $ ax^n + ...$ | Highest exponent $ n $ |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- Confusing the **degree** of a polynomial with the **coefficient** of the highest term.  
- Forgetting to simplify expressions before evaluating $ p(k) $.  

### Memory Aid  
Use the acronym **Q-U-A-D-R-A-T-I-C** to remember that *quadratic* relates to squares ($ x^2 $).  

### Exam Tip  
Always verify your answers by substituting values back into the original polynomial.
