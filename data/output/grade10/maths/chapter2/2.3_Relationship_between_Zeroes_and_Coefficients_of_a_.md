## 2. Introduction

In this section, you’ll learn:  
- How to find the zeroes of a quadratic polynomial.  
- The formulas linking the **sum of zeroes** and **product of zeroes** to the coefficients of the polynomial.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Key Terms  
- **Zero of a polynomial**: A value of $ x $ for which $ p(x) = 0 $.  
- **Coefficients**: The numerical factors multiplying variables in a polynomial. For $ ax^2 + bx + c $, $ a $, $ b $, and $ c $ are coefficients.  
- **Quadratic polynomial**: A polynomial of degree 2, written as $ ax^2 + bx + c $, where $ a \neq 0 $.  

> **Important Theorem**: *If $ \alpha $ and $ \beta $ are the zeroes of a quadratic polynomial $ p(x) = ax^2 + bx + c $, then:*  
> - **Sum of zeroes**: $ \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} $  
> - **Product of zeroes**: $ \alpha \beta = \frac{c}{a} $  

### 3.2 Derivation of Relationships  
Consider the general quadratic polynomial $ p(x) = ax^2 + bx + c $. Suppose $ \alpha $ and $ \beta $ are its zeroes. Then, $ p(x) $ can be expressed as:

$$
p(x) = k(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)

$$  
Expanding the right-hand side:

$$
p(x) = k[x^2 - (\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha\beta]

$$

$$
p(x) = kx^2 - k(\alpha + \beta)x + k\alpha\beta

$$  
Compare this to the original polynomial $ ax^2 + bx + c $:  
- Coefficient of $ x^2 $: $ a = k $  
- Coefficient of $ x $: $ b = -k(\alpha + \beta) $  
- Constant term: $ c = k\alpha\beta $  

Substituting $ k = a $:  
- $ b = -a(\alpha + \beta) \Rightarrow \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} $  
- $ c = a\alpha\beta \Rightarrow \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} $  

This proves the fundamental relationships between zeroes and coefficients.  

### 3.3 Example Walkthrough  
Take the polynomial $ p(x) = 2x^2 - 8x + 6 $. Factor it as:

$$
2x^2 - 8x + 6 = 2(x^2 - 4x + 3) = 2(x - 1)(x - 3)

$$  
Thus, the zeroes are $ x = 1 $ and $ x = 3 $. Compute the sum and product:  
- **Sum**: $ 1 + 3 = 4 $. According to the formula, $ -\frac{-8}{2} = 4 $. ✅  
- **Product**: $ 1 \times 3 = 3 $. According to the formula, $ \frac{6}{2} = 3 $. ✅  

Similarly, for $ p(x) = 3x^2 + 5x - 2 $:  
- Factored form: $ (3x - 1)(x + 2) $, zeroes at $ x = \frac{1}{3} $ and $ x = -2 $.  
- **Sum**: $ \frac{1}{3} + (-2) = -\frac{5}{3} $. Formula: $ -\frac{5}{3} $. ✅  
- **Product**: $ \frac{1}{3} \times (-2) = -\frac{2}{3} $. Formula: $ \frac{-2}{3} $. ✅

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Find the zeroes of $ p(x) = x^2 + 7x + 10 $ and verify the relationships.  

**Solution**:  
Factorize:

$$
x^2 + 7x + 10 = (x + 2)(x + 5)

$$  
Zeroes: $ x = -2 $ and $ x = -5 $.  
- **Sum**: $ -2 + (-5) = -7 $. Formula: $ -\frac{7}{1} = -7 $. ✅  
- **Product**: $ (-2)(-5) = 10 $. Formula: $ \frac{10}{1} = 10 $. ✅  

### Example 2:  
Given $ p(x) = 4x^2 - 12x + 9 $, find its zeroes.  

**Solution**:  
Factorize:

$$
4x^2 - 12x + 9 = (2x - 3)^2

$$  
Zeroes: $ x = \frac{3}{2} $ (repeated root).  
- **Sum**: $ \frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{2} = 3 $. Formula: $ -\frac{-12}{4} = 3 $. ✅  
- **Product**: $ \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{4} $. Formula: $ \frac{9}{4} $. ✅  

### Example 3:  
If the sum and product of zeroes of a quadratic polynomial are $ 5 $ and $ 6 $, find the polynomial.  

**Solution**:  
Using the standard form $ x^2 - (\text{sum})x + (\text{product}) $:

$$
x^2 - 5x + 6

$$  
Verification: Factorize $ x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3) $. Zeroes: $ 2 $ and $ 3 $.  
- **Sum**: $ 2 + 3 = 5 $, **Product**: $ 2 \times 3 = 6 $. ✅  

### Example 4:  
For $ p(x) = 5x^2 - 13x + 6 $, compute the sum and product of zeroes.  

**Solution**:  
- **Sum**: $ -\frac{-13}{5} = \frac{13}{5} $.  
- **Product**: $ \frac{6}{5} $.  

Check via factorization:

$$
5x^2 - 13x + 6 = (5x - 3)(x - 2)

$$  
Zeroes: $ x = \frac{3}{5} $, $ x = 2 $.  
- **Sum**: $ \frac{3}{5} + 2 = \frac{13}{5} $, **Product**: $ \frac{3}{5} \times 2 = \frac{6}{5} $. ✅  

### Example 5:  
If $ \alpha $ and $ \beta $ are zeroes of $ p(x) = 2x^2 - 6x + 4 $, find $ \alpha^2 + \beta^2 $.  

**Solution**:  
Use identity: $ \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta $.  
From the polynomial:  
- $ \alpha + \beta = -\frac{-6}{2} = 3 $, $ \alpha\beta = \frac{4}{2} = 2 $.  
Compute:

$$
\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (3)^2 - 2(2) = 9 - 4 = 5

$$

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
1. Find the sum and product of zeroes for $ p(x) = x^2 - 7x + 12 $.  
2. Given $ p(x) = 3x^2 - 5x + 2 $, verify the sum and product of its zeroes.  

### 🟡 Medium  
3. If the sum of zeroes of $ p(x) = 6x^2 + bx + 12 $ is $ -5 $, find $ b $.  
4. Construct a quadratic polynomial with zeroes $ \frac{1}{2} $ and $ -3 $.  

### 🔴 Hard  
5. For $ p(x) = 2x^2 - 7x + 3 $, compute $ \alpha^3 + \beta^3 $ using identities.  

**Answers**:  

1. **Answer**:  
   - Sum: $ 7 $, Product: $ 12 $.  
   - Verification: $ x^2 - 7x + 12 = (x - 3)(x - 4) $. Zeroes: $ 3 $, $ 4 $.  

2. **Answer**:  
   - Sum: $ \frac{5}{3} $, Product: $ \frac{2}{3} $.  
   - Factorization: $ (3x - 1)(x - 2) $. Zeroes: $ \frac{1}{3} $, $ 2 $.  

3. **Answer**:  
   - $ \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{6} = -5 \Rightarrow b = 30 $.  

4. **Answer**:  
   - Polynomial: $ x^2 - (\frac{1}{2} + (-3))x + (\frac{1}{2})(-3) = x^2 + \frac{5}{2}x - \frac{3}{2} $.  

5. **Answer**:  
   - $ \alpha + \beta = \frac{7}{2} $, $ \alpha\beta = \frac{3}{2} $.  
   - $ \alpha^3 + \beta^3 = (\alpha + \beta)^3 - 3\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta) = \left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^3 - 3 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{2} = \frac{343}{8} - \frac{63}{4} = \frac{343 - 126}{8} = \frac{217}{8} $.

## 6. Summary  
- **Sum of zeroes**: $ \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} $  
- **Product of zeroes**: $ \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} $  
- These relationships hold for any quadratic polynomial $ ax^2 + bx + c $.  
- Factoring helps identify zeroes, while the formulas link them to coefficients.  
- Applications include solving equations and constructing polynomials from known zeroes.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} $ | Sum of zeroes of $ ax^2 + bx + c $ |  
| $ \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} $ | Product of zeroes of $ ax^2 + bx + c $ |  
| $ p(x) = k(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) $ | General form of a quadratic polynomial |  
| $ \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta $ | Identity for sum of squares |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mixing up signs**: Forgetting the negative sign in $ -\frac{b}{a} $.  
  → **Correction**: Remember $ \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} $, not $ \frac{b}{a} $.  
- **Ignoring leading coefficient**: When factoring, scaling affects the zeroes.  
  → **Correction**: Always divide by the leading coefficient after factoring.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Verify results**: Substitute zeroes into the polynomial to confirm they satisfy $ p(x) = 0 $.  
- **Use identities**: For advanced problems, leverage identities like $ \alpha^3 + \beta^3 $ instead of computing individually.  

### Memory Aid  
**"SOAP"** for remembering the formulas:  
- **S**um: $ -\frac{b}{a} $  
- **O**pposite sign of $ b $, **A**lways positive denominator, **P**roduct is $ \frac{c}{a} $.
