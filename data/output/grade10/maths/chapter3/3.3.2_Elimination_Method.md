## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 What is the Elimination Method?  
The **elimination method** is an algebraic technique used to solve a system of two linear equations in two variables. It involves **eliminating one variable** by making its coefficients equal (or opposites) across the equations, then solving the resulting single-variable equation.  

> **Definition**: The elimination method removes one variable by manipulating equations to create identical coefficients for that variable, allowing it to cancel out when equations are combined.  

### 3.2 Steps of the Elimination Method  
The process follows these structured steps:  
1. **Multiply equations** by non-zero constants to make the coefficients of one variable equal.  
2. **Add or subtract** the equations to eliminate that variable.  
3. **Solve** the resulting equation for the remaining variable.  
4. **Substitute** the found value back into one of the original equations to find the other variable.  

#### Case Analysis:  
- **Case 1**: If the elimination results in a valid equation (e.g., $ x = 5 $), the system has a **unique solution**.  
- **Case 2**: If the elimination leads to a **true statement** (e.g., $ 0 = 0 $), the system has **infinitely many solutions**.  
- **Case 3**: If the elimination yields a **false statement** (e.g., $ 0 = 5 $), the system is **inconsistent** (no solution).  

### 3.3 Applying the Elimination Method  
Consider the example from the source text:

$$
\begin{cases}
9x - 4y = 2000 \quad \text{(Equation 1)} \\
7x - 3y = 2000 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Step 1**: Make coefficients of $ y $ equal. Multiply Equation 1 by 3 and Equation 2 by 4:

$$
\begin{cases}
27x - 12y = 6000 \quad \text{(Equation 3)} \\
28x - 12y = 8000 \quad \text{(Equation 4)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Step 2**: Subtract Equation 3 from Equation 4 to eliminate $ y $:

$$
(28x - 27x) - (12y - 12y) = 8000 - 6000 \implies x = 2000

$$  
**Step 3**: Substitute $ x = 2000 $ into Equation 1:

$$
9(2000) - 4y = 2000 \implies 18000 - 4y = 2000 \implies y = 4000

$$  
Thus, the solution is $ x = 2000 $, $ y = 4000 $.  

### 3.4 Key Observations  
- The elimination method is particularly effective when coefficients are multiples of each other.  
- Always verify the solution by substituting it into the original equations.  
- The method can be applied to eliminate either variable, depending on which simplifies calculations.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1:  
Solve the system:

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 3y = 12 \quad \text{(Equation 1)} \\
4x - y = 10 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
**Step 1**: Make coefficients of $ y $ equal. Multiply Equation 2 by 3:

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 3y = 12 \quad \text{(Equation 1)} \\
12x - 3y = 30 \quad \text{(Equation 3)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Step 2**: Add Equations 1 and 3 to eliminate $ y $:

$$
(2x + 12x) + (3y - 3y) = 12 + 30 \implies 14x = 42 \implies x = 3

$$  
**Step 3**: Substitute $ x = 3 $ into Equation 2:

$$
4(3) - y = 10 \implies 12 - y = 10 \implies y = 2

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = 3 $, $ y = 2 $.  

### Example 2:  
Solve:

$$
\begin{cases}
5x + 2y = 16 \quad \text{(Equation 1)} \\
3x - 4y = 8 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
**Step 1**: Make coefficients of $ y $ equal. Multiply Equation 1 by 2:

$$
\begin{cases}
10x + 4y = 32 \quad \text{(Equation 3)} \\
3x - 4y = 8 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Step 2**: Add Equations 3 and 2 to eliminate $ y $:

$$
(10x + 3x) + (4y - 4y) = 32 + 8 \implies 13x = 40 \implies x = \frac{40}{13}

$$  
**Step 3**: Substitute $ x = \frac{40}{13} $ into Equation 1:

$$
5\left(\frac{40}{13}\right) + 2y = 16 \implies \frac{200}{13} + 2y = 16 \implies 2y = 16 - \frac{200}{13} = \frac{208 - 200}{13} = \frac{8}{13} \implies y = \frac{4}{13}

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = \frac{40}{13} $, $ y = \frac{4}{13} $.  

### Example 3:  
Determine if the system:

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 3y = 6 \quad \text{(Equation 1)} \\
4x + 6y = 12 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
has infinite solutions.  

**Solution**:  
**Step 1**: Notice Equation 2 is a multiple of Equation 1 (multiply Equation 1 by 2):

$$
4x + 6y = 12

$$  
**Step 2**: Subtract Equation 1 multiplied by 2 from Equation 2:

$$
(4x + 6y) - (4x + 6y) = 12 - 12 \implies 0 = 0

$$  
Since this is a true statement, the system has **infinitely many solutions**.

## 6. Summary  
- The elimination method eliminates one variable by equating coefficients.  
- Steps: Multiply equations, add/subtract to eliminate a variable, solve, substitute.  
- Systems may have **one solution**, **infinitely many solutions**, or **no solution**.  
- Always verify solutions by plugging into original equations.  
- The method is efficient for systems with integer coefficients.

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
1. **Incorrect Multiplication Factors**: Forgetting to multiply *both* sides of an equation.  
   ✅ Correction: Always apply the multiplier to the entire equation.  
2. **Sign Errors During Subtraction**: Mishandling negative signs.  
   ✅ Correction: Use parentheses to track signs (e.g., $ -(12y - 12y) $).  

### Exam Tips  
- **Check for special cases** (infinite/no solutions) early to avoid unnecessary work.  
- **Use the elimination method** when coefficients are easily adjustable.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Equalize, Eliminate, Solve"** — Remember the three-step process: make coefficients equal, eliminate a variable, solve the simplified equation.
