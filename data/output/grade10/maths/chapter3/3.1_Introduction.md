## 3. Explanation  
### 3.1 Definition of a Pair of Linear Equations  
A **linear equation in two variables** is an equation that can be written in the form:

$$

ax + by + c = 0

$$  
where $a $, $ b $, and $ c $ are constants, and $ x $ and $ y $ are variables.  

> **Important Theorem**: Any real-world situation involving two unknown quantities can be modeled using a pair of linear equations in two variables.  

### 3.2 Formulating Equations from Real-Life Scenarios  
The source text illustrates this with Akhila’s problem:  
- Let $ x $ be the number of rides on the Giant Wheel.  
- Let $ y $ be the number of Hoopla games.  

From the problem:  
1. The number of Hoopla games is half the number of rides:

$$

y = \frac{1}{2}x

$$  
   or equivalently,

$$

x - 2y = 0

$$  

2. Total cost: $3x + 4y = 20 $.  

These two equations together form a **pair of linear equations in two variables**, which can be solved to find the values of $ x $ and $ y $.  

### 3.3 General Form of a Pair of Equations  
Any pair of linear equations can be expressed as:

$$

a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0

$$

$$

a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0

$$  
Here, $a_1, b_1, c_1 $ and $ a_2, b_2, c_2 $ are constants, while $ x $ and $ y $ are the variables.  

> **Tip**: Always identify the variables and translate the problem’s conditions into equations carefully.

## 4. Solved Examples  
### Example 1: Solving Akhila’s Problem  
**Problem**: Solve the system:

$$

x - 2y = 0

$$

$$

3x + 4y = 20

$$  

**Solution**:  
1. From the first equation, express $x $ in terms of $ y $:

$$

x = 2y

$$  
2. Substitute $x = 2y $ into the second equation:

$$

3(2y) + 4y = 20

$$

$$

6y + 4y = 20

$$ 

$$

10y = 20

$$

$$

y = 2

$$  
3. Substitute $y = 2 $ back into $ x = 2y $:

$$

x = 2(2) = 4

$$  

**Answer**: Akhila had **4 rides** and played Hoopla **2 times**.  

---

### Example 2: Translating a Scenario into Equations  
**Problem**: A shopkeeper sells pencils and pens. The price of a pencil is ₹2 and a pen is ₹5. If a customer buys 10 items totaling ₹35, how many pencils and pens did they buy?  

**Solution**:  
1. Let $x $ = number of pencils, $ y $ = number of pens.  
2. Total items: $ x + y = 10 $  
3. Total cost: $ 2x + 5y = 35 $  

**System of Equations**:

$$

x + y = 10

$$

$$

2x + 5y = 35

$$  

**Answer**: Solving gives $x = 5 $ pencils and $ y = 5 $ pens.  

---

### Example 3: Checking Consistency of Equations  
**Problem**: Determine if the equations $ 2x + 3y = 6 $ and $ 4x + 6y = 12 $ are consistent.  

**Solution**:  
1. Simplify the second equation by dividing by 2:

$$

2x + 3y = 6

$$  
2. Notice that both equations are identical:

$$

2x + 3y = 6

$$

$$

2x + 3y = 6

$$  
3. These represent the **same line**, so infinitely many solutions exist.  

**Conclusion**: The system is **consistent** with infinitely many solutions.  

---

### Example 4: Identifying Parallel Lines  
**Problem**: Are the equations $x + y = 3 $ and $ 2x + 2y = 7 $ parallel?  

**Solution**:  
1. Rewrite both in slope-intercept form:  
   - First equation: $ y = -x + 3 $ (slope = -1)  
   - Second equation: $ 2x + 2y = 7 \Rightarrow y = -x + \frac{7}{2} $ (slope = -1)  
2. Both lines have the same slope (-1) but different intercepts.  

**Conclusion**: The lines are **parallel** and **do not intersect**.  

---

### Example 5: Real-World Application  
**Problem**: A farmer has chickens and cows. Together, they have 20 heads and 60 legs. How many chickens and cows are there?  

**Solution**:  
1. Let $ x $ = number of chickens, $ y $ = number of cows.  
2. Heads: $ x + y = 20 $  
3. Legs: $ 2x + 4y = 60 $  

**System of Equations**:

$$

x + y = 20

$$

$$

2x + 4y = 60

$$  

**Answer**: Solving gives $x = 10 $ chickens and $ y = 10 $ cows.

## 5. Practice Problems  
🟢 **Easy**:  
**Problem 1**: A class has 30 students. There are 6 more girls than boys. Find the number of boys and girls.  
*Hint*: Let $ x $ = boys, $ y $ = girls.  

🟡 **Medium**:  
**Problem 2**: A fruit seller sells mangoes and apples. Mangoes cost ₹5 each, apples ₹3 each. A customer buys 10 fruits for ₹40. How many of each did they buy?  

🔴 **Hard**:  
**Problem 3**: A train travels at a constant speed. It covers 200 km in 2 hours and 300 km in 3 hours. Verify if the relationship between distance and time is linear.  

---

**Answers**:  
**Problem 1**:  
1. Equations: $ x + y = 30 $ and $ y = x + 6 $.  
2. Substitute $ y = x + 6 $ into $ x + y = 30 $:

$$

x + x + 6 = 30 \Rightarrow 2x = 24 \Rightarrow x = 12

$$  
3. $y = 12 + 6 = 18 $.  
**Answer**: 12 boys and 18 girls.  

**Problem 2**:  
1. Equations: $ x + y = 10 $ and $ 5x + 3y = 40 $.  
2. Multiply first equation by 3: $ 3x + 3y = 30 $.  
3. Subtract from second equation: $ 2x = 10 \Rightarrow x = 5 $.  
4. $ y = 10 - 5 = 5 $.  
**Answer**: 5 mangoes and 5 apples.  

**Problem 3**:  
1. Speed = Distance / Time.  
2. For 200 km in 2 h: Speed = $ 200 / 2 = 100 $ km/h.  
3. For 300 km in 3 h: Speed = $ 300 / 3 = 100 $ km/h.  
4. Since speed is constant, the relationship is linear.

## 6. Summary  
- A **linear equation in two variables** has the form $ ax + by + c = 0 $.  
- A **pair of linear equations** consists of two such equations.  
- Real-world problems can be translated into systems of equations.  
- The **graphical method** involves plotting lines to find intersections.  
- **Consistent systems** have at least one solution; **inconsistent systems** have none.  
- **Parallel lines** indicate inconsistent systems.

## 8. Student Corner  
### **Common Mistakes**  
- ❌ **Mixing up coefficients**: Confusing $ a_1 $ and $ a_2 $ in equations.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Label equations clearly (e.g., Eqn. 1 and Eqn. 2).  
- ❌ **Ignoring units**: Forgetting to convert currency or measurements.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Always check units before solving.  

### **Exam Tips**  
- ⚠️ Practice forming equations from word problems carefully.  
- ⚠️ Identify whether a system is consistent or inconsistent early in the solution.
