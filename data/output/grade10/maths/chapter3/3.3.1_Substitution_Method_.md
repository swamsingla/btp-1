## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever wondered how to solve a puzzle with two unknowns? Imagine you’re trying to figure out how many apples and oranges you bought if you know the total cost and the price per fruit. This is where the **substitution method** shines!  

In this topic, you’ll learn how to solve pairs of linear equations in two variables by expressing one variable in terms of the other and substituting it into the second equation. This technique simplifies the system into a single-variable equation, making it easier to find solutions. By mastering this method, you’ll gain a powerful tool for tackling real-world problems like age-related puzzles or mixture problems.

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Definition of Substitution Method  
The **substitution method** is an algebraic technique used to solve a system of two linear equations in two variables. It involves **expressing one variable in terms of the other** from one equation and **substituting** this expression into the second equation to solve for the remaining variable. Once found, the value is substituted back to determine the other variable.  

> **Definition**: To solve a system of equations using substitution, isolate one variable in one equation, substitute its expression into the other equation, solve for the isolated variable, and then back-substitute to find the value of the second variable.  

### 3.2 Steps of the Substitution Method  
1. **Choose one equation** and solve for one variable (e.g., $ x $ or $ y $) in terms of the other.  
   - Example: From $ x + 2y = 3 $, solve for $ x $: $ x = 3 - 2y $.  
2. **Substitute** the expression from Step 1 into the other equation to eliminate one variable.  
   - Example: Replace $ x $ in $ 7x - 15y = 2 $ with $ 3 - 2y $.  
3. **Solve the resulting equation** for the remaining variable.  
4. **Back-substitute** the found value into the expression from Step 1 to find the other variable.  
5. **Verify** the solution by plugging both values into the original equations.  

### 3.3 Important Considerations  
- If substitution leads to a **true statement** (e.g., $ 0 = 0 $), the system has **infinitely many solutions**.  
- If substitution results in a **false statement** (e.g., $ 5 = 0 $), the system is **inconsistent** (no solution exists).

## 4. Solved Examples  

### **Example 1:**  
Solve the system:

$$
\begin{cases}
7x - 15y = 2 \quad \text{(1)} \\
x + 2y = 3 \quad \text{(2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution:**  
1. From Equation (2):

$$
   x = 3 - 2y \quad \text{(3)}

$$  
2. Substitute $x = 3 - 2y $ into Equation (1):

$$
   7(3 - 2y) - 15y = 2 \\
   21 - 14y - 15y = 2 \\
   -29y = -19 \implies y = \frac{19}{29}

$$  
3. Substitute $y = \frac{19}{29} $ into Equation (3):

$$
   x = 3 - 2\left(\frac{19}{29}\right) = \frac{87 - 38}{29} = \frac{49}{29}

$$  
**Answer:** $x = \frac{49}{29}, y = \frac{19}{29} $.  

### **Example 2:**  
Aftab’s age is seven times his daughter’s age seven years ago, and three times her future age three years from now. Represent this algebraically and solve.  
**Solution:**  
Let $ s $ = Aftab’s current age, $ t $ = daughter’s current age.  
From the problem:

$$
\begin{cases}
s - 7 = 7(t - 7) \quad \text{(1)} \\
s + 3 = 3(t + 3) \quad \text{(2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
1. Simplify Equation (2):

$$
   s = 3t + 6 \quad \text{(3)}

$$  
2. Substitute $s = 3t + 6 $ into Equation (1):

$$
   (3t + 6) - 7 = 7t - 49 \\
   3t - 1 = 7t - 49 \\
   -4t = -48 \implies t = 12

$$  
3. Substitute $t = 12 $ into Equation (3):

$$
   s = 3(12) + 6 = 42

$$  
**Answer:** Aftab is 42 years old, and his daughter is 12 years old.  

### **Example 3:**  
Solve:

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y = 5 \quad \text{(1)} \\
x - y = 1 \quad \text{(2)}
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution:**  
1. From Equation (2):

$$
   x = y + 1 \quad \text{(3)}

$$  
2. Substitute $x = y + 1 $ into Equation (1):

$$
   2(y + 1) + y = 5 \implies 2y + 2 + y = 5 \implies 3y = 3 \implies y = 1

$$  
3. Substitute $y = 1 $ into Equation (3):

$$
   x = 1 + 1 = 2

$$  
**Answer:** $x = 2, y = 1 $.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1:** Solve:

$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 4 \\
x - y = 2
\end{cases}

$$  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2:** Solve:

$$
\begin{cases}
3x + 2y = 12 \\
x - y = 1
\end{cases}

$$  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3:** Solve:

$$
\begin{cases}
\frac{x}{2} + \frac{y}{3} = 4 \\
\frac{x}{3} - \frac{y}{2} = 1
\end{cases}

$$  

**Answers:**  
**Problem 1:**  
1. From Equation (2): $x = y + 2 $.  
2. Substitute into Equation (1): $ (y + 2) + y = 4 \implies 2y = 2 \implies y = 1 $.  
3. $ x = 1 + 2 = 3 $.  
**Answer:** $ x = 3, y = 1 $.  

**Problem 2:**  
1. From Equation (2): $ x = y + 1 $.  
2. Substitute into Equation (1): $ 3(y + 1) + 2y = 12 \implies 3y + 3 + 2y = 12 \implies 5y = 9 \implies y = \frac{9}{5} $.  
3. $ x = \frac{9}{5} + 1 = \frac{14}{5} $.  
**Answer:** $ x = \frac{14}{5}, y = \frac{9}{5} $.  

**Problem 3:**  
1. Multiply Equation (1) by 6: $ 3x + 2y = 24 $.  
2. Multiply Equation (2) by 6: $ 2x - 3y = 6 $.  
3. Solve the simplified system:  
   - From Equation (1): $ 3x = 24 - 2y \implies x = \frac{24 - 2y}{3} $.  
   - Substitute into Equation (2): $ 2\left(\frac{24 - 2y}{3}\right) - 3y = 6 \implies \frac{48 - 4y}{3} - 3y = 6 $.  
   - Multiply through by 3: $ 48 - 4y - 9y = 18 \implies -13y = -30 \implies y = \frac{30}{13} $.  
4. Substitute $ y = \frac{30}{13} $ into $ x = \frac{24 - 2y}{3} $:

$$
   x = \frac{24 - \frac{60}{13}}{3} = \frac{\frac{312 - 60}{13}}{3} = \frac{252}{39} = \frac{84}{13}.

$$  
**Answer:** $x = \frac{84}{13}, y = \frac{30}{13} $.

## 6. Summary  
- The substitution method solves systems by expressing one variable in terms of the other and substituting.  
- Follow these steps: isolate a variable, substitute, solve, back-substitute, and verify.  
- If substitution yields $ 0 = 0 $, the system has infinite solutions; if it yields a contradiction, no solution exists.  
- Always verify solutions by plugging into both original equations.

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ x = a - by $ | Expressing $ x $ in terms of $ y $ |  
| Substitute $ x $ into second equation | Reduces to single-variable equation |  
| Solve for $ y $ | Obtain numerical value |  
| Back-substitute $ y $ into $ x = a - by $ | Find $ x $ |  
| Verify $ x $ and $ y $ in original equations | Confirm solution |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Mistake:** Forgetting to substitute the entire expression (e.g., missing parentheses).  
  **Correction:** Always enclose expressions in parentheses when substituting.  
- **Mistake:** Arithmetic errors during expansion or simplification.  
  **Correction:** Perform calculations step-by-step and double-check signs.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Tip 1:** Label each step clearly to avoid confusion during exams.  
- **Tip 2:** Always verify your solution in both original equations to catch errors early.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Substitute, Solve, Substitute Back"** – a simple acronym to remember the process.
