# 3.2 Graphical Method of Solution of a Pair of Linear Equations

> **Grade 10** | **Chapter 3: PAIR OF LINEAR EQUATIONS IN TWO VARIABLES**

---

## 1. Prerequisites  
Before studying this topic, students should:  
- Understand the standard form of a linear equation in two variables: $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0 $ and $ a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0 $.  
- Know how to plot linear equations on a coordinate plane by finding intercepts or using slope-intercept form.  
- Recall the concept of **slope** as the ratio of rise over run ($ m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} $) and its role in determining the steepness of a line.  

---

## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever tried to solve two puzzles at once? Imagine two friends planning a trip: one says, *"I’ll pay $ x $ dollars for gas and $ y $ dollars for food,"* while the other says, *"I’ll pay $ 2x $ dollars for gas and $ y $ dollars for food."* How would you figure out how much each person owes? This is where systems of linear equations come into play!  

In this topic, we explore the **graphical method** of solving pairs of linear equations. By analyzing the lines represented by these equations, we determine whether they intersect (one solution), overlap (infinitely many solutions), or never meet (no solution). This method helps visualize relationships between variables and classify systems as *consistent* or *inconsistent*.  

---

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Types of Solutions  
> **Consistent Pair**: A pair of equations that has at least one solution.  
> **Inconsistent Pair**: A pair of equations that has no solution.  
> **Dependent Pair**: A special case of a consistent pair where the equations represent the same line, resulting in infinitely many solutions.  

When solving graphically, the behavior of the lines determines the nature of the solution:  
- **Intersecting Lines**: Unique solution exists where the lines cross.  
- **Parallel Lines**: No solution since they never meet.  
- **Coincident Lines**: Infinitely many solutions because the lines are identical.  

### 3.2 Ratio of Coefficients  
The source text provides a critical insight: the relationship between the coefficients determines the lines’ behavior. Let’s analyze this:  

#### Case 1: Intersecting Lines  
If $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2} $, the lines intersect at exactly one point.  
**Example**: For $ x - 2y = 0 $ and $ 3x + 4y - 20 = 0 $:  
- $ \frac{1}{3} \neq \frac{-2}{4} $ → Lines intersect.  

#### Case 2: Coincident Lines  
If $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} $, the lines overlap completely.  
**Example**: For $ 2x + 3y - 9 = 0 $ and $ 4x + 6y - 18 = 0 $:  
- $ \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{-9}{-18} = \frac{1}{2} $ → Lines coincide.  

#### Case 3: Parallel Lines  
If $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2} $, the lines are parallel but distinct.  
**Example**: For $ x + 2y - 4 = 0 $ and $ 2x + 4y - 12 = 0 $:  
- $ \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} \neq \frac{-4}{-12} = \frac{1}{3} $ → Lines are parallel.  

These conditions allow us to classify systems without graphing.  

---

## 4. Solved Examples  

### Example 1: Consistent System with One Solution  
**Problem**: Solve the equations $ x + 3y = 6 $ and $ 2x - 3y = 12 $ graphically.  
**Solution**:  
1. Rewrite both equations in slope-intercept form ($ y = mx + b $):  
   - $ x + 3y = 6 $ → $ y = -\frac{1}{3}x + 2 $  
   - $ 2x - 3y = 12 $ → $ y = \frac{2}{3}x - 4 $  
2. Plot both lines:  
   - First line passes through $ (0, 2) $ and $ (6, 0) $.  
   - Second line passes through $ (0, -4) $ and $ (6, 0) $.  
3. Identify the intersection point: Both lines intersect at $ (6, 0) $.  
**Answer**: The solution is $ x = 6 $, $ y = 0 $.  

### Example 2: Inconsistent System  
**Problem**: Determine the nature of the system $ x + 2y = 4 $ and $ 2x + 4y = 12 $.  
**Solution**:  
1. Compare coefficients:  
   - $ \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} \neq \frac{4}{12} $ → Parallel lines.  
2. Conclude: No solution exists because the lines are parallel.  
**Answer**: The system is **inconsistent** and has **no solution**.  

### Example 3: Dependent System  
**Problem**: Verify if $ 3x + 6y = 12 $ and $ x + 2y = 4 $ represent the same line.  
**Solution**:  
1. Simplify the first equation: Divide by 3 → $ x + 2y = 4 $, which matches the second equation.  
2. Conclusion: The equations are coincident; infinitely many solutions exist.  
**Answer**: The system is **dependent** and **consistent** with infinitely many solutions.

## 5. Practice Problems  

🟢 **Easy**:  
1. Classify the system $ 2x + y = 5 $ and $ 4x + 2y = 10 $ as consistent, inconsistent, or dependent.  
2. Find the solution graphically for $ x + y = 4 $ and $ x - y = 0 $.  

🟡 **Medium**:  
3. Determine the nature of the system $ 3x - 2y = 6 $ and $ 6x - 4y = 12 $.  
4. Graphically solve $ y = 2x + 1 $ and $ y = -x + 4 $.  

🔴 **Hard**:  
5. Given the system $ ax + by = c $ and $ dx + ey = f $, derive the condition for the lines to be parallel.  

**Answers**:  
1. **Dependent** (since $ \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{10} $).  
2. **Solution**: Intersection at $ (2, 2) $.  
3. **Dependent** (coefficients are proportional but constants differ).  
4. **Solution**: Intersection at $ (1, 3) $.  
5. **Condition**: $ \frac{a}{d} = \frac{b}{e} \neq \frac{c}{f} $.  

---

## 6. Summary  
- A **consistent** system has either one solution (intersecting lines) or infinitely many (coincident lines).  
- An **inconsistent** system has no solution (parallel lines).  
- To classify systems, compare $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} $, $ \frac{b_1}{b_2} $, and $ \frac{c_1}{c_2} $.  
- Graphical representation reveals the relationship between lines and their solutions.  

---

## 7. Key Formulas  
| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2} $ | Intersecting lines (unique solution) |  
| $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} $ | Coincident lines (infinitely many solutions) |  
| $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2} $ | Parallel lines (no solution) |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- ❌ **Misinterpreting Ratios**: Forgetting to check all three ratios ($ a $, $ b $, $ c $) when classifying systems.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Always compare $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} $, $ \frac{b_1}{b_2} $, and $ \frac{c_1}{c_2} $.  
- ❌ **Incorrect Slopes**: Mixing up positive and negative slopes when plotting lines.  
  ✅ **Fix**: Use the slope formula $ m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} $ carefully.  

### Exam Tips  
- ⚠️ **Sketch the Lines First**: Even if you’re confident in algebra, drawing rough graphs saves time during exams.  
- ⚠️ **Check for Coincidence**: If two equations simplify to the same line, mark it as dependent.  

### Memory Aid  
**"CIP" Rule**:  
- **C**oincident: All ratios equal → Infinite solutions.  
- **I**ntersecting: Ratios unequal → One solution.  
- **P**arallel: Two ratios equal, third different → No solution.
