## 1. Prerequisites  
Before diving into algebraic methods for solving pairs of linear equations, students should already understand:  
- **Linear equations**: The general form $ ax + by + c = 0 $, where $ a $, $ b $, and $ c $ are constants, and $ x $, $ y $ are variables.  
- **Variables and coefficients**: Recognize how variables (like $ x $, $ y $) and coefficients (numbers multiplying variables) interact in equations.  
- **Basic operations**: Ability to manipulate equations through addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

## 2. Introduction  
Have you ever tried solving a puzzle where two pieces depend on each other? Imagine planning a road trip where you need to calculate both distance and time simultaneously. Such situations often require solving **pairs of linear equations**, where two conditions must hold true at the same time. While graphical methods work for simple cases, they become impractical when solutions involve fractions or decimals. This section introduces **algebraic methods**—specifically, the **Substitution Method** and **Elimination Method**—to find precise solutions efficiently. By mastering these techniques, you’ll gain tools to tackle real-world problems with confidence!

## 3. Explanation  

### 3.1 Substitution Method  
> **Definition**: The substitution method solves a system of equations by expressing one variable in terms of the other and substituting it into the second equation.  

#### Steps to Solve Using Substitution:  
1. **Solve one equation for one variable** (e.g., solve for $ y $ in terms of $ x $).  
2. **Substitute the expression** into the second equation to eliminate one variable.  
3. **Solve the resulting equation** for the remaining variable.  
4. **Plug the found value** back into the expression from Step 1 to find the other variable.  

#### Example:  
Consider the equations:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
2x + y = 5 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
x - y = 1 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
From Equation (2): $ y = x - 1 $. Substitute $ y $ into Equation (1):

$$  
2x + (x - 1) = 5 \implies 3x - 1 = 5 \implies 3x = 6 \implies x = 2

$$  
Substitute $ x = 2 $ into $ y = x - 1 $:

$$  
y = 2 - 1 = 1

$$  
**Solution**: $ x = 2 $, $ y = 1 $.  

### 3.2 Elimination Method  
> **Definition**: The elimination method solves a system by adding or subtracting equations to eliminate one variable, simplifying the system to a single-variable equation.  

#### Steps to Solve Using Elimination:  
1. **Align the equations** so like terms are vertically aligned.  
2. **Multiply one or both equations** by constants to make coefficients of one variable equal in magnitude.  
3. **Add or subtract the equations** to eliminate one variable.  
4. **Solve for the remaining variable**.  
5. **Substitute the value** back into one of the original equations to find the other variable.  

#### Example:  
Using the same equations:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
2x + y = 5 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
x - y = 1 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
Add Equations (1) and (2) to eliminate $ y $:

$$  
(2x + y) + (x - y) = 5 + 1 \implies 3x = 6 \implies x = 2

$$  
Substitute $ x = 2 $ into Equation (2):

$$  
2 - y = 1 \implies y = 1

$$  
**Solution**: $ x = 2 $, $ y = 1 $.  

### 3.3 Conditions for Solutions  
> **Important Theorem**: A system of two linear equations has:  
- **One unique solution** if the lines intersect (non-parallel).  
- **No solution** if the lines are parallel (coefficients of $ x $ and $ y $ are proportional, but constants are not).  
- **Infinitely many solutions** if the equations represent the same line (all coefficients and constants are proportional).  

**Proof**: Consider two equations $ a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0 $ and $ a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0 $.  
- If $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2} $, the lines intersect at one point.  
- If $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2} $, the lines are parallel.  
- If $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} $, the lines coincide.

## 4. Solved Examples  

### **Example 1:**  
Solve:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
3x + 2y = 12 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
x - y = 1 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
From Equation (2): $ x = y + 1 $. Substitute into Equation (1):

$$  
3(y + 1) + 2y = 12 \implies 3y + 3 + 2y = 12 \implies 5y = 9 \implies y = \frac{9}{5}

$$  
Substitute $ y = \frac{9}{5} $ into $ x = y + 1 $:

$$  
x = \frac{9}{5} + 1 = \frac{14}{5}

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = \frac{14}{5} $, $ y = \frac{9}{5} $.  

### **Example 2:**  
Solve:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
2x + 3y = 11 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
4x - y = 1 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
Multiply Equation (2) by 3: $ 12x - 3y = 3 $. Add to Equation (1):

$$  
(2x + 3y) + (12x - 3y) = 11 + 3 \implies 14x = 14 \implies x = 1

$$  
Substitute $ x = 1 $ into Equation (2):

$$  
4(1) - y = 1 \implies y = 3

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = 1 $, $ y = 3 $.  

### **Example 3:**  
Solve:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
5x - 2y = 4 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
3x + 4y = 10 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
Multiply Equation (1) by 2: $ 10x - 4y = 8 $. Add to Equation (2):

$$  
(10x - 4y) + (3x + 4y) = 8 + 10 \implies 13x = 18 \implies x = \frac{18}{13}

$$  
Substitute $ x = \frac{18}{13} $ into Equation (2):

$$  
3\left(\frac{18}{13}\right) + 4y = 10 \implies \frac{54}{13} + 4y = \frac{130}{13} \implies 4y = \frac{76}{13} \implies y = \frac{19}{13}

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = \frac{18}{13} $, $ y = \frac{19}{13} $.  

### **Example 4:**  
Check for consistency:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
2x + 3y = 6 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
4x + 6y = 12 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
Equation (2) is exactly twice Equation (1). Thus, the equations represent the same line. **Infinite solutions** exist.  

### **Example 5:**  
Check for inconsistency:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
2x + 3y = 6 \quad \text{(1)} \\  
4x + 6y = 15 \quad \text{(2)}  
\end{cases}

$$  
**Solution**:  
Multiply Equation (1) by 2: $ 4x + 6y = 12 $. Subtract Equation (2):

$$  
(4x + 6y) - (4x + 6y) = 12 - 15 \implies 0 = -3

$$  
This is impossible, so **no solution** exists.

## 5. Practice Problems  

### 🟢 Easy  
**Problem 1:**  
Solve:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
x + y = 5 \\  
2x - y = 4  
\end{cases}

$$  

### 🟡 Medium  
**Problem 2:**  
Solve:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
3x - 2y = 8 \\  
x + 4y = 6  
\end{cases}

$$  

### 🔴 Hard  
**Problem 3:**  
Solve:

$$  
\begin{cases}  
5x + 3y = 19 \\  
2x - 4y = 6  
\end{cases}

$$  

---

### Answers:  

**Problem 1:**  
**Solution**:  
From Equation (1): $ x = 5 - y $. Substitute into Equation (2):

$$  
2(5 - y) - y = 4 \implies 10 - 2y - y = 4 \implies -3y = -6 \implies y = 2

$$  
Substitute $ y = 2 $ into $ x = 5 - y $:

$$  
x = 5 - 2 = 3

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = 3 $, $ y = 2 $.  

**Problem 2:**  
**Solution**:  
Multiply Equation (2) by 2: $ 2x + 8y = 12 $. Subtract Equation (1):

$$  
(2x + 8y) - (3x - 2y) = 12 - 8 \implies -x + 10y = 4 \implies x = 10y - 4

$$  
Substitute $ x = 10y - 4 $ into Equation (2):

$$  
(10y - 4) + 4y = 6 \implies 14y = 10 \implies y = \frac{5}{7}

$$  
Substitute $ y = \frac{5}{7} $ into $ x = 10y - 4 $:

$$  
x = 10\left(\frac{5}{7}\right) - 4 = \frac{50}{7} - \frac{28}{7} = \frac{22}{7}

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = \frac{22}{7} $, $ y = \frac{5}{7} $.  

**Problem 3:**  
**Solution**:  
Multiply Equation (1) by 4: $ 20x + 12y = 76 $. Multiply Equation (2) by 3: $ 6x - 12y = 18 $. Add both:

$$  
20x + 12y + 6x - 12y = 76 + 18 \implies 26x = 94 \implies x = \frac{47}{13}

$$  
Substitute $ x = \frac{47}{13} $ into Equation (2):

$$  
2\left(\frac{47}{13}\right) - 4y = 6 \implies \frac{94}{13} - 4y = \frac{78}{13} \implies -4y = -\frac{16}{13} \implies y = \frac{4}{13}

$$  
**Answer**: $ x = \frac{47}{13} $, $ y = \frac{4}{13} $.

## 6. Summary  
- **Substitution Method**: Solve one equation for a variable, substitute into the second equation, and solve for the remaining variable.  
- **Elimination Method**: Align equations, multiply to eliminate a variable, solve the resulting equation, and back-substitute.  
- **Conditions for Solutions**: Unique, no, or infinitely many solutions depending on proportionality of coefficients.  
- **Key Steps**: Always verify solutions by plugging values back into original equations.  
- **Formulas**: Standard form $ ax + by + c = 0 $; elimination and substitution steps.  

---  

## 7. Key Formulas  

| Formula | Description |  
|--------|-------------|  
| $ ax + by + c = 0 $ | General form of a linear equation. |  
| $ y = mx + c $ | Slope-intercept form. |  
| Substitution Step: $ y = kx + m $ | Express one variable in terms of the other. |  
| Elimination Step: $ a_1x + b_1y = c_1 $, $ a_2x + b_2y = c_2 $ | Align equations for elimination. |  
| Condition for Infinite Solutions: $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} $ | Lines coincide. |  
| Condition for No Solution: $ \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2} $ | Parallel lines. |

## 8. Student Corner  

### Common Mistakes  
- **Error 1**: Forgetting to substitute the entire expression into the second equation.  
  **Fix**: Replace the variable in both sides of the equation.  
- **Error 2**: Miscalculating signs during elimination.  
  **Fix**: Double-check arithmetic and track negative signs carefully.  

### Exam Tips  
- **Tip 1**: Always verify solutions by plugging values into both equations.  
- **Tip 2**: When coefficients are large, simplify equations before applying methods.  

### Memory Aid  
**"Substitute First, Eliminate Later"** – Remember to express one variable first (substitution) or align coefficients (elimination) before proceeding.
